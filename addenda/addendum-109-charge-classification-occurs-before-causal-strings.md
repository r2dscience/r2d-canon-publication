## Abstract

**We can read a horizon without seeing through it.** We then define objects to account for the read we retain: persistent particles, causal strings, or separately occupied worlds. The first error occurs when a defined object is assigned the *physical identity* of the unreadable side without a boundary that makes that identity readable. A second error assigns the projected object causal priority over the boundary relation from which it was inferred. R²D starts with the boundary and its count relation. It does not need to derive dynamics for the projected object to establish either distinction.

The quantum and gravitational horizons hide opposite recursive reads. At the quantum horizon, recurrence survives while subordinate distinguishability is unreadable; a persistent miniature particle is introduced to picture that subordinate side. At the gravitational horizon, area and the horizon count survive while directed *enclosing* recurrence is unreadable. A string or D-brane description can be used to calculate the multiplicity associated with that horizon without observing the missing enclosing relation. A valid calculation does not turn its object labels into occupied states of an unreadable domain.

The Strominger–Vafa calculation counts a fixed-charge BPS sector in a weak-coupling description of certain five-dimensional extremal black holes. Its leading logarithmic growth agrees with the horizon area entropy in the large-charge regime. This is a substantial count correspondence. It does not establish that the selected string-state labels are the physically occupied horizon realizations, make strings the primitive causes of that state, or provide a direct observation of the unreadable enclosing recurrence.

R²D separates three objects often called “the black-hole microstates”: the fixed-charge BPS sector selected in a string representation, the protected weighted index used to carry its leading count across descriptions, and the complete compatible realizations classified by an operative horizon boundary $B_H$. The first is a mathematically defined sector, the second is a signed count, and the third is an R²D compatibility fiber. Their leading entropies can agree without the three objects being identical.

The formal result is decisive about dependency: a state counted *under a classification* does not primitively define the classification that makes it count. The physical question is more specific: why does this BPS sector read the multiplicity of the horizon state? Conservation of the charge labels, protected counting across the relevant descriptions, and the leading area-entropy match supply the count-level bridge. They do not, by themselves, construct an exact identification of individual BPS state labels with complete horizon-compatible realizations. R²D does not undertake to derive equations governing invented particles or strings as primitive mechanisms of an unreadable side of either horizon.

**The calculation counts. Equating its counted state labels with physically occupied horizon states is the first projection error. Making those projected states the causes of horizon classification is the second.**

## 109.1. The Calculation and Its Actual Domain

Consider the original example of type IIB string theory compactified on $K3\times S^1$, with five noncompact spacetime dimensions. Fix the full conserved charge data

$$
q=(Q_F,Q_H),
$$

where $Q_F$ denotes the relevant Ramond–Ramond charge vector and $Q_H$ is the second charge identified with momentum $P$ in the dual description. The invariant $Q_F^2$ enters the leading formulas, but fixing only that invariant is not the same instruction as fixing a particular charge sector. The black-hole description also specifies a BPS, extremal sector and the regime in which a regular horizon exists. In the original example, both types of charge must be nonzero for the finite-area solution at issue. [1]

In the weak-coupling worldvolume representation, let $\mathcal H$ be the relevant Hilbert space. Its selected sector is

$$
\mathcal H^{\mathrm{BPS}}_q
=\bigl\{\psi\in\mathcal H:
\widehat Q_F\psi=Q_F^{(q)}\psi,\quad
\widehat P\psi=Q_H\psi,\quad
\psi\text{ satisfies the specified BPS condition}\bigr\}.
$$

The supersymmetric worldvolume description takes the appropriate right-moving sector to its ground state and counts allowed left-moving excitations at momentum $P=Q_H$. The dimension

$$
d_q=\dim\mathcal H^{\mathrm{BPS}}_q
$$

counts independent states of that selected representation. It does not count every normalized vector in the subspace as a separate physical microstate: there are infinitely many such vectors even when $d_q$ is finite. Nor does an arbitrary choice of basis automatically supply the complete joint realizations required by an R²D horizon fiber. [1]

The paper uses the elliptic genus, a protected *weighted* count, to control the leading growth. Let $I_q$ denote its appropriate charge coefficient. A signed or weighted index need not equal the unsigned number $d_q$, and $d_q$ itself need not remain unchanged with all moduli. In the regime analyzed, the two counts have the same leading logarithmic growth. Their difference matters when anyone asserts an **exact** horizon multiplicity. [1]

The large-charge results are, in the conventions of the original calculation,

$$
\ln d_q\ \sim\
2\pi\sqrt{Q_H\!\left(\frac{Q_F^2}{2}+1\right)},
\qquad
S_{\mathrm{BH}}(q)
=\frac{A_H(q)}{4G_5}
=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{Q_H Q_F^2}{2}}.
$$

The expressions agree at leading order when the relevant charges are large. This is the result to preserve. Neither an exact equality $d_q=e^{A_H/(4G_5)}$ for all charges nor a physical identity between each weak-coupling state and one geometric horizon realization follows from it. [1]

## 109.2. The Same Projection Error at Opposite Horizons

The two horizons are defined by what a finite observer can still read. Part III, Chapter 10 places them on opposite sides of a thermodynamic interval in which adjacent-boundary recurrence and distinguishability can be read jointly. [4]

| | Quantum horizon | Gravitational horizon |
|---|---|---|
| Retained read | Recurrence, including a frequency or spectral relation | Horizon distinguishability, including area and its entropy read |
| Unreadable directed side | Associated subordinate distinguishability and continuous trajectory geometry | Directed recurrence connecting the horizon to a recursively enclosing occurrence |
| Explanatory object introduced beyond the read | A persistent miniature particle and its supposed trajectory | Strings or D-branes whose selected count is promoted into a causal mechanism of the horizon |
| What the equations may legitimately do | Represent measured quantum relations and predict boundary-specific outcomes | Count a selected BPS sector and reproduce the leading horizon entropy |
| First projection error | Assign an independently persistent occupied particle to the unreadable subordinate domain | Identify a string-state label with a physically occupied horizon realization or the unreadable enclosing recurrence from the count match alone |
| Second causal inversion | Make that particle the primitive cause of the enclosing quantum state | Make that string the primitive cause of horizon statehood or enclosing organization |

This is the symmetry: a retained observation motivates a representation extending beyond the available read. The first mistake is to call a state of that representation *physically occupied on the unreadable side* merely because the representation works. Making the projected state the causal primitive compounds that mistake. In neither case does R²D deny the retained observation or require an equation of motion for the imagined object to establish the direction of the count relation. Part III explicitly describes the quantum horizon as a loss toward subordinate recursion and the black-hole horizon as a loss toward enclosing recursion. Addendum 102 identifies the persistent microscopic particle as an imagined explanatory object at the first horizon. [4, 5]

Addendum 108 identifies a third projection: branches of a quantum representation become purportedly *separately occupied worlds*. That assignment of physical occupation is already the error; no further claim that branches cause the universal state is needed to make it an error. The Everettian world claim is not itself the unreadable enclosing recurrence of a black hole, and it is not the same physical horizon as the BPS example. It shares the invalid promotion from represented possibility to occupied state without a boundary-defined occurrence relation. [6]

The symmetry does **not** make a BPS string state literally an enclosing state. In the calculation below, it is a state of a selected *weak-coupling representation* used to read the entropy of a horizon. The hidden gravitational-side quantity in Part III is specifically **directed enclosing recurrence**. The horizon itself, its area, and its count relation are not declared unknowable. String-state degeneracy matches a retained *count*; it is not a direct observation or a derivation of that missing recurrence. Conflating those roles would repeat the very projection error under examination.

R²D need not derive worldvolume string equations to establish its own count architecture, just as it need not derive trajectories of independently persistent miniature particles to establish the quantum boundary. The models' successful calculations can be compared with the surviving boundary reads. **A mathematical definition of a particle, string, or world does not make that object a physically occupied state at the boundary.**

**The first EP projection is ontological.** A particle path, string state, or wavefunction branch is defined in a mathematical representation and then substituted for physical state identity. The definition supplies no occupied particle beneath the unchanged quantum read, no identified string realization of the horizon, and no second occupied world. The observed spectra, charge fluxes, detector records, and horizon area are physical reads. The invented object is not made real by the calculation that fits those reads.

## 109.3. Two Boundaries, Two Classifications

Call the boundary at which the conserved charges are classified $B_Q$. Its job in this comparison is to define the charge sector $q$. The original black-hole charges are expressed through fluxes on an enclosing $S^3$ in the five-dimensional solution. This charge read does not, merely by using an enclosing surface, identify $B_Q$ with the horizon boundary. [1]

Call the proposed R²D horizon count boundary $B_H$. At $B_H$, the relevant readable state $i_H(q)$ includes the selected horizon-classification data: the charge sector, extremal solution class, and horizon area read, under their stated physical conditions. If the R²D mapping is valid, it has a domain of complete compatible subordinate joint realizations $\mathfrak M_H$ and a classification

$$
\pi_H:\mathfrak M_H\longrightarrow\mathcal M_H,
\qquad
F_H(q)=\pi_H^{-1}\bigl(i_H(q)\bigr),
\qquad
W_H(q)=|F_H(q)|.
$$

This is what it would mean for $B_H$ to *own* a compatible realization count. The boundary defines the admissible domain and the equivalence of its readable horizon states. It does not follow that a D-brane basis vector, a signed term in an index, or an asymptotic charge label is already an element of $F_H(q)$.

An ordering can therefore be stated without merging the boundaries:

$$
\begin{aligned}
B_Q &: \text{select the conserved sector }q,\\
\mathcal H_q^{\mathrm{BPS}} &: \text{count the selected weak-coupling states},\\
B_H &: \text{classify complete realizations of }i_H(q).
\end{aligned}
$$

The first two lines make the string count well-defined. The third specifies the R²D horizon read. A physical correspondence relating the lines is an additional statement; treating the labels as interchangeable hides precisely the step this addendum investigates.

## 109.4. What the Entropy Match Supplies

The original calculation supplies three linked reasons for comparing the BPS sector with the horizon:

1. **Matching charge and BPS conditions.** The worldvolume sector has the charges assigned to the extremal black-hole solution, with $P=Q_H$ in the dual description.
2. **A transportable count.** The protected weighted index retains the relevant count information through changes of the description; the unsigned degeneracy and index have the same leading growth in the calculation's regime.
3. **A matching horizon read.** In the large-charge domain where the five-dimensional area calculation applies, that leading logarithmic growth agrees with $A_H/(4G_5)$. [1]

In R²D, if the area law is the calibrated read of the horizon multiplicity, these relations give the proposed *leading count correspondence*

$$
\ln |I_q| \sim\ \ln d_q\ \sim\
S_H(q)=\ln W_H(q)\ \sim\
\frac{A_H(q)}{4G_5}.
$$

Here $I_q\ne0$ is understood where its logarithm appears, and every $\sim$ denotes leading entropy growth in the specified regime. The area law is a physical calibration in this chain. This equation says what numerical relation the protected sector reads. It is not an exact equality of sets, of individual state identities, or of a signed index with a positive integer fiber size.

Why should this be called a horizon count at all? Because the *same specified conserved sector* organizes a regular horizon state, and the protected weak-description count has the *same leading entropy* as that horizon's area read. Those facts make the BPS calculation a substantive read of horizon-compatible multiplicity rather than an arbitrary numerical coincidence. They establish its leading count role in the model. They do not yet tell us which complete physical realization in $F_H(q)$ corresponds to any individual weak-description label.

## 109.5. The Stronger Fiber Identification

An exact R²D physical identification would require more than equal leading logarithms. One would need to define the horizon's compatible realization domain independently of the string basis, specify the physical relation between charge selection at $B_Q$ and horizon classification at $B_H$, and show that the counted distinctions survive with the required meaning under that relation.

For example, let $E_q$ denote physically distinguishable state classes selected in the weak BPS description, *after* an appropriate physical equivalence relation has been specified. A proposed correspondence $\Phi_q$ would have to satisfy, for each class $e$ it maps,

$$
\Phi_q(e)\in\mathfrak M_H,
\qquad
\pi_H\bigl(\Phi_q(e)\bigr)=i_H(q).
$$

An exact identification of the unsigned counts would additionally require a justified match between distinguishability classes in $E_q$ and all the elements of $F_H(q)$. It cannot be obtained by choosing a convenient Hilbert-space basis, declaring each vector a complete horizon realization, or replacing the fiber's cardinality with a signed index. At leading order, a weaker correspondence of count growth is sufficient and is what the original entropy result supplies.

This formulation does **not** demand that a particular weakly coupled D-brane configuration remain the same readable object after the black-hole description becomes appropriate. Recursive replacement permits distinct physical reads at distinct boundaries. What must persist for the count comparison is the justified relation among the charge sector, protected count, and horizon state—not the literal identity of their pictures.

## 109.6. The Formal Causal Result

There are two distinct steps by which a valid count can become an invalid physical account. At any defined R²D boundary $B$,

$$
\pi_B:\mathfrak M_B\to\mathcal M_B,
\qquad
W_{B,i}=|\pi_B^{-1}(i)|.
$$

An element of $\pi_B^{-1}(i)$ is a compatible realization **because it is classified by** $\pi_B$ as belonging to $i$. A label in a different representation cannot be declared an *occupied* member of this fiber merely because the two representations have equal counts. This is the first type change: a mathematical count is promoted to an assertion about physical state identity and occupation at $B$. An individual member of the fiber also cannot, *as that member*, primitively define the domain, classification, and macrostate whose compatible realization it is. That second inversion assigns causal priority to what already depends on the enclosing state definition. These are distinct errors. Part I and Addendum 105 establish the boundary-relative dependency. [2, 3]

The BPS calculation already displays a version of that dependency within its own representation: first specify the compactification, full charge sector, BPS condition, momentum level, and allowed worldvolume state space; then count. A state excluded by those conditions is not part of the answer, however string-like its description. **The calculation does not start with unclassified causal strings and recover the meaning of its own charge sector from their occurrence.**

The further R²D physical assignment to $B_H$ is justified at the *leading count* level as described in §109.4. To go from that count correspondence to “this particular BPS string state is an occupied horizon microstate” without a physical state-identity relation already crosses the first boundary. Calling that projected string the primitive cause of $i_H(q)$ then reverses the classification on which its proposed identity as a *horizon microstate* depends. Claiming that a string state directly reveals the enclosing recurrence made unreadable by the gravitational horizon makes another unsupported physical identification. None of these errors requires rejecting the BPS calculation.

The conclusion is therefore exact about its scope:

$$
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
\text{A successful count does not establish occupied state identity}\\
\text{across an unreadable boundary;}\\
\text{a counted realization does not primitively cause its classification.}
\end{gathered}}
$$

No claim about a new quantitative correction to the Strominger–Vafa formula is needed for this conclusion.

## 109.7. Five Dimensions and the Horizon Read

The calculation is formulated with five **noncompact spacetime dimensions**. Its regular horizon has a spatial $S^3$ section; the three-dimensional area measure of that section enters $A_H/(4G_5)$. Five-dimensional spacetime is a feature of the selected physical model, not an intrinsic dimensionality assigned to the abstract R²D count boundary $B_H$. [1]

R²D's four-dimensional spherical projections in Addenda 41–43, including $A=4\pi r^2$ and any proposed radial Planck-step construction, cannot simply be imported into this five-dimensional case. The count definition $W_H=|\pi_H^{-1}(i_H)|$ is dimensionless; its geometric area read depends on the stated physical mapping. [4]

This matters for the string claim. The compactification and the five-dimensional horizon geometry select which calculation is under comparison. Neither the number of compactified dimensions nor the existence of a successful string representation makes a string the formal primitive of $B_H$.

## 109.8. Result and Status

**Formal result.** A boundary-defined compatible realization does not primitively define the enclosing classification that gives it identity. The selected BPS sector also depends on fixed charge and supersymmetry conditions before its degeneracy can be counted. [2, 3]

**Established calculation.** In the specified five-dimensional extremal system, the protected BPS calculation and horizon area entropy agree in leading large-charge growth. The index is weighted, the unsigned degeneracy is a separate number, and the leading result does not identify each counted state with a horizon fiber element. [1]

**R²D physical bridge.** The charge-preserving, BPS-protected leading entropy correspondence motivates reading the BPS calculation as a representation of the multiplicity at $B_H$. The full relation from $B_Q$ to $B_H$, and an exact identification of compatible realization classes, remain to be specified if an exact fiber claim is made. R²D's formal organizing-causality result does not depend on that stronger identification.

**Paired horizon result.** Quantum physics can represent an unreadable subordinate side with particles; the gravitational calculation can represent a horizon count with strings while the directed enclosing side remains unreadable. R²D does not claim to derive primitive object dynamics for either invented side. The surviving frequency read and surviving area read give the respective comparisons. [4, 5]

**First projection error.** A string-state label or protected-index contribution is made an occupied state of the horizon or its unreadable enclosing side solely because the count agrees. The corresponding particle and Everettian errors assign persistent subordinate identity or plural world occupation where the operative read has not established it. [5, 6]

**Second causal inversion.** The newly projected object is made the primitive cause of the enclosing statehood, count relation, or recurrence it was introduced to explain. The string count itself entails neither projection.

**The fixed-charge count is real mathematics. Its leading horizon match is real physics within the specified model. Neither result makes a string-state label an occupied horizon microstate or turns such a projected object into the cause of horizon classification.**
