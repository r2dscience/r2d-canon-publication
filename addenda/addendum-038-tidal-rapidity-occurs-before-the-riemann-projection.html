Addendum 38 — Tidal Rapidity Occurs Before the Riemann Projection

Peer Separation, Geodesic Deviation, and the Continuous Geometry of Differential Enclosure

Addendum 38 — Tidal Rapidity Occurs Before the Riemann Projection

Abstract

General relativity represents tidal gravity through spacetime curvature. Neighboring freely falling trajectories satisfy the geodesic-deviation equation

\[\frac{D^2\zeta^\mu}{D\tau^2}=-R^\mu{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}u^\nu\zeta^\rho u^\sigma.\]

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) asks what physical distinction must already exist before that geometric representation can be assigned.

Addendum 37 identified the finite locally irreducible gravitational relation. For neighboring freely falling boundaries Ba and Bb ,

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\equiv\delta_{ab}(\Delta_j\bar\Xi_G)\]

measures the failure of the enclosing gravitational rapidity change to remain common mode across one finite realization step.

If

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}=0,\]

the gravitational relation remains locally common mode.

If

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\ne0,\]

the neighboring free-fall references acquire different motion changes. Tidal distinguishability therefore exists before continuous spacetime curvature is introduced.

The present addendum asks how that finite peer relation acquires its mature geometric representation.

First assign neighboring peers a continuous physical separation vector

\[\zeta^\mu.\]

In a local freely falling orthonormal frame, infinitesimal relative rapidity components \(\delta\xi^{\hat i}\) satisfy, to first order,

\[\frac{D\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau}=c\,\delta\xi^{\hat i}.\]

Therefore,

\[\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=c\frac{D\,\delta\xi^{\hat i}}{D\tau}.\]

The continuous limit of the finite tidal-rapidity relation is thus relative free-fall acceleration.

For infinitesimal separation, assume smooth local projection:

\[\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}\zeta^{\hat j}.\]

The operator

\[\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}\]

is the local tidal map.

General relativity represents the same observable relation as

\[\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=-c^2R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat j\hat0}\zeta^{\hat j}.\]

Therefore,

\[\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}=-c^2R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat j\hat0}.\]

The electric tidal part of the Riemann tensor is therefore the continuous geometric representation of a prior differential free-fall relation.

This does not mean that one local tidal map determines the entire Riemann tensor in a general spacetime. The remaining curvature components require additional directional, transport, or observer information. Nor does position-dependent rapidity alone imply curvature. Accelerated coordinates can vary through flat spacetime.

Curvature appears when neighboring local Lorentz relations cannot be integrated into one common inertial geometry. In the continuous metric projection this nonclosure is represented by the Riemann tensor:

\[[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho=R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma.\]

The logical order is therefore

finite peer rapidity difference

⇓

relative free-fall acceleration

⇓

tidal operator

⇓

geodesic deviation

⇓

Riemann projection.

Thus:

tidal rapidity occurs before the Riemann projection.

1. Addendum 37 Established Gravity Before Curvature

Addendum 37 did not begin with a metric.

It began with neighboring freely falling reference relations.

For local boundary Ba ,

\[\xi_{a\mid E}=\eta_a+\bar\Xi_{G,a},\]

where

\[\eta_a\]

is the local differential rapidity relative to the immediate gravitational background and

\[\bar\Xi_{G,a}\]

is the rapidity of that background relative to a larger enclosure.

For free fall,

\[\Delta_j\eta_a=0.\]

Thus the gravitational realization is carried by

\[\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

For two neighboring peers,

\[B_a,\qquad B_b,\]

the locally irreducible gravitational distinction is

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}=\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,b}-\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

This quantity exists before curvature.

2. The Finite Tidal Relation Is the Primitive Observable

The gravitational relation remains common mode if

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}=0.\]

It becomes locally distinguishable if

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\ne0.\]

Thus the primitive physical question is not initially:

What is the curvature at a point?

It is:

Do neighboring freely falling boundaries acquire the same enclosing rapidity change?

That question requires neither a differentiable manifold nor a curvature tensor.

3. Geometry Enters When Peer Distinction Receives Continuous Coordinates

To map the finite peer relation into continuous geometry, assign the local free-fall comparison a smooth physical representation.

Choose Ba as the central freely falling reference.

Let the neighboring boundary Bb be represented by a separation vector

\[\zeta^\mu.\]

This vector does not create the distinction between a and b.

It represents it.

Therefore:

peer distinction → separation coordinate.

4. The Separation Vector Is Boundary Relative

The vector

\[\zeta^\mu\]

is defined relative to the selected central free-fall reference.

It is not an absolute displacement in primitive spacetime.

In the local freely falling orthonormal frame,

\[\zeta^{\hat0}=0\]

for an instantaneous spatial peer separation, while

\[\zeta^{\hat i}\]

represent the locally resolved spatial distinction.

The geometric separation is therefore already a projected physical read.

5. Multi-Directional Gravity Requires Local Components, Not a Global Rapidity Vector

Addendum 37 developed collinear rapidity.

A general gravitational tidal relation requires several local directions.

One should not simply replace the scalar rapidity by a globally additive three-vector.

Finite non-collinear Lorentz boosts do not commute.

Instead, introduce a local orthonormal frame

\[e_{\hat0},\quad e_{\hat1},\quad e_{\hat2},\quad e_{\hat3}\]

along the central freely falling boundary.

Infinitesimal relative motion can then be resolved into local rapidity components

\[\delta\xi^{\hat i}.\]

These are local tangent-space quantities.

The construction requires only their infinitesimal linear relation.

6. Infinitesimal Relative Rapidity Is the Local Relative Velocity Coordinate

At the instant when neighboring free-fall peers are locally comoving,

\[\delta\xi^{\hat i}=0.\]

After an infinitesimal realization interval, the peers may acquire a small relative rapidity.

For small relative rapidity,

\[\tanh(\delta\xi^{\hat i})=\delta\xi^{\hat i}+O((\delta\xi)^3).\]

Therefore,

\[\delta v^{\hat i}=c\,\delta\xi^{\hat i}+O((\delta\xi)^2).\]

The linear tidal limit retains only the leading term.

7. Relative Rapidity Is the Rate of Change of Peer Separation

In the local freely falling frame,

\[\delta v^{\hat i}=\frac{D\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau}.\]

Thus, to first order,

\[\frac{D\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau}=c\,\delta\xi^{\hat i}.\]

This is the first continuous bridge from the finite peer relation of Addendum 37 to local geometric motion.

8. Tidal Rapidity Change Becomes Relative Acceleration

Differentiate again along the proper time of the central free-fall reference:

\[\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=c\frac{D\,\delta\xi^{\hat i}}{D\tau}.\]

Therefore,

\[\delta g^{\hat i}=c\frac{D\,\delta\xi^{\hat i}}{D\tau}.\]

The changing peer rapidity has acquired the continuous physical read of differential free-fall acceleration.

Thus:

tidal rapidity change → relative free-fall acceleration.

9. The Finite R²D Quantity Survives the Continuum Limit

Recall

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}=\Delta_j(\delta_{ab}\bar\Xi_G).\]

If a smooth proper-time limit exists,

\[\lim_{\Delta\tau\to0}\frac{\mathcal T_{ab}^{(\xi)}}{\Delta\tau}=\frac D{D\tau}\delta_{ab}\bar\Xi_G.\]

Multiplying by the background calibration c gives

\[\delta g=c\lim_{\Delta\tau\to0}\frac{\mathcal T_{ab}^{(\xi)}}{\Delta\tau}.\]

The continuous acceleration relation is therefore downstream of the finite count-compatible distinction.

10. Smoothness Produces a Tidal Operator

For sufficiently small peer separation, assume that the relative acceleration varies smoothly with separation.

Then the leading local relation is linear:

\[\delta g^{\hat i}=\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}\zeta^{\hat j}.\]

The object

\[\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}\]

is the local tidal operator.

It maps a local separation into relative free-fall acceleration.

11. The Tidal Operator Is Not Yet the Riemann Tensor

The operator

\[\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}\]

is physically defined through peer motion:

\[\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}\zeta^{\hat j}.\]

Nothing in this definition requires a four-dimensional curvature tensor.

The observable differential relation therefore exists before its mature geometric representation.

This preserves the R²D ordering:

distinguishable occurrence → geometric coordinate.

12. Geodesic Deviation Is the Mature Metric Representation

Assume now that the gravitational projection is represented by a smooth metric spacetime with torsion-free Levi-Civita connection.

Neighboring freely falling boundaries are represented by neighboring geodesics.

Their separation satisfies

\[\frac{D^2\zeta^\mu}{D\tau^2}=-R^\mu{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}u^\nu\zeta^\rho u^\sigma,\]

for the curvature-sign convention adopted here.

This equation does not create relative free-fall motion.

It geometrically encodes it.

13. Choose the Local Free-Fall Orthonormal Frame

At the central free-fall boundary choose an orthonormal frame in which

\[u^{\hat0}=c,\]

and

\[u^{\hat i}=0.\]

For an instantaneous spatial separation,

\[\zeta^{\hat0}=0.\]

The spatial geodesic-deviation equation becomes

\[\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=-c^2R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat j\hat0}\zeta^{\hat j}.\]

14. The Riemann Projection of the Tidal Operator

Compare

\[\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}\zeta^{\hat j}\]

with

\[\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=-c^2R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat j\hat0}\zeta^{\hat j}.\]

Therefore,

\[\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}=-c^2R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat j\hat0}.\]

This is the central geometric bridge.

The local electric tidal curvature is the continuous spacetime representation of the prior differential free-fall relation.

15. Conditional Theorem — Tidal Rapidity Maps to Electric Riemann Curvature

Let neighboring freely falling R²D boundaries possess finite differential enclosing rapidity change

\[\mathcal T_{ab}^{(\xi)}=\delta_{ab}(\Delta\bar\Xi_G).\]

Assume:

  1. a smooth continuum limit exists;
  2. neighboring free-fall realizations admit a separation vector \(\zeta^\mu\);
  3. local physical time is represented by proper time;
  4. the relative acceleration is linear in infinitesimal separation;
  5. the mature gravitational projection is metric and torsion free.

Then the finite tidal relation acquires the continuous representation

\[\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}\zeta^{\hat j},\]

and the metric curvature projection satisfies

\[\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}=-c^2R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat j\hat0}.\]

Thus the locally measured electric Riemann curvature is downstream of differential free-fall rapidity.

16. One Tidal Map Does Not Determine the Full Riemann Tensor

The relation

\[\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}=-c^2R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat j\hat0}\]

determines the tidal curvature measured by one chosen freely falling observer.

It does not, in a general spacetime, determine every independent component of

\[R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}.\]

Additional curvature information can appear in:

 • different observer congruences;
 • velocity-dependent relative motion;
 • gravitomagnetic structure;
 • transport around differently oriented loops.

Therefore:

one local tidal operator does not imply the full Riemann tensor. [PDF line clipped; author review required.]

17. The Tidal Operator Is the Electric Curvature Sector

Relative to the selected timelike free-fall direction uμ , define the electric curvature tensor schematically by

\[\mathcal E_{\mu\nu}\propto R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}u^\alpha u^\beta.\]

In the local orthonormal frame,

\[\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}=-c^2R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat j\hat0}\]

under the present sign convention.

This is the curvature sector directly accessible through relative acceleration of initially comoving free-fall peers.

The full spacetime curvature contains more structure.

18. Symmetry Can Make the Tidal Sector More Informative

In highly symmetric situations, additional symmetry relations constrain the remaining curvature components.

For example, static spherical gravity strongly restricts the allowed tensor structure.

In such cases, the local tidal operator may determine much more of the physical curvature than it does in a generic spacetime.

That is a symmetry consequence.

It should not be promoted to a general identity between one tidal map and the entire Riemann tensor.

19. Position-Dependent Rapidity Is Not Sufficient for Curvature

A noninertial coordinate system can assign different rapidities or coordinate accelerations to different spatial locations even in flat spacetime.

Therefore,

\[\bar\Xi(x)\ne\text{constant}\]

does not by itself establish curvature.

Likewise,

\[\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\ne0\]

does not establish curvature.

The physically stronger condition is the irreducible differential motion of freely falling peers.

Thus:

coordinate variation ≠ tidal curvature.

20. Connection and Curvature Have Different Logical Roles

A connection describes how local coordinate bases are related between neighboring points.

It can be nonzero even in flat spacetime.

Curvature measures whether those local relations close consistently around infinitesimal loops.

Therefore:

local reference change → connection,

whereas

irreducible reference nonclosure → curvature.

This distinction is essential.

21. Principle — Curvature Is Geometric Nonclosure of Local Reference Compatibility

Addendum 35 established local Lorentz reference geometry.

Suppose neighboring local free-fall regions each admit such a local Lorentz read.

If those local frame relations can be integrated consistently into one common inertial frame, the region is flat.

If they cannot, the mature geometric projection possesses curvature.

In differential geometry this nonclosure appears as

\[[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho=R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma.\]

Thus the Riemann tensor measures the failure of local reference compatibility to close under continuous transport.

22. Parallel Transport Makes the Nonclosure Visible

Transport a vector around an infinitesimal closed loop.

In flat geometry the vector returns unchanged, modulo coordinate description.

In curved geometry the transported vector acquires a net change proportional to the enclosed infinitesimal area and the Riemann tensor.

Schematically,

\[\delta V^\rho\propto R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma\delta\Sigma^{\mu\nu}.\]

The important R²D ordering is:

local reference relations

⇓

attempted closure around neighboring relations

⇓

nonclosure

⇓

Riemann curvature.

23. Riemann Curvature Is Therefore a Closure Error of the Geometric Projection

The phrase “closure error” here refers to geometric transport, not to irreversible R²D closure failure.

The physical meaning is that no single globally flat coordinate relation can represent all local free-fall references simultaneously.

Thus:

\[R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\ne0\]

means that the local Lorentz reads fail to assemble into one global inertial geometry.

This is precisely what Addendum 37 anticipated when one common rapidity shift failed across neighboring peers.

24. The Local Lorentz Geometry Remains Valid

Curvature does not destroy the local Lorentz result of Addendum 35.

At a sufficiently small freely falling neighborhood, one may choose coordinates for which the metric is locally Minkowskian and first-order gravitational acceleration is removed at the chosen event.

What cannot in general be eliminated is the neighboring differential relation.

Thus:

local Lorentz geometry + nonzero tidal difference

is not contradictory.

It is the defining architecture of curved spacetime.

25. Einstein Curvature Is Therefore Across Local Lorentz Reads

The transition from special to general relativity can now be written:

one uniform reference domain → Lorentz geometry,

while

multiple locally Lorentzian domains + irreducible differential relation → curved geometry.

The curvature does not replace Lorentz geometry.

It organizes how local Lorentz geometries differ.

26. Area Before Radius Remains the Correct Gravitational Ordering

No radial coordinate was required to define

\[\mathcal T_{ab}^{(\xi)}.\]

No radial coordinate was required to define

\[\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}.\]

For a spherical physical projection, first define the enclosing symmetry-sphere area

\[\mathcal A_G.\]

Only afterward define areal radius:

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal A_G}{4\pi}}.\]

Thus:

\[\mathcal A_G\longrightarrow r_A.\]

The radius remains a coordinate read of the enclosure.

27. A Radial Tidal Gradient Is Therefore a Later Projection

Once rA has been defined, a spherically symmetric gravitational acceleration may be written as

\[g_G(r_A).\]

Then the radial variation can be represented by

\[\frac{dg_G}{dr_A}.\]

But this derivative is downstream of:

enclosing gravitational relation → peer distinction → area → areal radius.

Thus a radial gradient does not define gravity primitively.

28. The Spherical Weak-Field Test

The previously reconstructed Keplerian gravitational relation is

\[\mathbf g_G=-\frac{GM}{r_A^3}\mathbf r.\]

Equivalently,

\[\mathbf g_G=-\frac{GM}{r_A^2}\mathbf n,\]

where

\[\mathbf n=\frac{\mathbf r}{r_A}.\]

Now consider neighboring freely falling peers separated by

\[\boldsymbol\zeta.\]

To first order,

\[\Delta\mathbf g=(\boldsymbol\zeta\cdot\nabla)\mathbf g_G.\]

29. Define the Spherical Tidal Map

Define

\[(\mathcal E_G)^i{}_{j}\equiv\frac{\partial g_G^i}{\partial x^j}.\]

For

\[\mathbf g_G=-GM\frac{\mathbf r}{r_A^3},\]

differentiation gives

\[(\mathcal E_G)^i{}_{j}=\frac{GM}{r_A^3}(3n^i n_j-\delta^i{}_{j}).\]

Therefore,

\[\Delta g^i=(\mathcal E_G)^i{}_{j}\zeta^j.\]

This is the weak-field spherical realization of the general tidal operator.

30. The Principal Tidal Reads

Choose the local radial direction as one eigenvector.

Then the principal values are

\[\lambda_r=\frac{2GM}{r_A^3},\]

and two transverse values

\[\lambda_\perp=-\frac{GM}{r_A^3}.\]

Thus neighboring freely falling peers:

• separate radially; • approach one another transversely.

This is tidal gravity as differential peer realization.

31. The Exterior Tidal Map Is Trace Free

Outside the matter source,

\[\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal E_G=\frac{2GM}{r_A^3}-\frac{GM}{r_A^3}-\frac{GM}{r_A^3}.\]

Therefore,

\[\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal E_G=0.\]

Yet

\[\mathcal E_G\ne0.\]

Thus:

zero local source trace does not imply zero local tidal gravity. [PDF line clipped; author review required.]

32. Vacuum Tidal Structure Anticipates Weyl Curvature

The exterior spherical gravitational relation contains nonzero tidal structure even where local matter density vanishes.

In general relativity this trace-free gravitational curvature is associated with the Weyl sector.

The important R²D conclusion is prior:

local matter density does not exhaust the enclosing gravitational relation.

Boundary conditions and larger-scale organization remain physically relevant.

33. Matter Introduces a Tidal Trace

When local matter density is present, the Newtonian weak-field source relation is

\[\nabla\cdot\mathbf g_G=-4\pi G\rho.\]

Since

\[\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal E_G=\nabla\cdot\mathbf g_G,\]

we obtain

\[\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal E_G=-4\pi G\rho.\]

The source-dependent part of the tidal map therefore appears in its trace.

34. The Tidal Trace Maps Onto Ricci Curvature

From the local curvature mapping,

\[\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}=-c^2R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat j\hat0}.\]

Take the spatial trace:

\[\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal E=-c^2\sum_{\hat i}R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat i\hat0}.\]

For the curvature convention adopted here, this is

\[\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal E=-c^2R_{\hat0\hat0}.\]

Combine with

\[\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal E=-4\pi G\rho.\]

Therefore,

\[R_{\hat0\hat0}=\frac{4\pi G}{c^2}\rho.\]

This is the weak-field Ricci source calibration.

35. Ricci and Weyl Have Distinct R²D Roles

The tidal map now naturally separates two kinds of gravitational structure.

The trace

\[\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal E\]

is locally source sensitive.

The trace-free part can remain outside matter.

Thus:

source-dependent focusing ↔︎ Ricci sector,

while

source-free tidal organization ↔︎ Weyl sector.
