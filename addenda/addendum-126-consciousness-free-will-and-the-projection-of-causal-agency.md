# R²D Addendum 126 — Consciousness, Free Will, and the Projection of Causal Agency

*Recorded Experience, Controller Dependence, and Enclosing-Defined Possibility*

**Status:** Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026  
**Canonical basis:** R²D Part I (authoritative September 25, 2026 revision); Addenda 85–88, 103, 105, 107, 108, and 111  
**Companion addenda:** Addenda 122–125  
**Scope:** Boundary-defined experience; retained projection; prospective constraint; controller programming and revision; random selection; functional consciousness; free will as a boundary-relative read of control; and the observational inversion of causal agency

## Abstract

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) assigns native state identity, compatible possibilities, and the organization of their realization to an enclosing count boundary. A subordinate action can be consequential without independently defining or causing the enclosing organization in which its consequence has meaning. Addendum 105 identifies the contrary inference—promoting a reconstructable subordinate realization into the primitive cause of its enclosing state—as an observational inversion. This addendum investigates how recorded experience and controllers make that inversion especially compelling.

Addendum 88 proposes experience as sustained closure at a specified boundary. The present extension distinguishes recorded experience, a retained projection of enclosing organization, from functional consciousness, the use of that record to constrain a prospective read and subsequent realization. Neither records nor prospective models create the possibilities they represent. An autonomous vehicle can satisfy this functional criterion when retained observations actually constrain its predictions and actions; the criterion does not establish phenomenal awareness.

A controller may be programmed to avoid a collision or to seek one. A small change in its configuration can correspond to very different realized enclosing outcomes. The changed controller does not create either possibility. In a designed autonomous vehicle, the policy may be supplied by a human programmer; that programmer's action is in turn a subordinate realization within further enclosing relations. Self-modification supplies no exemption. Nor does random selection: a pseudorandom generator depends on its algorithm and seed or internal state, and a physical random generator still has boundary-defined admissible outputs. Unpredictability does not confer independent organizing causal agency.

The addendum therefore revises the proposed account of free will. Control over a controller is not evidence of independence from enclosing organization. Free will, if retained as a term in the R²D extension, denotes the conscious *read or experience of exercising local control*, not a power to originate enclosing possibilities or outcomes. A formal subset result distinguishes controller-conditioned realizations from the full enclosing domain; a finite example shows that small controller differences can be associated with large outcome differences; and a further example shows that the same local action can participate in different enclosing outcomes. These elementary results, together with the R²D causal axioms, explain why an account of how a decision was implemented is not an account of the primitive organizing cause of the enclosing occurrence.

The psychological proposition that consciousness thereby produces the felt appearance of bottom-up causality is an explanatory hypothesis, not a theorem of finite-set counting. The central claim is narrower and sharper: **recorded experience can direct subordinate realization, but neither the experiencing system nor its programmer independently causes the enclosing possibilities that organize it.**

## 126.1. The R²D question is causal ownership, not the location of computation

At an operative count boundary $B$, let $\mathfrak M_B$ be its admitted native domain and $\pi_B:\mathfrak M_B\to\mathcal S_B$ its readable classification. For readable class $s$,

$$
W_B(s)=|\pi_B^{-1}(s)|.
$$

The enclosing boundary defines the state distinctions and compatible joint realizations that this count measures. A realized subordinate event can occupy a possibility without generating the domain of possibilities. Part I expresses the causal-realization relation as

$$
\chi_{n+1}^{(r)}\Longrightarrow
(\mathcal C_{n,a})_{a\in I_n}\in\mathfrak D_{n+1,\chi},
$$

whereas recursive carry reclassifies a completed subordinate realization when read at an enclosing boundary. The latter is not a bottom-up causal arrow.

A decision made readable through a controller is not thereby independently defined by that controller. A computer executes instructions; an actuator moves; a human acts. These are real local realizations. The R²D question is which boundary defines the admissibility and count meaning of the *complete* occurrence in which they participate.

Addendum 105 proves within the R²D architecture that enclosing classification defines its measurable transitions and compatible subordinate realization classes, and that enclosing possibility can reverse local count support without changing native local multiplicity. Its causal conclusion is **top-down organizing priority**, not the denial that subordinate mechanisms operate. A controller computation can reconstruct or implement an outcome without becoming the primitive source of the enclosing organization.

## 126.2. Experience, recorded experience, and recursive surveying

Addendum 88 proposed the distinct **experience postulate**:

$$
\text{Experience at }A\ \equiv\ \text{sustained closure at }A.
$$

That proposed identification is broader than human consciousness and is not proved by native-count definitions alone. An experiencing system may sustain an organization without preserving a retrievable record of every encounter.

A **recorded experience** is a retained, boundary-specific projection of encountered enclosing organization. Schematically, for an enclosing realization $\omega$ and a local read boundary $A$,

$$
e_A=\sigma_A(\omega),\qquad h_A=\operatorname{Record}_A(e_A).
$$

The maps are proposed physical comparison relations, not new R²D primitives. A record does not reproduce the complete $\omega$ and cannot acquire causal ownership of the world by representing it.

Records may take very different physical forms: neural changes, learned parameters or retained sensor observations, tissue remodeling, protein modifications, or growth rings. A retained trace alone does not show that its bearer uses the trace to organize subsequent realization. The relevant follow-up is whether the record is *read again* as an operative constraint.

The retained projection $h_A$ must also remain distinct from Part I's productive-retention quantity $R$. Both involve a word like *retention*, but their count domains and physical interpretations are not automatically identical.

## 126.3. Consciousness as prospective constraint from a recorded projection

**Proposed Definition 1 — Functional consciousness.** A sustained experiencing organization is functionally conscious when its retained projection of enclosing organization participates as an operative constraint on a prospective read of compatible possibilities and on subsequent realization.

This definition adds a recursive relation:

$$
\boxed{
\text{enclosing read}\ \longrightarrow\
\text{retained projection}\ \longrightarrow\
\text{prospective constraint}\ \longrightarrow\
\text{locally realized response}.
}
\tag{126.1}
$$

The prospective possibilities are not produced by the representation or projected into physical existence by a forecast. They belong to enclosing compatibility; a model surveys them imperfectly. The controller's own implemented goal can participate in how it constrains its response without becoming the primitive origin of the surrounding situation.

For a fixed current read $x$, compare two physically admitted retained records $h,h'$. An operational test is whether changing the record changes the prospective relation or subsequent action while other operative conditions are held fixed. In schematic notation, $F(x,h)\ne F(x,h')$ is one possible witness. The test is neither an exhaustive definition of every conscious system nor a proof of phenomenal feeling.

Under this proposed functional definition, an autonomous vehicle that retains sensor information and uses that record in subsequent prediction and control can qualify as conscious. This assigns a specified *functional* relation; it does not assert that the vehicle possesses human subjective experience. A reactive device with no operative retained survey has not met this criterion merely by producing a response.

## 126.4. Conditional control does not change the enclosing possibility domain

Fix one complete enclosing compatibility specification $\Gamma_B$. Let $h$ denote an admitted retained record and $u$ an admitted controller configuration. Define the conditioned realizations

$$
\Gamma_{B\mid h,u}
=\{\omega\in\Gamma_B:\omega\text{ is compatible with }h,u\}.
$$

**Proposition 1 — Controller-conditioned admission.** For every admitted controller and record,

$$
\boxed{\Gamma_{B\mid h,u}\subseteq\Gamma_B.}
\tag{126.2}
$$

**Proof.** Membership in the conditioned set requires membership in $\Gamma_B$. $\square$

This exact subset result is modest: it is a consequence of the stated complete enclosing specification. It does not, by itself, prove the R²D causal ontology. R²D supplies the further interpretation that the enclosing boundary defines the primitive count meaning and admissibility of the complete realization.

Changing $h$ or $u$ can change the conditioned relation, the action performed, and the realized outcome. It does not mean a subordinate controller has rewritten the prior enclosing domain $\Gamma_B$. When later physical circumstances admit a different operative enclosing read, that read must be defined at its own boundary; it cannot be construed as a primitive causal landscape created by the earlier subordinate action.

No assumption is made that $\Gamma_B$ uniquely selects one future. Several complete outcomes may be admitted. Enclosing ownership of possibilities and determination of the individual occupied realization are different claims.

## 126.5. A small controller change, a radically different occurrence

Let $u_s$ be a collision-avoiding controller configuration and $u_c$ a collision-directed configuration. Consider the following admitted comparison records under one enclosing organization:

| Environmental condition | Controller | Local action | Enclosing outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Child crosses; other vehicles stop | $u_s$ | Brake | Child survives |
| Child crosses; other vehicles stop | $u_c$ | Proceed | Collision |
| Child crosses; another vehicle fails to stop | $u_s$ | Brake | Collision caused by another vehicle's realization |

The records are schematic complete comparisons, not a claim of exhaustive road physics or of a particular controller's native R²D microstates. Both controller configurations and all the stated outcomes belong to the selected enclosing possibility domain.

**Proposition 2 — Outcome difference does not establish possibility creation.** Two conditioned controller domains can contain radically different enclosing outcomes even though both are subsets of the same complete enclosing domain.

**Proof.** The first and second records admit different outcomes under $u_s$ and $u_c$. Both are members of $\Gamma_B$ by specification. The difference between their outcomes therefore does not entail that either controller created the possibility of its outcome. $\square$

The amount of program code changed has no necessary quantitative relation to the difference between the enclosing outcomes. A one-bit or one-instruction modification may change which locally implemented action is compatible with the controller specification; the enclosing consequences may differ dramatically. The logical result is **not** that code changes are physically ineffective. It is that local efficacy and primitive enclosing causal authorship are different assertions.

The third record supplies an additional limit. The same braking action can belong to an enclosing occurrence in which the child survives or one in which the child is struck by another vehicle. The local action therefore does not uniquely identify the complete outcome. Successful local control is not sovereignty over the enclosing occurrence.

## 126.6. The external programmer is another subordinate realization

For an autonomous vehicle, a human programmer or design team can supply the controller's architecture, objective, and constraints. A vehicle may then execute actions locally, but its operative decision rule is not authored independently by the vehicle simply because the computation runs on board.

The programmer does not escape the R²D hierarchy. Programming is also a realized action within further technological, economic, institutional, physical, and social compatibility relations. No programmer can claim independent authorship of the enclosing possibilities in which the design, its implementation, and its outcomes are possible.

This does not require a literal programmer to provide every later action. Learning systems can modify internal parameters; organizations can revise goals; human operators can intervene. All of these are additional admitted realizations. In R²D, finding a more proximate agent explains a local step of realization; it does not invert causal ownership of the enclosing domain.

It is therefore misleading to ask whether a controller *makes* a decision without specifying what *makes* means. The computation and selected action can be locally readable and fully reproducible. But the controller does not independently define its available alternatives, their compatibility, or the enclosing outcome in which the action acquires meaning.

## 126.7. Controller self-revision cannot create independent free will

Suppose a controller uses recorded experience to alter its own operative policy:

$$
u'=\Psi(u,h,x).
$$

Here $u'$ is a subsequent controller configuration, not a new free-standing source of physical possibility. If the revision is realized within an enclosing specification $B$, its complete comparison must admit $u'$:

$$
\boxed{u'\in\operatorname{pr}_{U'}\Gamma_B.}
\tag{126.3}
$$

The revision mechanism also has its own physically admitted configurations and constraints. Self-modification may increase the range of locally realizable behaviors without producing independence from the enclosing organization in which modification is possible.

**Proposition 3 — Recursive controller governance does not establish primitive independence.** Within a specified R²D enclosing comparison, a controller revision is a subordinate realization; the presence of an internal map $u\mapsto u'$ does not make that map the owner of the enclosing compatibility relation.

**Argument.** The completed revision is classified within $\Gamma_B$. Its local description may be necessary to reproduce or predict which configuration is realized. Neither the description nor its repeated application defines the enclosing native state identity or admissibility that makes the revision a possible event. The asserted independent causal ownership therefore does not follow. The stronger exclusion of primitive bottom-up organizing priority is inherited from the R²D causal architecture, not deduced from Equation (126.3) alone.

To establish *independence from every enclosing boundary* empirically would require excluding dependencies that may lie beyond the system's readable horizon. No finite survey of a controller establishes such independence. More importantly, R²D does not assign that independence to subordinate realizations in the first place. We need not pretend to know all enclosing boundaries to apply the causal relation at an explicitly specified pair; nor should ignorance of a further boundary be mistaken for evidence that none exists.

## 126.8. Random selection is not independence from enclosing causality

A controller may use a random-number generator in selecting an action:

$$
a=F(x,h,u,z).
$$

For a **pseudorandom generator**, $z$ depends on a generator rule and seed or internal state. A seed may be supplied by an external programmer, operating system, hardware observations, or earlier retained state. A deterministic pseudorandom sequence can be unpredictable to the controller's observer without being an unconditioned source of possibilities.

A **physical random generator** need not have a software seed in this sense. Its output still belongs to a physically specified domain of admissible generator realizations and a corresponding readout boundary. Calling an output random does not grant its numerical representation primitive causal authority over the surrounding world.

Both cases obey the relevant ordering:

$$
\boxed{
\text{enclosing admissibility}\ \longrightarrow\
\text{generator realization and read}\ \longrightarrow\
\text{controller action}.
}
\tag{126.4}
$$

Randomization can change which action is selected and can alter observed transition frequencies. It does not create those actions, establish their native count identity, or emancipate the controller from enclosing constraint. Addendum 107's distinction applies: **probabilities do not push**. A normalized weight or random draw does not acquire organizing causal priority by being useful for prediction.

## 126.9. Free will is a conscious read of control, not controller independence

The earlier draft defined free will as the capacity to govern or revise one's own controller. That proposed definition made a further degree of self-governance seem like a route to independent agency. Sections 126.6–126.8 show why no such inference is justified.

**Revised Definition 2 — Free will as a boundary-relative experience of control.** Where the term *free will* is used in this R²D extension, it denotes a conscious system's retained and prospective **read of its own exercise of control over subordinate realization**. It does not denote independent creation of the enclosing possibilities or primitive causal authorship of the enclosing occurrence.

The *exercise of control* is locally real: a system implements one admissible action rather than another under its operative controller. The *conscious read of control* is an additional recorded projection of that realization. The inference from either relation to causal independence is unsupported within R²D.

A human can report a felt experience of free will. A machine can exhibit local control and even an internal representation of its own action selection. That does not establish that the machine has human subjective feelings. Functional control, self-monitoring, self-modification, phenomenal experience, and independent organizing causality must not be equated.

Nothing in the R²D primitives requires *free will* as an additional physical substance or a special exception to recursive enclosure. The term can describe a particular conscious read while the count ontology remains unchanged.

## 126.10. The projection of causal agency

A conscious driver may record the sequence:

$$
\text{I saw a child}\rightarrow
\text{I chose to brake}\rightarrow
\text{I braked}\rightarrow
\text{the child survived}.
$$

The record may be accurate as a sequence of local observations. But it is a projection of one completed realization. It is not a complete read of the enclosing relation in which another car might strike the child despite the same braking action.

**Proposition 4 — A local action–outcome record cannot establish independent enclosing authorship.** If the same locally readable action belongs to admitted complete realizations with different enclosing outcomes, the action label alone is insufficient to determine the enclosing outcome. A record of one action followed by one outcome does not establish that the action defined or independently caused that enclosing occurrence.

**Proof.** In Section 126.5, the same action Brake belongs to both a survival outcome and a collision outcome. Thus the complete outcome is not a single-valued function of the local action label within the given enclosing relation. Observing one pairing cannot supply the missing enclosing distinctions. $\square$

This finite result does not by itself disprove every conventional local counterfactual or intervention analysis; such analyses can legitimately quantify consequences *given specified boundary conditions*. What it blocks is the inference from a successful local reconstruction to **primitive ownership of the enclosing organization**. Addenda 85 and 105 distinguish those claims.

**Projection-of-agency hypothesis.** Because consciousness records and reuses its own predictions, decisions, actions, and consequences, its locally readable controller can become the protagonist of an explanatory narrative. The surrounding conditions that define the compatible outcomes are only partially readable. The system may then experience its locally implemented control as bottom-up causal authorship of the enclosing occurrence.

The mathematical claim concerns loss of enclosing information in the projection. The psychological claim about why humans *feel* causal authorship requires independent evidence and is not proved by a finite compatibility table.

## 126.11. The future chooses the path: possibility, not retrocausation

The statement **“The future chooses the self-driving car's path”** has a precise R²D meaning. *The future* denotes the encompassing landscape of possibilities compatible with the operative world, not a later occupied event acting backward in time. The car's model reads some of those possibilities; its controller realizes an admitted action under its configured constraints.

When the enclosing relation and goal leave one compatible complete course of action, its direction is fixed in that comparison. When several courses remain, the existence of the landscape does not itself select a unique occupied realization. That distinction is essential: possible count, realized occurrence, structural occupancy, and recurrence remain different R²D readouts.

The model does not create possible pedestrians, signals, vehicles, or outcomes. Its representation may fail to capture them correctly. Even a perfectly accurate world model would not own the enclosing compatibility relation it represents. This is why world-model success is a compelling **illustration** of enclosing-first organization, not a stand-alone empirical proof that every aspect of the system's physical behavior has been quantitatively derived from R²D.

The autonomous vehicle is especially instructive because its subordinate mechanism is visible: code, learned records, hardware, randomization if used, and actuation. Making that mechanism visible explains its implementation. It does not reverse the enclosing dependence that gives each action its possible meaning.

## 126.12. Human achievement, consequence, and responsibility

The same distinction applies to a scientist, entrepreneur, driver, programmer, or institution. A founder may recognize and realize the possibility of a company; this does not mean the founder created the technological, economic, and social compatibility landscape admitting that organization. Nor does the existence of the possibility make its realization inevitable.

A person can install a traffic signal, but neither the act nor the intention guarantees the complete enclosing outcome. The installation itself belongs to a larger relation in which harm or benefit may occur. A driver can brake to protect a child while a different vehicle still causes a fatal outcome. The driver performs a meaningful local action without becoming the primitive cause of the entire enclosing occurrence.

**Consequential realization is not independent organizing causation.** That distinction does not abolish responsibility, obligations, or local assessments of harm. Such evaluations belong to specified human and institutional boundaries. Addendum 85 already treats responsibility as a boundary-relative practice; the R²D denial of primitive bottom-up causal priority is not a claim that local conduct is irrelevant to ethics or law.

## 126.13. Logical status, tests, and conclusion

The logical status of the results must be explicit.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| The enclosing boundary defines native identity, states, and compatible subordinate realization | R²D axiomatic architecture and Addendum 105's organizing-causality theorem |
| Controller-conditioned realized domains are subsets of the admitted complete domain | Exact finite-set consequence of the specified comparison |
| A controller change can accompany a different enclosing outcome without creating its possibility | Finite compatibility example; interpretation follows R²D ontology |
| One local action can participate in different complete outcomes | Exact finite counterexample |
| Self-revision or randomized action establishes independent enclosing agency | Does not follow; excluded as primitive organizing sufficiency within R²D |
| Experience is sustained closure | Extension postulate of Addendum 88 |
| Consciousness is recorded experience used as prospective constraint | Proposed operational definition |
| Free will is the conscious read of exercising local control, not independent authorship | Revised interpretive definition |
| Felt bottom-up causality arises from promoting one's recorded action to enclosing cause | Testable explanatory hypothesis, not a combinatorial theorem |
| Autonomous-vehicle success uniquely proves R²D against every conventional controller theory | Not established without a discriminating physical count mapping |

A functional consciousness test can vary the retained record under the same current read and check whether it constrains the prospective model and action. A controller-dependence test can vary operative policies, including self-modifying or randomized policies, and separately enumerate the enclosing-compatible complete outcomes. A stronger physical R²D test requires independently specified native count domains, compatible extensions, productive retention, and observed occurrence relations. A locally accurate control-theoretic prediction does not by itself settle primitive causal ownership.

The complete organizing conclusion is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{The enclosing world defines the possibilities.}\\
\text{Experience reads and retains a projection.}\\
\text{Consciousness reuses that record as prospective constraint.}\\
\text{Control directs a subordinate realization.}\\
\text{Free will is the conscious read of that control,}\\
\text{not independent authorship of the enclosing outcome.}
\end{gathered}
}
$$

A small controller revision can accompany a large difference in realized outcome. An externally programmed controller, a self-revising controller, and a randomizing controller are nevertheless all compatible subordinate realizations of enclosing organization. None can create the enclosing possibilities in which its activity acquires meaning. The appearance of bottom-up causal authorship arises when a recorded local realization is mistaken for the primitive organizer of the whole.

**We can realize possibilities and consciously control our participation. We do not originate the enclosing world that makes those possibilities possible.**
