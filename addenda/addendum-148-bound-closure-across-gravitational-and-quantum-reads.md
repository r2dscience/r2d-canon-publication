## Abstract

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) defines physical states through enclosing compatibility before assigning physical objects, forces, fields, spatial coordinates, or clocks. A bound planetary orbit and a bound atomic orbital therefore pose the same primitive question: which distinguishable relationships and completed recurrences are admitted by their enclosing boundary?

This addendum constructs one finite dimensionless count architecture consistent with both gravitational and quantum bound-state organization.

An enclosing boundary admits two bounded ordinal count classifications, $a,b\in\{0,\ldots,N\}$, with complete square compatibility. Their joint domain contains $(N+1)^2$ distinct native realizations. Recursive shell classification by $\ell=\max(a,b)$ gives exactly $2\ell+1$ distinguishable oriented classes per shell. The oriented difference $m=a-b$ and remaining count capacity $r=N-\ell$ establish a bijection with the integer triples

$$
r+\ell=N,\qquad -\ell\le m\le\ell.
$$

With $n=N+1$, the complete classification has cardinality $n^2$, matching the orbital-state count of the ideal nonrelativistic hydrogen atom, excluding spin.

A compatible cyclic recurrence is then specified on the same enclosing domain. It admits both a cumulative incidence-count read and cyclic phase characters. Under a gravitational background calibration, the incidence recurrence can be represented as the constant swept-area relation of a closed Keplerian ellipse. Under a quantum background calibration, the square-shell labels and cyclic characters admit the hydrogenic $(n,\ell,m)$ orbital classification and its angular phase closure.

The construction is an existence and consistency result. It does not assert that all enclosing count domains possess square compatibility, that every closure is Keplerian, or that physical energy spectra and orbital geometries follow from integer counting alone. Its result is that one explicitly specified R²D organization admits both physical projections without importing physical force, wavefunction, or particle trajectories into the primitive count definitions.

The gravitational and quantum systems differ in their physical readings, not in the primitive status of their objects, backgrounds, or enclosing relationships.

## 148.1. The question: one count architecture, two physical reads

R²D Part I establishes that a formal microstate is a compatible joint realization of immediately subordinate peers relative to an enclosing boundary. Possibility depends upon the number of compatible enclosing extensions. Recursive replacement changes the native state domain rather than preserving subordinate identities as primitive objects.

Part IV applies this architecture to physical fields and bound systems. Addendum 40 establishes the logical precedence of Keplerian closure over its Newtonian force representation. Addendum 72 establishes that cyclic recurrence admits phase representations before complex amplitude and wavefunction descriptions are introduced.

The present question is more specific:

**Can one finite enclosing compatibility support both the bound areal recurrence observed in planetary systems and the radial–angular state classification observed in atoms?**

The requirement is mathematical consistency.

The construction must satisfy R²D's primitive counting definitions and admit both physical mappings. It need not establish uniqueness among all conceivable compatible count domains.

The logical order is

$$
\boxed{
\text{Enclosing distinguishability}
\longrightarrow
\text{joint compatibility}
\longrightarrow
\text{closure and recurrence}
\longrightarrow
\text{physical calibration}.
}
\tag{148.1}
$$

The gravitational and quantum physical systems instantiate different enclosing owners. The claim is that they can instantiate the same abstract count architecture, not that one physical boundary literally contains the other or that their physical objects are identical.

## 148.3. Theorem 1 — Recursive square-shell multiplicity

Define the shell classification

$$
\boxed{
\ell(a,b)=\max(a,b).
}
\tag{148.4}
$$

For each $\ell\in\{0,\ldots,N\}$, let

$$
\mathcal S_\ell
=
\{(a,b)\in\mathfrak M_{J_N}:\max(a,b)=\ell\}.
$$

**Theorem 148.1 — Square-shell counting.** The number of distinct native realizations in shell $\ell$ is

$$
\boxed{
|\mathcal S_\ell|=2\ell+1.
}
\tag{148.5}
$$

**Proof.**

The number of ordered pairs satisfying

$$
0\le a,b\le\ell
$$

is

$$
(\ell+1)^2.
$$

For $\ell\ge1$, the number satisfying

$$
0\le a,b\le\ell-1
$$

is

$$
\ell^2.
$$

The difference counts precisely those pairs for which $\max(a,b)=\ell$:

$$
|\mathcal S_\ell|
=
(\ell+1)^2-\ell^2
=
2\ell+1.
$$

For $\ell=0$, the shell consists of the single pair $(0,0)$, and the same formula holds. $\square$

The complete domain is partitioned into these shells. Hence

$$
\boxed{
|\mathfrak M_{J_N}|
=
\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}(2\ell+1)
=
(N+1)^2.
}
\tag{148.6}
$$

The odd-numbered shell multiplicities are exact consequences of the specified enclosing square compatibility.

### Recursive interpretation

Consider two successive square-count capacities, $N-1$ and $N$, represented in one common comparison domain.

The larger admissible label set contains

$$
(N+1)^2
$$

classes, while the smaller contains

$$
N^2.
$$

Their count difference is

$$
\boxed{
\Delta W_N=2N+1.
}
\tag{148.7}
$$

This is a recursive *capacity-comparison identity*. It does not assert that individual formal microstates remain physically identical when the native count boundary changes. In R²D, exact comparisons across distinct native owners must preserve the specified enclosing compatibility and identification rules.

The integer $N$ is an enclosing ordinal capacity, not automatically the recursive boundary index, a spatial radius, or a physical principal quantum number.

## 148.4. Theorem 2 — Radial–angular–orientation classification

\boxed{

\tag{148.10}

$$
\boxed{

\begin{array}{l}
r,\ell\ge0,\quad r+\ell=N,\\
m\in\mathbb Z,\quad-\ell\le m\le\ell
\end{array}
\right\}.

\tag{148.11}
$$

**Proof.**

The definitions of $r,\ell,m$ assign one triple to every admitted pair.

Conversely, take any triple satisfying (148.11). Recover

$$
a=\ell+\min(m,0),
$$

$$
b=\ell-\max(m,0).

Both lie between $0$ and $\ell$, at least one equals $\ell$, and their difference is $m$.

Thus the recovered pair has

$$
\max(a,b)=\ell,\qquad a-b=m.

The inverse is unique. Therefore the map is bijective. $\square$

$$
\boxed{

\ell=0,\ldots,n-1,\qquad
m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell.

\tag{148.13}

The total number of triples is

$$
\boxed{
|\mathcal T_N|
=
\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(2\ell+1)
=n^2.
}
\tag{148.14}
$$

This is the principal combinatorial result.

The radial classification $r$, shell classification $\ell$, oriented difference $m$, and square multiplicity $n^2$ are not four independently postulated rules. They are different readings of one specified enclosing square-count relation.

## 148.9. Gravitational projection — Keplerian areal closure

\boxed{

$$
\mathcal A_K(E_j)
=

$$
\boxed{

\frac{\mathcal A_K(j+1)-\mathcal A_K(j)}

## 148.12. Cross-domain consistency theorem

**Theorem 148.4 — Bound gravitational–quantum consistency construction.**

There exists a finite dimensionless enclosing compatibility with the following properties:

1. Its native realizations are the complete square-count domain $\mathfrak M_{J_N}$.

**Proof.**

Properties 1–3 follow from Theorems 148.1 and 148.2 and the explicitly specified native-identification rule.

Neither projection changes the primitive count identities. Both are defined on explicitly specified compatible structures without requiring a primitive force, physical trajectory, or wavefunction.

Therefore the two physical projections are jointly consistent with the stated dimensionless enclosing architecture. $\square$

### Scope of the theorem

The theorem is an existence result. It demonstrates that the proposed R²D counting and recurrence architecture can accommodate both physical classifications.

It does not prove that the complete square compatibility is uniquely selected by the general R²D axioms. Nor does it imply that the finite $n^2$ classification is a necessary feature of every gravitationally bound system.

The gravitational and quantum observations test different consequences of the construction. The common primitive count architecture is sufficient to accommodate both, but each physical projection retains its own calibration and domain-specific conditions.
