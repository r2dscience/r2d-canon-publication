R²D Addendum 136 — Native Identification Constrains Symmetric Recurrence

Joint Incidence, Exact Admissibility, and Possibility Before Realization

Status: Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026
Canonical basis: R²D Part I, authoritative scientific revision September 25, 2026; Addenda 120 and 122 for boundary-owned identification; Addendum 135 for the finite response-pattern domain; Addenda 131–134 for downstream research context.
Purpose: Determine exactly which complete, possible spatial–recurrence patterns follow from one enclosing owner’s specified native-comparison identifications, and separate these conditional theorems from primitive axioms and physical realization.

Abstract

Addendum 135 showed that symmetric recurrence, reciprocal response classifications, and unreadability of subordinate identities do not by themselves select translating patterns. The present addendum investigates a stronger, independently specified enclosing condition: an address-to-address response comparison is identified as the same receiving-boundary relation across all recurrence-comparison stages. Equivalently, a stage-to-stage response comparison is identified across all addresses. These are not two independent mathematical premises; over an additive response domain either one implies the other.

For a finite address set of size \(L\), a recurrence-stage set of size \(T\), and a cyclic relative-response set of size \(m\), the complete possible patterns satisfying this identification are exactly the additively separable patterns \(\delta(j,n)=c+F(j)+G(n)\). Their count is \(m^{L+T-1}\), as compared with \(m^{LT}\) unrestricted patterns. The mixed spatial–recurrence difference vanishes on every admitted pattern. At \(L=T=m=4\), the restriction admits precisely \(16,384\) of \(4,294,967,296\) candidate patterns. Of those, \(256\) are stage-stationary, \(24\) satisfy at least one of the two one-address-per-stage translating incidence conditions, and \(16,360\) satisfy neither translation condition. The translating union contains four constant patterns and twenty nonconstant patterns. These are possible read-class counts, not realized occurrence counts.

The restriction excludes a specific nontranslation pattern having the same separate row and column histograms as a permitted translating pattern. Yet it still admits a second nontranslation pattern with exactly those histograms. Consequently, cross-stage native identification supplies a genuine, nontrivial admissibility restriction without selecting wave propagation. A read that preserves the complete pattern can preserve this restriction after subordinate identity is lost; a read preserving only marginal histograms cannot generally preserve translating incidence. The identification itself is an extra enclosing-owned specification, not a requirement established by Part I for every boundary. No physical propagation, acoustic or electromagnetic law, mass-energy relation, or realization rule is asserted.

136.1. Place in the unitless architecture

Part I defines native distinguishability, compatible multiplicity, across-boundary possibility, and realized occurrence as different count types. Part II introduces physical thermodynamic readings. Parts III–IV investigate what remains readable and how the physical domains represent it. The present problem is upstream of those physical mappings: what can an enclosing owner admit as a complete recurrence-response relationship, even when no candidate has been physically realized?

The addendum does not amend Part I’s primitive axioms. It proves consequences of a declared kind of enclosing native identification, audits what this declaration assumes, and gives a finite test for any proposed physical mapping to satisfy later.

No directional closure-production law, retaining-work equation, microscopic trajectory, physical clock, physical spatial ruler, or propagation speed enters the proofs.

136.2. Count types and the complete pattern domain

Let \(J\) own an enclosing compatibility domain and let \(B\) be the receiving count boundary. Choose finite cyclic address labels \(X=\mathbb Z_L\), recurrence-comparison stage labels \(T=\mathbb Z_T\), and a relative-response label group \(Q=\mathbb Z_m\), where \(L,T,m\ge2\).

These are dimensionless classifications. A stage is not a second, an address is not a meter, and a relative-response label is not a physical electromagnetic phase.

Following Addendum 135, two response presentations \(a_{j,n},b_{j,n}\in Q\) determine a readable relative class

\[ \delta(j,n)=b_{j,n}-a_{j,n}\pmod m. \tag{136.1} \]

A common presentation-reference change \(a_{j,n}\mapsto a_{j,n}+g_{j,n}\), \(b_{j,n}\mapsto b_{j,n}+g_{j,n}\) leaves \(\delta\) unchanged. The different presentations are not counted as distinct native states unless another boundary specifically distinguishes them.

The unrestricted receiving pattern candidate domain is

\[ \mathcal P=Q^{X\times T},\qquad |\mathcal P|=m^{LT}. \tag{136.2} \]

One enclosing owner selects an admitted subset \(\mathcal C_J\subseteq\mathcal P\). For a specified pattern class \(\mathcal K\), its possible readable-pattern count is

\[ W_J^{\mathrm{pat}}(\mathcal K)=|\mathcal C_J\cap\mathcal K|. \tag{136.3} \]

If distinct finer possible realizations belong to the same readable pattern, their multiplicity must be counted separately through the specified identification fibers. Equation (136.3) is not automatically the canonical across-boundary logarithmic possibility \(P_{B,k}\), and neither possible count supplies realized occurrence or occupancy.

A symmetric possible turbine recurrence signature, such as a candidate complete record with \(N_+=N_->0\), may be attached to all patterns. These candidate whole-return counts are not the realized frequencies of any physical system, nor are the translating-incidence directions defined later the same variables as the turbine’s \(N_\pm\).

136.3. A native comparison identity, not a persistent subordinate identity

For any complete relative-response pattern define its local comparison differences

\[ H_{j,n}=\delta(j+1,n)-\delta(j,n), \qquad V_{j,n}=\delta(j,n+1)-\delta(j,n), \tag{136.4} \]

with cyclic indices. These differences compare receiving-boundary classifications. They do not identify subordinate beat realizations at the two addresses or stages.

Now specify an enclosing owner with the following cross-stage address-comparison identification:

\[ \boxed{H_{j,n}=u_j\quad\text{for all }n,} \tag{136.5} \]

where \(u_j\in Q\) may vary with \(j\). This is an independently stated rule of the enclosing compatibility: the native address-pair comparison labeled \(j\to j+1\) receives the same response-difference label at each recurrence stage.

The apparently dual cross-address recurrence-comparison identification is

\[ \boxed{V_{j,n}=v_n\quad\text{for all }j.} \tag{136.6} \]

Neither condition means that the same subordinate object persists from one stage to another. It means that the enclosing owner identifies complete comparisons across their respective receiving-boundary presentations.

Logical qualification: In this additive model (136.5) and (136.6) are mathematically equivalent, not independent restrictions. We retain both descriptions to expose the two readable directions of one identifying condition. Their joint physical or R²D-native legitimacy is not guaranteed simply because either equation can be written.

136.4. Theorem 1 — The exact mixed-incidence criterion

Theorem 1 (equivalent comparison-identification criteria). For any pattern \(\delta\in\mathcal P\), the following are equivalent:

  1. \(H_{j,n}\) is independent of \(n\) for each \(j\).
  2. \(V_{j,n}\) is independent of \(j\) for each \(n\).
  3. The mixed difference vanishes at every address–stage rectangle:

\[ \boxed{\Delta_j\Delta_n\delta(j,n) =\delta(j+1,n+1)-\delta(j+1,n)-\delta(j,n+1)+\delta(j,n)=0.} \tag{136.7} \]

Proof. The difference between \(H_{j,n+1}\) and \(H_{j,n}\) equals the mixed difference in (136.7). The difference between \(V_{j+1,n}\) and \(V_{j,n}\) is the same expression. Thus either independence condition is equivalent to vanishing of every mixed difference. \(\square\)

Important distinction. Equation (136.7) is not the ordinary assertion that the signed sum of actual edge increments around a rectangle telescopes to zero. That telescoping identity holds for every node-labeled pattern and supplies no exclusion. Equation (136.7) instead equates parallel address-comparison differences at neighboring stages, or equivalently parallel stage-comparison differences at neighboring addresses. It is a substantive compatibility restriction.

136.5. Theorem 2 — Complete form of every admissible pattern

Let \(\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}\subseteq\mathcal P\) denote the patterns meeting the identification criterion (136.7).

Theorem 2 (separable-incidence classification). The admissible set is exactly

\[ \boxed{\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}= \left\{\delta(j,n)=c+F(j)+G(n): c\in Q,\ F(0)=G(0)=0\right\}.} \tag{136.8} \]

Proof. From the zero mixed difference, \(\delta(j,n)-\delta(0,n)\) is independent of \(n\) and equals \(\delta(j,0)-\delta(0,0)\). Thus

\[ \delta(j,n)=\delta(j,0)+\delta(0,n)-\delta(0,0). \tag{136.9} \]

Set \(c=\delta(0,0)\), \(F(j)=\delta(j,0)-c\), and \(G(n)=\delta(0,n)-c\). These determine a unique normalized decomposition. Conversely, every such separable pattern has zero mixed difference. \(\square\)

No wave equation, characteristic speed, net direction, or existence of a physically traveling object has been assumed. The result classifies the owner’s permitted complete relations.

136.6. Theorem 3 — Exact possible counts and exclusions

Theorem 3 (admissible-pattern cardinality). For \(L\) address labels, \(T\) stage labels, and \(m\) response values,

\[ \boxed{|\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}|=m^{L+T-1}.} \tag{136.10} \]

Proof. Choose \(c\) in \(m\) ways; the \(L-1\) nonreference values of \(F\) independently in \(m^{L-1}\) ways; and the \(T-1\) nonreference values of \(G\) independently in \(m^{T-1}\) ways. Uniqueness in Theorem 2 prevents double counting. \(\square\)

For \(L=T=m=4\),

\[ |\mathcal P|=4^{16}=4{,}294{,}967{,}296, \tag{136.11} \]

\[ \boxed{|\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}|=4^7=16{,}384.} \tag{136.12} \]

Hence the specified owner excludes exactly

\[ \boxed{4^{16}-4^7=4{,}294{,}950{,}912} \tag{136.13} \]

receiving pattern possibilities. No statement about their frequencies of realization follows. Without the identification premise, the count restriction does not follow from Part I’s universal definitions alone.

136.7. Theorem 4 — Permitted stationary and translating incidence

Now specialize to \(L=T=m=4\). Classify patterns after deriving (136.8), using Addendum 135’s definitions:

\[ \mathcal S=\{\delta(j,n)=F(j)\}, \quad \mathcal W_\pm=\{\delta(j,n)=H(j\mp n)\}. \tag{136.14} \]

The \(\mathcal W_\pm\) labels are unitless diagonal incidence, not an asserted physical wave or the realized turbine-cycle orientations.

Theorem 4 (exact admitted subclasses). In \(\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}\):

\[ |\mathcal S\cap\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}|=256, \tag{136.15} \]

\[ |\mathcal W_+\cap\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}| =|\mathcal W_-\cap\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}|=16, \tag{136.16} \]

\[ |\mathcal W_+\cap\mathcal W_-\cap\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}|=8, \tag{136.17} \]

and therefore

\[ \boxed{|(\mathcal W_+\cup\mathcal W_-)\cap\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}|=24.} \tag{136.18} \]

Proof. Every stage-stationary profile is separable, giving \(4^4=256\). A diagonal pattern \(H(j-n)\) is separable if and only if its cyclic successive difference is constant, so \(H(x)=b+\alpha x\) with \(b,\alpha\in\mathbb Z_4\). There are \(4^2=16\) choices for each orientation. Such a pattern is in both orientations exactly when \(\alpha=-\alpha\pmod4\), or \(2\alpha=0\), giving \(\alpha=0\) or \(2\) and therefore \(8\) patterns. Inclusion–exclusion gives \(16+16-8=24\). \(\square\)

A disjoint partition of the admitted possibilities is:

Admitted response class Distinct possible patterns
Constant in address and stage 4
Nonconstant and stage-stationary 252
Nonconstant and in at least one \(\mathcal W_\pm\) 20
Neither stage-stationary nor in either \(\mathcal W_\pm\) 16,108
Total 16,384

The complete admitted domain has \(16{,}360\) patterns outside both translating classes, including the \(256\) stage-stationary possibilities. Four constant patterns belong to the stationary and to both translating classes simultaneously; this overlap is removed in the partition above. The enclosing owner has therefore not selected traveling incidence as compulsory.

136.8. Two zero/nonzero tests without reference to physical motion

Consider the complete patterns from Addendum 135:

\[ \delta_T(j,n)=j-n\pmod4, \qquad \delta_X(j,n)=j\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}n. \tag{136.19} \]

They have identical response histograms in every row and every column. Yet \(\delta_T\) obeys (136.7), whereas at \(j=n=0\) the mixed difference of \(\delta_X\) is \(0-1-1+0=2\pmod4\). Accordingly, in the native pattern-class domain of the declared owner,

\[ \boxed{W_J^{\mathrm{pat}}(\{\delta_T\})=1, \qquad W_J^{\mathrm{pat}}(\{\delta_X\})=0.} \tag{136.20} \]

This is an exact possible-count exclusion derived from independent comparison-identification conditions, not from a propagation postulate.

There is a stronger check against overinterpretation. Let \(G=(0,2,1,3)\) and define

\[ \delta_A(j,n)=j+G(n)\pmod4. \tag{136.21} \]

Every row and every column of \(\delta_A\) contains all four response labels exactly once. It therefore has the same marginal histograms as \(\delta_T\). It satisfies the new mixed-difference condition and is admitted. Yet its successive stage offsets are not a uniform \(\pm1\), so it belongs to neither one-address-per-stage translating class.

Thus even within the restricted admitted domain, equal marginal counts do not determine whether a pattern has translating incidence. The theorem identifies a proper subset of complete possibilities; it does not assign a physical wave to every member.

136.9. Symmetric possibility does not determine realized incidence

The admitted set \(\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}\) is closed under address reversal \(j\mapsto-j\), stage reversal \(n\mapsto-n\), and response inversion \(\delta\mapsto-\delta\). In particular, address reversal exchanges the two translating subclasses. Their admitted possible counts are equal: \(16\) versus \(16\), with shared patterns handled once in the untagged count.

None of these cardinalities supplies the realized number of positive or negative propagation occurrences, the realized recurrence of an individual turbine, or the occupancy of a response class. Such claims require a separate realized domain and physical mapping.

If a specific candidate complete record includes an orientation-balanced turbine signature, that is a property of a possible complete record; it is not evidence of equal observed forward and reverse cycling. This is the count discipline of Part I and Addendum 131.

136.10. Theorem 5 — Native incidence under recursive replacement

Let \(\mathcal H_J\) be the owner’s admitted domain of complete possible records, including any finer subordinate realization details \(\eta\). Let \(q:\mathcal H_J\to\mathcal D\) be the receiving-boundary read. A property \(I\) survives as a well-defined readable classification precisely when it is constant on every \(q\)-fiber:

\[ \boxed{q(h)=q(h')\Longrightarrow I(h)=I(h').} \tag{136.22} \]

This is the established finite factorization criterion of Addenda 120, 122, and 132, applied to the present unitless incidence domain.

If \(q(\delta,\eta)=\delta\), the zero-mixed-difference property and translation-class membership survive after the finer \(\eta\) identities are lost. If \(q\) retains only row and column phase histograms, translation membership does not generally survive, even on \(\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{id}}\): the admitted \(\delta_T\) and \(\delta_A\) of Section 136.8 have the same marginals but different translating incidence.

The receiving boundary can therefore preserve a complete response relation without retaining subordinate identities, but it need not preserve every relation after a poorer read. Recursive replacement does not reconstruct the full incidence from marginal count summaries.

136.11. What is, and is not, native identification

The identification in (136.5) says that one enclosing owner recognizes the address comparison \(j\to j+1\) as the same comparison class across its stage labels. It does not say that the same subordinate state persists, that a physically located object travels, or that a measured response value must remain static.

Yet this identification is more restrictive than the general Part I requirement that an enclosing boundary define some compatible joint realization domain. It must be declared in the owner’s native identification and shown to be independently admissible. No universal Part I theorem currently requires every enclosing comparison to be additive-separable or zero-mixed-difference.

An owner with stage-dependent spatial comparisons may legitimately admit patterns excluded here. Therefore this addendum supplies a nontrivial conditional mathematical theorem, not a proof that R²D universally forbids coupled spatial–recurrence structure.

Neither (136.7) nor (136.8) should be identified with zero electromagnetic field curvature or a physical gauge-flatness law. For any node-valued \(\delta\), the sum of actual edge increments around a rectangle telescopes regardless of (136.7); local reference equivalence and electromagnetic gauge curvature are different mathematical constructions.

136.12. Exact mathematical status and implications

Established for the explicitly specified finite enclosing owner:

  1. Either one of the two comparison-identification conditions is equivalent to zero mixed spatial–recurrence difference (Theorem 1).
  2. Every admitted pattern is uniquely represented as \(c+F(j)+G(n)\), with no asserted persistence of subordinate identities (Theorem 2).
  3. The admitted possible-pattern count is \(m^{L+T-1}\); for \(4\times4\) with four response values it is \(16,384\) (Theorem 3).
  4. Both stage-stationary and translating-incidence patterns remain permitted, in exactly counted subclasses (Theorem 4).
  5. A specified nonseparable pattern is excluded even though its marginals match those of an admitted translating pattern; another nontranslating pattern with the same marginals remains admitted.
  6. Complete incidence classifications survive recursive replacement exactly when they factor through the receiving-boundary read (Theorem 5).

Not established: the native necessity of the identification premise at every R²D boundary; an exclusive propagation law; a unique phase group or number of response components; how many admitted patterns nature realizes; an actual wave, electromagnetic field, thermal pressure, frequency in hertz, propagation speed, mass, or \(mc^2\).

A stronger result would require an independent native-identification or compatibility criterion that selects (136.5), or another restrictive relation, without importing a later physical wave law. Only then could a specific excluded possibility be called a consequence of the more primitive R²D axioms rather than of the chosen enclosing model.

136.13. Conclusion and next mathematical target

The structure of complete possible symmetric recurrence is richer than its marginal count summaries. One enclosing owner can declare equivalences among response comparisons across addresses and recurrence stages, and those declarations have exact consequences for the admissible complete patterns. They exclude almost the entire unrestricted candidate domain in the finite example, yet they do not force propagation or any realized orientation.
