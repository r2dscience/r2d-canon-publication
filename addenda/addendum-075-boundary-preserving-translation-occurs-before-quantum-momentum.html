Addendum 75 — Boundary-Preserving Translation Occurs Before Quantum Momentum

Spatial Phase Translation, the Inverse-Length Generator, and the Origin of the Quantum Momentum Operator

Abstract

Addendum 74 derived the abstract temporal structure of quantum evolution from boundary-preserving recurrence.

A quantum boundary that evolves without recursive replacement preserves its normalized realization relations. Continuous preservation of that relational structure selects a unitary representation. For an autonomous recurrence, the resulting one-parameter unitary group possesses a self-adjoint generator Kt :

\[ U(t)=e^{-iK_t t}. \]

Only afterward does the empirical physical calibration

\[ H=\hbar K_t \]

assign the generator an energetic read, yielding

\[ i\hbar\frac{d}{dt}|\psi\rangle=H|\psi\rangle\]

The present addendum develops the spatial complement.

Once a quantum boundary has acquired a physical spatial projection, its spatial relation may change without changing the count boundary itself.

A physical translation by displacement

\[\mathbf a\]

therefore has the boundary structure

\[ B_n\to B_n\]

The spatial coordinate changes.

The state classification need not.

The normalized realization relations among quantum states must consequently remain invariant.

Continuous translations are therefore represented unitarily:

\[ T^\dagger(a)T(a)=I. \]

Spatial translations compose according to

\[ T(a)T(b)=T(a+b), \]

with

\[ T(0)=I\]

The continuous physical translation group therefore possesses commuting self-adjoint generators.

Write

\[\mathbf K=(K_x,K_y,K_z)\]

Then:

\[ T(\mathbf a)=e^{-i\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf K}. \]

The generator K has dimensions of inverse length.

It is not yet physical momentum.

The position representation determines its form.

Let

\[\psi(\mathbf x)=\langle\mathbf x|\psi\rangle\]

An active translation by a acts as

\[ [T(a)\psi](x)=\psi(x-a). \]

For infinitesimal displacement,

\[\psi(\mathbf x-\mathbf a)=\psi(\mathbf x)-\mathbf a\cdot\nabla\psi(\mathbf x)+O(a^2)\]

Thus:

\[ T(\mathbf a)=I-\mathbf a\cdot\nabla+\cdots\]

But the generator form gives

\[ T(\mathbf a)=e^{-i\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf K}=I-i\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf K+\cdots\]

Comparison gives

\[ \mathbf K=-i\nabla. \]

This result arises before momentum is introduced.

The spatial translation generator is fixed by the physical action of translation on the projected position representation.

Only afterward does the quantum action calibration assign a mechanical place value:

\[ \mathbf P=\hbar\mathbf K. \]

Therefore:

\[\mathbf P=-i\hbar\nabla\]

The quantum momentum operator is thus the physical mechanical read of the generator of boundary- preserving spatial translation.

For a spatial phase eigenstate

\[\psi_{\mathbf k}(\mathbf x)=e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}\]

one has

\[-i\nabla e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}=\mathbf k e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}\]

Therefore:

\[\mathbf K|\mathbf k\rangle=\mathbf k|\mathbf k\rangle\]

and hence

\[\mathbf P|\mathbf k\rangle=\hbar\mathbf k|\mathbf k\rangle\]

The familiar de Broglie relation follows:

\[ \mathbf p=\hbar\mathbf k. \]

R2D therefore reverses the ordinary explanatory order.

It does not begin with a particle possessing momentum and then assign the operator

−iℏ∇.

It begins with a boundary-preserving translation of a phase-carrying quantum state.

The translation has an inverse-length generator.

The position representation forces that generator to be

\[-i\nabla\]

The physical action calibration ℏ then converts inverse-length phase translation into momentum.

Thus:

spatial translation → inverse-length generator → action calibration → momentum.

This spatial construction is exactly parallel to the temporal construction of Addendum 74:

\[ K_t\xrightarrow{\hbar}H\]

and

\[\mathbf K\xrightarrow{\hbar}\mathbf P\]

The two familiar Planck relations,

\[ E=\hbar\omega\]

and

\[\mathbf p=\hbar\mathbf k\]

are therefore not unrelated quantum rules.

They are temporal and spatial versions of one physical mapping:

ℏ × phase generator → physical action-conjugate coordinate.

Temporal phase recurrence acquires the physical read of energy.

Spatial phase translation acquires the physical read of momentum.

The result also fixes the canonical position-momentum commutator.

Because the projected position operator acts by multiplication,

Xi ψ(x) = xi ψ(x),

while

\[ P_j=-i\hbar\partial_j\]

one obtains

\[ [X_i,P_j]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}. \]

Noncommutation is therefore not inserted as an additional primitive quantum law.

The position coordinate labels the spatial classification.

Momentum generates changes of that classification.

The two operations cannot commute.

The corresponding uncertainty relation,

\[\Delta x_i\,\Delta p_i\ge\frac\hbar2\]

follows afterward from the general operator uncertainty relation.

R2D does not interpret this result as evidence that a primitive particle secretly possesses an exact but inaccessible trajectory.

Addendum 72 established the opposite ordering.

At the quantum boundary, subordinate path identity has already been recursively replaced.

The position representation belongs to the projected quantum boundary.

Translation relates equivalent spatial classifications of that boundary.

Thus:

translation symmetry≠hidden continuous trajectory.

The central statement is therefore:

once a quantum boundary has acquired a physical spatial read, continuous displacement that preserves the count boundary must preserve its normalized realization geometry and is therefore represented unitarily. The translation group possesses an inverse-length self-adjoint generator, and the action of translation in the position representation forces that generator to be \(\mathbf K=-i\nabla\). Momentum appears

only after the physical action calibration \(\mathbf P=\hbar\mathbf K\), yielding \(\mathbf P=-i\hbar\nabla\) and \(\mathbf p=\hbar\mathbf k\). Quantum momentum is therefore the mechanical read of boundary-preserving spatial translation rather than a primitive property of > a hidden particle trajectory.

And hence:

boundary-preserving translation occurs before quantum momentum.

1. The Spatial Problem Begins After Space Has Been Projected

R2D does not begin with primitive Euclidean position.

A quantum boundary must first acquire a physical spatial read.

Only then is a coordinate such as

\[\mathbf x\]

defined.

2. The Position Coordinate Belongs to the Physical Projection

Let

\[|\mathbf x\rangle\]

denote the state coordinate corresponding to projected physical position.

Then:

\[\psi(\mathbf x)=\langle\mathbf x|\psi\rangle\]

3. Position Is Not the Recovered Subordinate Path

The coordinate

\[\mathbf x\]

does not restore the trajectory erased by recursive replacement.

It classifies the state relative to the physical spatial projection of the present boundary.

4. A Spatial Translation Need Not Replace the Boundary

Displace the same quantum boundary by

\[\mathbf a\]

The translated state can remain in the same count domain:

\[ B_n\to B_n\]

5. This Is the Spatial Analogue of Addendum 74

Addendum 74 considered

recurrence advance without boundary replacement.

The present addendum considers

spatial displacement without boundary replacement.

6. Both Must Preserve Realization Geometry

Let two quantum rays be

\[|\psi\rangle\]

and

\[|\phi\rangle\]

Their normalized transition measure is

\[|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|^2\]

7. Translate Both States by the Same Physical Displacement

\[|\psi\rangle\to T(\mathbf a)|\psi\rangle\]

\[|\phi\rangle\to T(\mathbf a)|\phi\rangle\]

8. A Common Translation Must Not Change Their Relational Measure

Therefore:

\[|\langle\phi|T^\dagger(\mathbf a)T(\mathbf a)|\psi\rangle|^2=|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|^2\]

9. Transition-Measure Preservation Again Restricts the Representation

As in Addendum 74, preservation of all quantum transition measures gives a unitary or antiunitary ray representation.

10. Translation Is Continuously Connected to Identity

At zero displacement,

\[ T(0)=I\]

For infinitesimal displacement,

\[ T(\mathbf a+\delta\mathbf a)\to T(\mathbf a)\]

as

\[\delta\mathbf a\to0\]

11. The Continuous Translation Branch Is Therefore Unitary

\[ T^\dagger(\mathbf a)T(\mathbf a)=I\]

12. Translation Composition Is Geometric

Translate first by

\[\mathbf b\]

and then by

The net physical displacement is

\[\mathbf a+\mathbf b\]

Thus:

\[ T(\mathbf a)T(\mathbf b)=T(\mathbf a+\mathbf b)\]

13. Flat Spatial Translation Is Abelian

For ordinary Euclidean translation,

\[\mathbf a+\mathbf b=\mathbf b+\mathbf a\]

Therefore:

\[ T(\mathbf a)T(\mathbf b)=T(\mathbf b)T(\mathbf a)\]

14. This Spatial Geometry Is Already a Physical Projection

The translation group

\[\mathbb R^3\]

is not introduced here as primitive R2D geometry.

It belongs to the mature spatial representation being deprojected.

15. Continuous Unitary Translation Has Self-Adjoint Generators

There exist operators

\[ K_x,K_y,K_z\]

such that

\[ T(\mathbf a)=e^{-i(a_xK_x+a_yK_y+a_zK_z)}\]

16. Write the Generator Vector

\[\mathbf K=(K_x,K_y,K_z)\]

Then:

\[ T(\mathbf a)=e^{-i\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf K}\]

17. The Generators Commute in the Flat Translation Projection

Because spatial translations commute,

\[[K_i,K_j]=0\]

18. Their Dimensions Are Inverse Length

The exponent must be dimensionless.

Therefore:

\[[K_i]=L^{-1}\]

19. K Is Not Yet Momentum

At this stage,

K = spatial translation generator.

No mechanical units have yet been assigned.

20. The Position Representation Determines Its Differential Form

Let

\[\psi(\mathbf x)=\langle\mathbf x|\psi\rangle\]

An active translation of the physical state by a gives

\[[T(\mathbf a)\psi](\mathbf x)=\psi(\mathbf x-\mathbf a)\]

21. Expand for Small Translation

\[\psi(\mathbf x-\mathbf a)=\psi(\mathbf x)-\mathbf a\cdot\nabla\psi(\mathbf x)+O(a^2)\]

22. Therefore

\[ T(\mathbf a)=I-\mathbf a\cdot\nabla+O(a^2)\]

23. Expand the Generator Representation

\[ e^{-i\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf K}=I-i\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf K+O(a^2)\]

24. Compare the Infinitesimal Forms

\[-i\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf K=-\mathbf a\cdot\nabla\]

Since this holds for arbitrary a,

\[\mathbf K=-i\nabla\]

25. This Result Precedes Momentum

The differential operator is fixed by translation geometry before any physical momentum calibration is introduced.

26. The Spatial Generator Is Therefore

\[ K_i=-i\partial_i\]

27. The Generator Is Self-Adjoint Under Appropriate Boundary Conditions

The factor

\[-i\]

converts the anti-Hermitian differential translation generator into a self-adjoint observable generator on its appropriate domain.

28. Spatial Phase Is an Eigenfunction of Translation

Consider

\[\psi_{\mathbf k}(\mathbf x)=e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}\]

29. Apply the Generator

\[-i\nabla e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}=\mathbf k e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}\]

Therefore:

\[\mathbf K\psi_{\mathbf k}=\mathbf k\psi_{\mathbf k}\]

30. k Is First an Inverse-Length Phase-Translation Eigenvalue

At this stage,

\[ k\]

is not yet physical momentum.

It measures spatial phase change per unit projected distance.

31. The Phase Difference Across Displacement Is

\[\phi(\mathbf x)=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x+\phi_0\]

\[\phi(\mathbf x+\mathbf a)-\phi(\mathbf x)=\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf a\]

32. Thus k Is the Spatial Gradient of Closure Phase

33. This Makes the Parallel With Temporal Frequency Explicit

Temporal recurrence gives

Spatial recurrence gives

\[\nabla\phi=\mathbf k\]

34. The Phase Four-Gradient Structure Comes Later

The pair

\[(\omega,\mathbf k)\]

It is downstream of boundary-native closure.

35. Physical Momentum Requires a New Calibration

The inverse-length generator must be mapped to mechanical units.

Introduce:

\[\mathbf P=\hbar\mathbf K\]

36. Therefore

\[\mathbf P=-i\hbar\nabla\]

This is the quantum momentum operator.

37. The Operator Is Not Assumed From Particle Mechanics

Its mathematical form follows from continuous spatial translation.

The factor ℏ supplies the physical momentum place value.

38. Momentum Eigenstates Follow Immediately

Since

\[\mathbf K|\mathbf k\rangle=\mathbf k|\mathbf k\rangle\]

one obtains

\[\mathbf P|\mathbf k\rangle=\hbar\mathbf k|\mathbf k\rangle\]

39. Therefore the Momentum Eigenvalue Is

\[\mathbf p=\hbar\mathbf k\]

40. The de Broglie Relation Is Consequently Downstream

For magnitude

\[ k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda},\qquad p=\hbar k=\frac{h}{\lambda}\]

Thus:

\[ p=\frac h\lambda\]

41. Therefore

\[\lambda=\frac hp\]

The de Broglie wavelength is the spatial phase-recurrence read of the translation generator after momentum calibration.

42. This Does Not Mean a Particle Is Literally a Wave

The particle count and the spatial recurrence read remain different projections.

A wavelength need not be a physical material oscillation carried inside a corpuscle.

closure → phase → wave.

spatial phase translation → k → p.

boundary-native recurrence

\[ℏ\]

48. ℏ Has Not Yet Been Derived From Primitive R2D

This addendum does not claim that the numerical value or universality of ℏ has been obtained from recursive counting.

It remains a physical calibration constant.

49. But Its Structural Role Is Now Sharper

Rather than entering as an unexplained “quantum of action,” it functions as

phase-generator place-value conversion.

50. Position and Momentum Have Different Operator Roles

In the position representation,

\[ X_i\psi(\mathbf x)=x_i\psi(\mathbf x)\]

Position labels the physical spatial classification.

51. Momentum Generates Translation of That Classification

\[ P_j=-i\hbar\partial_j\]

Thus position and momentum cannot play identical algebraic roles.

52. Compute Their Commutator

Act on arbitrary ψ :

\[ X_iP_j\psi=-i\hbar x_i\partial_j\psi\]

53. Reverse the Order

\[ P_jX_i\psi=-i\hbar\partial_j(x_i\psi)\]

Using the product rule,

\[ P_jX_i\psi=-i\hbar(\delta_{ij}\psi+x_i\partial_j\psi)\]

54. Subtract

\[[X_i,P_j]\psi=i\hbar\delta_{ij}\psi\]

Therefore:

\[[X_i,P_j]=i\hbar\delta_{ij}\]

55. Canonical Noncommutation Is Therefore a Translation Result

It follows because:

Xi = spatial classification coordinate,

whereas

Pj = generator of spatial reclassification.

56. Translation Changes Position

Indeed,

\[ T^\dagger(\mathbf a)\mathbf X T(\mathbf a)=\mathbf X+\mathbf a\]

Thus the generator cannot commute with the coordinate it changes.

57. Noncommutation Is Not Primitive Indeterminacy

The algebra comes first from the relation between a coordinate and its continuous translation generator.

58. The Uncertainty Relation Follows

For any pair of self-adjoint operators A and B ,

\[\Delta A\,\Delta B\ge\frac12|\langle[A,B]\rangle|\]

59. Apply It to Position and Momentum

Using

\[[X_i,P_i]=i\hbar\]

one obtains

\[\Delta x_i\,\Delta p_i\ge\frac\hbar2\]

60. Thus Heisenberg Uncertainty Comes After Translation Structure

The sequence is

translation → generator → commutator → uncertainty.

61. R2D Does Not Interpret This as Hidden-Path Ignorance

The uncertainty relation does not mean that an exact subordinate trajectory exists but cannot be measured sufficiently well.

The subordinate path has already been recursively replaced at the quantum boundary.

62. Nor Does Translation Restore That Path

A translated quantum state relates two spatial representations of the same boundary.

It does not interpolate a hidden primitive trajectory between them.

63. This Distinction Is Essential

The statement

\[[T(\mathbf a)\psi](\mathbf x)=\psi(\mathbf x-\mathbf a)\]

is a transformation law on the physical quantum projection.

It is not an ontological assertion that a point particle traversed every intermediate coordinate.

64. Translation Is a Relation Between Boundary Classifications

The same boundary is classified relative to different physical spatial coordinates.

That is enough to define the generator.

65. The Translation Operator Preserves Born Geometry

Because

\[ T^\dagger T=I\]

\[|\langle\phi|\psi\rangle|^2=|\langle T\phi|T\psi\rangle|^2\]

Thus the normalized realization relations are unchanged.

66. This Is Why Translation Is Boundary Preserving

The state acquires a different spatial coordinate read without changing its underlying realization measure.

67. A Spatially Inhomogeneous Environment Is Different

If physical constraint varies with position, a translation can change the relation to the enclosing environment.

Then translation need not be a symmetry of the Hamiltonian.

68. But the Momentum Operator Still Generates Kinematic Translation

The distinction is between:

translation representation

and

translation symmetry of dynamics.

69. Translation Symmetry of the Hamiltonian Requires

\[[H,\mathbf P]=0\]

This is an additional physical condition.

70. Therefore Momentum Exists as Generator Even When It Is Not Conserved

A spatially varying constraint may break translation symmetry while leaving the translation generator mathematically defined.

71. Momentum Conservation Is a Further Result

If

[H, P] = 0,

then momentum expectation is conserved under unitary evolution.

This is the quantum projection of spatial homogeneity.

72. The Present Addendum Does Not Need That Conservation Law

Its primary result is the origin of the momentum operator itself.

73. Principle — Spatial Projection Precedes Quantum Position

Position is a physical coordinate assigned to the quantum boundary after recursive realization.

It is not a primitive R2D variable.

\[ B_n\to B_n\]
