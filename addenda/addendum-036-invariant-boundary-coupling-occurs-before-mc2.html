Background Rapidity, Mechanical Calibration, and the Origin of Rest Energy

Addendum 36 — Invariant Boundary Coupling Occurs Before mc²

Abstract

Mass-energy equivalence is conventionally written

\[E_0=mc^2.\]

The equation is often read as though mass contains a primitive reservoir of energy whose magnitude is obtained by multiplying mass by c2 .

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) permits a different logical order.

Addendum 35 established the reference architecture prior to relativistic mechanics. A local boundary does not define its own zero of motion. Its velocity is read relative to an enclosing rapidity landscape. For a fixed local boundary Bm relative to reference Φ,

\[\frac{v_{m\mid\Phi}}{c_{\Phi}}=\tanh\xi_{m\mid\Phi}.\]

The rapidity coordinate belongs to the cross-boundary motion relation

\[B_m\mid\Phi,\]

whereas mass belongs to the local boundary coupling itself.

Thus:

\(m\to B_m,\quad\xi\to B_m\mid\Phi,\quad c_{\Phi}\to\Phi\) calibration.

Relativity is therefore not generated by rest mass. The background-relative rapidity geometry exists before mass is represented mechanically within it.

This addendum asks:

What mechanical and energetic coordinates does an orientation-even mass coupling acquire when it is represented in the background rapidity geometry derived in Addendum 35?

Begin with the most general positive reciprocal mechanical pair compatible with additive rapidity,

\[\Pi_+=\Pi_0e^{a\xi},\]

\[\Pi_-=\Pi_0e^{-a\xi},\]

where the invariant mechanical scale Π0 and rapidity weight a > 0 are initially unknown.

Define the orientation-odd additive mechanical read as momentum,

\[p=\frac{\Pi_+-\Pi_-}{2}=\Pi_0\sinh(a\xi).\]

Use the operational low-speed inertial-mass calibration

\[m=\left.\frac{dp}{dv}\right|_{v=0}.\]

Since

\[v=c\tanh\xi,\]

this gives

\[\Pi_0=\frac{mc}{a}.\]

Define the corresponding orientation-even mechanical coordinate

\[Q=\frac{\Pi_++\Pi_-}{2}=\Pi_0\cosh(a\xi).\]

Allow its most general constant energetic calibration,

\[E=bcQ,\]

where b > 0 is also unknown.

Mechanical work and momentum satisfy

\[dE=F\,dx,\qquad F=\frac{dp}{dt},\]

and therefore

\[\frac{dE}{dp}=v.\]

Substitution gives the identity

\[b\tanh(a\xi)=\tanh\xi.\]

Requiring this relation near zero rapidity forces

\[a=1,\qquad b=1.\]

Therefore

\[\Pi_0=mc,\]

and the mechanical coordinates become

\[p=mc\sinh\xi,\]

\[E=mc^2\cosh\xi.\]

Consequently,

\[E^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4.\]

At zero background-relative rapidity,

\[\xi=0,\]

momentum vanishes while the orientation-even energetic read remains:

\[E_0=mc^2.\]

Rest energy is therefore not inserted as a primitive property of mass and is not used to construct Lorentz geometry.

It is the energetic calibration of an invariant local boundary coupling after that coupling is represented within an already-existing background rapidity geometry.

The central claim is:

invariant boundary coupling occurs before \(mc^2\).

1. Addendum 35 Removed Mass From the Foundation of Relativity

The preceding addendum established Lorentz geometry without beginning from rest mass.

For a local boundary Bm contained within enclosing reference Φ,

\[\xi_{m\mid\Phi}\]

is the logarithmic directional coordinate of the local realization relative to the background rapidity landscape.

Its physical velocity read is

\[\frac{v_{m\mid\Phi}}{c_{\Phi}}=\tanh\xi_{m\mid\Phi}.\]

The reference zero belongs to the enclosure.

The clock-ruler conversion

\[c_{\Phi}\]

belongs to the background calibration.

Neither is produced by mass.

Therefore the logical ordering is

reference boundary → rapidity geometry → mechanical representation of mass.

This prevents mass-energy equivalence from being used implicitly to create the geometry from which it will later be derived.

2. Mass and Rapidity Have Different Boundary Owners

Let

\[B_m\]

be a selected local mass-bearing boundary.

Its orientation-even physical coupling read is

\[m.\]

Its motion relative to enclosing reference Φ is

\[\xi_{m\mid\Phi}.\]

Thus

\[m=m[B_m],\]

whereas

\[\xi=\xi[B_m\mid\Phi].\]

Likewise,

\[c_{\Phi}=c[\Phi].\]

These symbols may appear in the same physical equation.

They do not belong to the same primitive boundary relation.

This distinction is essential.

3. Rest Is Not Defined by Mass

Mechanical rest means

\[\xi_{m\mid\Phi}=0.\]

It does not mean that mass has ceased to possess an internal or enclosing physical relation.

Nor does mass itself define the zero.

The condition

\[\xi=0\]

is a relation between Bm and Φ.

Thus:

rest is background relative.

The problem of rest energy must therefore be posed after the reference background has already been defined.

4. What Must Be Derived

The familiar relativistic formulas are

\[p=\gamma mv,\]

\[E=\gamma mc^2,\]

and

\[E^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4.\]

These equations cannot be premises.

Likewise, the invariant mechanical scale must not be assumed to be

\[mc.\]

The energetic zero must not be assumed to be

\[mc^2.\]

The task is to derive all of these from:

  1. the background rapidity geometry already established;
  2. the independently defined physical mass coupling;
  3. low-speed inertial mass;
  4. mechanical momentum and work.

5. Mechanical Representation Must Respect Rapidity Composition

Addendum 35 established that nested collinear reference shifts add:

\[\xi_{02}=\xi_{01}+\xi_{12}.\]

Let

\[K(\xi)\]

be a positive mechanical scaling associated with that directional relation.

Successive reference changes must satisfy

\[K(\xi_1+\xi_2)=K(\xi_1)K(\xi_2).\]

Under the ordinary continuity requirement, the positive solutions are exponential:

\[K(\xi)=e^{a\xi},\]

where

\[a>0\]

is initially unknown.

Reversing direction gives

\[K(-\xi)=e^{-a\xi}.\]

Thus the most general positive reciprocal mechanical representation is

\[\Pi_+=\Pi_0e^{a\xi},\]

\[\Pi_-=\Pi_0e^{-a\xi}.\]

6. Do Not Assume Π0 = mc

The quantity

\[\Pi_0\]

has dimensions of momentum.

It is invariant under

\[\xi\longrightarrow-\xi.\]

But nothing yet establishes

\[\Pi_0=mc.\]

Likewise, nothing yet establishes that the rapidity weight of the mechanical pair is exactly unity.

Therefore both

\[\Pi_0\]

and

\[a\]

must remain unresolved.

This is necessary to avoid inserting the desired result into the starting equations.

7. The Mechanical Pair Has an Area-Before-Radius Structure

Before energy or momentum is defined, the reciprocal pair already possesses the invariant product

\[\Pi_+\Pi_-=\Pi_0^2.\]

Define the prior mechanical enclosure measure

\[\mathcal A_m\equiv\Pi_+\Pi_-.\]

Then define the corresponding invariant norm

\[\rho_m\equiv\sqrt{\mathcal A_m}=\Pi_0.\]

The logical order is therefore

reciprocal mechanical enclosure → invariant mechanical norm.

The invariant radius-like quantity is derived from the product.

It does not construct the product.

This is the mechanical form of area before radius.

8. Momentum Must Be Orientation Odd

Physical momentum reverses when the direction of motion reverses.

Therefore momentum belongs to the orientation-odd linear sector of the reciprocal mechanical pair.

Define

\[p\equiv\frac{\Pi_+-\Pi_-}{2}.\]

Then

\[p=\frac{\Pi_0}{2}(e^{a\xi}-e^{-a\xi}),\]

so

\[p=\Pi_0\sinh(a\xi).\]

Immediately,

\[p(-\xi)=-p(\xi).\]

This has the required directional symmetry.

9. The Orientation-Even Mechanical Coordinate Is Not Yet Energy

The corresponding even linear combination is

\[Q\equiv\frac{\Pi_++\Pi_-}{2}.\]

Therefore,

\[Q=\frac{\Pi_0}{2}(e^{a\xi}+e^{-a\xi}),\]

so

\[Q=\Pi_0\cosh(a\xi).\]

Under motion reversal,

\[Q(-\xi)=Q(\xi).\]

The quantity Q has momentum dimensions.

It has not yet been identified with energy divided by c.

10. The Even/Odd Mechanical Mapping Is a Physical Mapping

The use of linear even and odd combinations should remain explicit as a mapping step.

Energy and momentum are additive physical coordinates.

Orientation symmetry requires that an additive directional quantity be odd under reversal, whereas an additive scalar energetic quantity be even.

The two linear combinations

\[\Pi_+-\Pi_-\]

and

\[\Pi_++\Pi_-\]

are therefore the unique odd and even additive combinations of the reciprocal pair, up to normalization.

Thus:

\(p\) ↔︎ odd mechanical read,

\(Q\) ↔︎ even mechanical read.

This is a physical mapping principle, not a primitive Part I theorem.

11. Low-Speed Inertial Mass Independently Calibrates the Mechanical Norm

Define inertial mass operationally through the low-speed slope of momentum:

\[m=\left.\frac{dp}{dv}\right|_{v=0}.\]

From Addendum 35,

\[v=c\tanh\xi.\]

Near

\[\xi=0,\]

\[v\simeq c\xi.\]

Meanwhile,

\[p=\Pi_0\sinh(a\xi)\simeq a\Pi_0\xi.\]

Therefore,

\[p\simeq\frac{a\Pi_0}{c}v.\]

Hence

\[m=\frac{a\Pi_0}{c}.\]

Thus,

\[\Pi_0=\frac{mc}{a}.\]

No mass-energy relation has been used.

12. mc Has Not Yet Been Derived

At this stage,

\[\Pi_0=\frac{mc}{a}.\]

Therefore

\[\Pi_0=mc\]

cannot yet be written.

That result requires

\[a=1.\]

The value of a must therefore follow from a physical relation independent of the low-speed mass calibration.

13. Energy Must Belong to the Even Sector

Energy does not reverse sign merely because momentum reverses.

Its inertial motion-dependent read therefore belongs to the orientation-even sector.

Since Q has momentum dimensions, introduce the most general constant conversion

\[E=bcQ,\]

where

\[b>0\]

is an unknown dimensionless normalization.

Therefore,

\[E=bc\Pi_0\cosh(a\xi).\]

Neither a nor b has yet been fixed.

14. Why c Appears Here

The quantity Q has momentum dimensions.

Energy requires momentum multiplied by a velocity conversion.

Addendum 35 already established the relevant physical velocity calibration:

\[c=c_{\Phi}.\]

Thus the appearance of one factor of c in

\[E=bcQ\]

does not assume

\[E=mc^2.\]

It converts the even mechanical coordinate into energy units using the already-established clock-ruler calibration of the enclosing rapidity geometry.

The remaining normalization is b.

15. Mechanical Work Provides the Independent Constraint

For a mechanical displacement,

\[dE=F\,dx.\]

Momentum changes according to

\[F=\frac{dp}{dt}.\]

Therefore,

\[\frac{dE}{dt}=F\frac{dx}{dt}.\]

Since

\[v=\frac{dx}{dt},\]

we obtain

\[\frac{dE}{dt}=Fv.\]

Substitute

\[F=\frac{dp}{dt}.\]

Then

\[\frac{dE}{dt}=v\frac{dp}{dt}.\]

Hence

\[\frac{dE}{dp}=v.\]

This is the independent physical constraint linking the even energetic read to the odd momentum read.

16. Evaluate dE/dp

From

\[p=\Pi_0\sinh(a\xi),\]

we obtain

\[\frac{dp}{d\xi}=a\Pi_0\cosh(a\xi).\]

From

\[E=bc\Pi_0\cosh(a\xi),\]

we obtain

\[\frac{dE}{d\xi}=abc\Pi_0\sinh(a\xi).\]

Therefore,

\[\frac{dE}{dp}=bc\tanh(a\xi).\]

Mechanical work requires

\[\frac{dE}{dp}=v.\]

But Addendum 35 gives

\[v=c\tanh\xi.\]

Therefore,

\[bc\tanh(a\xi)=c\tanh\xi.\]

Cancel c:

\[b\tanh(a\xi)=\tanh\xi.\]

This identity must hold throughout the inertial domain.

17. The Rapidity Weight and Energy Normalization Are Forced Locally

The relation

\[b\tanh(a\xi)=\tanh\xi\]

can be evaluated near zero rapidity.

Use

\[\tanh\xi=\xi-\frac{\xi^3}{3}+O(\xi^5).\]

Then

\[b\tanh(a\xi)=ba\xi-\frac{ba^3}{3}\xi^3+O(\xi^5).\]

Equality with

\[\xi-\frac{\xi^3}{3}+O(\xi^5)\]

requires

\[ba=1,\]

and

\[ba^3=1.\]

Divide the second relation by the first:

\[a^2=1.\]

Since

\[a>0,\]

we obtain

\[a=1.\]

Then

\[ba=1\]

gives

\[b=1.\]

No infinite-rapidity limit is required.

The normalization is fixed by the local mechanical relation around the empirically accessible low-speed regime.

18. The Mechanical Invariant Is mc

Recall

\[\Pi_0=\frac{mc}{a}.\]

Since

\[a=1,\]

we obtain

\[\Pi_0=mc.\]

Therefore,

\[\mathcal A_m=\Pi_+\Pi_-=m^2c^2.\]

And the invariant mechanical norm is

\[\rho_m=mc.\]

The quantity mc has therefore been derived.

It was not assumed.

19. The Full Reciprocal Mechanical Pair

The two oriented mechanical coordinates are now

\[\Pi_+=mc\,e^\xi,\]

\[\Pi_-=mc\,e^{-\xi}.\]

These are not two hidden particles or two independent material momenta.

They are the opposed orientation-resolved mechanical coordinates of one invariant mass-bearing boundary represented in the enclosing rapidity geometry.

Their product is

\[\Pi_+\Pi_-=m^2c^2.\]

20. Momentum Is the Odd Read

The physical momentum is

\[p=\frac{\Pi_+-\Pi_-}{2}.\]

Therefore,

\[p=\frac{mc}{2}(e^\xi-e^{-\xi}).\]

Hence,

\[p=mc\sinh\xi.\]

Momentum is the orientation-odd mechanical read of the local mass coupling in the background rapidity geometry.

21. Energy Is the Even Read

The even mechanical coordinate is

\[Q=\frac{\Pi_++\Pi_-}{2}.\]

Thus,

\[Q=mc\cosh\xi.\]

Since

\[b=1,\]

the energetic read is

\[E=cQ.\]

Therefore,

\[E=mc^2\cosh\xi.\]

Energy is the energetic calibration of the orientation-even mechanical coordinate.

22. Rest Energy Follows at Zero Background-Relative Rapidity

Mechanical rest is

\[\xi=0\]

relative to the selected enclosing reference.

Then

\[\sinh0=0,\]

so

\[p=0.\]

But

\[\cosh0=1.\]

Therefore,

\[E_0=mc^2.\]

Rest energy has emerged without being used to define mass, rapidity, Lorentz geometry, or the invariant mechanical norm.

23. mc2 Is Not Energy Added to Mass

The equation

\[E_0=mc^2\]

should not be read as though a primitive material substance called mass has been multiplied by a mysterious universal number to reveal another primitive substance called energy.

The derivation instead gives:

\(m\) = local orientation-even coupling read,

\(c\) = background clock/ruler calibration,

\(mc\) = invariant mechanical norm,

and

\(mc^2\) = even energetic read of that norm.

Thus:

boundary coupling + background calibration → rest-energy read.

24. Area Before Radius Appears Again

The energy-momentum relation has the factorized form

\[E+pc=mc^2e^\xi,\]

\[E-pc=mc^2e^{-\xi}.\]

Multiply:

\[(E+pc)(E-pc)=m^2c^4.\]

Thus the prior multiplicative enclosure measure is

\[\mathcal A_E\equiv(E+pc)(E-pc).\]

The associated invariant norm is

\[\rho_E=\sqrt{\mathcal A_E}=mc^2.\]

So again:

enclosing product → invariant norm.

Rest energy is the radius-like energetic norm reconstructed from a prior multiplicative relation.

25. The Energy-Momentum Invariant

Using

\[E=mc^2\cosh\xi\]

and

\[pc=mc^2\sinh\xi,\]

we find

\[E^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4(\cosh^2\xi-\sinh^2\xi).\]

Since

\[\cosh^2\xi-\sinh^2\xi=1,\]

we obtain

\[E^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4.\]

The conventional energy-momentum mass shell is therefore the energetic/mechanical projection of the invariant local boundary coupling.

26. Velocity Follows From Energy and Momentum

Take the ratio

\[\frac{pc}{E}=\frac{\sinh\xi}{\cosh\xi}.\]

Therefore,

\[\frac{pc}{E}=\tanh\xi.\]

Addendum 35 gives

\[\frac vc=\tanh\xi.\]

Hence,

\[\frac{pc}{E}=\frac vc.\]

Therefore,

\[p=\frac{Ev}{c^2}.\]

This is a consequence, not a premise.

27. Recover the Standard Relativistic Forms

Since

\[\gamma=\cosh\xi,\]

we obtain

\[E=\gamma mc^2.\]

And since

\[\sinh\xi=\gamma\frac vc,\]

we obtain

\[p=\gamma mv.\]

Neither relation has been assumed.

Both follow from the background rapidity geometry, the local mass coupling, and independent mechanical calibration.

28. The Newtonian Limit

For

\[|\xi|\ll1,\]

\[\sinh\xi\simeq\xi,\]

\[\cosh\xi\simeq1+\frac{\xi^2}{2},\]

and

\[v=c\tanh\xi\simeq c\xi.\]

Therefore,

\[p\simeq mc\xi\simeq mv.\]

Likewise,

\[E\simeq mc^2+\frac12mc^2\xi^2.\]

Using

\[v\simeq c\xi,\]

we obtain

\[E\simeq mc^2+\frac12mv^2.\]

Thus Newtonian mechanics appears as the small-rapidity projection of the same invariant relation.

29. Kinetic Energy Is the Motion-Dependent Residual

Define

\[K\equiv E-E_0.\]

Then

\[K=mc^2(\cosh\xi-1).\]

For small rapidity,

\[K\simeq\frac12mv^2.\]

Thus kinetic energy is the directional-motion-dependent excess above the invariant zero-rapidity energetic read.

30. Rest Energy Is Not Hidden Translational Motion

At

\[\xi=0,\]

the reciprocal mechanical coordinates are equal:

\[\Pi_+=\Pi_-=mc.\]

Their odd difference vanishes:

\[p=0.\]

Their even sum remains:

\[Q=mc.\]

Therefore,

\[E_0=mc^2.\]

Rest energy is therefore not the energy of hidden net translational motion.

It is the energetic read of the invariant orientation-even mechanical relation when the background-relative odd coordinate vanishes.

31. Mass Is Not the Temporal Component

The invariant relation can be written

\[m=\frac{\sqrt{E^2-p^2c^2}}{c^2}.\]

Thus mass is not

\[E,\]

and it is not

\[p.\]

It is the local coupling whose mechanically calibrated invariant norm generates the combined energy- momentum relation.

Therefore:

mass ≠ energy component,

mass ≠ momentum component.

Mass belongs to the invariant boundary coupling class.

Energy and momentum belong to its background-relative physical decomposition.

32. The Null Branch Is the One-Sided Mechanical Limit

For a massive boundary,

\[E^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4>0.\]

Both reciprocal mechanical coordinates remain nonzero.

A null relation satisfies

\[E^2-p^2c^2=0.\]

Therefore,

\[E=|p|c.\]

This corresponds to a one-sided limit of the reciprocal mechanical relation.

A finite massless occurrence is not obtained by simply taking

\[m=0\]

at finite rapidity.

Instead one must take a joint limit such as

\[m\longrightarrow0,\qquad\xi\longrightarrow+\infty,\]

while

\[mc\,e^\xi\]

remains finite and

\[mc\,e^{-\xi}\longrightarrow0.\]

Thus the massless branch is the one-sided projection limit of the two-sided massive relation.

33. Two Massless Occurrences Can Define a Massive Enclosing Boundary

Consider two massless propagating occurrences with equal and opposite momenta:

\[p_1=-p_2.\]

Then

\[p_{\mathrm{tot}}=0.\]
