Complete Enclosing Extension Profiles, Native Readability, and the Limits of Recursive Carry

R²D Addendum 140 — Compatibility Fingerprints and Relational Persistence

Complete Enclosing Extension Profiles, Native Readability, and the Limits of Recursive Carry

Status: Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026
Canonical basis: R²D Part I (authoritative September 25, 2026 revision); Parts II–IV for downstream physical interpretation; Addenda 120, 122, 131–132, and 135–139.
Purpose: Determine whether a nontrivial relationship can be derived from complete enclosing compatibility, remain recognizable under changes of native read, and require neither persistent subordinate microstate identity nor an imported physical law of motion. Distinguish this conditional count theorem from physical realization and any universal preservation principle.

Abstract

Addendum 139 established that equal instantaneous and adjacent two-observation possible counts do not determine complete observational coherence. It also characterized the most informative classification common to specified background-native reads, but did not show that the primitive R²D axioms force that common classification to be nontrivial. The present addendum develops a more constructive, still conditional, result. It derives a nontrivial complete compatibility fingerprint from the independently declared native-matching owner of Addendum 137, without stipulating a motion or translation law and without selecting a desired surviving invariant in advance.

In the four-address, four-stage, four-response matching domain, every pair of stages has exactly two or four compatible complete extensions, according to the cycle type of its induced response permutation. The multiset of all six pair-extension counts has exactly two possibilities: \((4,4,4,4,4,4)\) for 144 complete patterns, and \((2,2,2,2,4,4)\) for 432. This exhaustive classification follows from the owner’s matching conditions. In both classes, every stage-anchored profile of its three comparisons with the other stages gives the same class-specific multiset: \((4,4,4)\) or \((2,2,4)\). Thus a relational distinction derived from complete enclosing compatibility remains identifiable through multiple context-complete stage reads, although none of those reads needs to retain the identity of a persistent subordinate object.

The fingerprint survives a specified native reclassification that forgets the labels of individual pair comparisons. But it need not survive every recursive replacement: a valid less-discriminating read can merge the two classes while retaining the aggregate original possible count through fiber weights. A single instantaneous stage or one fixed two-stage comparison does not universally identify the fingerprint. The derived result is therefore conditional relational readability, not automatic physical persistence. No realized recurrence count, measured correlation, wave equation, speed, mass-energy relation, or universal requirement that a new boundary retain this particular distinction follows from the primitive definitions alone.

140.1. The precise question after Addendum 139

An observed moving or recurring relationship need not be described primitively as a succession of states of one enduring subordinate object. In R²D, a boundary defines the complete compatibility of its peer realizations and assigns native count identities. Recursive carry reclassifies a completed local realization; it is not the passage of that local formal microstate intact into the enclosing count domain.

The question is whether enclosing compatibility can do more than admit arbitrarily stipulated relational persistence:

Can an independently specified, unitless enclosing compatibility rule yield a nonconstant relationship that survives multiple observational or native reclassifications, without putting a motion law or persistent subordinate identity into the premises?

Three different meanings must remain separate:

  1. Complete-pattern admissibility: a full relationship has at least one compatible possible enclosing record.
  2. Relational readability: a property of that relationship is uniquely determined by a selected receiving-boundary read, even when finer realizations are identified.
  3. Realized physical persistence: the physically occurring records maintain the relationship across observations, producing a measured recurrence or correlation.

The first two admit exact finite theorems. The third requires a realized domain and independently justified physical measurement maps. The present construction supplies no physical time, distance, or directional closure-production law.

140.2. The count boundary and its complete matching domain

Use the single enclosing owner \(J\) of Addendum 137. Let \(X\), \(T\), and \(Q\) be three distinguishable four-element label sets of addresses, recurrence-comparison stages, and native response classes. A possible complete response-incidence record is

\[ h:X\times T\longrightarrow Q. \tag{140.1} \]

For each \(t\in T\), define the stage classification

\[ p_t(x)=h(x,t). \tag{140.2} \]

The owner admits precisely the records satisfying two prior native completeness requirements:

  • (M1) Stage completeness: each \(p_t:X\to Q\) is bijective.
  • (M2) Address completeness: for each \(x\in X\), the map \(t\mapsto h(x,t)\) is bijective.

Write this complete admitted possible domain as \(H_J=\mathcal C_J^{\mathrm{match}}\). Addendum 137 proved

\[ \boxed{|H_J|=576.} \tag{140.3} \]

These premises do not specify traveling incidence, a physical stage duration, an identified object passing between addresses, or any realized occurrence. Part I permits the owner to declare such native compatibility; Part I does not require complete matching for every boundary.

A presentation label repeated at different addresses or stages denotes the same native response class, not persistence of a particular subordinate constituent.

140.3. Enclosing extension counts as relational data

For distinct stages \(t,u\), the complete record determines a two-stage restriction \(b_{tu}(h)=h|_{X\times\{t,u\}}\) and a response-class comparison permutation

\[ g_{ut}=p_u\circ p_t^{-1}:Q\to Q. \tag{140.4} \]

Because each address cannot repeat a response class at two distinct stages, \(g_{ut}\) is fixed-point-free. On four response labels, every fixed-point-free permutation is either a four-cycle or a product of two disjoint transpositions.

Let the complete enclosing extension count of the two-stage restriction be

\[ E_{tu}(h)=\Theta_J\big(b_{tu}(h)\big) =\left|\left\{h'\in H_J:h'|_{X\times\{t,u\}}=b_{tu}(h)\right\}\right|. \tag{140.5} \]

By the extension trichotomy of Addendum 137,

\[ \boxed{E_{tu}(h)=\begin{cases} 2,&g_{ut}\text{ is a four-cycle},\\ 4,&g_{ut}\text{ is two disjoint transpositions}. \end{cases}} \tag{140.6} \]

These are raw possible compatible-extension counts, not observed return counts or probabilities. For a receiving boundary that distinguishes the individual two-stage record with native local multiplicity \(W_{B,b}=1\), the corresponding across-boundary logarithmic possibility values are \(\ln2\) and \(\ln4\). Without that specified local multiplicity, \(E_{tu}\) must not be renamed the canonical possibility coordinate \(P\).

140.4. Definition — The complete compatibility fingerprint

There are six unordered pairs of distinct stages. Define the multiset of their extension counts

\[ \boxed{\mathcal F_J(h)=\left\{\!\left\{E_{tu}(h):\{t,u\}\subset T,\ t\ne u\right\}\!\right\}.} \tag{140.7} \]

The fingerprint retains the complete collection of pair-extension multiplicities, but not the identity of the stage pair attached to each number. Define also the derived scalar

\[ \boxed{I_J(h)=\#\big\{\{t,u\}:E_{tu}(h)=2\big\}.} \tag{140.8} \]

Neither \(\mathcal F_J\) nor \(I_J\) is declared to be a velocity, recurrence frequency, physical coherence, or mass class. Their values are computed only after \(H_J\) and its compatible extension counts have been specified. They are properties of the complete possible record in its enclosing domain.

140.5. Theorem 1 — An exact two-class fingerprint

Theorem 1 (complete matching selects two extension fingerprints). Every admitted complete record has exactly one of the two fingerprints

\[ \boxed{\begin{aligned} \mathcal F_A&=\{\!\{4,4,4,4,4,4\}\!\},& I_J&=0,\\ \mathcal F_B&=\{\!\{2,2,2,2,4,4\}\!\},& I_J&=4. \end{aligned}} \tag{140.9} \]

Their exact possible complete-record counts are

\[ \boxed{|H_A|=144,\qquad |H_B|=432,\qquad H_J=H_A\sqcup H_B.} \tag{140.10} \]

Proof. Fix an initial stage bijection \(p_0\), with \(4!=24\) choices. Relabel response coordinates temporarily so that \(p_0\) is the identity. Every other stage permutation must be a derangement relative to \(p_0\). There are six four-cycles and three double transpositions.

If \(p_1\) is a four-cycle, Addendum 137’s two-stage extension theorem gives exactly two ordered completions \((p_2,p_3)\). Directly imposing stage and address completeness shows that each has two stage pairs related by double transpositions and four related by four-cycles.

If \(p_1\) is a double transposition, there are four ordered completions. Of these, two have all six relative stage comparisons equal to double transpositions; the other two have two double-transposition and four four-cycle comparisons. These statements can be checked using representatives \(p_1=(0123)\) and \(p_1=(01)(23)\), respectively: every derangement of the same cycle type is conjugate to its representative, and conjugation preserves the complete matching test and cycle types.

Consequently the all-double-transposition class contains

\[ 24\cdot3\cdot2=144 \]

complete records. The mixed class contains

\[ 24\cdot(6\cdot2+3\cdot2)=432. \]

No other type is admitted. Formula (140.6) converts the cycle-type patterns directly into (140.9). \(\square\)

The enumeration is an elementary property of labeled order-four Latin-square compatibility; the claim here is its explicit enclosing extension-count and native-readability interpretation, not discovery of a new standalone combinatorial formula.

This classification is not the distinction between traveling and stationary incidence. Addendum 137 already showed that most matching-admissible patterns are not in its fixed diagonal-translation classes. The present fingerprint classifies complete enclosing compatibility, not physical motion.

140.6. Corollary — The complete pair-label signature has four classes

Let \(q_{\mathrm{pair}}(h)\) retain the six extension counts, each attached to its unordered stage pair. The fingerprint theorem has a sharper labeled form:

  • One signature has \(E_{tu}=4\) for all six pairs; it contains 144 complete records.
  • In each of three further signatures, exactly two disjoint stage pairs have \(E_{tu}=4\) and the remaining four pairs have \(E_{tu}=2\). Each signature contains 144 complete records.

The two \(E=4\) pairs in the mixed class form one of the three perfect matchings of four labeled stages. Thus

\[ \boxed{|q_{\mathrm{pair}}(H_J)|=4,\qquad |q_{\mathrm{pair}}^{-1}(s)|=144\text{ for every reached signature }s.} \tag{140.11} \]

Forgetting which of those three labeled pairings occurred merges the three mixed signatures into one class of 432. This is a loss of presentation-specific relational distinction, not a conversion of 432 possible earlier records into one original microstate.

140.7. Theorem 2 — Stage-anchored complete-context persistence

For each stage \(t\in T\), define its stage-anchored enclosing compatibility profile to be the multiset of its three complete extension counts to the other stages:

\[ \boxed{A_t(h)=\left\{\!\left\{E_{tu}(h):u\in T,\ u\ne t\right\}\!\right\}.} \tag{140.12} \]

This is not an instantaneous snapshot \(p_t\). It is a complete-context response profile anchored at \(t\); its definition interrogates the enclosing relation connecting \(t\) to the other stages.

Theorem 2 (nontrivial relational classification at every anchor). For every \(h\in H_J\) and every \(t\in T\),

\[ \boxed{A_t(h)=\begin{cases} \{\!\{4,4,4\}\!\},&h\in H_A,\\ \{\!\{2,2,4\}\!\},&h\in H_B. \end{cases}} \tag{140.13} \]

Therefore the same nonconstant enclosing classification \(I_J\) is determined by each of the four stage-anchored complete-context reads, although no one subordinate response identity is followed across stages.

Proof. In \(H_A\), all six pair extension counts are four, so every anchor reports three fours. In \(H_B\), the two four-valued pairs form a perfect matching of the four stages. Every stage touches exactly one of those pairs and two other pairs with extension count two. Each anchor therefore reports \(\{\!\{2,2,4\}\!\}\). The two profiles distinguish \(H_A\) from \(H_B\) and thus determine \(I_J\). \(\square\)

The result is a specific persistence of relational readability over multiple anchored comparisons. It does not assert a time-ordered continuing object, a persistent directional displacement, or the physical realization of any one complete record.

140.8. Theorem 3 — Survival under a specified native read

Let one common enclosing comparison domain be \(D_L=H_J\). Consider two candidate receiving-boundary reads:

\[ q_B(h)=q_{\mathrm{pair}}(h),\qquad q_{B'}(h)=A_{t_0}(h) \tag{140.14} \]

for an independently selected anchor \(t_0\). Their reached read sets have four and two labels, respectively. The original domain need not be the native microstate domain of either receiver; those native domains and their multiplicities are defined by the corresponding boundaries.

Theorem 3 (conditional survival). The fingerprint class \(I_J\) factors through both reads:

\[ \boxed{I_J=I_B\circ q_B=I_{B'}\circ q_{B'}.} \tag{140.15} \]

The most informative classification readable independently from both \(q_B\) and \(q_{B'}\) is exactly the two-class partition \(H_A\sqcup H_B\).

Proof. The labeled six-count signature determines the fingerprint. By Theorem 2, so does \(A_{t_0}\). Hence \(I_J\) is constant on both read-fiber families. Moreover \(A_{t_0}\) merges precisely the three mixed pair signatures while retaining the all-four signature. Thus the equivalence relation generated by the two read kernels has exactly the fibers \(H_A,H_B\). Applying Addenda 132 and 139’s common-read factorization criterion gives (140.15) and the claimed most informative common classification. \(\square\)

The result provides a concrete common-owner replacement model. Calling it an actual comparison of two independently established physical backgrounds requires a separately justified enclosing span and physical native identification. The abstract construction does not guarantee that nature realizes these two particular read maps.

140.9. Theorem 4 — Why snapshots and one fixed pair do not suffice

Theorem 4 (lower-order insufficiency). On the complete matching domain, neither one fixed instantaneous stage read \(p_t\) nor one fixed two-stage restriction \(b_{tu}\) uniquely determines \(I_J\) for every admitted record.

Proof. Fix one stage bijection. It has compatible completions in both \(H_A\) and \(H_B\), by the counts of Theorem 1; therefore its read fiber contains records with \(I_J=0\) and \(I_J=4\).

For a fixed pair of stages with double-transposition comparison, the extension theorem gives four complete records. The representative completion audit in Theorem 1 shows two belong to \(H_A\) and two to \(H_B\). Hence a fixed two-stage read of this type likewise merges different fingerprints. A four-cycle pair comparison implies membership in \(H_B\), but that special case does not make the pair read sufficient over the entire admitted domain. \(\square\)

This distinction is essential. Theorem 2 does not create a persistent relation by relabeling a snapshot. It uses a stronger complete enclosing extension profile, which cannot generally be reconstructed from its individual or pairwise observed margins. It is consistent with Addendum 139’s counterexample to lower-order coherence reconstruction.

140.10. Theorem 5 — Recursive carry does not force fingerprint preservation

Now introduce a different permissible receiving read \(q_*:H_J\to\{R\}\) that identifies all 576 possible complete records as one label. This is a deliberately less-discriminating abstract native classification, not a claim that every physical replacement behaves this way.

Theorem 5 (no universal preservation). The primitive definitions of boundary-relative compatibility and recursive reclassification alone do not require the fingerprint \(I_J\) to survive every receiving read.

Proof. For \(h\in H_A\) and \(h'\in H_B\), one has \(q_*(h)=q_*(h')=R\) but \(I_J(h)=0\ne4=I_J(h')\). Therefore \(I_J\) is not constant on the \(q_*\) fiber and cannot factor through \(q_*\). Nevertheless the original domain’s total possible count can still be retained as the fiber weight \(|q_*^{-1}(R)|=576\). Exact aggregation of that old count does not reconstruct the lost distinction or assign the new boundary’s own native multiplicity. \(\square\)

More generally, for a specified read \(q:H_J\to Y\), the necessary and sufficient condition for this fingerprint to remain readable is

\[ \boxed{q(h)=q(h')\ \Longrightarrow\ I_J(h)=I_J(h').} \tag{140.16} \]

This is a specialization of the established factorization theorem, not a new universal requirement imposed on \(q\) by Part I.

For several independently specified observing backgrounds, a common owner and compatible read maps must first exist. Their most informative common classification is the quotient by the equivalence relation generated by all read fibers, as proved in Addenda 132 and 139. The present finite owner demonstrates that quotient can be nontrivial; the erasure read demonstrates it need not be.

140.11. The distinction between persistence and realization

All numbers above count admitted possible complete records or their compatible possible extensions. They do not count how often the records physically occur. In particular,

\[ |H_A|=144,\qquad |H_B|=432 \tag{140.17} \]

is not a probability assignment \(1/4\) versus \(3/4\), a measured fraction of time, or a recurrence-rate prediction.

A realized domain \(H_J^{\mathrm{real}}\) must be specified separately, with its correct occurrence and occupancy counts. If the realized records all lie in one fingerprint class, that gives a realized relation consistent with the owner; the allowed possibility counts alone do not decide which class is occupied. If a physical process changes its enclosing owner, the relevant matching domain and read maps must also be reconsidered.

The same distinction applies to Addendum 139’s nested coherent-support counts. A nonzero count of admissible complete observations proves that coherence is possible; it does not establish that measured velocity autocorrelation, spectral coherence, or any other correlation persists for a particular physical duration.

There is a further distinction between classification persistence across reads (demonstrated here) and continuous observed motion through successive stages (not demonstrated here). The full compatibility fingerprint is a relationship of a complete record. It does not imply that any identifiable response class is transported from address to address, and it does not turn the comparison stages into a physical time parameter.

140.12. Relation to symmetric recurrence and the two horizons

Addendum 131 distinguishes positive orientation-even completed recurrence from directed output. Addenda 135–137 then study unitless complete response-incidence domains. The matching owner here does not even need the orientation-even turbine count to derive its extension fingerprint. A separately specified symmetric turbine signature could accompany every complete record without changing the proof. The fingerprint is not the completed-return sum \(N_++N_-\), nor is it the background recurrence ratio \(\chi_J\).

The construction is relevant to Part III’s complementary horizons in a narrower way. At a quantum-side read, one may retain a recurrence-related response classification after subordinate distinctions become unreadable. At a gravitational-side read, one may retain an enclosing distinguishability or mass-bearing class after the relevant enclosing recurrence becomes unreadable. For those physical reads to share an invariant, their complete comparison must preserve a nontrivial common classification; the fiber criterion tests precisely whether they do.

The present \(I_J\) is a proof-of-possibility for such common native readability, not an identification with a measured frequency, \(c\), area, mass, or \(mc^2\). It neither derives the physical mappings nor guarantees their common calibration. In particular, a spectrum cannot be assumed to measure this matching fingerprint, and a gravitational mass label cannot be assigned by retrospectively grouping its values.

The broader lesson is structural: an observable relationship can possess an enclosingly defined identity even when the subordinate presentations required to realize it are not continuously identifiable. Its physical interpretation remains a separate question of what the specified observer actually reads.

140.13. What has been proved, and what remains to be selected

Established for the declared finite complete-matching owner:

  1. Complete two-stage enclosing extension counts are two or four, depending on the derived relative permutation type.
  2. The six-count compatibility fingerprint has exactly two values, represented by 144 and 432 admitted complete records.
  3. The labeled pair-extension signature has four possible values, each represented by 144 admitted records.
  4. At every stage anchor, the full three-comparison enclosing profile identifies the same two-class fingerprint without following subordinate identity.
  5. The fingerprint survives a specified coarser native read that erases the individual pair labels.
  6. No one fixed instantaneous or two-stage read determines the fingerprint across the full admitted domain.
  7. Another admissible read can erase the fingerprint entirely while retaining the correct aggregate possible count of the original domain through a fiber weight.

Not established: that complete matching is required by all R²D boundaries; that independent physical backgrounds must retain the three-comparison profile; that physical observations have access to it; that a possible fingerprint class is realized; that its persistence gives a measurable correlation law; or that it determines acoustic propagation, electromagnetic \(c\), gravitational mass, or \(mc^2\).

Two logically distinct next tasks follow.

Native necessity. Which independent requirements on a change of count boundary compel preservation of a nontrivial enclosing extension-profile distinction, without demanding preservation of every subordinate detail? This cannot be answered by defining the new read to retain the fingerprint.

Physical realization. For an independently justified native read map, which measured sequence of thermal, electromagnetic, or gravitational observations faithfully distinguishes those complete compatibility classes, and what realized recurrence or correlation does it report? A successful account must compare realized counts with the same enclosing classification rather than turn possible-count fractions into probabilities by declaration.

Conclusion

R²D’s enclosing-first counting architecture permits a complete recurrent organization to have a native relational identity before any subordinate trajectory is constructed. A concrete unitless owner—the four-address, four-stage native matching relation—does more than permit that idea. Its complete compatible-extension counts derive two nonconstant relational classes, with exact cardinalities 144 and 432. Each stage-anchored complete-context profile identifies the same derived class, and a specified recursive reclassification can preserve it while erasing finer pair labels.

The constructive result is

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered} \text{independently declared complete enclosing compatibility}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{derived nontrivial extension-count fingerprint}\\ \Downarrow\quad\text{(specified fiber-respecting native reads)}\\ \text{persistent relational readability without subordinate identity}. \end{gathered}} \tag{140.18} \]

The condition on the final arrow is substantive. Recursive carry changes count meaning; it does not require every later boundary to preserve the fingerprint. The remaining foundational question is therefore exact:

Can R²D’s native boundary and recursive-carry architecture constrain the admissible cross-background reads enough to force preservation of some nontrivial compatibility-derived relationship, rather than merely allowing one to be preserved?

Only after that count-level question is answered can one test whether the surviving relationship accounts quantitatively for measured correlation persistence and the complementary quantum and gravitational readings of symmetric enclosing recurrence.
