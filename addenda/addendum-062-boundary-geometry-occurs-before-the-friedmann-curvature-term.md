# Addendum 62 — Boundary Geometry Occurs Before the Friedmann Curvature Term

## Abstract

Addendum 60 showed that the Friedmann equation partitions one already-defined physical rate magnitude,

$$ H^2$$

into several downstream gravitational coordinates.

Addendum 61 then deprojected the matter-energy contribution,

$$\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon_\zeta$$

into two prior physical reads:

> peer-energy read

and

> boundary-support read.

The next Friedmann contribution is

$$ F_k=-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}$$

This term has fundamentally different physical ownership.

It is not a source-density term.

For normalized FLRW spatial geometry,

$$ k\in\{-1,0,+1\}.$$

labels the constant-curvature projection class, whereas

$$ K_\zeta\equiv\frac{k}{a^2}$$

<!-- source_pdf_page: 2097 -->

is the state-dependent physical sectional curvature of the spatial geometry.

Equivalently, the intrinsic scalar curvature of a constant-cosmological-time spatial slice is

$${}^{(3)}R_\zeta=\frac{6k}{a^2}=6K_\zeta$$

Thus the Friedmann curvature contribution can be written

$$ F_k=-c^2K_\zeta.$$

or

$$ F_k=-\frac{c^2}{6}\,{}^{(3)}R_\zeta$$

The curvature term is therefore already a physical geometric read.

Addendum 56 established that, for a fixed comoving shell χ,

$$ A_\zeta=4\pi a^2S_k^2(\chi).$$

Solving for the scale factor gives

$$ a^2=\frac{A_\zeta}{4\pi S_k^2(\chi)}$$

Therefore,

$$ K_\zeta=\frac{k}{a^2}=\frac{4\pi k S_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}$$

The spatial scalar curvature is correspondingly

$${}^{(3)}R_\zeta=\frac{24\pi kS_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}$$

And the Friedmann curvature contribution becomes

$$ F_k=-\frac{4\pi kc^2 S_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}$$

Once the curvature class and comoving comparison domain are fixed, physical spatial curvature therefore
scales as the inverse of cosmological boundary area:

<!-- source_pdf_page: 2098 -->

$$ K_\zeta\propto A_\zeta^{-1}$$

The geometric ordering is consequently

$$ B_\zeta\to A_\zeta\to K_\zeta\to F_k$$

The normalized label k does not supply physical curvature magnitude by itself.

Instead,

$k$ = geometric projection class

and

$A_\zeta$ = state-dependent geometric place value

jointly determine

$K_\zeta$ = physical spatial curvature

This also explains why cosmological expansion changes the magnitude of curvature without changing the
FLRW curvature class.

For fixed nonzero k ,

$$ K_\zeta\propto A_\zeta^{-1}$$

Hence,

$$ d\ln|K_\zeta|=-d\ln A_\zeta$$

Using

$$ H=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}$$

we obtain

$$\frac{\dot K_\zeta}{K_\zeta}=-2H$$

and therefore

$$\dot K_\zeta=-2HK_\zeta.$$

Likewise,

<!-- source_pdf_page: 2099 -->

$$\dot{{}^{(3)}R}_\zeta=-2H\,{}^{(3)}R_\zeta$$

The curvature class does not evolve:

$$ k=\mathrm{constant}.$$

while its physical magnitude does.

The distinction is crucial for R²D.

The primitive cosmological state is defined through compatible peer-boundary realization before FLRW
geometry is assigned.

The curvature class k therefore cannot be identified by definition with R²D constraint, retention, possibility,
or asymmetry.

In particular,

$$ k\ne C_\zeta$$

and

$$ K_\zeta\ne R_\zeta$$

Any such relation would require an independent bridge from peer-compatibility structure to the physical
curvature class.

The present addendum does not yet derive why a particular R²D cosmological realization projects as

$$ k=+1,\quad k=0,\quad\text{or}\quad k=-1$$

It derives the later relation:

> Once an isotropic constant-curvature projection class is selected, the magnitude of its physical curvature is determined by the cosmological boundary-area read.

This separates curvature from matter-energy.

For pressureless conserved source energy, Addendum 61 gave

$$ F_{\mathrm{dust}}\propto A_\zeta^{-3/2}$$

By contrast,

$$ F_k\propto A_\zeta^{-1}$$

<!-- source_pdf_page: 2100 -->

The different scalings follow because the terms have different physical ownership.

Matter-energy is a source-energy read divided by a three-dimensional support volume:

$$ V_\zeta\propto A_\zeta^{3/2}$$

$$ K_\zeta\propto A_\zeta^{-1}$$
