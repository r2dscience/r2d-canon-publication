Source–Area Compatibility, Physical Rate, and the Missing Sign of Cosmological Evolution

Addendum 57 — Directional Asymmetry Occurs Before the Friedmann Branch

Abstract

Addendum 56 established a conditional bridge between the R²D cosmological structural state and the conventional scale factor.

A cosmological structural transition,

\[i_\zeta^\ast \to k_\zeta^\ast\]

can acquire a gravitational boundary-area read,

\[A_{\zeta,i} \to A_{\zeta,k}\]

provided physical area factors through the cosmological macrostate classification.

Under an isotropic FLRW projection,

\[\frac{a_k}{a_i}=\sqrt{\frac{A_{\zeta,k}}{A_{\zeta,i}}}\]

Once a physical clock coordinate is independently supplied,

\[H=\frac{\dot a}{a}=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\]

The next question is therefore not what the scale factor means.

It is:

What constrains the physical rate of the projected cosmological geometry, and what / determines its direction?

Conventional cosmology answers the first question through the Friedmann equations.

For physical energy density

\[\epsilon\]

the first Friedmann equation is

\[H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

Using the area read,

\[H=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\]

this becomes

\[\frac14\left(\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\right)^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon_\zeta-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

This equation relates a physical source read to the magnitude of a physical geometric rate.

But it does not determine the sign of that rate.

For positive right-hand side,

\[H=\pm\sqrt{\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}}\]

Thus the same instantaneous source–geometry values are compatible, at the level of the Friedmann constraint, with two oppositely oriented branches:

\[H>0\]

and

\[H < 0\]

The first Friedmann equation therefore does not by itself answer the primitive R²D question:

Which way is the cosmological state being realized?

R²D places that question earlier.

At a nonterminal cosmological boundary,

\[A_{\zeta,i\to k} = P_{\zeta,i\to k} - R_{\zeta,i\to k}\]

This is the directional count asymmetry that biases realization of the cosmological multiplicity landscape.

It is not identified with

\[H\]

\[H^2\]

\[\epsilon\]

\[p\]

\[\Lambda\]

or any other mature cosmological coordinate.

Instead, the proposed causal ordering is

\[A_\zeta\]

↓

biased structural realization

\[i_\zeta^\ast \to k_\zeta^\ast\]

↓

geometric projection

\[A_{\zeta,i} \to A_{\zeta,k}\]

↓

physical clock read

\[H=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\]

The source variables appearing in Friedmann cosmology constitute another physical read of the same cosmological organization.

They too must satisfy the macrostate factorization condition introduced in Addendum 56.

If energy density is to represent a cosmological macrostate coordinate, there must exist

\[\bar\epsilon_\zeta:\mathcal M_\zeta\to\mathbb R\]

such that

\[\epsilon\circ\Pi_G=\bar\epsilon_\zeta\circ\pi_\zeta\]

Likewise, a macrostate pressure read requires

\[p\circ\Pi_G=\bar p_\zeta\circ\pi_\zeta\]

The Friedmann equations can then be interpreted as compatibility relations among multiple physical reads of one prior cosmological state:

source read ⟷ geometric read.

They do not establish the primitive causal direction between those reads.

Define the physical Friedmann branch variable

\[\sigma_\zeta=\operatorname{sgn}H.\]

away from a turning point.

Define

\[F_\zeta=\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon_\zeta-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

Then

\[H=\sigma_\zeta\sqrt{F_\zeta}.\]

This separates two different pieces of physical information:

Fζ = allowed squared-rate compatibility,

whereas

σζ = orientation of the realized physical branch.

R²D proposes that the primitive origin of this directional information lies upstream in the enclosing asymmetry.

But the theory must not simply assert

\[\sigma_\zeta=\operatorname{sgn}A_\zeta\]

The physical projection can preserve or reverse orientation.

Let

\[s_A\]

denote the orientation of the macrostate-to-area map for a transition,

\[s_A=\operatorname{sgn}\left(\frac{\Delta A_\zeta}{\lambda_{\zeta,i\to k}}\right)\]

Then, if structural response follows the sustained sign of the R²D asymmetry, the area projection is monotonic, and the physical clock preserves the realized transition ordering, the candidate branch relation is

\[\sigma_\zeta\sim s_A\,\operatorname{sgn}A_\zeta\]

This is a conditional bridge to be derived, not an established identity.

The important result is more primitive:

the Friedmann constraint loses the directional sign that R²D assigns to the prior realization process

The acceleration and continuity equations provide additional physical evolution constraints.

The acceleration equation is

\[\frac{\ddot a}{a}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3c^2}(\epsilon+3p)+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

The source continuity relation is

\[\dot\epsilon+3H(\epsilon+p)=0.\]

Using the area rate,

\[H=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\]

the continuity relation becomes

\[\dot\epsilon+\frac32(\epsilon+p)\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}=0\]

These equations describe the coupled evolution of projected physical source and geometric reads.

They still do not justify identifying

\[\epsilon\]

with Pζ ,

with Rζ ,

or

with Aζ .

The R²D turbine remains upstream.

the Friedmann equation does not determine cosmological direction. It constrains a physical geometr

1. Friedmann Dynamics Enter Only After Physical Projection

The primitive cosmological sequence is already defined before the Friedmann equations appear:

\[{B_{\zeta-1,\alpha} } \to \Omega_\zeta \to \mathcal M_\zeta \to i_\zeta^\ast \to k_\zeta^\ast\]

The Friedmann equations belong to the later gravitational physical description.

2. The Geometric Read Has Already Been Defined

When area satisfies the macrostate factorization condition,

\[A\circ\Pi_G=\bar A_\zeta\circ\pi_\zeta\]

the cosmological state possesses the area coordinate

\[A_{\zeta,i}\]

Under isotropic projection,

\[a\propto\sqrt{A_\zeta}\]

3. A Physical Clock Produces the Rate Read

Once physical time t is independently calibrated,

\[H=\frac{\dot a}{a}=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\]

The Hubble parameter is therefore downstream of both the state projection and the clock projection.

4. A Source Variable Must Pass the Same Projection Test

Physical energy density cannot simply be inserted into the primitive count architecture.

If it represents the cosmological macrostate, it must factor through the same classification.

Thus require

\[\epsilon\circ\Pi_G=\bar\epsilon_\zeta\circ\pi_\zeta\]

5. Energy Density Must Be Constant Across the Macrostate Fiber

If

\[\pi_\zeta(\mu_\alpha)=\pi_\zeta(\mu_\beta)=i\]

then a genuine macrostate energy-density read requires

\[\epsilon[\Pi_G(\mu_\alpha)]=\epsilon[\Pi_G(\mu_\beta)]\]

Only then is

\[\epsilon_{\zeta,i}\]

well defined.

6. Pressure Requires the Same Condition

Likewise, if pressure is a boundary-level physical coordinate,

\[p\circ\Pi_G=\bar p_\zeta\circ\pi_\zeta\]

Then

\[p_{\zeta,i}\]

is a well-defined source read of cosmological macrostate i.

7. Source and Geometry Can Therefore Be Parallel Reads

One cosmological state

\[i\]

may acquire both

\[A_{\zeta,i}\]

and

\[\epsilon_{\zeta,i},\quad p_{\zeta,i}\]

These are different physical coordinates of one prior R²D classification.

8. Neither Read Is Automatically Primitive Cause of the Other

The existence of a relation

\[\epsilon\longleftrightarrow A\]

does not by itself establish

\[\epsilon\to A\]

as the primitive causal ordering.

Both may descend from one enclosing count relation.

9. Introduce the First Friedmann Constraint

For an FLRW projection,

\[H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

This is an imported mature physical relation.

10. Rewrite the Constraint in Boundary-Area Coordinates

Using

\[H=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\]

we obtain

\[\frac14\left(\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\right)^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon_\zeta-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

11. Friedmann Is Now a Source–Area Compatibility Equation

The equation relates:

source-property reads

to

geometric-rate reads.

Its physical role can therefore be written schematically as

\[{\epsilon, p, \ldots} \longleftrightarrow {A, a, H, \ldots}\]

12. This Is Not Yet the Primitive R²D Turbine

The R²D turbine is

\[A_\zeta=P_\zeta-R_\zeta.\]

The Friedmann equation contains none of these quantities explicitly.

Therefore it should not be treated as the primitive R²D causal equation.

13. The First Friedmann Equation Is Direction Blind

Because it contains

\[H^2\]

the constraint determines only the square of the physical rate.

If

\[H\]

is a solution of the instantaneous constraint, then

\[-H\]

has the same square.

14. Define the Friedmann Rate Function

Let

\[F_\zeta=\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon_\zeta-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

Then the constraint is

\[H^2 = F_\zeta\]

15. Positive Fζ Permits Two Instantaneous Branches

Where

\[F_\zeta > 0\]

the constraint gives

\[H=+\sqrt{F_\zeta}\]

Thus two oppositely oriented physical-rate branches satisfy the same squared-rate constraint.

16. Expansion and Contraction Are Different Branches

The branch

\[H>0\]

corresponds to increasing isotropic spatial scale.

The branch

\[H<0\]

corresponds to decreasing isotropic spatial scale.

The first Friedmann constraint alone does not select between them.

17. Define the Physical Branch Orientation

Away from

\[H = 0\]

define

\[\sigma_\zeta=\operatorname{sgn}H.\]

Then

\[H=\sigma_\zeta\sqrt{F_\zeta}.\]

18. Magnitude and Direction Are Now Explicitly Separated

The quantity

\[F_\zeta\]

controls the allowed squared rate.

The quantity

\[\sigma_\zeta\]

specifies the physical orientation of the realized branch.

Thus:

H = direction × allowed magnitude.

19. This Separation Mirrors the R²D Problem

R²D already distinguishes:

available realization structure

from

directional bias of realization.

The Friedmann branch structure exposes an analogous separation in the mature physical projection.

20. The Source Terms Do Not Alone Supply the Branch Sign

The values

\[\epsilon, a, k, \Lambda\]

determine

\[F_\zeta\]

They do not, through the first Friedmann constraint alone, determine

\[\sigma_\zeta\]

21. This Is the Missing Directional Datum

The conventional constraint therefore requires additional branch information:

\[\sigma_\zeta\]

22. R²D Already Possesses a Primitive Directional Quantity

For a nonterminal cosmological transition,

\[A_{\zeta,i\to k} = P_{\zeta,i\to k} - R_{\zeta,i\to k}\]

This quantity biases realization before the gravitational rate is defined.

23. But Aζ Is Not H

Dimensional and causal considerations immediately give

\[A_\zeta \ne H\]

One is a primitive dimensionless count asymmetry.

The other is a physical inverse-time rate.

24. Nor Is Aζ = H 2

Likewise,

\[A_\zeta \ne H^2\]

The primitive R²D asymmetry is directional.

biased structural transition

area change

\[A_{\zeta,k} > A_{\zeta,i}\]

The physical map must be checked.

\[s_A=\operatorname{sgn}\left(\frac{A_{\zeta,k}-A_{\zeta,i}}{\lambda_{\zeta,i\to k}}\right)\]

\[s_A = +1\]

means the physical area projection preserves the count orientation.

If

then a positive count distinction projects as decreasing physical area.

R²D must therefore not identify count direction and spatial direction without this bridge.

The rate

\[H\]

\[\sigma_\zeta\sim s_A\,\operatorname{sgn}A_\zeta\]

\[B_\zeta = B_\star\]

A subordinate cosmological boundary can possess an expanding physical branch while remaining inside a larger open relation.

\[\sigma_\zeta\]

\[A_\zeta=0\iff H=0\]

These conditions belong to different layers.

\[A_\zeta\]

\[\dot A_\zeta\]

change in source-property read

Thus:

Friedmann dynamics = physical source–geometry compatibility.

The first Friedmann constraint is

The branch variable

is additional directional information.

56. Principle — Directional Asymmetry Precedes Branch Orientation

R²D assigns directional organization before the physical rate is defined:

No direct numerical identity between Aζ and σζ is asserted.

57. Principle — Physical Branch Orientation Requires a Projection Bridge

The sign of the physical rate depends on:

• the sign of the realized structural transition;

  • the orientation of the macrostate-to-area projection;
