# R²D Addendum 151 — Retained Constraint as Enclosing Compatibility

*The 35-to-125-Record Extension, Reciprocal Realization, and Thermal–Quantum–Gravitational Reads*

**Status:** Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026  
**Canonical basis:** R²D Parts I–IV (September 25, 2026); Addenda 105, 112, 116–117, 120, 122, 125, 131–132, 139–141, 143–147, and 150.

## Abstract

Addendum 150 constructed one complete molecule–reservoir count domain with thirty-five compatible native records, molecular conditional counts of twenty and fifteen, and sixty reciprocal exchange incidences. It also selected a productive retention contrast, $\Delta R=\ln(4/3)$, which—under the canonical proportional state-occurrence and occupancy relations—gives a molecular structural occupancy ratio $c_1/c_0=9/16$. The native counts $20:15$ do not, by themselves, equal this realized occupancy ratio. Nor do the sixty *possible* exchange edges determine a realized cycling rate.

We construct a larger enclosing owner in which the selected retained-constraint factor is represented by an explicitly counted *additional compatible environment*. Every original native record remains a distinguishable projection. The extra environment admits four configurations above molecular class $q=0$ and three above $q=1$. The resulting owner contains exactly 125 complete records, partitioned into classes of eighty and forty-five, and a connected reciprocal exchange graph of 720 undirected edges. Under one specified uniform complete-record realization, the projected molecular occupancy ratio is exactly $45/80=9/16$. A symmetric rate assigned to each complete allowed edge yields distinct effective exit rates, $9\gamma$ and $16\gamma$, and positive, equal forward and reverse fluxes. A deterministic Euler-circuit comparison independently demonstrates the existence of 720 paired local returns and permits a positive background-relative recurrence count without primitive random choices.

The nested comparison has two complementary canonical descriptions. At the original owner, possibility contrast $-d$ and selected productive retention $+d$, where $d=\ln(4/3)$, give local asymmetry $-2d$. At the enlarged owner, the complete possibility contrast is itself $-2d$; the additional factor is already included in its extension count. Applying the same retention again would double-count the preparation. Under a thermodynamic calibration, the auxiliary $4:3$ factor is a *configurational free-energy bias*, not automatically heat, an intrinsic excitation-energy shift, or an additional gravitational mass. The quantum spectrum and locally resolved mass-energy read can therefore remain unchanged while occupation and recurrence change. A different, energy-shifting preparation is also possible but requires correspondingly enlarged energy accounting.

The results are explicit finite existence and consistency statements. All possible counts and incidence identities are derived from one specified enclosing compatibility. Realized occupations, background clocks, kinetic rates, and thermal, quantum, and gravitational physical interpretations are distinguished as separately specified read maps. The construction does not introduce new primitive definitions of $P$, $R$, $A$, background, energy, or mass.

## 151.1. The question left by Addendum 150

The existing canon separates native multiplicity $W$, compatible extension count $\Theta$, canonical possibility $P$, retained constraint $R$, asymmetry $A$, state occurrence $\nu$, structural occupancy $c$, and complete closure recurrence $N_{\mathcal C}$. Their relationships are not licenses to identify the counts themselves. In the selected two-class molecular presentation,

$$
P_{M}(q)=\ln\frac{\Theta_{J\mid M,q}}{W_M(q)},\qquad
\Delta R=C_J^{out}\lambda,\qquad
\Delta A=\Delta P-\Delta R.
\tag{151.1}
$$

Part I's occupancy relation is

$$
\boxed{\Delta G_{0\to1}=\Delta\tau_{0\to1}+\Delta P_{0\to1}-\Delta R_{0\to1}.}
\tag{151.2}
$$

Its proportional state-occurrence condition, when appropriate, gives $\Delta\tau\approx\Delta S$; it does not identify state occurrences with structural occupancy. Addenda 125 and 131 separately distinguish state occurrences, opposed-leg occurrences, and whole-closure recurrence. The task is to construct a complete enclosing preparation in which a selected $R$ has a possible physical meaning while all these count distinctions remain explicit.

The work below extends Addendum 150 rather than revising its native mathematics. It addresses **how a constraint that is retained relative to one boundary can be represented as additional compatibility at a larger boundary**, and what realized thermal, quantum, and gravitational comparisons that permits.

## 151.2. The unchanged thirty-five-record owner

Let $q\in\{0,1\}$ classify one selected molecule and $b_i\in\{0,1\}$ label six distinguishable reservoir sites. The original owner $J$ admits

$$
\mathcal H_J=\left\{(q,b_1,\ldots,b_6):q+\sum_{i=1}^{6}b_i=3\right\}.
\tag{151.3}
$$

Because $W_M(0)=W_M(1)=1$, its enclosing extension counts and possibility coordinates are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Theta_{J\mid M,0}&=\binom63=20,&P_M^J(0)&=\ln20,\\
\Theta_{J\mid M,1}&=\binom62=15,&P_M^J(1)&=\ln15.
\end{aligned}
\tag{151.4}
$$

Write

$$
\boxed{d=\ln\frac43>0,\qquad\Delta P_M^J=\ln\frac{15}{20}=-d.}
\tag{151.5}
$$

In Addendum 150's selected productive-retention comparison,

$$
C_J^{out}=d,\qquad\lambda_{0\to1}=+1,\qquad\lambda_{1\to0}=-1.
\tag{151.6}
$$

Hence $\Delta R^J_{0\to1}=d$ and $\Delta A^J_{0\to1}=-2d$. Both increments reverse sign on the return leg; the complete local cycle has $\Delta_{\mathcal C}P=\Delta_{\mathcal C}R=\Delta A_{\mathcal C}=0$.

Under the selected state-occurrence realization $\nu_1/\nu_0=1$, Equation (151.2) reads

$$
\boxed{\frac{c_1}{c_0}=\exp(-2d)=\frac9{16}.}
\tag{151.7}
$$

This is an occupancy read **conditional on the specified realization**. The native $20:15$ partition remains a count of possible enclosing records, not a measured population.

## 151.3. Define the larger constraint-bearing owner

Introduce one larger owner $L$. Let the auxiliary compatibility label $a$ have $a_q$ values conditional on the molecular class. Select

$$
\boxed{a_0=4,\qquad a_1=3.}
\tag{151.8}
$$

Define the complete enlarged native domain, including its projection $\pi_{L\to J}$, by

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_L&=\{(h,a):h\in\mathcal H_J,\ 1\le a\le a_{q(h)}\},\\
\pi_{L\to J}(h,a)&=h.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{151.9}
$$

The distinct auxiliary labels are genuine distinguishable possibilities of $L$ by explicit declaration; they are not multiple coordinate descriptions of one native record. Nothing has been added to the molecule's intrinsic two-state multiplicity or removed from the original $J$-native domain.

**Theorem 151.1 — Exact enclosing extension and molecular partition.** The enlarged owner admits

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
|H_{L,0}|&=20(4)=80,\\
|H_{L,1}|&=15(3)=45,\\
|\mathcal H_L|&=125.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{151.10}
$$

**Proof.** Each original $q=0$ record has exactly four distinct $L$-extensions, and each original $q=1$ record exactly three. Add the disjoint conditional fibers. $\square$

The ratio of the two enlarged possible classes is

$$
\boxed{\frac{|H_{L,1}|}{|H_{L,0}|}=\frac{45}{80}=\frac9{16}.}
\tag{151.11}
$$

This remains a **possible-count ratio** until a particular realization rule is selected.

## 151.4. Nested possibility and retained constraint: one factor, not two

Calculate canonical molecular possibility relative to the new complete owner:

$$
\boxed{P_M^L(0)=\ln80,\qquad P_M^L(1)=\ln45,\qquad\Delta P_M^L=-2d.}
\tag{151.12}
$$

Also, the new extension factor per original $J$-native record is

$$
K_{L\mid J}(q)=a_q,\qquad
\Delta\ln K_{L\mid J}=\ln\frac34=-d.
\tag{151.13}
$$

Thus the same $4:3$ auxiliary constraint can be described in two *boundary-indexed* ways:

$$
\boxed{\underbrace{\Delta P_M^J-\Delta R_M^J}_{-d-d=-2d}
=\underbrace{\Delta P_M^L-\Delta R_M^L}_{-2d-0=-2d}.}
\tag{151.14}
$$

Equation (151.14) is an **explicit matching of two selected occupancy-read descriptions**. On the left, retain the selected inner comparison $\Delta R_M^J=d$. On the right, choose the enlarged comparison to carry no *further* productive-retention bias, $\Delta R_M^L=0$, because its auxiliary $3/4$ factor is already present in $\Theta_{L\mid M,q}$. It is not a universal identity asserting that canonical retention always converts into possibility under enlargement.

**Proposition 151.2 — No double counting of an admitted auxiliary factor.** For this specified nested owner, applying $\Delta R_M^J=d$ *again* to the already enlarged $\Delta P_M^L=-2d$ would give $\Delta A=-3d$ and count the same auxiliary restriction twice, unless an independently specified further constraint were actually present.

**Proof.** The enlarged $P_M^L$ is computed using all 125 compatible complete records, including the $a_1/a_0=3/4$ factor. Multiplication by a second identical occupancy factor would append another, not specified, $3/4$ restriction. $\square$

The physical significance is not an equation $R=P$, but the ownership of the compatibility factor: **a constraint explicitly retained at one comparison can be resolved into the admissible possibilities of a larger comparison**.

## 151.5. Exact reciprocal exchange incidence in the 125-record owner

Recall Addendum 150's exchange graph: there are sixty undirected edges between $H_{J,0}$ and $H_{J,1}$. Each $q=0$ native record has three admissible exchanges and each $q=1$ record four. Lift every original allowed edge to all pairs of auxiliary labels on its two endpoints. One original edge then admits

$$
a_0a_1=4(3)=12
$$

new undirected edges. Hence

$$
\boxed{|E_L|=60(12)=720.}
\tag{151.15}
$$

Every enlarged $q=0$ record has $3a_1=9$ neighboring $q=1$ records. Every enlarged $q=1$ record has $4a_0=16$ neighbors. The same edges are double-counted consistently:

$$
\boxed{80(9)=45(16)=720.}
\tag{151.16}
$$

**Theorem 151.3 — Reciprocal graph lifting.** The lifted graph contains 125 vertices, 720 undirected edges, 720 possible incidence records in each orientation, and is connected. Every possible directed exchange has a uniquely defined inverse exchange.

**Proof.** The vertex and edge counts follow from (151.10), (151.15), and (151.16). Every original graph edge has a complete bipartite lift $K_{4,3}$ across its endpoint fibers, so all new edges have inverses. The original 35-record graph is connected (Addendum 150). Every auxiliary copy is incident to the nonempty lift of each original neighboring vertex; therefore the lifted graph is connected. $\square$

The numbers 720 and 720 count *possible oriented incidence records*. They are not, without a realization condition, physical transition counts.

## 151.6. An explicit finite realized recurrence witness

Replace each undirected lifted edge by its two opposed directed arcs. The resulting finite directed comparison graph has equal positive incoming and outgoing degree at every vertex and is connected. It therefore admits a closed Euler circuit traversing each directed arc once.

Choose one such complete admitted circuit as a **realization witness**. The owner may admit this ordered complete recurrence as a possible record; realization of the record is an additional condition. If realized, it contains

$$
\boxed{\nu_{0\to1}^{leg}=\nu_{1\to0}^{leg}=720.}
\tag{151.17}
$$

Its molecular class alternates at every arc, so it admits 720 nonoverlapping local two-leg returns $0\to1\to0$ when the circuit is indexed from a $q=0$ vertex:

$$
\boxed{N_{\mathcal C_M}=720>0.}
\tag{151.18}
$$

These are returns of the **molecular classification**; a two-leg return need not recover the same six-site reservoir record or auxiliary label. The *full* complete native comparison returns only after the circuit closes. There is no requirement that a persistent subordinate microstate travel between them.

The realized opposed-leg ratio is

$$
\eta=\frac{720}{720}=1,
\qquad
\delta_{\mathcal C_M}\Phi_L=\ln\frac{720}{720}=0.
\tag{151.19}
$$

Positive reciprocal local cycling is consistent with zero background *increment*. It is not the same as positive recurrence of the corresponding positively constrained directed enclosing-transition class in Part I's recurrence-weighted law. If $\Delta A_{\mathcal C_M}=0$ and that specific enclosing transition has $C_L\lambda_L>0$, its $N_{\chi_L}$ is zero. The completed local returns and the corresponding constrained enclosing-transition recurrence are different count identities.

## 151.7. Realized state occurrences, structural occupancy, and the background clock

Every $q=0$ complete record has nine outgoing arcs and is visited nine times by the Euler circuit; every $q=1$ complete record has sixteen and is visited sixteen times. Counting molecule-class events at the successive arc-comparison positions gives

$$
\boxed{\nu_{M,0}=80(9)=720,\qquad\nu_{M,1}=45(16)=720.}
\tag{151.20}
$$

This agrees exactly with proportional realization of the *molecule's* native landscape $W_M(0)=W_M(1)=1$ in the chosen event-count comparison. It must not be conflated with a uniformly weighted sample of the **125 complete states**, whose two class counts have ratio $45/80$.

To obtain the structural **residence** read, assign to each event visit the enclosing background-reference comparison counts

$$
\boxed{t_0=16\tau_\Phi,\qquad t_1=9\tau_\Phi.}
\tag{151.21}
$$

Here one $\tau_\Phi$ is a selected calibrated background-reference interval, not an R²D state-occurrence count or a primitive second. The integers 16 and 9 are a compatible physical recurrence specification, not forced by the unordered 125 native possibilities. The realized residence totals are

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_0&=720(16\tau_\Phi)=11520\tau_\Phi,\\
T_1&=720(9\tau_\Phi)=6480\tau_\Phi,\\
T_{tot}&=18000\tau_\Phi.
\end{aligned}
\tag{151.22}
$$

Consequently the downstream residence-based occupancy read is

$$
\boxed{\frac{c_1}{c_0}=\frac{T_1}{T_0}=\frac9{16},\qquad
(p_0,p_1)=\left(\frac{16}{25},\frac9{25}\right).}
\tag{151.23}
$$

Each *individual* complete $q=0$ record has total residence $9(16\tau_\Phi)=144\tau_\Phi$; each complete $q=1$ record has $16(9\tau_\Phi)=144\tau_\Phi$. Thus the complete-record residence is equal even though the molecule's event occurrence counts and structural residence readings have different class ratios.

With one native background-reference recurrence per $\tau_\Phi$, the selected dimensionless local-to-background completed-return ratio is

$$
\boxed{\chi_{M|\Phi}=\frac{N_{\mathcal C_M}}{N_L^\Phi}=\frac{720}{18000}=\frac1{25},\qquad f_{\mathcal C_M}=\frac1{25\tau_\Phi}.}
\tag{151.24}
$$

This calculation explicitly specifies $N_L^\Phi=18000$ for the selected complete comparison. Neither this reference count nor its physical duration can be inferred from the possible edge count 720 alone. The molecular closure frequency is not automatically a molecular spectral frequency $\epsilon/h$.

## 151.8. A symmetric physical rate projection

There is an alternative, familiar physical representation of the same complete compatibility. Assign one common rate calibration $\gamma>0$ to every directed edge of the enlarged graph, identically in both directions. This is an optional kinetic projection, *not a primitive stochastic transition law*. It admits uniform stationary weights on the 125 native complete states:

$$
\pi_L(h,a)=\frac1{125}.
\tag{151.25}
$$

The outgoing rates from a complete state are

$$
\boxed{k_0^{exit}=9\gamma,\qquad k_1^{exit}=16\gamma.}
\tag{151.26}
$$

The stationary molecular populations are

$$
\boxed{p_0=\frac{80}{125}=\frac{16}{25},\qquad
p_1=\frac{45}{125}=\frac9{25}.}
\tag{151.27}
$$

The positive gross fluxes, obtained from these stationary weights and exit rates, are

$$
\boxed{J_{0\to1}=\frac{80}{125}(9\gamma)
=\frac{144}{25}\gamma
=\frac{45}{125}(16\gamma)=J_{1\to0}.}
\tag{151.28}
$$

Thus $J_{net}=0$ while $J_{gross}>0$. Summing over the auxiliary labels connected to *one original exchange edge* gives effective projected rate contributions in ratio

$$
\boxed{k_{J,0\to1}=a_1\gamma=3\gamma,\qquad
k_{J,1\to0}=a_0\gamma=4\gamma.}
\tag{151.29}
$$

These projected rates depend on the specified auxiliary conditional fiber and uniform-rate calibration. They have not been appended as independent primitive molecular rate parameters.

The mean holding times of this kinetic read are $1/(9\gamma)$ for $q=0$ complete records and $1/(16\gamma)$ for $q=1$. Choosing

$$
\tau_\Phi=\frac1{144\gamma}
\tag{151.30}
$$

makes them $16\tau_\Phi$ and $9\tau_\Phi$, respectively. The gross transition flux per unit physical time and the finite Euler-circuit recurrence per unit calibrated comparison time agree:

$$
\frac{144}{25}\gamma=\frac1{25\tau_\Phi}.
\tag{151.31}
$$

This equality is a constructive calibration between two **selected** physical realizations. It does not equate possible edges with actual rates or assert that the uniform-rate projection is the unique physical dynamics.

## 151.9. Thermodynamic reading: an entropic constraint is not heat

The original six-site reservoir has possible multiplicities $W_B(3)=20$ and $W_B(2)=15$, giving the count entropy difference

$$
S_B(3)-S_B(2)=d.
\tag{151.32}
$$

Give each local excitation distinction a common physical energy calibration $\epsilon>0$. For the finite reservoir interval, one may define the thermal conjugate read

$$
\boxed{\frac{\epsilon}{k_BT_*}=d.}
\tag{151.33}
$$

This defines a selected finite-difference temperature scale for the six-site reservoir. It need not equal the temperature inferred from a different finite subsystem or from the *entire* auxiliary-enlarged comparison.

Under a thermal preparation at temperature $T$, a multiplicity factor $a_1/a_0=3/4$ has the standard configurational free-energy-difference read

$$
\boxed{\Delta F_{aux}(T)=-k_BT\ln\frac{a_1}{a_0}=k_BT\,d.}
\tag{151.34}
$$

When $T=T_*$, this becomes $\Delta F_{aux}(T_*)=\epsilon$. The equality of magnitudes does **not** make the configurational penalty an additional intrinsic excitation energy: it results from combining a dimensionless entropy contrast with the selected thermal calibration.

Indeed, if all auxiliary configurations carry the same energy, their $q$-dependent multiplicities change thermodynamic *accessibility* but do not change the microscopic energy assigned to the molecule. The added factor is an entropic free-energy bias. It is not by itself heat lost, dissipated energy, or an additional work-producing occurrence.

A crucial accounting distinction follows. The original six-site reservoir contrast reads $d$; the enlarged complete class contrast reads

$$
\ln\frac{80}{45}=2d.
\tag{151.35}
$$

The latter is the full finite compatible-population contrast for the larger owner. One must not use $T_*$ as though it had been independently shown to be the derivative-defined temperature of that different complete finite reservoir. The two boundaries define different thermodynamic reads.

## 151.10. Quantum spectral read: two distinguishable physical cases

**Case A — Configurational constraint without a level-energy shift.** Choose physical energies

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_M(q)&=E_{M,0}+q\epsilon,\\
E_B(b)&=E_{B,0}+\epsilon\sum_i b_i,\\
E_{aux}(a)&=E_{aux,0}\quad\text{for all admitted }a.
\end{aligned}
\tag{151.36}
$$

Then every admissible complete state has the same total energy,

$$
\boxed{E_L=E_{M,0}+E_{B,0}+E_{aux,0}+3\epsilon.}
\tag{151.37}
$$

The molecular intrinsic transition gap remains $\epsilon$, with quantum spectral calibration

$$
\boxed{f_Q=\frac{\epsilon}{h}.}
\tag{151.38}
$$

The auxiliary $4:3$ compatibility can change occupancy and effective exchange recurrence without moving that intrinsic spectral line, provided the chosen spectroscopy resolves the same allowed molecular transition and the auxiliary states introduce no separate energy shifts. Line intensities or selection rules can still depend on the preparation.

**Case B — Energetic constraint that shifts the transition.** A different preparation may add a physical relative energy $\delta$ to $q=1$, producing

$$
\epsilon_{eff}=\epsilon+\delta,
\qquad f_{eff}=\frac{\epsilon+\delta}{h}.
\tag{151.39}
$$

At a specified thermal calibration, one may choose $\delta=k_BT\,d$, but such a choice is a *separate physical coupling*. If the molecule gains $\epsilon+\delta$ while the six-site reservoir loses only $\epsilon$, the additional $\delta$ must be accounted for by the enclosing preparation, work, or another exchange channel. Case B is not the same constant-energy physical realization as Case A.

The experimentally distinguishable possibilities are thus: a changed occupancy ratio with unchanged intrinsic line position, or a changed occupancy ratio accompanied by an independently justified spectral shift.

## 151.11. Gravitational read: local excitation versus total mass

For Case A, let the molecule's selected physical mass-energy coupling have the calibrated level difference

$$
\boxed{\Delta m_M=\frac{\epsilon}{c_\Phi^2}.}
\tag{151.40}
$$

The reservoir loses $\epsilon$ when the molecule gains it, so under the specified additive energy accounting $\Delta E_B=-\epsilon$. The auxiliary states remain energetically degenerate. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{\Delta E_L=0,\qquad\Delta M_L=0}
\tag{151.41}
$$

for the invariant mass of the complete isolated system in this selected idealization, with no further changing interaction, field, or binding-energy terms.

The same physically realized exchange therefore has **two distinct orientation-even mass-bearing reads**. A local molecule-resolving read can distinguish its excitation-related mass difference $\epsilon/c_\Phi^2$, while a coarse complete-system monopole read returns the same total mass. A more detailed gravitational measurement could in principle depend on how energy and stresses are distributed even at fixed total mass; (151.41) concerns the selected total-energy/monopole comparison, not every gravitational observable.

The auxiliary entropy-derived free-energy penalty (151.34) is **not** an independent added mass $\Delta F_{aux}/c_\Phi^2$. Free energy is a temperature-dependent boundary-adapted potential, whereas local gravitational mass-energy reads depend on the appropriately accounted energetic and stress response of the complete physical system.

For Case B, the added energetic shift $\delta$ must also appear in the complete energetic and gravitational accounting, with its attribution determined by the boundary including the preparation and interaction energy. No count factor alone forces the molecule, rather than the enclosing apparatus, to carry the additional mass-energy.

## 151.12. General $(N,K,a_0,a_1)$ construction

The $35\to125$ example is part of an exact family. Let the inner owner have $N$ binary reservoir labels and fixed total excitation classification $K$:

$$
\mathcal H_{N,K}=\{(q,b):q+\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_i=K\},
\qquad1\le K\le N.
\tag{151.42}
$$

Let the larger owner provide $a_0,a_1\in\mathbb N_{>0}$ distinguishable auxiliary extensions for $q=0,1$. Then

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
H_0^L&=a_0\binom NK,\\
H_1^L&=a_1\binom N{K-1},\\
\frac{H_1^L}{H_0^L}&=\frac{K}{N-K+1}\frac{a_1}{a_0}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{151.43}
$$

The enlarged reciprocal exchange graph has

$$
\boxed{|E_L|=\binom NK K\,a_0a_1
=\binom N{K-1}(N-K+1)a_0a_1.}
\tag{151.44}
$$

Its degrees are $K a_1$ on the $q=0$ class and $(N-K+1)a_0$ on the $q=1$ class. Under a symmetric equal-rate-per-edge physical mapping, its complete-state equilibrium is uniform and the projected molecular ratio is the class-size ratio in (151.43).

The canonical possibility difference changes between owners by

$$
\boxed{\Delta P_M^L-\Delta P_M^J=\ln\frac{a_1}{a_0}.}
\tag{151.45}
$$

When $a_0\ge a_1$, the selected inner-owner productive retention

$$
\boxed{\Delta R_M^J=\ln\frac{a_0}{a_1}}
\tag{151.46}
$$

can reproduce the enlarged possibility effect in the local occupancy comparison **provided that** the enlarged read has no further retained bias and that the same state-occurrence relation is used. These are explicit matching conditions, not a universal identity of entropy and canonical constraint.

The original example $(N,K,a_0,a_1)=(6,3,4,3)$ gives exactly $|\mathcal H_L|=125$, $|E_L|=720$, and projected occupancy $9/16$.

## 151.13. Count types, logical status, and empirical discrimination

The construction establishes several results at different logical levels.

1. **Exact native possibilities:** $35$ original records, $125$ enlarged records, $20:15$ and $80:45$ molecular partitions, $60$ original edges and $720$ lifted undirected edges.
2. **Exact canonical count comparison:** the inner selected $\Delta P^J=-d$, $\Delta R^J=d$ and enlarged $\Delta P^L=-2d$, $\Delta R^L=0$ produce the same local asymmetry without counting the auxiliary factor twice.
3. **Possible and realized reciprocal recurrence:** symmetric possible incidence counts and an explicitly selected Euler realization with $720$ legs of each orientation. Positive local completed recurrence and zero realized directional imbalance coexist; their background recurrence ratio requires the separately specified reference-count calibration.
4. **Physical kinetic consistency:** a common edge-rate scale $\gamma$ admits equal stationary weights per enlarged complete record, different effective exit rates, reciprocal gross fluxes, and the same projected occupancy ratio.
5. **Thermal energetic mapping:** auxiliary multiplicity gives a configurational free-energy contrast $k_BT\ln(4/3)$ without identifying that contrast with dissipated heat or intrinsic molecular excitation energy.
6. **Quantum and gravitational calibration:** Case A retains an intrinsic quantum gap $\epsilon=hf_Q$, a local molecular mass difference $\epsilon/c_\Phi^2$, and unchanged total complete-system mass. Case B modifies the gap and demands enlarged energy accounting.

The count theorems do not prescribe realized Euler histories, absolute molecular exchange rates, a unique finite-reservoir temperature, spectroscopic selection rules, or an experimentally achievable gravitational mass resolution. Those are physical preparation and read-map questions. Their openness does not weaken the proven existence of the stated complete count and calibrated consistency witnesses.

The most direct experimental distinction within the proposed physical comparison is whether modifying an enclosing configurational constraint changes molecular equilibrium occupancy **without** shifting the intrinsic spectroscopic line, or whether an energetic perturbation changes both occupancy and the line. Both are allowed; their separate mass-energy budgets are testable physical requirements, not primitive definitions of R²D.

## Conclusion

The original thirty-five-record owner has unequal compatible molecular extensions, $20:15$, and sixty reciprocal exchange edges. A larger, explicitly declared auxiliary compatibility admits four extensions of each lower-state record and three of each excited-state record. It possesses

$$
\boxed{|\mathcal H_L|=125,\qquad |E_L|=720,\qquad
\frac{|H_{L,1}|}{|H_{L,0}|}=\frac9{16}.}
$$

An admissible complete reciprocal realization supports positive molecular cycling with equal forward and reverse leg counts and zero net background increment. A selected background residence and kinetic calibration makes structural occupancy $9/16$ while preserving the distinction from native multiplicity, state occurrence, and closure recurrence.

The most consequential across-boundary result is

$$
\boxed{\Delta P_M^J-\Delta R_M^J
=\Delta P_M^L-\Delta R_M^L=-2\ln\frac43,}
$$

**under the explicitly matched descriptions of the same auxiliary restriction.** The retained constraint of the inner comparison is already part of the larger owner's compatible extension multiplicity and must not be charged twice.

In the thermodynamic projection, the extra multiplicity gives configurational free-energy bias. In the quantum projection, it can modify occupancy without modifying intrinsic spectral energy. In the gravitational projection, it does not independently add mass when the auxiliary alternatives are energetically degenerate. The enclosing compatibility defines the possible organization; compatible local occurrences realize it; physical backgrounds provide the distinct energetic, spectral, and mass-bearing readings.
