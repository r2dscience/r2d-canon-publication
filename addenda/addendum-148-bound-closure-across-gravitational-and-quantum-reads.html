R²D Addendum 148 — Bound Closure Across Gravitational and Quantum Reads

Square-Shell Counting, Enclosing Recurrence, and Cross-Domain Combinatorial Consistency

Status: Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026
Canonical basis: R²D Parts I and IV; Addenda 40, 72, 120, 122, 139, and 141
Scope: Finite enclosing count domains, radial–angular classifications, bound recurrence, recursive replacement, and gravitational and quantum physical projections

Abstract

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) defines physical states through enclosing compatibility before assigning physical objects, forces, fields, spatial coordinates, or clocks. A bound planetary orbit and a bound atomic orbital therefore pose the same primitive question: which distinguishable relationships and completed recurrences are admitted by their enclosing boundary?

This addendum constructs one finite dimensionless count architecture consistent with both gravitational and quantum bound-state organization.

An enclosing boundary admits two bounded ordinal count classifications, \(a,b\in\{0,\ldots,N\}\), with complete square compatibility. Their joint domain contains \((N+1)^2\) distinct native realizations. Recursive shell classification by \(\ell=\max(a,b)\) gives exactly \(2\ell+1\) distinguishable oriented classes per shell. The oriented difference \(m=a-b\) and remaining count capacity \(r=N-\ell\) establish a bijection with the integer triples

\[ r+\ell=N,\qquad -\ell\le m\le\ell. \]

With \(n=N+1\), the complete classification has cardinality \(n^2\), matching the orbital-state count of the ideal nonrelativistic hydrogen atom, excluding spin.

A compatible cyclic recurrence is then specified on the same enclosing domain. It admits both a cumulative incidence-count read and cyclic phase characters. Under a gravitational background calibration, the incidence recurrence can be represented as the constant swept-area relation of a closed Keplerian ellipse. Under a quantum background calibration, the square-shell labels and cyclic characters admit the hydrogenic \((n,\ell,m)\) orbital classification and its angular phase closure.

The construction is an existence and consistency result. It does not assert that all enclosing count domains possess square compatibility, that every closure is Keplerian, or that physical energy spectra and orbital geometries follow from integer counting alone. Its result is that one explicitly specified R²D organization admits both physical projections without importing physical force, wavefunction, or particle trajectories into the primitive count definitions.

The gravitational and quantum systems differ in their physical readings, not in the primitive status of their objects, backgrounds, or enclosing relationships.

148.1. The question: one count architecture, two physical reads

R²D Part I establishes that a formal microstate is a compatible joint realization of immediately subordinate peers relative to an enclosing boundary. Possibility depends upon the number of compatible enclosing extensions. Recursive replacement changes the native state domain rather than preserving subordinate identities as primitive objects.

Part IV applies this architecture to physical fields and bound systems. Addendum 40 establishes the logical precedence of Keplerian closure over its Newtonian force representation. Addendum 72 establishes that cyclic recurrence admits phase representations before complex amplitude and wavefunction descriptions are introduced.

The present question is more specific:

Can one finite enclosing compatibility support both the bound areal recurrence observed in planetary systems and the radial–angular state classification observed in atoms?

The requirement is mathematical consistency.

The construction must satisfy R²D’s primitive counting definitions and admit both physical mappings. It need not establish uniqueness among all conceivable compatible count domains.

The logical order is

\[ \boxed{ \text{Enclosing distinguishability} \longrightarrow \text{joint compatibility} \longrightarrow \text{closure and recurrence} \longrightarrow \text{physical calibration}. } \tag{148.1} \]

The gravitational and quantum physical systems instantiate different enclosing owners. The claim is that they can instantiate the same abstract count architecture, not that one physical boundary literally contains the other or that their physical objects are identical.

148.2. Definition — The enclosing square-count domain

Fix an integer

\[ N\ge0. \]

Let the enclosing boundary \(J_N\) distinguish two subordinate ordinal classifications

\[ a,b\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}. \]

The symbols \(a\) and \(b\) label distinguishable count classes. They do not denote physical particles, radii, actions, or independent causal agents.

Specify the complete enclosing compatibility relation

\[ \boxed{ \mathfrak M_{J_N} = \{(a,b):0\le a,b\le N\}. } \tag{148.2} \]

For this construction, every admitted ordered pair is one distinct native realization at \(J_N\). The native-identification rule distinguishes different pairs; no additional presentation copies are counted.

The total native count is therefore

\[ \boxed{ |\mathfrak M_{J_N}|=(N+1)^2. } \tag{148.3} \]

The complete square relation is an explicit choice of enclosing compatibility. It does not follow merely from the existence of two binary or ordinal classifications.

This distinction preserves the causal priority of R²D: the enclosing owner admits the joint domain. The two subordinate classifications do not independently construct that domain.

148.3. Theorem 1 — Recursive square-shell multiplicity

Define the shell classification

\[ \boxed{ \ell(a,b)=\max(a,b). } \tag{148.4} \]

For each \(\ell\in\{0,\ldots,N\}\), let

\[ \mathcal S_\ell = \{(a,b)\in\mathfrak M_{J_N}:\max(a,b)=\ell\}. \]

Theorem 148.1 — Square-shell counting. The number of distinct native realizations in shell \(\ell\) is

\[ \boxed{ |\mathcal S_\ell|=2\ell+1. } \tag{148.5} \]

Proof.

The number of ordered pairs satisfying

\[ 0\le a,b\le\ell \]

is

\[ (\ell+1)^2. \]

For \(\ell\ge1\), the number satisfying

\[ 0\le a,b\le\ell-1 \]

is

\[ \ell^2. \]

The difference counts precisely those pairs for which \(\max(a,b)=\ell\):

\[ |\mathcal S_\ell| = (\ell+1)^2-\ell^2 = 2\ell+1. \]

For \(\ell=0\), the shell consists of the single pair \((0,0)\), and the same formula holds. \(\square\)

The complete domain is partitioned into these shells. Hence

\[ \boxed{ |\mathfrak M_{J_N}| = \sum_{\ell=0}^{N}(2\ell+1) = (N+1)^2. } \tag{148.6} \]

The odd-numbered shell multiplicities are exact consequences of the specified enclosing square compatibility.

Recursive interpretation

Consider two successive square-count capacities, \(N-1\) and \(N\), represented in one common comparison domain.

The larger admissible label set contains

\[ (N+1)^2 \]

classes, while the smaller contains

\[ N^2. \]

Their count difference is

\[ \boxed{ \Delta W_N=2N+1. } \tag{148.7} \]

This is a recursive capacity-comparison identity. It does not assert that individual formal microstates remain physically identical when the native count boundary changes. In R²D, exact comparisons across distinct native owners must preserve the specified enclosing compatibility and identification rules.

The integer \(N\) is an enclosing ordinal capacity, not automatically the recursive boundary index, a spatial radius, or a physical principal quantum number.

148.4. Theorem 2 — Radial–angular–orientation classification

Define an oriented difference

\[ \boxed{ m=a-b } \tag{148.8} \]

and the unused enclosing count capacity

\[ \boxed{ r=N-\ell. } \tag{148.9} \]

Because

\[ \ell=\max(a,b), \]

every shell member satisfies

\[ -\ell\le m\le\ell. \]

The remaining capacity satisfies

\[ r+\ell=N. \]

Theorem 148.2 — Exact classification bijection.

The map

\[ \boxed{ (a,b)\longmapsto(r,\ell,m) } \tag{148.10} \]

is a bijection between \(\mathfrak M_{J_N}\) and the integer domain

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal T_N= \left\{ (r,\ell,m): \begin{array}{l} r,\ell\ge0,\quad r+\ell=N,\\ m\in\mathbb Z,\quad-\ell\le m\le\ell \end{array} \right\}. } \tag{148.11} \]

Proof.

The definitions of \(r,\ell,m\) assign one triple to every admitted pair.

Conversely, take any triple satisfying (148.11). Recover

\[ a=\ell+\min(m,0), \]

\[ b=\ell-\max(m,0). \]

Both lie between \(0\) and \(\ell\), at least one equals \(\ell\), and their difference is \(m\).

Thus the recovered pair has

\[ \max(a,b)=\ell,\qquad a-b=m. \]

The inverse is unique. Therefore the map is bijective. \(\square\)

Set

\[ \boxed{n=N+1.} \tag{148.12} \]

Then

\[ \boxed{ r=n-\ell-1,\qquad \ell=0,\ldots,n-1,\qquad m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell. } \tag{148.13} \]

The total number of triples is

\[ \boxed{ |\mathcal T_N| = \sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(2\ell+1) =n^2. } \tag{148.14} \]

This is the principal combinatorial result.

The radial classification \(r\), shell classification \(\ell\), oriented difference \(m\), and square multiplicity \(n^2\) are not four independently postulated rules. They are different readings of one specified enclosing square-count relation.

148.5. Orientation reversal and retained distinction

Exchange the two ordinal classifications:

\[ \iota(a,b)=(b,a). \]

This operation satisfies

\[ \iota^2=\mathrm{id}. \]

Under the exchange,

\[ \ell\longmapsto\ell, \]

\[ r\longmapsto r, \]

\[ m\longmapsto-m. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{ (r,\ell,m)\overset{\iota}{\longmapsto}(r,\ell,-m). } \tag{148.15} \]

Each shell has one orientation-even central class \(m=0\), together with \(\ell\) opposed orientation pairs:

\[ (-m,+m),\qquad m=1,\ldots,\ell. \]

Its multiplicity is consequently

\[ 1+2\ell. \]

This gives a second derivation of the odd-numbered shell count, now emphasizing retained orientation rather than square subtraction.

The construction has no preferred absolute sign. It preserves the relation between opposed orientations.

This is consistent with Part IV’s classification of retained orientation across recursive replacement.

The oriented difference \(m\) is not identified with physical electric charge. Charge orientation is a distinct coupling classification. The present \(m\) describes orientation within the candidate bound-state count architecture.

148.6. Worked finite example

Choose

\[ N=3,\qquad n=4. \]

The complete domain has

\[ |\mathfrak M_{J_3}|=16 \]

distinct native realizations.

Its shell classification is:

Shell \(\ell\) Remaining capacity \(r\) Oriented classes \(m\) Count
0 3 0 1
1 2 −1, 0, 1 3
2 1 −2, −1, 0, 1, 2 5
3 0 −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 7
Total 16

The construction contains exactly the integer pattern

\[ r+\ell=3, \]

\[ -\ell\le m\le\ell. \]

No physical parameters enter the count.

Native multiplicity versus readable classification

Specify a coarse receiving classification

\[ \pi_{\mathrm{coarse}}(a,b)=n. \]

All \(n^2\) native realizations belong to one readable coarse class. Its native multiplicity is

\[ \boxed{ W_{J_N}(n)=n^2. } \tag{148.16} \]

Alternatively, specify a finer read

\[ \pi_{\mathrm{fine}}(a,b)=(n,\ell,m). \]

By Theorem 148.2, each fine label has exactly one native preimage under the chosen identification.

Thus the same native domain admits both:

  • one coarse bound-class read with multiplicity \(n^2\);
  • \(n^2\) distinct fine orbital-like classifications.

These are different resolutions of the same enclosing native count domain.

If the ordinal labels are merely presentations of other underlying native realizations, Equation (148.16) must be replaced by the count under that actual native-identification rule. The theorem does not authorize counting duplicated descriptions as additional microstates.

148.7. Definition — The common enclosing recurrence

A static square-count domain does not by itself supply completed recurrence.

We now specify a compatible ordered recurrence, using no physical clock or force law.

Fix an integer

\[ M\ge\max(2,2N+1). \]

Let the native recurrence-position domain be

\[ \mathcal C_M=\mathbb Z_M. \]

Its ordered replacement is

\[ \boxed{ j\longmapsto j+1\pmod M. } \tag{148.17} \]

After \(M\) advances,

\[ j+M\sim j. \]

This is one complete native return.

For each admitted square-count label \((a,b)\), let the complete enclosing comparison record carry this cyclic relation, together with the radial–angular–orientation classification already determined by \((a,b)\).

The recurrence positions are comparison labels within a complete record. Their number is not multiplied into the instantaneous native multiplicity \(W_{J_N}(n)\).

Joint radial–angular recurrence

A possible complete enclosing matching rule admits two recurrence-coordinate reads

\[ u_r(j)=j\pmod M, \]

\[ u_\alpha(j)=j\pmod M. \]

Their difference is constant:

\[ \boxed{ u_r(j)-u_\alpha(j)=0\pmod M. } \tag{148.18} \]

Both return after one common completed recurrence.

This is a dimensionless one-to-one radial–angular recurrence classification.

The symbols \(r\) and \(\alpha\) here identify recurrence roles, not physical radial displacement or swept area. In particular, the radial count capacity \(r=N-\ell\) of Theorem 148.2 is not a count of physical radial oscillations.

Other enclosing recurrence relations are mathematically possible. The present owner explicitly admits this matched class because the purpose is to test its consistency across physical projections.

Accumulated incidence

Define a cumulative oriented incidence count along the complete recurrence by

\[ \boxed{ A_J(j)=j,\qquad j=0,\ldots,M. } \tag{148.19} \]

Then

\[ A_J(j+1)-A_J(j)=1. \]

The relational state returns at \(j=M\), while the accumulated incidence has increased by \(M\).

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{ \text{State closure does not require accumulated count to vanish.} } \tag{148.20} \]

This is consistent with Part I’s distinction between local state return, enclosing recurrence, and accumulated realized count.

The incidence variable \(A_J\) is not primitive physical area. It is a candidate dimensionless quantity for an areal projection.

148.8. Theorem 3 — Cyclic phase compatibility

The same native recurrence group admits complex character representations

\[ \boxed{ \chi_m(j) = \exp\left(\frac{2\pi i mj}{M}\right). } \tag{148.21} \]

These satisfy

\[ \chi_m(j+k)=\chi_m(j)\chi_m(k) \]

with addition modulo \(M\), and

\[ \boxed{ \chi_m(j+M)=\chi_m(j). } \tag{148.22} \]

The parameter \(m\) is the oriented count classification of Theorem 148.2.

Because the permitted orientations satisfy

\[ -N\le m\le N \]

and \(M\ge2N+1\), distinct permitted \(m\) values correspond to distinct characters of \(\mathbb Z_M\).

Thus every permitted square-shell orientation possesses a compatible cyclic phase representation.

Theorem 148.3 — Phase-representation consistency.

For the finite enclosing domain \(\mathfrak M_{J_N}\) and the specified recurrence group \(\mathcal C_M\), every oriented classification \(m=a-b\) admits a closed cyclic character \(\chi_m\). Distinct permitted \(m\) values remain distinguishable under the chosen recurrence resolution.

Proof.

Closure follows immediately from the integer character representation of \(\mathbb Z_M\). If two permitted integers \(m,m'\) define the same character, then \(m-m'\) is divisible by \(M\). But

\[ |m-m'|\le2N<M. \]

Therefore \(m=m'\). \(\square\)

The phase is a representation of the native recurrence, not a new primitive physical wave.

This is consistent with Addendum 72.

The theorem does not infer the physical frequency of atomic transitions from the cyclic comparison count.

148.9. Gravitational projection — Keplerian areal closure

Consider a gravitational background \(G\) admitting a bound two-body relationship.

Its local physical objects may be read as a star and planet. Their common orbital compatibility belongs to the enclosing gravitational system.

The background assigns physical spatial and temporal calibrations to a selected native recurrence.

Let one completed recurrence have physical period \(T_G\).

Map the \(M\) ordered recurrence comparisons to

\[ \boxed{ t_j=\frac{j}{M}T_G. } \tag{148.23} \]

Let the physical orbital read be an ellipse with semimajor axis \(a_G>0\), eccentricity \(0\le e<1\), and semiminor axis

\[ b_G=a_G\sqrt{1-e^2}. \]

For each comparison, choose the eccentric-anomaly coordinate \(E_j\) satisfying

\[ \boxed{ E_j-e\sin E_j=\frac{2\pi j}{M}. } \tag{148.24} \]

The corresponding physical relative-position read is

\[ x_j=a_G(\cos E_j-e), \]

\[ y_j=b_G\sin E_j. \]

The swept area about the focus from the initial comparison through \(E_j\) is

\[ \mathcal A_K(E_j) = \frac{a_Gb_G}{2}(E_j-e\sin E_j). \]

Substituting Equation (148.24) gives

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal A_K(j) = \pi a_Gb_G\frac{j}{M}. } \tag{148.25} \]

Consequently,

\[ \frac{\mathcal A_K(j+1)-\mathcal A_K(j)} {t_{j+1}-t_j} = \frac{\pi a_Gb_G}{T_G}. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{ \frac{d\mathcal A_K}{dt} = \frac{\pi a_Gb_G}{T_G} } \tag{148.26} \]

in the continuous Keplerian representation.

This is exactly Kepler’s constant areal-recurrence relationship.

Logical status

The primitive construction supplies a closed ordered recurrence and uniform accumulated incidence counting.

The gravitational physical map supplies the ellipse, the time calibration, and the geometric correspondence between accumulated incidence and swept area.

Equation (148.26) demonstrates that the two are mutually consistent.

It does not claim that the square-count multiplicity alone determines eccentricity or the physical orbit’s shape.

The gravitational map can also satisfy Kepler’s third law through its background-specific calibration:

\[ T_G^2=\frac{4\pi^2a_G^3}{\mu_G}, \]

where \(\mu_G\) is the conventional shared gravitational parameter.

This parameter is part of the physical projection, not a primitive R²D count.
