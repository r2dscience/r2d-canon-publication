# Addendum 65 — Boundary-Area Scaling Occurs Before Cosmological Epochs

*Partition Crossovers, Equality Areas, and the Physical Origin of Radiation-, Matter-, Curvature-, and Λ-Dominated Regimes*

## Abstract

Addendum 64 then showed that evolution of these already-defined physical reads produces the
cosmological acceleration equation.

The next question is therefore:

> Why does one cosmological history appear as successive radiation-, matter-, curvature-, and $\Lambda$-dominated epochs?

R2D does not require a different primitive law for each epoch.

Nor does a change of dominant Friedmann contribution necessarily imply recursive boundary replacement.

The distinction follows from the different ways in which downstream physical reads scale with the same
evolving cosmological boundary area.

For a separately conserved homogeneous source component j with constant equation-of-state parameter

$$ p_j=w_j\varepsilon_j, $$

$$ \varepsilon_j\propto a^{-3(1+w_j)}. $$

$$ A_\zeta\propto a^2, $$

$$ F_j(A_\zeta)=B_j A_\zeta^{-s_j}, $$

$$ s_j=\frac{3}{2}(1+w_j). $$

$$ s_r=2, $$

$$ s_m=\frac32, $$

$$ s_k=1, $$

$$ s_\Lambda=0. $$

The familiar hierarchy is therefore

$$2>\frac32>1>0$$

Radiation therefore falls relative to matter.

Matter falls relative to curvature magnitude.

Matter and curvature both fall relative to a constant Λ contribution.

The cosmological history is consequently a history of changing relative weights within one physical rate
partition.

Define a reference area

$$ A_0$$

and write

$$ F_j(A)=F_{j,0}\left(\frac{A}{A_0}\right)^{-s_j}$$

For two positive contributions i and j , their equality occurs at

$$ A_{i\mid j}=A_0\left(\frac{F_{i,0}}{F_{j,0}}\right)^{1/(s_i-s_j)}. $$

This defines an equality area.

Matter-radiation equality, matter-Λ equality, and analogous crossovers are therefore not intrinsically new
count boundaries.

They are values of the same cosmological boundary area at which two downstream physical contributions
to $H^2$ become equal.

The normalized partition weights can be defined by

$$ \Omega_j=\frac{F_j}{H^2}, $$

$$\sum_j\Omega_j=1$$

On an expanding branch, positive contributions with

$$ s_j > \bar s$$

$$ s_j < \bar s$$

The equation is not a new primitive R2D law.

It is the downstream consequence of one evolving boundary area acting on physical reads that carry

The central result is therefore:

Equality epochs are therefore equality areas within one projected history.
