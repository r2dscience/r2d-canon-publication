Addendum 82 — Joint Boundary Statehood Occurs Before Spatial Nonlocality

Joint Compatibility, Quantum Nonseparability, and the Emergence of Local Geometry

Abstract

Quantum entanglement is commonly presented as a relation between two spatially separated objects.

The objects are first imagined to exist at distinct locations,

\[ x_A\]

and

\[ x_B\]

with separation

\[ r_{AB}=|x_A-x_B|, \]

and the subsequent problem is to explain how their observed correlations persist across that distance.

R2D reverses this ordering.

A recursively realized joint state belongs first to an enclosing count boundary

\[ B_{AB}\]

The local systems later read as

\[ B_A\]

and

\[ B_B\]

are peer realizations classified relative to that enclosing relation.

Their joint compatibility is therefore defined before either local peer supplies an independently complete description of the whole relation.

Schematically,

\[ B_{AB}\Longrightarrow\{B_A,B_B\}\]

This causal arrow does not mean that a physical signal is transmitted from an enclosing object to two distant locations.

It means that the enclosing boundary defines which peer realizations are jointly compatible with one recursively realized state.

At the level of the enclosing count relation, the later metric separation

\[ r_{AB}\]

need not yet be a primitive state coordinate.

This is structurally analogous to the cosmological problem.

At the largest presently unclosed cosmological boundary, the primitive object is a count relation whose full recurrence has not completed. Mature gravitational coordinates such as

\[ G,\quad A,\quad r\]

belong to later subordinate physical projections.

Likewise, for an entangled relation, the primitive joint count structure need not begin with two independently complete spatial objects separated by a pre-existing metric distance.

The ordering is instead

joint count relation → recursive peer realization → local physical statehood → local spatial coordinates.

Thus,

\[ B_{AB}\to\{B_A,B_B\}\to\{x_A,x_B\}\to r_{AB}. \]

The statement is not that spatial distance is unreal.

Once local boundaries become physically readable, their separation is an entirely real physical coordinate.

The statement is that this subordinate coordinate should not automatically be projected backward as the primitive geometry of the enclosing joint state.

At the quantum readability horizon, Addendum 3 established that subordinate geometric state identity is not jointly readable while recurrence remains accessible:

\[(\lambda,\nu)_Q=(\text{unreadable},\text{readable})\]

The surviving joint recurrence can then acquire the conventional quantum representation

\[\rho_{AB}\]

For a pure state,

\[|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\]

If this state factorizes,

\[ |\Psi_{AB}\rangle=|\psi_A\rangle\otimes|\psi_B\rangle, \]

the local quantum boundaries possess independently complete state representations.

If it does not,

\[ |\Psi_{AB}\rangle\ne|\psi_A\rangle\otimes|\psi_B\rangle, \]

the recurrent quantum relation remains jointly owned.

For a general mixed state, the correct distinction is separability.

A separable state can be written

\[\rho_{AB}=\sum_kp_k\rho_A^{(k)}\otimes\rho_B^{(k)}\]

An entangled state cannot be written in that form.

Thus quantum entanglement is not merely correlation.

It is the physical quantum read of a joint relation that cannot be decomposed into a statistical mixture of independently complete local quantum states.

The local reduced states appear only afterward:

\[\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_B\rho_{AB}\]

\[\rho_B=\operatorname{Tr}_A\rho_{AB}\]

The mathematical ordering therefore mirrors the R2D ordering:

joint state → local reads.

Spatial separation of those local reads does not by itself factorize the joint count relation.

Therefore:

metric separation ⇏ state separability.

This changes the logical meaning of quantum nonlocality.

R2D does not require a physical influence to traverse

\[ r_{AB}\]

instantaneously.

Nor does it introduce a hidden common variable carrying predetermined local answers.

The joint state is primitive relative to the local outcome classifications.

When Alice and Bob later choose local measurement settings

\[ a\]

and

\[ b\]

the detector boundaries define which local distinctions become readable.

The standard quantum joint realization law is

\[ p(i,j\mid a,b)=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho_{AB}\left(E_i^{(a)}\otimes F_j^{(b)}\right)\right]\]

The local marginals follow from the joint measure:

\[ p(i\mid a)=\sum_jp(i,j\mid a,b)\]

\[ p(j\mid b)=\sum_ip(i,j\mid a,b)\]

Quantum no-signaling requires

\[ p(i\mid a,b)=p(i\mid a)\]

and

\[ p(j\mid a,b)=p(j\mid b)\]

The correlations can therefore depend on the joint state and both measurement classifications without supplying either observer with a locally readable signal from the other’s choice.

Thus:

joint correlation≠local signal propagation.

Bell experiments sharpen the result.

A Bell-local hidden-variable description assumes that the joint statistics can be reduced to locally defined outcomes or probabilities conditioned on a shared hidden variable,

\[\lambda_h\]

Schematically,

\[ p(A,B\mid a,b,\lambda_h)=p(A\mid a,\lambda_h)p(B\mid b,\lambda_h)\]

Such theories satisfy the CHSH bound

\[|S|\le2\]

Quantum mechanics permits

\[|S|\le2\sqrt2\]

and experiments violate the Bell-local bound.

R2D does not interpret this violation as evidence that one local particle instantaneously acts on another.

It interprets it as evidence against projecting independently complete local outcome structure beneath a state whose physical quantum representation is still jointly nonseparable.

The enclosing R2D relation is also not a Bell hidden variable.

It does not assign predetermined values to all possible future local measurements.

The measurement classification participates in defining which distinction becomes readable at each detector boundary.

The primitive ordering is therefore

joint compatibility → quantum nonseparable state → chosen local classifications → joint detector realization.

The central statement is:

quantum entanglement is the nonseparable physical read of a recursively realized joint boundary whose compatibility relation is defined before its later local detector states and spatial separation. The local systems are therefore not primitively independent objects connected afterward by a superluminal influence. They are local physical projections of a prior joint state relation. Metric distance becomes real once the local boundaries are physically defined, but that downstream coordinate should not be projected backward as the primitive geometry of the enclosing joint state. Bell violations reject independently predetermined local outcome structure, while no-signaling shows that the resulting joint correlations do not require readable causal transmission between the local detector boundaries.

And hence:

joint boundary statehood occurs before spatial nonlocality.

1. Begin With the Enclosing Joint Boundary

Consider two eventual peer systems

\[ A\]

and

\[ B\]

Let their local count boundaries be

\[ B_A\]

and

\[ B_B\]

R2D does not begin by assuming these two boundaries are primitively complete.

2. Define the Enclosing Relation

Let

\[ B_{AB}\]

be the boundary defining their joint compatibility.

Its admissible peer realizations belong to

\[\Omega_{AB}\subseteq\mathcal X_A\times\mathcal X_B\]

3. Joint Compatibility Belongs to BAB

Neither peer boundary independently contains the complete rule determining which pair

\[(x_A,x_B)\]

belongs to the enclosing state.

That rule belongs to

\[ B_{AB}\]

4. The Causal Ordering Is Top-Down

Thus:

\[ B_{AB}\longrightarrow\{B_A,B_B\}\]

The local peer realizations instantiate the joint relation.

They do not first exist as independently complete states and then generate the enclosing compatibility from below.

5. Correlated Peers Do Not Require Direct Peer Causation

If stable enclosing compatibility repeatedly admits the same classes of peer realizations, persistent correlation follows.

Therefore:

stable enclosing compatibility → persistent peer correlation.

6. This Result Is More General Than Quantum Entanglement

Ordinary classical systems can also contain correlated peer realizations.

Therefore:

correlation ≠ entanglement.

7. A Classical Joint Constraint Can Produce Correlation

Suppose an enclosing relation permits only

\[(0,0)\]

and

(1, 1).

The local reads are correlated.

Nothing in that statement alone requires quantum nonseparability.

8. Entanglement Requires an Additional Quantum-Horizon Condition

Quantum entanglement appears when the joint relation crosses the quantum readability horizon while remaining nonseparable in the physical quantum representation.

9. Addendum 3 Supplies the Readability Condition

At the quantum horizon,

\[(\lambda,\nu)=(U,R)\]

Subordinate continuous geometry is no longer jointly readable.

Recurrence remains accessible.

10. Joint State Identity Therefore Need Not Be Two Local Trajectories

The subordinate realization of

\[ B_{AB}\]

does not have to reduce to two persistent independently defined spatial paths.

11. The Surviving Relation Is Joint Recurrence

Schematically:

joint compatibility → recursive realization → joint recurrence.

12. The Quantum State Is a Later Representation

The surviving recurrence relation can be represented by

\[\rho_{AB}\]

For a pure state,

\[\rho_{AB}=|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\langle\Psi_{AB}|\]

13. The Wavefunction Is Not the Primitive Enclosing Boundary

The ordering is

\[ B_{AB}\to\text{joint recurrence}\to|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\]

Not

\[|\Psi_{AB}\rangle\to\text{creation of joint statehood}\]

14. Pure-State Separability Defines Independent Quantum Statehood

If

\[|\Psi_{AB}\rangle=|\psi_A\rangle\otimes|\psi_B\rangle\]

then the two quantum state representations factorize.

15. A Product State Has No Quantum Entanglement

Each local quantum boundary has a complete pure-state representation independent of the other.

The joint state contains no irreducible quantum nonseparability.

16. An Entangled Pure State Does Not Factorize

If

\[ |\Psi_{AB}\rangle\ne|\psi_A\rangle\otimes|\psi_B\rangle \]

for all local choices of ∣ψA ⟩ and ∣ψB ⟩, the state is entangled.

17. R2D Interprets This as Persistent Joint Ownership

The recurrent relation cannot be assigned completely to

\[ B_A\]

and

\[ B_B\]

as independently complete local quantum states.

Its statehood remains jointly owned by

\[ B_{AB}\]

18. Mixed States Require a Stronger Criterion

A classically correlated mixed state may satisfy

\[ \rho_{AB}\ne\rho_A\otimes\rho_B \]

without being entangled.

Therefore product failure alone is insufficient.

19. Separable Mixed States Have the Form

\[ \rho_{AB}=\sum_k p_k\rho_A^{(k)}\otimes\rho_B^{(k)}, \]

where

\[ p_k\ge0\]

and

\[\sum_kp_k=1\]

20. A Separable State Is an Ordinary Mixture of Local Quantum Relations

The joint statistics may be correlated, but they can still be represented as a mixture of independently defined local quantum states.

21. Entangled Mixed States Cannot Be Written This Way

For an entangled state,

\[ \rho_{AB}\ne\sum_k p_k\rho_A^{(k)}\otimes\rho_B^{(k)} \]

for every possible decomposition of that form.

22. This Is the Proper R2D Quantum Criterion

Generic peer correlation comes from enclosing compatibility.

Quantum entanglement is the stronger condition that the projected joint quantum state is nonseparable.

23. Local Statehood Is Read From the Joint State

The local state of A is

\[\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_B\rho_{AB}\]

The local state of B is

\[\rho_B=\operatorname{Tr}_A\rho_{AB}\]

24. The Mathematical Ordering Is Therefore

\[\rho_{AB}\to\rho_A,\rho_B\]

This mirrors the R2D ordering

\[ B_{AB}\to B_A,B_B\]

25. The Marginals Are Local Reads of a Larger Relation

The reduced states do not necessarily reconstruct the joint state.

Information about joint correlation can disappear when the peer relation is omitted.

26. Entanglement Is Therefore Not a Property Stored Independently in Either Peer

Neither

\[\rho_A\]

nor

\[\rho_B\]

alone contains the full joint relation.

Entanglement belongs to

\[\rho_{AB}\]

27. This Is the Multi-Boundary Extension of Interference

Addendum 81 considered several unresolved alternatives contributing to one detector read.

Entanglement considers one unresolved joint relation contributing to multiple detector reads.

28. Interference and Entanglement Differ in Where Separation Fails

Interference:

alternative identity remains unresolved within one read.

Entanglement:

local state identity remains nonseparable across multiple reads.

29. Both Follow the Same R2D Architecture

enclosing compatibility → recursive realization → quantum recurrence → projected quantum state.

30. Now Introduce Physical Spatial Readout

Suppose the eventual detector boundaries occupy physical locations

\[ x_A\]

and

\[ x_B\]

Their physical separation is

\[ r_{AB}=|x_A-x_B|\]

31. rAB Is Real

R2D does not deny the physical spatial separation of the detector boundaries.

The detectors can be meters, kilometers, or larger distances apart.

32. But rAB Belongs to the Local Physical Projection

The metric separation is defined only after spatially readable local boundaries exist.

Thus:

\[ B_{AB}\to\{B_A,B_B\}\to\{x_A,x_B\}\to r_{AB}\]

33. Metric Separation Need Not Be Primitive at BAB

The joint count boundary is defined by compatibility.

It need not be defined by the later spatial distance between local detector reads.

Therefore:

\(D_{AB}\ne r_{AB}\),

where DAB denotes the joint compatibility structure.

34. Recursive Scale Is Not Metric Scale

A logically enclosing boundary need not occupy a physically larger region.

The recursive relation concerns count ownership.

Its physical geometry appears only after mapping.

35. This Is the Same Problem Encountered in Cosmology

At the largest presently unclosed cosmological boundary, the primitive relation is not initially specified by a mature gravitational radius.

Its state is first a count relation.

36. Full Terminal Recurrence Is Also Unreadable

For the presently unclosed terminal boundary,

\[ O_\star\ne C_\star\]

A completed terminal cycle is not presently available.

37. Mature Gravitational Coordinates Appear Below That Boundary

Subordinate gravitational projections introduce physical coordinates such as

\[ G,\quad A,\quad r\]

These are not primitive definitions of the terminal count relation.

38. Entanglement Repeats the Same Projection Problem

The local metric separation

rAB

is measured after the joint relation has been physically projected into separately readable local boundaries.

39. The Conventional Nonlocality Question Therefore Begins Too Late

It asks:

How does object A affect object B across distance \(r_{AB}\)?

R2D asks first:

Were A and B already independently complete state domains before that local geometry was assigned?

40. If Not, the Distance Is Being Projected Upward

The paradox can arise from taking a coordinate belonging to the subordinate physical read and imposing it as the primitive geometry of the enclosing joint relation.

41. This Does Not Mean Distance Does Not Exist

The correct statement is narrower:

local metric geometry≠primitive joint count geometry.

Both can be physically real at their own boundaries.

42. Spatial Separation Does Not Force State Factorization

Therefore:

\[ r_{AB}>0\]

does not imply

\[\rho_{AB}=\rho_A\otimes\rho_B\]

More generally,

metric separation⇏quantum separability.

43. This Is the Root of Apparent Quantum Nonlocality

The local objects appear spatially distinct.

The state relation remains jointly owned.

Projecting local completeness backward makes the joint correlation appear to require action across space.

44. Measurement Creates New Local Readability

Let Alice select measurement classification

\[ a\]

and Bob select

\[ b\]

The detector settings define which local distinctions can become readable.

45. Let Alice’s Outcomes Be

\[ i\]

with measurement operators

\[ E_i^{(a)}\]

Let Bob’s outcomes be

\[ j\]

with operators

\[ F_j^{(b)}\]

46. Quantum Mechanics Gives the Joint Read

\[ p(i,j\mid a,b)=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\rho_{AB}\left(E_i^{(a)}\otimes F_j^{(b)}\right)\right]\]

47. This Is a Joint Realization Measure

The weighting belongs first to the combination

\[(i,j)\]

within the measurement context

\[(a,b)\]

48. The Local Reads Are Marginals

Alice reads

\[ p(i\mid a)=\sum_jp(i,j\mid a,b)\]

Bob reads

\[ p(j\mid b)=\sum_ip(i,j\mid a,b)\]

49. No-Signaling Requires Local Independence of the Remote Setting

For ordinary quantum theory,

\[ p(i\mid a,b)=p(i\mid a)\]

and

\[ p(j\mid a,b)=p(j\mid b)\]

50. Joint Dependence Does Not Produce Local Signal Readability

The correlations may depend on

\[ a\]

and

\[ b\]

jointly while neither local marginal supplies information about the remote choice.

51. Therefore

joint correlation ≠ signal transmission.

52. R2D Does Not Require Peer-to-Peer Causal Transmission

The architecture is

\[ B_{AB}\longrightarrow\{B_A,B_B\}\]

not

\[ B_A\to B_B\]

Nor is it

\[ B_B\to B_A\]

53. This Is Not a Superluminal Hidden Mechanism

The enclosing count relation is not a physical pulse propagating between the detector locations.

Its role is state classification.

54. Conditional State Update Must Also Be Distinguished From Signal Transmission

If Alice obtains outcome i, the conventional conditional state assigned to Bob is

\[\rho_{B\mid i,a}=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}_A[(E_i^{(a)}\otimes I)\rho_{AB}]}{p(i\mid a)}\]

55. This Is a Conditional Read of the Joint Relation

The state has been restricted to the portion compatible with Alice’s realized outcome.

It does not imply a physical message traveled from Alice to Bob.

56. Bob’s Unconditioned State Remains

\[\rho_B=\sum_ip(i\mid a)\rho_{B\mid i,a}\]

Under no-signaling, this local state is independent of Alice’s freely chosen measurement setting.

57. Conditional Reclassification Is Not Physical Transport

Thus:

conditional joint update≠superluminal causal signal.

58. Bell’s Theorem Tests a Stronger Claim

Bell does not merely test whether a visible signal travels between detectors.

It tests whether the joint correlations can be reduced to an appropriate locally factorizable hidden-variable structure.

59. Introduce a Hypothetical Hidden Variable

Let

\[\lambda_h\]
