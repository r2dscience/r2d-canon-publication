Addendum 108 — A Branch Is Not an Occupied World

Everettian Branching and the Multiplicity-to-Occupation Projection

Abstract

A quantum representation can contain several outcome components, and decoherence can make records associated with those components effectively noninterfering. Neither result supplies a rule that converts each component into a separately occupied world. The inference from branching representation to multiplicity of occupied worlds crosses the distinction between compatible possibilities and realized occurrences.

R²D fixes the order of the relevant count types. An operative boundary defines its distinguishable states, the compatible realizations of each state, and the occurrences readable there. Addendum 105 proves that an occurrence within this architecture does not create the multiplicity landscape that classifies it. Addendum 102 applies recursive replacement at the quantum horizon: possible subordinate histories and particle representations do not thereby become independently occupied trajectories. Addendum 107 distinguishes possible counts, normalized weights, and realized transitions. The same dependency exposes the Everettian world inference.

The result is categorical about that inference: a branch in a quantum representation is not, by virtue of branching or decoherence, an occupied world. Calling every branch a world adds an occupation claim not contained in the representation. A separate boundary and occurrence relation would have to give that claim its physical meaning. The argument does not turn a proof about one specified count architecture into a theorem that no other physically existing system could ever occur.

108.1. The Inferential Step

Consider an idealized measurement representation:

\[ |\Psi\rangle =c_A|A\rangle|D_A\rangle|E_A\rangle +c_B|B\rangle|D_B\rangle|E_B\rangle, \qquad \langle E_A|E_B\rangle\simeq0. \]

The two terms correlate alternative system labels with distinguishable detector and environmental records. Approximate environmental orthogonality suppresses interference between those record descriptions. This is a statement about the structure and dynamics of the quantum representation. It is not yet a statement that two independently occupied detector occurrences exist.

The disputed move can be written:

\[ \begin{aligned} &\text{several dynamically stable record components in }|\Psi\rangle\\ &\qquad\Longrightarrow \text{several independently occupied worlds}. \end{aligned} \]

The implication requires a bridge between a component of the representation and physical occupation. It is not supplied by linearity, approximate decoherence, or the absence of a collapse term. The latter says that the represented terms are not deleted by the specified unitary dynamics. A term remaining in a representation is not an occurrence rule.

This is the relevant claim about an Everettian multiverse. It concerns worlds inferred from branches of a quantum state. It does not address proposals for causally distant regions arising from cosmological inflation, which have different premises.

108.2. Boundary-Defined Possibility and Occurrence

At an operative count boundary \(B\), let \(\mathfrak M_B\) be its domain of compatible formal microstates and let

\[ \pi_B:\mathfrak M_B\longrightarrow\mathcal M_B \]

classify them into readable macrostates. For a state \(i\),

\[ F_{B,i}=\pi_B^{-1}(i), \qquad W_{B,i}=|F_{B,i}|. \]

\(W_{B,i}\) counts compatible possible realizations of the state \(i\). It does not count how many copies of that state have occurred. For exclusive state labels \(A\ne B\), the fibers are disjoint:

\[ F_{B,A}\cap F_{B,B}=\varnothing. \]

If one specified occurrence at \(B\) is classified by \(A\), then that same occurrence is not also classified by the exclusive label \(B\) under the same map. This is a classification result. It does not claim that no other occurrences can exist anywhere. To count more than one occupied detector world requires more than the presence of more than one member or image in a possible-state domain: it requires a specified collection of occurrences and the boundary at which each is occupied.

In R²D, the enclosing boundary owns the classification of its states and compatible subordinate realizations. Addendum 105 proves that changing or observing a subordinate realization does not create that count structure. The quantum analogue is immediate as a dependency audit: expanding a quantum state into possible record components does not create an independent occurrence for each component. Where a quantum-to-R²D mapping is invoked, its proposed detector boundary and occurrence read must be stated; the abstract classification theorem alone does not choose those physical coordinates.

108.3. The No-Occupation-From-Decomposition Result

Premises. Fix a boundary \(B\), its classification \(\pi_B\), and a set of mutually exclusive readable detector labels \(\{i\}\). Let \(R_B\) denote a mathematical representation that contains contributions associated with several labels. Let \(O_B(i)\) mean that an occurrence of label \(i\) is established at \(B\).

Result. The representation and its decomposition alone do not imply \(O_B(i)\) for every represented label:

\[ R_B=\sum_i R_{B,i} \quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad \bigwedge_i O_B(i). \]

Proof. A decomposition specifies contributions to \(R_B\) and their relations. It does not define \(O_B\) or provide a map from every contribution to a separate occupied occurrence. Adding \(O_B(i)\) for every \(i\) changes the premises by introducing such a map. No conclusion containing those new occurrence assertions follows from the decomposition alone. If the proposed assertions refer to exclusive outcomes of one specified occurrence at one boundary, they also violate that boundary’s single-valued classification. If they refer instead to separate occurrences at separate boundaries, those occurrences and boundaries must be defined independently of the notation \(\sum_i R_{B,i}\). Q.E.D.

This theorem is narrow and decisive. It proves that the inference is invalid. It is not a proof by itself that a separately posited world cannot exist. In particular, an argument cannot establish a second world simply by renaming a noninterfering component “world 2”: the rename is the very physical assignment that needs justification.

108.4. Why Decoherence Does Not Supply the Missing Map

Decoherence gives a powerful reason to treat certain record components as approximately autonomous for calculations. It can explain why interference between them becomes negligible in a selected system–environment description. These mathematical results remain in force.

But approximate autonomy is a relation among represented components. It answers how a quantum calculation separates stable records. It does not answer which boundary declares each record independently occupied, or whether its components are distinct physically realized enclosures rather than compatible representations within one larger read.

One sophisticated Everettian reply says that worlds are emergent patterns in the universal state rather than fundamental objects, and that no exact count of worlds is required. This avoids the crude claim that each microscopic basis vector is one universe. The R²D question survives that reply. A robust pattern may be a legitimate descriptive feature of the representation; calling each such feature an occupied world still assigns physical occupation to it. If “world” means only a dynamical pattern, the claim no longer establishes several occupied worlds. If it means a separately occupied reality, the pattern-to-occurrence rule remains unstated.

There is a further counting distinction. The Everettian branches of a chosen wavefunction decomposition need not be the elements of an R²D compatibility fiber. Nor is a branch amplitude a native multiplicity \(W_{B,i}\) or an occurrence count. Equating any pair of these objects requires a defined mapping. The error addressed here is not that branch counting gives the wrong numerical answer; it is that representational plurality is promoted to plural occupation without a count-type bridge.

108.5. A Finite Example Exposes the Type Change

Take ten distinguishable binary peers and the enclosing macrostate with five \(A\) and five \(B\) outcomes. At its specified count boundary,

\[ W_{5:5}=\binom{10}{5}=252. \]

The number 252 counts compatible assignments classified as that same macrostate. It does not assert 252 simultaneously occupied copies of the ensemble. If a resolving read distinguishes a particular assignment, the operative classification has changed; it has not revealed 252 independently occupied worlds beneath an unchanged read. This is the recursive-replacement point in Addendum 102 and the observational-inversion point in Addendum 105.

This example does not assert that 252 coin assignments are mathematically identical to Everettian branches. It isolates the shared invalid inference:

\[ \text{several compatible or represented alternatives} \quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad \text{several occupied realities}. \]

Likewise, the squared amplitude assigned to a quantum alternative can weight a calculation without acting as a force that pushes a world into existence. Possibilities pull; probabilities do not push. Neither a possible-count relation nor a normalized branch weight predicts which occurrence is realized, and neither multiplies the number of occupied worlds by mathematical fiat.

108.6. The Everettian Burden Stated Precisely

The decisive questions are:

  1. What exactly is the proposed occupied world: a component of a vector, a dynamically robust pattern, a detector occurrence, or an independently realized enclosing boundary?
  2. Which boundary defines the distinction among the proposed worlds, and which occurrence rule establishes their occupation?
  3. If every branch is occupied, what warrants the passage from a branching representation to distinct realized occurrences rather than treating the branches as compatible alternatives of the same encompassing state?
  4. If “occupation” has a different meaning from a boundary-defined detector occurrence, what is that meaning and what relation does it have to physical records?

An Everettian can stipulate that all decoherent patterns are real worlds. That yields a proposed ontology. It does not derive plural world occupation from unitary evolution and decoherence alone. Nor can observations within one detector record be used to count independent occupations of all the other record components without the missing bridge. Addendum 105 calls this promotion of a reconstruction into a primitive explanation an observational inversion.

In particular, the premise “no branch is dynamically privileged” does not entail “every branch has a separately occupied world.” Nonselection within a representation is not a universal occupation operation:

\[ \text{no represented branch selected for deletion} \quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad \text{every represented branch independently occupied}. \]

108.7. What Is Disproved

Addendum 105 already establishes that a realized subordinate event does not manufacture the enclosing possible-count landscape, and that reconstructing an enclosing classification from subordinate observations does not invert its organizing priority. Applied with Addenda 102 and 107, these results defeat the argument that the multiplicity of quantum components itself produces or proves a multiplicity of occupied worlds. The Everettian passage from branch to occupied world is exactly the unlicensed promotion of a representation into an occupation claim.

The result has a defined scope:

  • Formal conclusion: representation, multiplicity, and normalized weight cannot, without an occurrence map, establish how many occupied worlds there are.
  • R²D causal conclusion: an alternative subordinate representation cannot acquire independent organizing priority over the enclosing boundary whose state distinctions and compatibility it presupposes.
  • Physical assignment: applying those conclusions to a particular quantum experiment requires stating the detector and enclosing boundaries that make a record and its occurrence readable.
  • No universal negation: an invalid derivation of multiple worlds is not a derivation that every conceivable additional world is nonexistent.

This is a rejection of the multiverse as an explanation inferred from represented quantum branching, not a denial of quantum measurement, interference, environmental decoherence, or the validity of calculations made with a universal wavefunction. The equations remain available. The false step is asking those equations to produce occupied worlds without defining what occupation means at a boundary.

108.8. Conclusion

An Everettian branch is a structure in a quantum representation. An occupied world is a claim about physical realization. The latter does not follow from the former by superposition, decoherence, or an appeal to the fact that no term was removed.

R²D places the count relation first: the enclosing boundary defines possible subordinate realizations, and a realized occurrence has a boundary-specific read. Promoting every possible or represented alternative into a separate occupied reality reverses that order. It is the multiplicity-to-occupation version of the EP projection error.

A branch is not an occupied world merely because the mathematics retains it. The multiverse inference built on that conversion fails.
