# Addendum 43 — Horizon Multiplicity Occurs Before Planck-Area Calibration

## Abstract

The Bekenstein–Hawking relation is conventionally written

$$S_H=\frac{A_H}{4\ell_P^2},$$

where

$$\ell_P^2=\frac{\hbar G}{c^3}.$$

This form invites a geometric interpretation in which a black-hole horizon possesses a physical surface area
that is divided into Planck-sized elements, with the resulting number identified as entropy.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) reverses that explanatory order.

A horizon is first a boundary-defined count domain. Within that count domain, macrostates are
distinguishable and possess multiplicities

$$W_{H,i},$$

with logarithmic count coordinates

$$S_{H,i}=\ln W_{H,i}.$$

The boundary itself acquires a gravitational area projection,

$$A_i,$$

while the distinguishability between radially ordered macrostates acquires a radial projection,

$$r_i.$$

Thus area and radius do not play the same primitive role:

> boundary $\longrightarrow$ area,

whereas

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1484 -->

> distinguishability $\longrightarrow$ radius.

This addendum considers the hypothesis that one adjacent radial macrostate distinction receives the
physical calibration

$$\Delta r=\ell_P.$$

Planck length is then not a constituent of pre-existing space. It is the radial physical read of one
distinguishable macrostate interval.

For spherical Schwarzschild projection,

$$A_i=4\pi r_i^2.$$

The Bekenstein–Hawking relation then calibrates the multiplicity associated with each radial macrostate:

$$S_{H,i}=\ln W_{H,i}=\frac{A_i}{4\ell_P^2}.$$

Therefore, for adjacent Planck-distinguished radial macrostates,

$$\ln\frac{W_{H,i+1}}{W_{H,i}}=\frac{A_{i+1}-A_i}{4\ell_P^2}.$$

Under complete-retention proportional realization,

$$\Delta P_{H,i}=\Delta R_{H,i},$$

so the local asymmetry vanishes and the relative occupancy structure reproduces the relative multiplicity
structure:

$$\frac{c_{H,i+1}}{c_{H,i}}\approx\frac{W_{H,i+1}}{W_{H,i}}.$$

Hence

$$\frac{c_{H,i+1}}{c_{H,i}}\approx\exp\!\left[\frac{A_{i+1}-A_i}{4\ell_P^2}\right].$$

The exponential is therefore first a relative count relation. Probability appears only after normalization.

If

$$r_i=i\ell_P,$$

then

$$A_i=4\pi i^2\ell_P^2,$$

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1485 -->

and one radial macrostate step gives

$$\Delta S_{H,i}=\pi(2i+1).$$

Summing these logarithmic occupancy or multiplicity increments across the radially distinguishable horizon
domain gives

$$\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Delta S_{H,i}=\pi\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(2i+1)=\pi N^2.$$

Because

$$r_H=N\ell_P,$$

this becomes

$$S_H=\pi\frac{r_H^2}{\ell_P^2}.$$

The spherical boundary projection is

$$A_H=4\pi r_H^2.$$

Therefore,

$$S_H=\frac{A_H}{4\ell_P^2}.$$

This is not presented as an independent derivation of the Bekenstein–Hawking law, because its area–
multiplicity calibration has been used as physical input. Instead, the construction exposes what that law
means in the R²D architecture.

The horizon “area” is the geometric read of a boundary-defined count domain. Radius is the read of
distinguishability within that domain. Planck length calibrates the distinction between neighboring radial
macrostates. The Bekenstein–Hawking exponential defines their relative multiplicities, and at complete
retention those relative multiplicities are realized as relative occupancies.

Thus the horizon is not fundamentally tiled by Planck areas.

It is a recursively realized count domain whose mature Schwarzschild projection is area.

Therefore:

> horizon multiplicity occurs before Planck-area calibration.
