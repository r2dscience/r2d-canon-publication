# Addendum 37 — Differential Free-Fall Rapidity Occurs Before Curvature

## Abstract

General relativity conventionally describes gravity through curved spacetime. Freely falling bodies follow
geodesics, supported bodies possess proper acceleration, and neighboring geodesics exhibit tidal deviation
determined by curvature.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) asks what must exist before that continuous geometric
description can be assigned.

Addendum 35 established that physical motion is defined relative to an enclosing rapidity reference. A local
boundary does not define its own zero of motion. The enclosure defines the rapidity landscape within which
subordinate motion is realized.

Addendum 36 then showed that mass is a separate local boundary coupling represented mechanically
within that already-existing reference geometry.

Gravity therefore should not begin with mass bending spacetime or with force accelerating a body.

It should begin with a change in the enclosing reference relation.

Consider

$$B_a\subset\Phi_{G,a}\subset\Phi_E,$$

where Ba is a selected local boundary, ΦG,a is its immediate freely falling gravitational reference, and ΦE is
a more enclosing physical reference.

Define

$$\eta_a\equiv\xi_{B_a\mid\Phi_{G,a}},$$

as the local differential rapidity of the selected boundary relative to its immediate background, and

$$\bar\Xi_{G,a}\equiv\xi_{\Phi_{G,a}\mid\Phi_E},$$

as the rapidity of that immediate gravitational background relative to the larger enclosure.

Then

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1298 -->

$$\xi_{a\mid E}=\eta_a+\bar\Xi_{G,a}.$$

Before introducing acceleration, take a finite realization step j → j + 1:

$$\Delta_j\xi_{a\mid E}=\Delta_j\eta_a+\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.$$

For free fall,

$$\Delta_j\eta_a=0.$$

The local boundary remains comoving with its immediate gravitational background even while that
background changes relative to a larger enclosure:

$$\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}\ne0.$$

Thus free fall is zero local differential rapidity change, not zero change relative to every enclosing boundary.

For support that holds the local boundary fixed relative to the larger reference,

$$\Delta_j\xi_{a\mid E}=0,$$

so

$$\Delta_j\eta_a=-\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.$$

The support produces the local differential relation required to oppose common-mode gravitational
realization.

Gravity becomes locally distinguishable only when neighboring free-fall references cease to share the same
enclosing rapidity change.

For neighboring peers a and b, define

$$\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\equiv\delta_{ab}(\Delta_j\bar\Xi_G).$$

If

$$\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}=0,$$

the gravitational change is locally common mode.

If

$$\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\ne0,$$

neighboring free-fall boundaries acquire different enclosing motion changes.

This is the finite R²D precursor to tidal gravity.

Only afterward is a continuous clock supplied:

$$a_a^{\mathrm{prop}}=c\frac{d\eta_a}{d\tau_a},$$

$$g_{G,a}=c\frac{d\bar\Xi_{G,a}}{d\tau_a}.$$

And only after neighboring boundaries receive continuous separation coordinates does differential free-fall
acceleration acquire the geometric representation of geodesic deviation and curvature.

Thus curvature is not produced merely because an enclosing rapidity coordinate varies with position.
Accelerated coordinates can do that even in flat spacetime.

The stronger condition is:

> neighboring freely falling boundaries acquire different enclosing rapidity changes.

The central claim is therefore:

> differential free-fall rapidity occurs before curvature.
