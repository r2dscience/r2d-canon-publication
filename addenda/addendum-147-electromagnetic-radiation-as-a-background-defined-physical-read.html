Abstract

Electromagnetic radiation is conventionally described using propagating electric and magnetic fields, electromagnetic waves, or photons. These descriptions correctly reproduce measured physical relationships, but they begin downstream of the dimensionless count architecture developed in Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D).

Addendum 145 establishes propagation as an enclosing compatibility relation among recurring distinctions. Addendum 146 introduces the primitive quantum-background hypothesis: a medium of binary distinctions whose compatible local recurrence can be symmetric, with possibility and retention changing reciprocally through completed closure. Enclosing possibility defines the admissible continuation of that recurrence before physical space, time, fields, or particles are introduced.

The present addendum maps that architecture onto electromagnetic radiation.

The organizing sequence begins with binary directional distinguishability. The multiplicity of distinctions defines the native directional class, while the enclosing background determines its magnitude. A compatible possibility is realized as an occurrence within that background. Physical velocity is a subsequent measurement of this background-defined relationship, rather than a primitive quotient of distance and elapsed time.

A complete source–background–receiver compatibility provides the corresponding observational construction. A source oscillator admits reciprocal local recurrence. The enclosing quantum background defines a coherent propagation class. A receiving oscillator can realize a compatible recurrence with a source-relative phase relationship. More generally, a detector can register a new readable macrostate without resolving the faster or smaller subordinate recurrence.

The source and receiver need not share identical native oscillation rates. Their relative phases and frequencies may change while the complete enclosing propagation classification remains unchanged.

The electromagnetic physical mapping identifies a selected quantum-background propagation class with the vacuum null class. Under the appropriate calibration, its characteristic speed is \(c\), and admissible inertial reference changes preserve that physical propagation relationship. Wavelength and frequency are complementary downstream measurements satisfying \(\lambda f=c\).

The cyclic recurrence admits the phase representation developed in Addendum 72. Locally differing phase references require the comparison connection of Addendum 78, whose curvature is developed in Addendum 79. Under the source-free electromagnetic physical mapping, the curvature possesses transverse electric and magnetic readings satisfying Maxwell’s vacuum propagation equations.

Detector realization remains a separate count relation. The incoming recurrence changes the compatible possibility and retention landscape of the detector boundary. The detector realizes a new readable distinction, rather than reconstructing an invisible particle trajectory.

These correspondences demonstrate that the primitive R²D organization of Addendum 146 admits a coherent physical mapping onto electromagnetic propagation, source–receiver recurrence, polarization, and detection. The mapping does not derive every electromagnetic constitutive or calibration law from primitive counting alone.

147.1. Position within the R²D reconstruction

The objective is not to introduce a new primitive definition of electromagnetic radiation.

That definition has already been proposed in Addendum 146.

The objective is to show that the primitive count organization admits the physical relationships observed in electromagnetic radiation.

Three levels must remain distinct.

Primitive count organization

Boundary-indexed distinguishability, multiplicity, possibility, retention, closure, recurrence, and recursive replacement.

Quantum-background organization

An enclosing medium of binary distinctions whose complete compatibility defines directional propagation classes and their background-owned magnitudes.

Electromagnetic physical projection

Measured propagation speed, wavelength, frequency, phase, electric and magnetic field representations, polarization, energy, and detector response.

The logical order is

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Enclosing compatible possibilities}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Binary propagation classification}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Background-defined velocity relationship}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Compatible recurring source and receiver responses}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Physical electromagnetic readings}. \end{gathered} } \tag{147.1} \]

The physical readings describe realized relationships. They do not retrospectively define the primitive possibilities.

147.2. Velocity precedes its spatial and temporal measurements

Addendum 142 establishes that a one-dimensional relative-motion distinction begins with a binary comparison.

For a nonzero relative-motion classification,

\[ \boxed{ \sigma_{Q\mid O}\in\{-1,+1\}. } \tag{147.2} \]

The signs distinguish opposing orientations relative to a selected enclosing reference.

For \(N\) binary distinctions and \(k\) positive classifications, the native multiplicity is

\[ W_N(k)=\binom Nk. \]

The signed directional classification is

\[ \boxed{ d_N(k)=\frac{2k-N}{N}. } \tag{147.3} \]

The singleton endpoints satisfy

\[ W_N(0)=W_N(N)=1, \]

while retaining opposite orientations.

Multiplicity and orientation are therefore different aspects of the same native classification. The scalar multiplicity alone does not distinguish opposite orientations.

Background-defined magnitude

The directional classification does not independently determine velocity magnitude.

The enclosing background supplies the complete compatibility that assigns magnitude to the selected motion class.

Write the dimensionless native velocity relation as

\[ \boxed{ u_{Q\mid O} = \sigma_{Q\mid O}g_Q, } \tag{147.4} \]

where \(g_Q\geq0\) is the magnitude assigned by the complete quantum-background compatibility.

An equivalent joint comparison may use Addendum 141’s native ratio

\[ \boxed{ u_{Q\mid O} = \frac{\ell_{Q\mid O}}{n_Q}, } \tag{147.5} \]

where \(\ell_{Q\mid O}\) is an oriented distinction count and \(n_Q>0\) is a recurrence-comparison count.

Neither coordinate is intrinsically a physical distance or elapsed duration.

The count relation is established before physical calibration.

Physical velocity projection

Only after the native motion classification is established does the observing background assign physical magnitude.

Write

\[ \boxed{ v_{Q\mid O} = \mathcal V_{Q\mid O}\,u_{Q\mid O}, } \tag{147.6} \]

where \(\mathcal V_{Q\mid O}\) is the appropriate velocity-scale calibration.

When the background supplies separate physical ruler and clock calibrations, this becomes Addendum 141’s representation

\[ v_{Q\mid O} = \frac{L_{Q\mid O}}{\tau_{Q\mid O}}u_{Q\mid O}. \]

This is a physical projection identity.

It is not the primitive definition of velocity.

The background defines the magnitude of the compatible velocity class. Physical space and time subsequently provide measurements of that relationship.

147.3. Electromagnetic propagation as a selected quantum-background class

Addendum 145 establishes that a complete enclosing compatibility may admit coherent one-sided propagation.

For an explicitly unrestricted reciprocal binary propagation domain,

\[ W_{\mathrm{prop}}(k)=\binom Nk. \]

Its coherent one-sided endpoints are singleton classes:

\[ \boxed{ W_{\mathrm{prop}}(0) = W_{\mathrm{prop}}(N) =1. } \tag{147.7} \]

The singleton result concerns the selected propagation-history count domain.

It does not imply that the entire quantum background has one compatible realization.

Nor does it imply that the local reciprocal medium ceases recurring.

For the selected electromagnetic propagation class, let the background-defined native magnitude be \(g_Q^{\mathrm{EM}}\).

The downstream vacuum electromagnetic speed is

\[ \boxed{ c_Q = \mathcal V_Qg_Q^{\mathrm{EM}}. } \tag{147.8} \]

The identification of \(c_Q\) with the observed vacuum \(c\) is a physical calibration of this selected propagation class.

The quantum background therefore defines the propagation relationship before the observer measures a wavelength, period, or physical trajectory.

No primitive photon velocity is required.

147.4. The common source–background–receiver enclosure

A measured radiation experiment requires a source, an admitted propagation relationship, and a receiving boundary.

Let one enclosing owner \(J\) distinguish:

\[ B_S:\text{source oscillator}, \]

\[ B_Q:\text{quantum background}, \]

\[ B_D:\text{receiving boundary}. \]

Let \(\mathcal H_J\) be the complete domain of admitted source–background–receiver comparisons.

Define native read maps

\[ q_S:\mathcal H_J\to\mathcal S, \]

\[ q_Q:\mathcal H_J\to\mathcal Q, \]

\[ q_D:\mathcal H_J\to\mathcal D. \]

These are not independently specified dynamical systems subsequently joined by a propagating particle.

Their joint compatibility belongs to \(J\).

The source oscillator realizes a compatible local recurrence.

The quantum background supplies the propagation possibilities.

The receiver possesses a native response classification compatible with some of those possibilities.

The causal relationship is

\[ \boxed{ \text{Complete enclosing compatibility} \Longrightarrow \text{compatible source, background, and receiver realizations}. } \tag{147.9} \]

The corresponding observational sequence can be described as emission, propagation, and detection.

That sequence is a downstream narrative of one enclosingly organized relationship.

Source preparation and local closure

A source may possess reciprocal local recurrence:

\[ R_S\uparrow,\ P_S\downarrow \quad\rightleftarrows\quad R_S\downarrow,\ P_S\uparrow. \]

For a complete reciprocal local cycle,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{\mathcal C_S}=0, } \tag{147.10} \]

while positive recurrence remains compatible.

This does not independently guarantee sustained radiation.

A prepared source may also belong to a larger asymmetric enclosing relation that makes sustained outward radiation compatible and supplies its physical energetic accounting.

The source’s local reciprocal return and the larger radiation-producing response must retain their distinct count identities.

A speaker driven by an oscillator provides a useful mechanical analogy. Its recurring local response participates in a larger propagating relationship. The speaker does not independently create the enclosing possibilities that determine which disturbances can propagate.

The same causal interpretation is proposed for electromagnetic sources.

147.5. Phase matching between source and receiver oscillators

Addendum 72 establishes that completed ordered recurrence admits a cyclic phase representation.

Let a source recurrence contain \(m\) distinguishable ordered comparison positions:

\[ \theta_S(n)\in\mathbb Z_m. \]

For the minimal cyclic representation, write

\[ \boxed{ \theta_S(n)=n\pmod m. } \tag{147.11} \]

Now suppose the quantum background admits a coherent directional propagation relation.

For a selected positive propagation class, let the incoming recurrence at native comparison separation \(\ell\) be

\[ \boxed{ \theta_Q(\ell,n) = \theta_S(n-\ell) \pmod m. } \tag{147.12} \]

The coordinate \(\ell\) counts native propagation comparisons, not meters.

Oscillator receiver

Let the receiving oscillator possess its own cyclic recurrence \(\theta_D(n)\).

The common enclosing compatibility may admit a fixed relative phase \(\delta\):

\[ \boxed{ \theta_D(n) = \theta_Q(\ell,n)+\delta \pmod m. } \tag{147.13} \]

Substitution gives

\[ \boxed{ \theta_D(n) = \theta_S(n-\ell)+\delta \pmod m. } \tag{147.14} \]

The source and receiver now possess a definite compatible phase relation.

The relative phase is not a property originating within either oscillator independently.

It belongs to the complete enclosing recurrence relationship.

Proposition 147.1 — Enclosing phase matching

Let a common enclosing compatibility admit a coherent propagation relation between two cyclic oscillators.

If the complete compatibility fixes one relative phase class \(\delta\) after the admitted propagation comparison, then the source and receiver possess a definite relative recurrence relation, even though their subordinate realization identities need not persist across the intervening background.

Proof.

The enclosing propagation relation fixes the incoming cyclic class at the receiving comparison. The receiving compatibility fixes its relative phase class. Their composition gives Equation (147.14). \(\square\)

Identical local possibility–retention cycles alone do not determine \(\delta\). The complete enclosing compatibility must retain the phase-matching relation.

This is a condition on distinguishable recurrence, not an independently introduced oscillator force.

147.6. Changing receiver recurrence need not change propagation magnitude

A receiver need not recur at the same rate as the source.

Let a selected source recurrence be represented by

\[ \theta_S(n)=n\pmod m. \]

A receiver can possess a different native recurrence classification,

\[ \theta_D(n)=rn+\delta\pmod m, \]

where \(r\) is an admitted integer recurrence multiplier.

Its relative phase can change from one background comparison to the next.

Nevertheless, the background’s propagation class may remain unchanged:

\[ \boxed{ u_Q=\text{constant}. } \tag{147.15} \]

The difference between the source and receiver recurrence readings is not automatically a change in propagation velocity.

It is a difference between native oscillator classifications inside the common enclosing comparison.

Physical Doppler reading

Under the electromagnetic physical mapping, observers in relative motion may assign different measured frequencies to the same propagating radiation relationship.

For a conventional longitudinal inertial reference change,

\[ \boxed{ \omega' = \gamma(\omega-vk), } \tag{147.16} \]

where \(v\) is the signed relative velocity of the receiving reference and \(\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}\).

The measured frequency changes with the observing reference.

This does not imply that the quantum-background propagation class has been replaced by a new primitive mechanism.

The relative recurrence changes while the compatible electromagnetic propagation relationship may remain invariant.

147.7. Invariant propagation belongs to the appropriate enclosing read

The invariant electromagnetic propagation speed should not be identified with every recurrence or motion reading available to a detector.

A detector participating in the quantum-background relationship can distinguish its own native response.

A larger observing boundary can additionally distinguish the complete propagation relationship, provided its read preserves that classification.

These are different questions.

Let \(\mathcal H_J\) be the common enclosing comparison domain, with quantum-background propagation class

\[ u_Q:\mathcal H_J\to\mathcal U_Q. \]

Let two enclosing observers possess reads

\[ q_O:\mathcal H_J\to\mathcal O, \]

\[ q_{O'}:\mathcal H_J\to\mathcal O'. \]

Addenda 132, 139, and 141 establish that \(u_Q\) remains recoverable from both observers precisely when it is constant on the fibers of both receiving maps.

Thus

\[ \boxed{ q_O(h)=q_O(h') \Longrightarrow u_Q(h)=u_Q(h') } \tag{147.17} \]

and

\[ \boxed{ q_{O'}(h)=q_{O'}(h') \Longrightarrow u_Q(h)=u_Q(h'). } \tag{147.18} \]

The observer-relative source and receiver phases may change without violating these conditions.

Physical preservation

For two calibrated observing backgrounds, the physical propagation magnitude is preserved when

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal V_Ou_O = \mathcal V_{O'}u_{O'}. } \tag{147.19} \]

For the electromagnetic null class, the physical identification is

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal V_O|u_O| = \mathcal V_{O'}|u_{O'}| = c. } \tag{147.20} \]

This is the background-calibrated preservation condition of Addendum 141 applied to electromagnetic propagation.

It does not require numerical equality of all observer-specific count ratios.

Lorentz-compatible physical mapping

For the downstream physical wave coordinates, the longitudinal inertial transformation is

\[ \begin{aligned} \omega'&=\gamma(\omega-vk),\\ k'&=\gamma\left(k-\frac{v\omega}{c^2}\right). \end{aligned} \tag{147.21} \]

For a positive vacuum null mode satisfying

\[ \omega=ck, \]

the transformed coordinates obey

\[ \boxed{ \omega'=ck'. } \tag{147.22} \]

More generally, the null classification satisfies

\[ \boxed{ K_\mu K^\mu=0 } \tag{147.23} \]

and is preserved under Lorentz transformations.

The physical frequency and wave number may change while the null propagation class remains unchanged.

This is the electromagnetic consistency reading of the more primitive R²D separation between background propagation and detector-relative recurrence.

The Lorentz transformation is an established downstream physical mapping. Its exact form is not derived from the binary multiplicity formula alone.

A participating inertial detector can also measure vacuum speed \(c\) when it performs a complete, properly calibrated propagation measurement. Its more limited native phase or beat read is not, by itself, such a measurement.

147.8. Wavelength and frequency are complementary physical readings

The propagation velocity has already been defined by the enclosing background.

Now introduce the physical wavelength and recurrence frequency.

Let

\[ \lambda_{\mathrm{EM}} \]

be the spatial period assigned to the selected coherent electromagnetic recurrence.

Let

\[ f_{\mathrm{EM}} \]

be its physical recurrence frequency.

For a traveling nondispersive vacuum mode, their readings satisfy

\[ \boxed{ \lambda_{\mathrm{EM}}f_{\mathrm{EM}}=c. } \tag{147.24} \]

This equation is not used to create the primitive propagation velocity.

It is a physical consistency relationship between two measurements of a background-defined recurring propagation class.

The corresponding angular coordinates are

\[ \omega=2\pi f_{\mathrm{EM}}, \qquad k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{\mathrm{EM}}}. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{ \omega^2=c^2k^2. } \tag{147.25} \]

The physical spatial wave representation follows only after the recurring propagation relation has acquired these calibrations.

The observed frequency is not automatically the rate of hidden source transitions or detector occurrences.

The recurrence relation, source transitions, and detector occurrences remain distinct count classifications.

147.9. Phase comparison and electromagnetic curvature

A completed cyclic recurrence admits a phase representation before a physical electromagnetic field is defined.

Addendum 72 represents an ordered closure group \(\mathbb Z_m\) through cyclic phase characters:

\[ \boxed{ z_r=e^{2\pi ir/m}. } \tag{147.26} \]

The representation describes recurrence. It is not a primitive oscillating substance.

Local phase-reference freedom

Addendum 78 establishes that independently selected phase origins require a compatible comparison relation.

Under the downstream electromagnetic mapping, this is represented by a connection one-form

\[ \mathcal A_{\mathrm{EM}}. \]

A local phase-reference change gives

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal A'_{\mathrm{EM}} = \mathcal A_{\mathrm{EM}}+d\chi. } \tag{147.27} \]

The connection is reference dependent.

Its curvature is

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal F_{\mathrm{EM}} = d\mathcal A_{\mathrm{EM}}. } \tag{147.28} \]

Because \(d^2=0\),

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal F'_{\mathrm{EM}} = \mathcal F_{\mathrm{EM}}. } \tag{147.29} \]

Thus the closed phase-comparison relationship is independent of arbitrary local phase-reference choices.

This is the reference-invariant curvature structure established in Addendum 79.

Curvature is not the local \(P\)–\(R\) cycle

The reciprocal possibility–retention cycle of Addendum 146 belongs to one entropic landscape.

Its local return does not automatically generate nonzero electromagnetic curvature.

In particular, a phase connection consisting only of a pure reference differential,

\[ \mathcal A=d\phi, \]

has
