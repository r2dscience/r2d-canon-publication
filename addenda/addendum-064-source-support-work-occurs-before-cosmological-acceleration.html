Addendum 64 — Source-Support Work Occurs Before Cosmological Acceleration

Pressure, Curvature Cancellation, and the Evolution of the Friedmann Partition

Abstract

Addenda 59–63 reconstructed the first Friedmann equation in successive physical layers.

Recursive throughput, geometric projection, and clock calibration supplied the physical rate

\[ H\]

The squared rate was then partitioned as

\[ H^2=F_\varepsilon+F_k+F_\Lambda, \]

where

\[ \begin{aligned}F_\varepsilon&=\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\varepsilon,\\F_k&=-\frac{kc^2}{a^2},\end{aligned} \]

and

\[ F_\Lambda=\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

These terms were subsequently deprojected.

The matter-energy contribution is a physical source-energy read divided by physical boundary support.

The curvature contribution is a state-dependent geometric read proportional to inverse boundary area.

The cosmological-constant contribution is a state-independent reference-geometric read.

The next question is therefore not:

What primitive R2D quantity is cosmological acceleration?

It is:

How does the already-defined physical cosmological rate change as its projected source and / geometric coordinates evolve?

Physical acceleration is

\[ \frac{\ddot a}{a}=\dot H+H^2. \]

Thus it occurs after the physical rate has already been defined.

For a homogeneous source, Addendum 61 gave the physical source-support relation

\[ dE=-p\,dV, \]

with

\[ E=\epsilon V\]

Because cosmological support scales as

\[ V\propto a^3, \]

this gives

\[\dot\epsilon+3H(\epsilon+p)=0\]

Differentiate the first Friedmann partition for a fixed gravitational projection class with constant

\[ G,\quad k,\quad\Lambda. \]

Then

\[2H\dot H=\dot F_\epsilon+\dot F_k+\dot F_\Lambda\]

The source term evolves through source-support work:

\[ \dot F_\varepsilon=-\frac{8\pi G}{c^2}H(\varepsilon+p). \]

The curvature term evolves through changing boundary geometry:

\[ \dot F_k=-2HF_k. \]

The constant reference term does not evolve:

ḞΛ = 0.

Therefore, on an interval with H ≠ 0,

\[\dot H=-\frac{4\pi G}{c^2}(\epsilon+p)-F_k\]

Using

\[ F_k=-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}\]

this is

\[\dot H=-\frac{4\pi G}{c^2}(\epsilon+p)+\frac{kc^2}{a^2}\]

Now add

\[ H^2=F_\epsilon+F_k+F_\Lambda\]

Then

\[\frac{\ddot a}{a}=-\frac{4\pi G}{c^2}(\epsilon+p)-F_k+F_\epsilon+F_k+F_\Lambda\]

The explicit curvature contribution cancels:

\[ -F_k+F_k=0. \]

Therefore,

\[\frac{\ddot a}{a}=-\frac{4\pi G}{3c^2}(\epsilon+3p)+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

This is the conventional acceleration equation.

Its structure now has a clear physical ordering.

Pressure appears because source energy changes as three-dimensional boundary support changes.

Curvature disappears because it belongs to the instantaneous geometric rate constraint and evolves exactly with the state geometry.

The fixed reference term remains because its physical scale does not dilute with the evolving cosmological area.

The second Friedmann equation is therefore not an independent primitive cosmological cause inserted beside the first.

Within the fixed FLRW projection considered here, it follows from evolution of the already-defined rate partition together with physical source-support continuity.

The central statement is:

the cosmological acceleration equation is not a second primitive law acting beside the Friedmann rate constraint. Within a fixed FLRW projection, it follows from the evolution of that already-defined rate partition together with source-support work. Pressure enters because physical source energy changes with three-dimensional boundary support; spatial curvature cancels because it belongs to the evolving geometric state rather than to the active source.

And hence:

Source-support work occurs before cosmological acceleration.

1. Cosmological Direction, Rate, and Acceleration Are Different Quantities

R2D cosmology now distinguishes three successive levels.

Primitive directional organization is represented by

\[ A_\zeta=P_\zeta-R_\zeta\]

for a nonterminal cosmological boundary.

Physical rate is represented by

\[ H=\frac{\dot a}{a}\]

Physical acceleration is represented by

\[\frac{\ddot a}{a}\]

These must not be identified.

2. Direction Occurs Before Rate

Primitive asymmetry biases which cosmological structural transition is realized.

The structural transition then acquires geometric and clock projections.

Only afterward does the physical rate

\[ H\]

appear.

3. Rate Occurs Before Acceleration

Acceleration requires change in the already-defined physical rate.

Thus:

\[\frac{\ddot a}{a}=\dot H+H^2\]

Acceleration therefore belongs one projection layer later than H .

4. In Boundary-Area Coordinates

Addendum 56 gave

\[ H=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\]

Therefore,

\[ H^2=\frac14\left(\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\right)^2\]

5. Differentiate the Area Rate

Using

\[ a\propto A_\zeta^{1/2}\]

we obtain

\[\frac{\ddot a}{a}=\frac12\frac{d^2\ln A_\zeta}{dt^2}+\frac14\left(\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\right)^2\]

Thus acceleration is a second-order physical read of changing cosmological boundary area.

6. Acceleration Is Not Primitive R2D Asymmetry

Consequently,

\[ A_\zeta\ne\frac{\ddot a}{a}\]

One is a primitive directional count relation.

The other is a dimensional second-order geometric-time read.

7. Recall the Completed Friedmann Partition

The first Friedmann equation is

\[ H^2=F_\epsilon+F_k+F_\Lambda\]

The three terms have already been assigned different physical ownership.

8. The Matter-Energy Term Is

\[ F_\epsilon=\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon\]

It belongs to the source/support projection.

9. The Curvature Term Is

\[ F_k=-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}\]

It belongs to the evolving geometric state.

10. The Constant Reference Term Is

\[ F_\Lambda=\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

For fixed Λ, it belongs to state-independent reference geometry.

11. Acceleration Follows From the Evolution of These Terms

Differentiate:

\[2H\dot H=\dot F_\epsilon+\dot F_k+\dot F_\Lambda\]

Each derivative must be evaluated according to the physical ownership of its term.

Addendum 61 defined

\[\epsilon=\frac EV\]

Therefore source-density evolution depends on both source-energy evolution and support-volume evolution.

For a homogeneous adiabatic source,

\[ dE=-p\,dV\]

This is a physical source-support relation.

It is not a primitive R2D count identity.

\[ E=\epsilon V\]

\[ dE=V\,d\epsilon+\epsilon\,dV\]

Thus:

\[ V\,d\epsilon+(\epsilon+p)\,dV=0\]

15. Divide by the Support Volume

\[ d\epsilon+(\epsilon+p)\,d\ln V=0\]

This relation exists before the physical clock is introduced.

\[\dot\epsilon+3H(\epsilon+p)=0\]

\[\dot F_\epsilon=-\frac{8\pi G}{c^2}H(\epsilon+p)\]

\[ E\]

\[ V\]
