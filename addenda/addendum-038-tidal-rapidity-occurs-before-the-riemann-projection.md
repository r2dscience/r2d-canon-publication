# Addendum 38 — Tidal Rapidity Occurs Before the Riemann Projection

## Abstract

General relativity represents tidal gravity through spacetime curvature. Neighboring freely falling
trajectories satisfy the geodesic-deviation equation

$$\frac{D^2\zeta^\mu}{D\tau^2}=-R^\mu{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}u^\nu\zeta^\rho u^\sigma.$$

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) asks what physical distinction must already exist before that
geometric representation can be assigned.

Addendum 37 identified the finite locally irreducible gravitational relation. For neighboring freely falling
boundaries Ba and Bb ,

$$\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\equiv\delta_{ab}(\Delta_j\bar\Xi_G)$$

measures the failure of the enclosing gravitational rapidity change to remain common mode across one
finite realization step.

If

$$\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}=0,$$

the gravitational relation remains locally common mode.

If

$$\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\ne0,$$

the neighboring free-fall references acquire different motion changes. Tidal distinguishability therefore
exists before continuous spacetime curvature is introduced.

The present addendum asks how that finite peer relation acquires its mature geometric representation.

First assign neighboring peers a continuous physical separation vector

$$\zeta^\mu.$$

In a local freely falling orthonormal frame, infinitesimal relative rapidity components $\delta\xi^{\hat i}$ satisfy, to first
order,

$$\frac{D\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau}=c\,\delta\xi^{\hat i}.$$

Therefore,

$$\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=c\frac{D\,\delta\xi^{\hat i}}{D\tau}.$$

The continuous limit of the finite tidal-rapidity relation is thus relative free-fall acceleration.

For infinitesimal separation, assume smooth local projection:

$$\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}\zeta^{\hat j}.$$

The operator

$$\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}$$

is the local tidal map.

General relativity represents the same observable relation as

$$\frac{D^2\zeta^{\hat i}}{D\tau^2}=-c^2R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat j\hat0}\zeta^{\hat j}.$$

Therefore,

$$\mathcal E^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}=-c^2R^{\hat i}{}_{\hat0\hat j\hat0}.$$

The electric tidal part of the Riemann tensor is therefore the continuous geometric representation of a prior
differential free-fall relation.

This does not mean that one local tidal map determines the entire Riemann tensor in a general spacetime.
The remaining curvature components require additional directional, transport, or observer information. Nor
does position-dependent rapidity alone imply curvature. Accelerated coordinates can vary through flat
spacetime.

Curvature appears when neighboring local Lorentz relations cannot be integrated into one common inertial
geometry. In the continuous metric projection this nonclosure is represented by the Riemann tensor:

$$[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho=R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma.$$

The logical order is therefore

> ⇓

> relative free-fall acceleration

> tidal operator

> ⇓
