Addendum 2 — Boundary Mass Occurs Before Relativity

Landscape, Background, and the Recursive Replacement of Mass

Abstract

Relativity ordinarily begins with an object that already possesses mass and asks how its measured energy, momentum, length, and clock rate depend upon relative motion. Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) requires an earlier question:

                            At what boundary is mass defined?

A physical mass is not a primitive object that retains identical ontological status as the count boundary changes. A mass belongs to a specified recursively realized boundary. That boundary possesses a native multiplicity landscape,

\[\mathcal W_n=\{W_{n,i}\}.\]

whose possible-count architecture is defined before any particular realization occurs. The landscape determines the boundary class to which an orientation-even physical coupling can belong. The enclosing background does something different. Background is realized nondirectional count, not part of the native multiplicity landscape. It supplies the recursive scale of the units counted by that landscape.

Thus two boundaries may possess isomorphic multiplicity landscapes while representing radically different physical mass scales. An ensemble of binary spins and an ensemble of binary protein switches may share \[W_i=\binom{N}{i}.\] without possessing the same physical mass, energy, or recurrence scale. The counting architecture is the same; the recursively realized units being counted are not.

The central proposal is therefore:

         the landscape defines mass; the background calibrates its physical size.

Let

\[\mu_n\]

denote the dimensionless orientation-even mass class defined by the native landscape of boundary \(B_n\) , and let

\[m_n^\Phi\]

denote the physical mass scale supplied by the background against which that boundary is read. Then the boundary-relative physical mass has the form

\[m_n=m_n^\Phi\mu_n.\]

The precise functional map

\[\mathcal W_n\longrightarrow\mu_n\]

and the quantitative map

\[\Phi_n\longrightarrow m_n^\Phi\]

remain physical mapping problems. They are not derived here.

What is established is the architecture required before either map can be attempted.

Mass belongs to the landscape-bearing boundary. Background does not belong to that landscape. It determines the recursive scale of what one count at that boundary represents. Enclosing possibility and constraint may then change occupancy, recurrence, and motion without reconstructing the native landscape.

A moving mass is therefore a turbine only when the mass boundary itself is the selected boundary. If the atmosphere is selected, the atmosphere is the turbine and molecular masses are subordinate physical realizers. If the solar system is selected, the solar-system boundary is the turbine and planetary masses are subordinate physical coordinates. Recursive replacement changes which mass is ontologically defined.

This addendum establishes the boundary discipline required before Lorentz geometry, \(mc^2\), Newtonian inertia, or gravitational geometry can be interpreted within R²D.

1. The Problem Begins Before Relativity

The usual relativistic problem begins with a mass \(m\).

The mass may move with velocity \(v\). One then introduces energy, \(E\),

momentum, \(p\),

proper time, \(\tau\),

and an observer-dependent spatial coordinate.

That ordering already assumes something R²D cannot assume:

             $m$ exists independently of the count boundary used to define it.

R²D begins earlier.

Before asking how a mass moves, one must ask:

                              Which boundary is the mass?

Before asking how its ruler transforms:

                            Which boundary owns the ruler?

Before asking how its clock transforms:

                  Which boundary owns the recurrence being clock-read?

The missing declaration is therefore the boundary itself.

2. Principle — Boundary Ownership of Physical Coordinates

A physical coordinate belongs to a specified recursively realized boundary or to a specified relation between boundaries.

It is not automatically preserved as the same primitive coordinate when the count boundary changes.

Thus an unindexed symbol such as

\[ m \]

is incomplete at the primitive level.

The more precise notation is

\[ m_{n\mid n+1}, \]

meaning:

the mass coupling read of boundary \(B_n\) when that boundary participates in an enclosing physical relation defined at \(B_{n+1}\).

When the enclosing relation is understood, the shorter notation

\[ m_n \]

may be used.

Likewise, a velocity is intrinsically relational:

\[ v_{n+1\mid n}. \]

A radial ruler can be relational:

\[ r_{n+1\mid n}. \]

A clock comparison is relational:

\[ t_{n+1\mid n}. \]

The boundary therefore comes before the coordinate.

3. Principle — The Turbine Is the Selected Boundary

R²D defines a turbine as an adjacent-boundary relation between a local closure and an enclosing occurrence.

For a selected local boundary \(B_n\) ,

\[ B_{n+1}\Rightarrow B_n \]

causally.

The enclosing occurrence determines the compatible local realizations through which it can be realized.

The local boundary possesses a native multiplicity landscape

\[ \mathcal W_n. \]

That landscape determines how the enclosing occurrence can be realized locally.

After closure, recursive carry gives the completed realization a different count meaning at the enclosing boundary.

The turbine is therefore not a universal object called “mass,” “molecule,” “planet,” or “particle.”

It is:

           the selected boundary whose landscape is being recurrently realized.

Changing the selected boundary changes the turbine.

4. The Native Landscape Belongs to the Boundary

At \(B_n\), macrostate \(i\) has multiplicity

\[W_{n,i}=|\pi_n^{-1}(i)|.\]

The native multiplicity landscape is

\[ \mathcal W_n=\{W_{n,i}\}_{i\in\mathcal M_n}. \]

The total boundary multiplicity is

\[ \Theta_n=\sum_{i\in\mathcal M_n}W_{n,i}. \]

and its logarithmic coordinate is

\[S_n^B=\ln\Theta_n.\]

These quantities belong entirely to the boundary-defined possible-count structure. Changing the boundary changes the count domain itself.

The landscape therefore exists logically before any particular trajectory through it.

Occurrence realizes the landscape.

Occupancy describes the realized distribution within it.

Neither creates it.

5. Enclosing Conditions Do Not Reconstruct the Native Landscape

The same native landscape can participate in different enclosing conditions.

Those conditions can create, cancel, or reverse directional support while leaving

\[ W_{n,i} \]

unchanged.

Thus

\[ \Delta\mathcal W_n=0 \]

does not imply

\[ \Delta P_n=0, \qquad \Delta R_n=0, \]

or

\[ \Delta A_n=0. \]

The landscape answers:

What distinctions and multiplicities exist at this boundary?

The enclosing relation answers:

Which realizations of that landscape are directionally supported and retained?

These are different questions.

6. Possibility, Retention, and Mass Must Remain Separate

For local state \(i\), the canon defines compatible enclosing possibility through

\[ P_{n,i}=\ln K_{n+1\mid n,i}, \]

where

\[ K_{n+1\mid n,i} = \frac{\Theta_{n+1\mid n,i}}{W_{n,i}}. \]

Direction appears through a difference,

\[ \Delta P_{n,i\to k} = \ln\frac{K_{n+1\mid n,k}}{K_{n+1\mid n,i}}. \]

An absolute \(P\) can be large while

\[ \Delta P=0. \]

Equal enclosing compatibility gives no preferred direction regardless of the total amount of compatible possibility.

Retention is separately defined by the productive constraint: \[ \Delta R_n=C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{out}}\lambda_n. \]

Residual directional asymmetry is

\[ \Delta A_n=\Delta P_n-\Delta R_n. \]

Mass is none of these.

In particular,

\[ m_n\ne A_n. \]

A mass can remain a mass when

\[ A_n=0. \]

Likewise,

\[ m_n\ne P_n. \]

Mass exists even in a directionally symmetric realization.

7. Mass Is Orientation Even

The existing R²D canon already establishes the appropriate physical projection class.

Suppose subordinate closure structure possesses opposed orientations

\[ \mathcal C_+,\;\mathcal C_-. \]

If the relevant enclosing classification identifies them,

\[ \pi_{n+1}(\mathcal C_+)=\pi_{n+1}(\mathcal C_-), \]

the orientation has been quotiented out.

What remains is an orientation-even coupling magnitude.

The physical mass projection reads this magnitude as

\[ m. \]

Thus,

\[ m(-\sigma)=m(\sigma). \]

Mass is orientation-even, whereas an orientation-retaining coupling such as electric charge changes sign under reversal.

This identifies the projection class of mass.

It does not yet determine its physical magnitude.

8. Principle — The Landscape Defines the Mass Class

The new proposal begins here.

A mass-bearing boundary is a boundary whose native multiplicity architecture admits an orientation-even scalar coupling read.

The native landscape therefore determines the dimensionless mass class of the boundary.

Write this abstractly as

\[ \mu_n=\mathfrak M[\mathcal W_n]. \]

Here

\[\mu_n\]

is not yet physical mass.

It is a dimensionless boundary coordinate associated with the orientation-even coupling class defined by the landscape.

The exact functional

\[ \mathfrak M \]

is not derived in this addendum.

In particular, one must not assume

\[ \mu_n=S_n^B. \]

The total boundary entropy

\[ S_n^B=\ln\Theta_n \]

is an important scalar of the landscape, but identical state-space capacities can support physically different mass scales.

Therefore:

       landscape defines the mass-bearing class, not by itself the dimensional mass.

9. Why \(S_n^B\) Cannot Be the Physical Mass

Consider two systems with the same abstract binary architecture.

For \(N\) binary elements,

\[ W_i=\binom{N}{i}. \]

The total multiplicity is

\[ \Theta=2^N, \]

so

\[ S^B=N\ln2. \]

This may describe an ensemble of spins.

It may also describe an ensemble of two-state protein switches.

The multiplicity landscape is mathematically identical.

Yet the physical energy scale, recurrence scale, mechanical coupling, and mass associated with the two systems can differ enormously.

Therefore,

\[ m\not\propto S^B \]

as a universal physical identity.

The landscape is scale-free counting structure.

Something else determines the physical size of the units it counts.

10. Background Does Not Belong to the Landscape

R²D background is a different count relation.

Let

\[ b_n \]

denote realized count whose relevant subordinate directional classification is no longer readable at the boundary where it functions as background.

Its logarithmic coordinate is

\[ \Phi_n=\ln b_n. \]

Background is realized nondirectional count.

Possibility is compatible possible count.

The canon therefore explicitly states

\[ \Phi_n\ne P_n \]

in general.

Background is also not native multiplicity:

\[ \Phi_n\notin\mathcal W_n. \]

The landscape defines the local possible-count architecture.

Background supplies an enclosing nondirectional reference.

That distinction must remain intact.

11. Principle — Background Defines the Recursive Carrying Scale

The same formal counting architecture can recur at different recursive boundaries.

A count of

does not represent the same ontological unit at every boundary.

At \(B_{n-1}\) , one counted unit may represent one subordinate closure.

At \(B_n\) , one counted unit may represent an already replaced ensemble of such closures.

At \(B_{n+1}\) , one count may represent an already replaced \(B_n\) landscape.

Thus,

\[ 1_{n-1},\;1_n,\;1_{n+1} \]

are numerically identical but recursively inequivalent.

The background defines the recursive scale carried by the unit being counted at the selected boundary.

Therefore:

multiplicity determines the counting architecture; background determines the recursive scale of the units counted

This is the sense in which background defines the carrying capacity of a landscape.

12. The Same Landscape Can Be Recursively Larger

Suppose two boundaries possess isomorphic landscapes:

\[ \mathcal W_A\cong\mathcal W_B. \]

Then they possess the same abstract combinatorial form.

For example,

\[ W_{A,i}=W_{B,i}=\binom{N}{i}. \]

But suppose \(B\) exists on a recursively more enclosing background.

Then one formal count at \(B\) represents a larger recursively replaced unit than one formal count at \(A\).

The landscape has not acquired more states.

Its multiplicities have not increased.

Its counting unit has changed scale.

Thus:

                    same landscape form ≠ same physical mass scale.

The larger boundary counts larger things.

13. Three Different Meanings of Size Must Be Distinguished

The word size should therefore be used carefully.

Combinatorial size

\[ \Theta_n=\sum_i W_{n,i}. \]

This asks:

How many formal realizations belong to the boundary-defined domain?

Recursive size

This asks:

What recursively replaced structure does one formal realization at this boundary represent?

This scale is supplied by background.

Physical size

This is a later dimensional mapping of recursive scale into quantities such as

\[ m,\;E,\;\ell,\;t. \]

These three meanings are not interchangeable.

  combinatorial capacity ≠ recursive carrying scale ≠ dimensional physical magnitude.

14. Candidate Principle — Boundary-Relative Mass Calibration

Let

\[ \mu_n \]

be the dimensionless orientation-even mass class defined by the landscape.

Let

\[m_n^\Phi\]

be the physical mass scale supplied by the background associated with the boundary.

Then define the physical mass read as

\[m_n=m_n^\Phi\mu_n.\]

This is the central new physical hypothesis of the addendum.

It says:

\[ \text{landscape}\longrightarrow\mu_n \]

and

\[ \text{background}\longrightarrow m_n^\Phi. \]

Their physical product gives

\[ m_n. \]

The precise maps remain to be established.

15. Mass Is Therefore Boundary Relative

The notation

\[ m_n \]

does not mean that one primitive substance has a different numerical label at different scales.

It means:

\(m_n\) = mass read belonging to boundary \(B_n\) under its background calibration.

If the boundary changes,

\[ B_n\longrightarrow B_{n+1}, \]

then the relevant native landscape changes,

\[ \mathcal W_n\longrightarrow\mathcal W_{n+1}, \]

and the background calibration can change,

\[ m_n^\Phi\longrightarrow m_{n+1}^\Phi. \]

Therefore there is no primitive requirement that

\[ m_{n+1}=m_n. \]

Nor is there a primitive requirement that

\[m_{n+1}=\sum_\alpha m_{n,\alpha}.\]

That can emerge as a physical approximation or special compositional limit.

It is not a primitive recursive identity.

16. Boundary-Relative Mass Is Not Relativistic Mass

This distinction is essential.

R²D does not propose

\[ m=m(v). \]

For one fixed turbine boundary,

\[ m_n \]

remains its orientation-even coupling read under a change of relative velocity.

Energy and momentum change.

The boundary mass does not become a different mass merely because it moves.

Boundary relativity instead means:

\[ m=m[B_n,\Phi_n], \]

not

\[ m=m(v). \]

A change in velocity changes the enclosing motion read.

A change in boundary changes which mass is being defined.

Those are different operations.

17. External Constraint Does Not Create the Mass

Choose mass boundary \(B_n\) .

Its landscape is

\[ \mathcal W_n. \]

Its mass class is

\[ \mu_n. \]

Its background calibration gives

\[m_n=m_n^\Phi\mu_n.\]

Now impose a differential external mechanical relation.

The enclosing relation changes directional compatibility and constraint.

Schematically,

\[ \Delta P_n\ne0, \qquad \Delta R_n\ne0, \]

and therefore

\[ \Delta A_n=\Delta P_n-\Delta R_n. \]

Yet if the boundary itself is unchanged,

\[ \Delta\mathcal W_n=0. \]

Therefore the applied force has not created a mass.

It acts on an already-defined mass-bearing boundary.

18. Inertia Belongs to the Boundary- Background Relation

The canon already separates local force from enclosing acceleration.

Applied force is the local physical read of differential constraint on the selected boundary.

Acceleration is the later physical read of changing motion relative to the enclosing background.

Thus:

                        force acts locally on the turbine boundary,

while

                acceleration is read in the enclosing background relation.

If no new closure changes the enclosing velocity classification,

\[ \Delta v_{\Phi,n}=0. \]

Uniform motion persists.

The persistence is what conventional mechanics calls inertia.

Inertia is therefore not a hidden motive substance residing inside mass.

It is the persistence of the mass boundary’s enclosing motion classification relative to background.

19. The Mass Turbine

The mass turbine can now be written explicitly.

Boundary structure

\[ \mathcal W_n\longrightarrow\mu_n. \]

Background calibration

\[ \Phi_n\longrightarrow m_n^\Phi. \]

Physical mass

\[m_n=m_n^\Phi\mu_n.\]

Enclosing directional relation

\[ \Delta P_n,\;\Delta R_n. \]

Residual asymmetry

\[ \Delta A_n=\Delta P_n-\Delta R_n. \]

Local positional closure

\[ \mathcal C_n^x. \]

Enclosing physical read

\[ \mathcal C_n^x\mapsto v_{n+1\mid n}. \]

The turbine is therefore the mass boundary itself.

Not the observer.

Not space.

Not the background.

Not a universal spacetime manifold.

20. A Moving Mass Is a Cross-Boundary Read

Suppose a laboratory applies force to a mass.

The selected turbine is

\[ B_m. \]

The local boundary owns

\[ \mathcal W_m,\;\mu_m,\;m_m. \]

The enclosing experimental relation supplies the directional constraint.

The resulting local positional closure is read at the enclosing boundary as changing velocity.

So the familiar phrase

“a mass moving through space”

compresses the recursive structure

mass boundary + enclosing background + local constraint + cross-boundary motion read.

The canon already identifies this compression as a characteristic mechanical projection.

21. The Atmosphere Is a Different Turbine

Now choose

\[ B_{\mathrm{atm}}. \]

The atmosphere is the selected boundary.

Therefore the atmosphere owns the native multiplicity landscape relevant to the atmospheric problem.

Molecules are subordinate realizers.

Their individual masses can remain useful physical coordinates in the projected thermodynamic equations.

But they are not the primitive mass ontology of the atmospheric boundary.

The atmospheric turbine is not

\[ N \]

independently complete molecular turbines simply added together.

It is the recursively realized landscape

\[ \mathcal W_{\mathrm{atm}} \]

defined at the atmospheric boundary.

Molecular transitions and trajectories realize occupancy within that landscape.

They do not create it.

22. The Solar System Is Likewise a Different Turbine

Now choose

\[ B_{\mathrm{SS}}. \]

The solar system becomes the selected boundary.

The Sun, Earth, Jupiter, and other bodies possess valid mass reads at their own local boundaries:

\[ m_\odot,\;m_\oplus,\;m_J,\ldots \]

But at \(B_{\mathrm{SS}}\) , those are subordinate physical realization coordinates.

They are not the native mass coordinate of the solar-system boundary.

The solar-system landscape is

\[ \mathcal W_{\mathrm{SS}}. \]

Its boundary-relative mass is

\[ m_{\mathrm{SS}}. \]

That mass becomes physically relevant when \(B_{\mathrm{SS}}\) itself participates in a larger enclosing relation.

For example,

\[ B_{\mathrm{gal}}\Rightarrow B_{\mathrm{SS}}. \]

Then \(B_{\mathrm{SS}}\) can be read as one mass-bearing peer within the galactic domain.

23. Molecules and Planets Occupy the Same Recursive Role

Molecules in an atmosphere and planets in a solar system are not the same physical objects.

But they can occupy the same recursive role.

Atmosphere:

\[\{B_{\mathrm{mol},\alpha}\}\longrightarrow B_{\mathrm{atm}}.\]

Solar system:

\[\{B_{\mathrm{body},\alpha}\}\longrightarrow B_{\mathrm{SS}}.\]

In both cases:

subordinate boundaries realize an enclosing landscape whose state ontology belongs to the enclosing boundary.

This is the relevant equivalence.

It is recursive, not material.

24. Recursive Replacement Applies to Mass Identity

The canon already requires that the next boundary does not simply recover all subordinate states as unchanged primitives.

In the coin construction, ten \(B_n\) landscapes are recursively replaced by one new \(B_{n+1}\) landscape. The enclosing boundary counts the already-defined landscapes rather than restoring all subordinate states as its primitive ontology.

Mass must obey the same rule.

Therefore:

subordinate mass identity does not imply unchanged primitive enclosing mass identity.

A subordinate boundary may remain physically measurable at its own level.

Its mass may remain useful in an effective description of the enclosing realization.

But recursive replacement means it is not automatically the primitive mass coordinate of the new boundary.

25. Independent Composition Explains Approximate Additivity

For independent count domains,

\[ \mathfrak M_{AB}=\mathfrak M_A\times\mathfrak M_B. \]

Therefore,

\[ \Theta_{AB}=\Theta_A\Theta_B. \]

Hence, \[ S_{AB}^B=S_A^B+S_B^B. \]

This exact additivity of logarithmic landscape capacity is a structural clue.

It shows why an extensive scalar coupling can emerge naturally from recursively composed landscapes.

However, it does not by itself prove

\[ m_{AB}=m_A+m_B. \]

That requires the physical mass mapping.

If two peer masses share one common background calibration

\[ m^\Phi, \]

and if their dimensionless mass coordinates compose additively,

\[ \mu_{AB}=\mu_A+\mu_B, \]

then

\[ m_{AB}=m^\Phi\mu_{AB} \]

gives

\[ m_{AB}=m_A+m_B. \]

Thus conventional mass additivity is naturally expected to be strongest among weakly coupled peers sharing one common background.

26. Binding Can Break Mass Additivity

A new bound boundary need not possess the unrestricted Cartesian-product state structure of its free constituents.

Therefore,

\[ \Theta_{AB}\ne\Theta_A\Theta_B \]
