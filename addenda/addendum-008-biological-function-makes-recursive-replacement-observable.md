# Addendum 8 — Biological Function Makes Recursive Replacement Observable

## Direct Recursive Deprojection Across Functional, Mechanical, Molecular, and Spectroscopic Boundaries

## Abstract

Addenda 4–7 developed recursive replacement from a biological system in which several neighboring
physical boundaries can be read independently.

Addendum 4 showed that the spectroscopically resolved myosin-switch asymmetry is organized by
chemical possibility and mechanical retention:

$$A_M=P_M-R_M.$$

Addendum 5 showed that individual myosin-switch identities and trajectories are replaced by ensemble
occupancy and multiplicity:

$$\pi_N:\{M_1,M_2\}^{N}\longrightarrow\{0,1,\ldots,N\},$$

with

$$W_N(i)=\left|\pi_N^{-1}(i)\right|=\binom{N}{i}.$$

Addendum 6 generalized this replacement recursively. A subordinate trajectory is physically defined at its
native boundary, completes there, and is replaced before the enclosing boundary acquires its own native
state:

$$\Gamma_n\longrightarrow\mathcal C_n\overset{\kappa_n}{\longmapsto}B_{n+1}.$$

Addendum 7 then showed that the boundary being experimentally read can be shifted without changing
the enclosing physical system. For a perturbation frequency $f_p$,

$$N_C^{(n\mid p)}=\frac{f_n^{\mathrm{cl}}}{f_p},$$

and the dynamically selected boundary can therefore depend on the clock of the measurement:

$B_{\mathrm{read}}=B_{\mathrm{read}}(f_p)$.

These results explain why recursive replacement was experimentally discoverable in biology.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1116 -->

Biological systems contain neighboring state domains whose native dynamics occur over experimentally
accessible timescales, while the enclosing biological function provides an independent marker of statehood.

The same muscle can therefore be interrogated at the scale of:

> seconds → milliseconds → microseconds → molecular spectroscopy.

The boundaries are not identified by timescale alone.

They are identified by their independently distinguishable states and functions.

The experimental clock then determines which of those already existing boundaries becomes dynamically
readable.

This makes biology unusually suited to direct recursive deprojection.

A recursive replacement is directly deprojected when a subordinate recurrence, the enclosing state into
which it is replaced, and the relation between them can be experimentally constrained independently.

Symbolically, the experiment can separately interrogate:

$$\Gamma_n,\qquad B_{n+1},\qquad\kappa_n.$$

Muscle satisfies this requirement across several neighboring scales.

The myosin switch can be read spectroscopically.

Its ensemble occupancy can be measured.

Muscle force can be measured independently.

Mechanical perturbation frequency can then move the read toward faster subordinate dynamics.

Still faster spectroscopic probes continue the deprojection into molecular recurrence.

This makes the usual statement that subordinate biological fluctuations “average out” incomplete.

Averaging can remain mathematically useful.

But the experimentally observed architecture is more specific:

> native recurrence → closure → replacement → new state domain.

Biology is therefore not assigned a special ontology in R²D.

It occupies an unusually readable physical window.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1117 -->

At several neighboring boundaries, both the subordinate recurrence and the enclosing state remain
experimentally accessible.

The universal recursive operation can therefore be observed before either side becomes unreadable.

The central statement is:

> **Biological systems make recursive replacement directly observable.**

## 1. Recursive Replacement Was First Exposed in an Accessible Physical Window

The central problem of recursive replacement is usually difficult to observe directly.

To demonstrate replacement rather than merely infer it, one must distinguish at least two neighboring
state domains.

One must observe a subordinate dynamics at the boundary where that dynamics is native.

One must also observe the enclosing state into which the subordinate realization is replaced.

Ideally, the larger function of that enclosing state should be independently measurable.

Biological systems provide this combination.

Muscle provided it directly.

## 2. Muscle Exposes Several Physical Reads at Once

The myosin experiments independently resolved:

> myosin-switch state,

> switch ensemble occupancy,

and

> muscle-scale force.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1118 -->

These measurements are physically related.

They are not the same coordinate.

Addendum 4 established that the switch occupancy asymmetry is organized by

$A_M=P_M-R_M$.

Thus the local molecular read depends jointly on chemical possibility and mechanical retention.

It is not a unique representation of force alone.

## 3. The Experiment Therefore Separates Adjacent State Domains

At the switch boundary:

$$M_1\leftrightarrow M_2.$$

At the ensemble boundary:

$i=\#\{\alpha:x_\alpha=M_2\}$.

At the muscle boundary:

$$F.$$

These are independently readable physical distinctions.

They are not simply one state variable written in three different units.

Thus:

> **switch state is not ensemble occupancy, and ensemble occupancy is not muscle force.**

Their relation is recursive.

## 4. Biological Function Supplies an Independent Boundary Marker

This is a critical experimental advantage.

Muscle force is not assigned to a boundary because it occurs at a particular frequency.

It is independently identifiable as a muscle-scale mechanical function.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1119 -->

Likewise, the myosin switch is not identified merely because it possesses a millisecond timescale.

Its states

$$M_1$$

and

$$M_2$$

are independently resolved biochemical-mechanical distinctions.

Therefore:

> frequency does not define the boundary.

Instead:

> state ownership defines the boundary.

Frequency then helps determine which boundary is dynamically read.

## 5. Function and Recurrence Play Different Experimental Roles

Biological function provides an enclosing state anchor.

Native recurrence provides a dynamical read of subordinate realization.

Thus:

> function → boundary identification,

while

> frequency → boundary selection during measurement.

The two should not be conflated.

A characteristic frequency alone does not establish a new recursive boundary.

An independently identifiable state classification does.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1120 -->

## 6. Definition — Direct Observation of Recursive Replacement

Recursive replacement is directly observable when an experiment can independently constrain:

> a subordinate native recurrence,

> an enclosing state classification,

and

> the many-to-one relation between them.

For adjacent boundaries $B_n$ and $B_{n+1}$, this requires experimental access to:

$$\Gamma_n,$$

$$B_{n+1},$$

and

$$\kappa_n.$$

The experiment need not reconstruct every subordinate history.

It must establish that the subordinate state and the enclosing state are independently defined and that
multiple subordinate realizations can correspond to the same enclosing state.

## 7. Definition — Direct Recursive Deprojection

A recursive relation is directly deprojected when the experiment changes its physical read so that a
subordinate boundary becomes observable at its native state domain rather than being inferred solely from
the enclosing state.

Thus:

$$\text{direct recursive deprojection}\ne\kappa_n^{-1}.$$

The replacement map is not mathematically inverted.

The experiment instead couples to a boundary before its native distinctions have been replaced in the read.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1121 -->

## 8. Muscle Directly Satisfies the Replacement Criterion

Addendum 5 established:

$$\pi_N:\{M_1,M_2\}^{N}\longrightarrow\{0,1,\ldots,N\}.$$

Multiple labeled switch configurations map to one ensemble state i.

Therefore:

$$\left|\pi_N^{-1}(i)\right|>1$$

for most ensemble states.

The subordinate assignments are distinguishable at the switch boundaries.

They are degenerate with respect to the enclosing ensemble classification.

This is directly observable recursive replacement.

## 9. The Same Experiment Separates Ensemble State From Muscle Function

The switch distribution is measurable.

Muscle force is separately measurable.

At active isometric stall:

$$[M_1]=[M_2],$$

so

$A_M=0$.

Yet force remains finite and varies with the phosphate coordinate:

$F_{\mathrm{stall}}\propto\ln[P_i]$.

Thus fixing the switch asymmetry does not uniquely fix the enclosing force.

The two observables belong to different physical state domains.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1122 -->

## 10. This Is Stronger Than Merely Finding Multiple Timescales

Many physical systems possess several relaxation times or normal modes.

That alone does not demonstrate recursive replacement.

The muscle experiments provide something stronger:

> different timescales + different state classifications + many-to-one replacement.

This combination gives the timescales physical boundary ownership.

## 11. The Biological Hierarchy Extends Beyond the Myosin Switch

The myosin switch is itself realized by faster protein dynamics.

Protein dynamics are realized through faster molecular recurrence.

The experimentally accessible hierarchy is therefore schematically:

> s ↔ muscle-scale function,

> ms ↔ switch-scale dynamics,

> μs ↔ faster protein dynamics,

> IR ↔ molecular vibrational recurrence.

These associations are schematic.

The numerical timescale alone does not establish the boundary.

The experimentally distinguishable state domain does.

## 12. Biological Systems Span an Unusually Broad Readability Window

Within one organized biological system, experimentally accessible recurrences can span many orders of
magnitude.

One can measure slow function.

One can perturb millisecond kinetics.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1123 -->

One can access faster protein motions.

One can spectroscopically interrogate molecular recurrence.

Thus a single physical system can expose several neighboring layers of recursive realization.

This constitutes a broad experimental readability window.

## 13. Define the Recursive Readability Window

Let

$$\mathcal W_{\mathrm{R2D}}$$

denote a region of recursive scale over which both subordinate recurrence and enclosing statehood remain
experimentally readable.

Within such a window:

$$\Gamma_n$$

can be constrained experimentally while

$$B_{n+1}$$

is independently identifiable.

Recursive replacement can therefore be deprojected rather than merely inferred.

Biological systems occupy a particularly broad

$$\mathcal W_{\mathrm{R2D}}.$$

## 14. The Window Is Defined by Readability, Not by Life

Nothing in this definition requires a uniquely biological physical law.

The important condition is:

> subordinate recurrence readable

together with

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1124 -->

> enclosing state readable.

Biology happens to satisfy this condition across many neighboring state domains.

Thus:

> **biological observability is not biological ontology.**

## 15. Biology Is Experimentally Exceptional, Not Ontologically Exceptional

R²D does not propose that living systems obey a special recursive rule.

The opposite claim is stronger.

The same recursive architecture is proposed to organize physical statehood generally.

Biological systems are useful because several neighboring boundaries remain experimentally exposed.

Therefore:

> biology reveals the recursion

rather than

> biology creates the recursion.

## 16. Addendum 7 Provides a Second Form of Access

Biology does not merely contain multiple readable boundaries.

The read can be shifted between them.

For a fixed muscle system,

$B_{\mathrm{sys}}=B_{\mathrm{muscle}}$.

But changing the perturbation frequency changes the dynamically selected read:

$B_{\mathrm{read}}=B_{\mathrm{read}}(f_p)$.

Thus the physical specimen can remain fixed while recursive depth becomes an experimental variable.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1125 -->

## 17. The Probe Clock Moves the Local Readability Horizon

For candidate boundary $B_n$,

$$N_C^{(n\mid p)}=\frac{f_n^{\mathrm{cl}}}{f_p}.$$

When

$$N_C^{(n\mid p)}\gg 1,$$

the subordinate recurrence closes repeatedly before the probe distinguishes the enclosing response.

When

$$N_C^{(n\mid p)}\sim 1,$$

the probe enters the native recurrence regime of that boundary.

Thus changing $f_p$ moves the local experimental readability horizon.

## 18. Biology Therefore Allows Recursive Depth to Be Scanned

The experimental sequence is schematic:

> $B_{\mathrm{function}}$
>
> ⇓ $f_p\uparrow$
>
> $B_{\mathrm{ensemble}}$
>
> ⇓
>
> $B_{\mathrm{switch}}$
>
> ⇓
>
> $B_{\mu\mathrm{s}}$.

At still faster recurrence, other experimental probes extend the read:

$$B_{\mu\mathrm{s}}\longrightarrow B_{\mathrm{IR}}\longrightarrow\cdots.$$

This is a direct experimental traversal of recursive depth.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1126 -->

## 19. Mechanical and Spectroscopic Reads Are Part of One Continuum

At slower boundaries, mechanical oscillation can supply the perturbation clock.

At faster boundaries, spectroscopic probes become more appropriate.

The physical instruments differ.

The principle remains:

> probe recurrence ↔ native boundary recurrence.

Thus mechanical response and molecular spectroscopy can be understood as different regions of one
cross-scale deprojection program.

## 20. The Same Muscle Can Be Read at Different Recursive Depths

This point is especially important.

The system does not need to be replaced experimentally to expose the replacement hierarchy.

The same muscle can remain intact while:

- Steady or slowly varying force reads the enclosing function;
- faster mechanical perturbations read switch and protein dynamics;
- molecular spectroscopy reads still faster subordinate recurrence.

Thus:

$$B_{\mathrm{sys}}=\text{constant}$$

while

$$B_{\mathrm{read}}$$

changes.

This is direct evidence that physical system identity and dynamically read statehood are different concepts.

## 21. Simultaneous Observability Does Not Imply One Boundary

Several boundary-owned observables may be measured from the same specimen.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1127 -->

One can measure:

$$F,$$

$$[M_1],$$

$$[M_2],$$

and faster spectroscopic dynamics within the same muscle.

This does not mean these observables belong to one state space.

Thus:

> **simultaneously measurable does not imply same boundary.**

The experiment can couple to multiple nested boundaries at once.

## 22. This Explains Why Subordinate Dynamics Can Remain Measurable After Replacement

Replacement means that a subordinate trajectory is not a state coordinate of the enclosing boundary.

It does not mean that the subordinate boundary has ceased to exist physically as a native count domain.

Therefore:

$$\Gamma_n\notin\text{state coordinates of }B_{n+1}$$

can coexist with

> $\Gamma_n$ experimentally measurable at $B_n$.

Biology makes this distinction unusually obvious.

## 23. Conventional Multiscale Language Usually Calls This Averaging

When a fast process is not visible in a slower functional read, the conventional description often says that
the fast fluctuations “average out.”

Schematically:

> fast fluctuations → average → slow function.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1128 -->

This can be mathematically useful.

But it does not specify what happens to state identity across boundaries.

## 24. R²D Replaces the Averaging Ontology

The observed architecture is instead:

> native subordinate recurrence
>
> ⇓
>
> closure
>
> ⇓
>
> recursive replacement
>
> ⇓
>
> new enclosing state.

The subordinate path does not merely become a small correction to a slower version of itself.

The state domain changes.

## 25. Why Averaging Works Anyway

Averaging can succeed because the enclosing read often depends only on the consequences of many
completed subordinate closures.

If:

$$N_C^{(n\mid p)}\gg 1,$$

then many subordinate recurrences occur before one enclosing observation.

An average can therefore provide an excellent projection of their cumulative physical consequence.

But:

> **successful averaging does not imply a persistent cross-scale trajectory.**

The mathematics can be correct while the ontological interpretation is incomplete.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1129 -->

## 26. Frequency-Dependent Readability Distinguishes the Two Pictures

If the system contained only one persistent trajectory, increasing temporal resolution would merely reveal
increasingly detailed samples of that same trajectory.

Recursive replacement predicts something different.

As the probe crosses successive native recurrence scales, the relevant state classification can change.

Thus:

$$f_p\uparrow$$

can produce:

> new boundary-owned dynamics

rather than merely:

> more detail of the same state coordinate.

This distinction is experimentally testable.

## 27. Function Provides the Anchor Needed to Recognize the Change

Without an independently identifiable enclosing state, a change in frequency response could always be
described as another mode of one large dynamical system.

Biological function prevents that ambiguity from being complete.

The muscle-scale mechanical state is independently identifiable.

The myosin switch state is independently identifiable.

Their relationship can therefore be studied rather than assumed.

Thus:

> function anchors the enclosing boundary.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1130 -->

## 28. A Biological Boundary Need Not Correspond to an Ordinary Named Function

Not every recursively realized biological state should be called a physiological function.

A bond vibration may not have an independently named function.

A microsecond protein recurrence may not.

Therefore R²D distinguishes:

> state boundary

from

> functional boundary.

Biological function is valuable because it supplies especially clear enclosing anchors.

It does not define every boundary in the hierarchy.

## 29. Observable Replacement Requires State Ownership

A practical criterion for direct recursive replacement is therefore:

1. a subordinate recurrence is independently measurable;
2. an enclosing state is independently measurable;
3. multiple subordinate realizations map to that state;
4. subordinate path identity is unnecessary to specify it;
5. the experimental read can be shifted between the boundaries.

This is direct recursive deprojection.

## 32. The Observable Chain Is Not Necessarily Sequential in Laboratory Time

The hierarchy should not be misunderstood as requiring the experimenter to observe each boundary one
after another in chronological order.

Several boundaries coexist as nested physical count domains.

Different probes can interrogate them simultaneously.

The recursive ordering is an ordering of state dependence and replacement, not necessarily an ordering of
laboratory observations.

## 33. The Same Biological State Can Contain Vast Subordinate Activity

A muscle can maintain a stable functional state while:

- Myosin switches continue to transition;
- protein conformations fluctuate;
- molecular vibrations recur;
- still faster subordinate dynamics remain active.

Thus:

> **stable function is not static subordinate structure.**

The stability belongs to the enclosing boundary.

The activity belongs to subordinate boundaries.

## 34. This Is Why Biological Noise Is So Informative

What appears from the enclosing functional boundary as noise or fluctuation can be native structured
recurrence at a subordinate boundary.

Therefore:

> enclosing fluctuation

may correspond to

> subordinate state dynamics.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1133 -->

By moving the read timescale, biology lets those apparently noisy fluctuations become organized native
dynamics.

This is another form of direct deprojection.

## 35. A Fluctuation Can Become a State When the Read Boundary Changes

At one experimental timescale, a recurrence may be too fast to remain unresolved:

$$N_C^{(n\mid p)}\gg 1.$$

It therefore appears only through its enclosing consequence.

At a faster probe:

$$N_C^{(n\mid p)}\sim 1,$$

the same recurrence can become directly readable.

Thus:

> fluctuation at $B_{\mathrm{read}}^{(1)}$

can become

> native state dynamics at $B_{\mathrm{read}}^{(2)}$.

This is a boundary-relative change in readability, not a change in the underlying physical recurrence.

## 36. The Experimental Window Has Two Edges

Direct recursive deprojection requires access to both sides of a replacement.

If the subordinate recurrence becomes unreadable, the lower side of the replacement can no longer be
directly followed.

If the enclosing recurrence becomes unreadable, the upper organization can no longer be directly followed.

Thus the observable recursive window is bounded by readability.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1134 -->

Biology occupies the region in which several neighboring boundaries remain accessible from both sides.

## 37. This Prepares the Two Readability Horizons

Addendum 3 established that different physical regimes can preserve different primitive coordinates.

Addenda 4–8 now supply the directly observable middle case.

Within biology, several adjacent state distinctions and recurrences remain accessible.

Recursive replacement can therefore be watched rather than merely reconstructed.

Outside this window, the same operation may remain physically active even when one side of it becomes
unreadable.

That is the problem addressed next.

## 38. Biology Is the Observable Middle, Not the Endpoint

The fact that biological replacement is directly visible does not mean the hierarchy begins or ends with
biology.

Faster boundaries continue below the spectroscopically accessible biological regime.

Larger boundaries continue above individual biological functions.

The biological system simply provides a region where several adjacent replacements can be experimentally
compared.

Thus:

> observable biological hierarchy ⊂ recursive physical hierarchy.

## 39. Principle — Function Supplies an Independent Enclosing Boundary Marker

A biological function is defined at the boundary on which that function is distinguishable.

Therefore function can anchor the enclosing state independently of the timescale of subordinate dynamics.

> function → boundary ownership.

## 40. Principle — Frequency Selects the Read, Not the Boundary's Existence

Changing probe frequency alters which native recurrence is dynamically accessible:

$B_{\mathrm{read}}=B_{\mathrm{read}}(f_p)$.

It does not create that boundary.

Thus:

> probe frequency selects readability.

## 41. Principle — Direct Recursive Deprojection Requires Access to Both Sides of Replacement

For

$$\Gamma_n\longrightarrow\mathcal C_n\overset{\kappa_n}{\longmapsto}B_{n+1},$$

direct deprojection requires independent experimental access to both:

$$\Gamma_n$$

and

$$B_{n+1}.$$

Without both, replacement must be inferred rather than directly observed.

## 42. Principle — Simultaneous Measurement Does Not Collapse Boundary Distinctions

Different recursively related observables may be measured from the same physical specimen.

Therefore:

> **same specimen does not imply same state boundary.**

## 43. Principle — Averaging Is a Projection of Replacement, Not Its Ontology

Fast subordinate closures can often be represented successfully by averaged variables at the enclosing
boundary.

But:

> **an average is not a persistent subordinate state.**

The state domain has already changed.

## 44. Principle — Biology Makes the Recursive Architecture Observable

Biology provides:

> subordinate recurrence

together with

> enclosing state

and often

> independent larger-scale function.

This permits repeated replacement to be deprojected experimentally across neighboring scales.

## 48. Conclusion

Recursive replacement is difficult to recognize when only one boundary is experimentally accessible.

If only the enclosing state is measured, subordinate dynamics appear as hidden microscopic activity,
thermal fluctuation, noise, or averaged structure.

If only the subordinate dynamics are measured, their enclosing organization can appear to be merely the
cumulative result of local trajectories.

Biological systems provide an unusual experimental situation in which both sides of several neighboring
replacements remain accessible.

In muscle, the switch states

$$M_1$$

and

$$M_2$$

can be resolved directly.

Their ensemble distribution can be measured.

Their enclosing mechanical state can be measured independently.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1140 -->

Addendum 4 showed that these coordinates satisfy the experimentally grounded architecture

$A_M=P_M-R_M$.

Addendum 5 showed that the individual switch identities are not retained as ensemble state coordinates.

They are replaced by occupancy and multiplicity.

Addendum 6 showed that this operation repeats across faster biological scales.

Addendum 7 showed that the experiment itself can move through those scales by changing the
perturbation clock:

$B_{\mathrm{read}}=B_{\mathrm{read}}(f_p)$.

Together, these results make recursive replacement directly observable.

At low experimental frequency, fast subordinate dynamics complete many closures before the functional
response is read.

They therefore appear only through their enclosing consequence.

At higher frequency, those same subordinate dynamics can become directly consequential to the measured
response.

At still faster recurrence, spectroscopic probes continue the deprojection.

The same physical specimen therefore exposes multiple nested state domains without requiring those state
domains to be one persistent trajectory.

This is why the familiar statement that microscopic fluctuations “average out” is incomplete.

Averages can remain excellent physical descriptions of the enclosing response.

But the deeper architecture is:

> native recurrence → closure → recursive replacement → new state domain.

Biological function provides an independently identifiable enclosing state against which this replacement
can be recognized.

Probe frequency then shifts the read toward progressively faster subordinate boundaries.

Biology therefore occupies a broad physical window in which both the subordinate recurrence and its
enclosing replacement remain readable.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1141 -->

The importance of biology in R²D is consequently not that living matter obeys a different physical ontology.

It is that living systems expose the recursive ontology more completely.

Several neighboring boundaries can be measured.

Their functions can be independently identified.

Their native dynamics can be perturbed.

Their replacement can be followed.

And their recursive depth can be scanned experimentally.

The central result is therefore:

> **Biological systems make recursive replacement directly observable.**
