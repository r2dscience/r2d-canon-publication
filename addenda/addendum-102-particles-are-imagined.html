Addendum 102 — Particles Are Imagined

Recursive Replacement, Quantum Readability, and the Primitive-Object Projection Error

Abstract

Quantum physics describes measurable phenomena through particles, wavefunctions, fields, and interactions. Its mathematical laws are extraordinarily predictive. But their success does not establish that independently existing microscopic particles cause the enclosing quantum transitions those laws describe.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) makes a stronger distinction. A quantum boundary defines its distinguishable macrostates and their compatible possible subordinate realizations before any particular realization is occupied. Recursive replacement changes the identity of the occupied count state. Subordinate identities do not persist as independently occupied primitive objects beneath the enclosing quantum classification.

At the quantum readability horizon, recurrence remains readable while subordinate continuous geometry does not. The enclosing quantum occurrence can be measured, counted, and represented through physical coordinates. The subordinate structure that realizes it cannot be reconstructed as a persistent trajectory within that same occupied boundary.

The conventional miniature particle imagined to travel along such a trajectory is therefore not a hidden object in R²D. It is an invented explanatory object: a physical representation promoted into a primitive causal agent without the corresponding boundary-defined observation.

This does not deny the reality of particle detections, measured charges, masses, or other experimentally established quantum phenomena. It distinguishes those boundary-defined observations from the imagined microscopic objects assigned responsibility for producing them.

The result is not a criticism of quantum physics. It is the direct ontological consequence of recursive replacement.

102.1. The Enclosing Quantum State Comes First

In R²D, an observable state is defined by its count boundary.

At boundary \(B_n\), a macrostate \(i\) has multiplicity

\[ \boxed{ W_{n,i}=|\pi_n^{-1}(i)|. } \]

This multiplicity counts complete compatible possible subordinate realizations.

It does not count a collection of independently occupied miniature objects that construct the macrostate.

The enclosing boundary defines which subordinate realizations are compatible with its distinguishable states.

For a readable enclosing transition \(\chi_{n+1}\), the causal realization relation is

\[ \boxed{ \chi_{n+1}^{(r)} \Longrightarrow (\mathcal C_{n,a})_{a\in I_n} \in\mathfrak D_{n+1,\chi}. } \]

The enclosing transition determines the compatible subordinate realization class.

The subordinate realizations do not independently determine the enclosing transition.

This ordering is foundational.

It precedes physical particle identity, quantum mechanics, wavefunctions, and the physical laws used to describe microscopic interactions.

The primitive R²D relation is not

\[ \text{particles} \longrightarrow \text{interactions} \longrightarrow \text{quantum state}. \]

It is

\[ \boxed{ \text{enclosing state} \longrightarrow \text{compatible possible realizations}. } \]

Physical representations of those realizations come afterward.

102.2. The Particle Is Imagined, Not Hidden

There is a fundamental difference between an imagined particle and a hidden particle.

A hidden particle is presumed to exist as a persistent object whose position or trajectory is presently inaccessible.

An imagined particle is an explanatory construction introduced to account for an observed physical relation.

R²D does not require the first assumption.

At the quantum boundary, the subordinate state identities have been recursively replaced. Their continuous spatial trajectories are not native readable states of that boundary.

Consequently, assigning a persistent microscopic particle to those trajectories is not the discovery of an independently existing primitive object.

It is the invention of a physical representation.

The distinction can be stated directly:

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Observed quantum occurrence}\\ \not\Rightarrow\\ \text{independently existing subordinate particle}. \end{gathered} } \]

Likewise,

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Mathematically possible subordinate history}\\ \ne\\ \text{occupied microscopic particle trajectory}. \end{gathered} } \]

R²D does not claim that the quantum occurrence lacks subordinate realization.

It claims that the subordinate realization cannot be assigned an independently persistent state identity after that identity has undergone recursive replacement.

The imagined particle is not waiting beneath the quantum state to be revealed.

The persistent miniature object is not a primitive R²D state.

This is stronger than saying that the particle’s position is unknown.

It is stronger than saying that measurement disturbs the particle.

It is stronger than saying that the particle exists but cannot be observed.

The little particle imagined as the cause of the enclosing quantum transition has no established primitive identity within the R²D count architecture.

102.3. Recursive Replacement Is Not Coarse-Graining

Ordinary coarse-graining permits a fully specified subordinate state to persist beneath a larger description.

R²D does not.

Changing the operative count boundary replaces the state classification.

Consider ten binary coins.

The enclosing 5:5 macrostate has multiplicity

\[ W_{5:5}=\binom{10}{5}=252. \]

Under complete individual observation, a particular configuration such as

\[ HTTHTTHHHT \]

has multiplicity

\[ W_{\mathrm{resolved}}=1. \]

Complete subordinate observation necessarily replaces the occupied enclosing classification.

Thus,

\[ \boxed{ W_{\mathrm{enclosing}}=252 \quad\longrightarrow\quad W_{\mathrm{resolved}}=1. } \]

The enclosing 252-microstate possibility landscape remains mathematically defined.

But it is no longer the occupied count classification.

We cannot simultaneously assign the realization the occupied enclosing multiplicity 252 and the fully resolved subordinate multiplicity one as though they were descriptions of an unchanged occupied primitive state.

That would restore coarse-graining.

The same principle applies to quantum measurement.

A quantum state may define many compatible possible subordinate realizations. Complete observation of subordinate distinctions changes the operative count boundary.

The observation does not uncover one microscopic particle configuration that was independently occupied beneath the unchanged enclosing state.

It establishes a new readable state classification through replacement.

The primitive-object error begins when a subordinate identity is assumed to persist across this replacement.

102.4. The Quantum Horizon Prevents Reconstruction of the Imagined Particle

R²D defines the quantum readability horizon by

\[ \boxed{ (\lambda_Q,\nu_Q)=(U,R). } \]

Subordinate continuous distinguishability is unreadable while recurrence remains readable.

An observed quantum boundary can therefore possess measurable recurrence, physical extent, spectral relationships, and countable outcomes without providing a continuously readable subordinate trajectory.

For an approximately spherical enclosing spatial read, an effective radius may be defined from a physically characterized area:

\[ r_Q=\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal A_Q}{4\pi}}. \]

But this radius is a coordinate of the enclosing quantum relation.

It does not become an independently primitive ruler that reveals trajectories of its subordinate realizations.

The enclosing structure is what the experiment characterizes.

The subordinate realizations are what the enclosing boundary classifies as possible.

Confusing those roles produces the imagined miniature particle.

The radius of an atomic state does not establish a little electron following a particular orbit.

A localized photon detection does not establish a little photon traversing an independently occupied path between emission and detection.

A reproducible quantum transition does not establish a microscopic actor whose private motion generated that transition.

Those pictures may be useful.

They are not primitive causal observations.

The unreadability of subordinate quantum geometry is therefore not a deficiency awaiting correction by a more detailed particle story.

It is the R²D condition under which the particle story is recognized as a projection.

102.5. Physical Laws Do Not Establish Their Imagined Causal Objects

A physical law can accurately relate observable quantities without establishing the ontological interpretation attached to those quantities.

Quantum laws relate measurable recurrence, energy, momentum, scattering, detector counts, and correlations.

These relations can be experimentally verified.

Their verification does not demonstrate that miniature independently acting particles are the primitive causes of the enclosing quantum states being observed.

R²D distinguishes

\[ \boxed{ \text{predictive mathematical consistency} \ne \text{demonstrated primitive causal agency}. } \]

This is the distinction Part I calls epiphenomenological (EP) projection.

The primitive-object projection occurs when a boundary-relative particle identity is promoted into an independently persistent object.

Primitive-interaction projection follows when correlations among such imagined objects are assigned direct causal agency after their common enclosing relation has been omitted.

The resulting explanation may be extraordinarily accurate within its physical domain.

Its predictive success does not reverse the causal ordering established by R²D.

This is the precise sense in which particle-based mechanisms can become scientific just-so stories.

The story begins with an imagined primitive actor. It assigns that actor physical laws. It then claims that the actor’s behavior explains the enclosing organization from which its observable state identity was defined.

The equations may describe the observations.

The claimed primitive agency does not follow from those equations.

This is not a criticism of the equations.

It is a rejection of an unsupported reversal of causal priority.

102.6. Feynman Paths Are Possible Histories, Not Occupied Little Trajectories

Feynman’s path-integral mathematics makes the distinction particularly clear.

A readable enclosing transition can possess many compatible possible subordinate histories:

\[ \mathfrak D_{n+1,\chi}. \]

These possibilities belong to the enclosing transition’s compatibility classification.

They need not all be occupied simultaneously.

They need not correspond to persistent little particles traveling through independently defined microscopic trajectories.

In the conventional quantum representation, complex amplitudes associated with possible histories may be combined to calculate an observed transition amplitude.

R²D does not reject this mathematical construction.

It locates the construction downstream of the enclosing transition and its boundary-native recurrence.

The enclosing transition is defined first.

Its compatible possible realization class comes next.

The physical phase-amplitude representation follows.

The sum over possible histories does not construct the enclosing state from a collection of subordinate causal agents.

Likewise, a Feynman diagram is a mathematical contribution to a quantum-field-theoretic calculation. Its internal lines and vertices do not establish a directly observed sequence of little objects undergoing literal microscopic interactions.

The central R²D interpretation is therefore:

Feynman paths are possible subordinate histories of an enclosing transition, not independently occupied physical trajectories whose collective action creates that transition.

This distinction is consistent with Addenda 72 and 81 and the four-way quantum consistency developed in Addendum 101.

It requires no reconstruction of the unreadable subordinate structure.

102.7. The Photon Is a Real Count, Not a Primitive Little Object

Part IV already establishes the ordering

\[ \boxed{ \text{EM recurrence} \longrightarrow \text{completed occurrence count} \longrightarrow \text{photon representation}. } \]

A physically readable radiative occurrence may contribute a discrete count increment:

\[ N\longrightarrow N+1. \]

After empirical energetic calibration,

\[ E=hf, \]

the completed occurrence receives the familiar photon interpretation.

The count is real.

The measured recurrence is real.

The energetic calibration is experimentally established.

But none requires an independently existing little photon to have traveled through an unobserved subordinate trajectory and produced the electromagnetic recurrence.

The imagined miniature photon is an explanatory object constructed after the observable count relation has already been defined.

R²D therefore does not deny the physical reality of photon detections.

It denies that the detection establishes a persistent microscopic projectile as the primitive cause of the enclosing electromagnetic occurrence.

The same distinction applies to electrons and other particle classifications.

Electric charge, mass, spectral states, scattering events, and detector records are legitimate physical quantities at their defining boundaries.

Their measurement does not establish that one miniature object retains an unchanged primitive identity through every enclosing transition and measurement replacement.

Particle names classify physical phenomena.

They do not independently establish the causal ontology imagined beneath those phenomena.

102.8. Why Particle Models Remain Useful

Imagining subordinate objects can make physical relations easier to calculate, communicate, and predict.

Particle language can organize conservation laws, scattering calculations, spectral transitions, detector events, and quantum-field representations.

The usefulness of this language is not in dispute.

R²D does not require scientists to abandon successful physical mathematics or familiar particle terminology.

It requires them to distinguish the physical read from the primitive explanation.

A particle model may reproduce the measurable relations of a quantum boundary without revealing what the quantum boundary is recursively realized through.

Its particles may therefore function as mathematical or physical coordinates of an observed relation.

What R²D rejects is promoting these coordinates into independently occupied primitive causes.

The distinction is not whether the model works.

It is whether the model’s explanatory objects have been independently established as primitive at the boundary where causal agency is assigned to them.

At the quantum horizon, the subordinate identity required by the miniature-particle story is not readable.

Recursive replacement supplies the stronger reason not to presume its persistence.

Consequently, the particle model remains a representation.

It does not become primitive ontology through predictive success.

102.9. Logical Status

Established by the R²D ontology

The enclosing boundary defines its compatible possible realizations.

Recursive replacement changes the occupied count classification.

Subordinate state identities do not remain independently occupied primitive states at an enclosing boundary after replacement.

The quantum readability horizon does not provide a continuous subordinate trajectory read.

A completed quantum occurrence can be counted without first introducing a miniature particle as its cause.

Consequence for particle explanations

A persistent little particle imagined beneath an enclosing quantum state is not an established primitive R²D object.

Its existence cannot be inferred from the enclosing observation.

A physical law written using particle coordinates does not independently establish that imagined particle’s primitive causal agency.

This is the primitive-object projection error already defined in Part I.

What is not claimed

R²D does not deny that real quantum measurements occur.

It does not deny the physical reality of boundary-defined particle counts, scattering events, or experimentally established quantum relations.

It does not claim to have determined the unreadable subordinate structure.

It does not infer that no subordinate realization is possible.

It does not require quantum laws to be derived from imagined subordinate mechanisms.

And it does not claim that one experimentally successful quantum representation uniquely determines the inaccessible primitive ontology.

The claim is categorical within the R²D architecture: imagined particles do not acquire primitive causal agency merely by being assigned to unreadable subordinate realizations.

102.10. Conclusion — The Particle Is Not the Cause of Its Own Boundary

A quantum observation establishes a boundary-defined physical relation.

It may establish a spectral transition, electromagnetic recurrence, scattering distribution, localized detector occurrence, or joint quantum correlation.

It does not establish an independently occupied miniature object that generated the enclosing relation from below.

That object is imagined.

The word imagined does not invalidate the physical observation or the mathematics used to represent it.

It identifies the status of the explanatory particle when its assumed primitive identity lies beyond the readable state distinctions of the enclosing quantum boundary.

R²D does not need to discover the imagined particle.

It does not need to derive its microscopic laws.

It does not need to reconstruct the trajectories assigned to it.

The enclosing boundary already defines the compatible possibilities of realization.

The particle representation comes afterward.

The causal ordering is

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Enclosing quantum relation}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Compatible possible realizations}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Readable occurrence and recurrence}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Particle representation}. \end{gathered} } \]

It is not

\[ \boxed{ \text{Imagined miniature particles} \Longrightarrow \text{enclosing quantum state}. } \]

Particles, when understood as little independently acting objects beneath the quantum boundary, are imagined causes. They are not hidden primitive realities established by observation.

This is not a criticism of quantum physics.

It is the consequence of R²D’s replacement ontology.

The physical observation is real. The mathematical representation can be exact. The invented microscopic causal story does not follow.
