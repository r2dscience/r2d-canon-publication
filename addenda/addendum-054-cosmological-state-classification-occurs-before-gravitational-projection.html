Addendum 54 — Cosmological State Classification Occurs Before Gravitational Projection

Abstract

Addendum 53 established that R²D cosmology must begin before mass, gravity, spacetime, and the Friedmann equations.

For a nonterminal cosmological boundary,

\(A_\zeta=P_\zeta-R_\zeta\) is defined entirely through recursive count relations.

The absence of mass,

\[m\]

radius,

\[r\]

the gravitational constant,

\[G\]

density,

\[\rho\]

or spacetime geometry does not imply that the cosmological count domain lacks physical content.

R²D specifies what is being counted.

A nonprimitive boundary counts the compatible joint realizations of its immediately subordinate peer boundaries.

Let

\[B_\zeta\]

denote a cosmological count boundary.

Let its immediately subordinate peers be

\[N\]

\[B_{\zeta-1\mid\zeta}=\{B_{\zeta-1,\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{N_\zeta}.\]

These peer boundaries are the primitive content of the cosmological count relation.

They are not yet defined by:

\[m_\alpha , r_\alpha , E_\alpha , G, \rho_\alpha\]

Those are later physical readings.

Each peer boundary possesses its own possible realization domain,

\[\mathcal X_{\zeta-1,\alpha}\]

A joint realization of the peer set is

\[\mathbf{x}_r=\bigl(x_1^{(r)},x_2^{(r)},\ldots,x_{N_\zeta}^{(r)}\bigr)\]

with

\[x_\alpha^{(r)}\in\mathcal X_{\zeta-1,\alpha}\]

The unrestricted product

\[\prod_{\alpha=1}^{N_\zeta}\mathcal X_{\zeta-1,\alpha}\]

contains all formally possible peer combinations.

But the cosmological boundary defines which combinations are jointly compatible.

The formal cosmological microstate domain is therefore the compatible subset

\[\Omega_\zeta\subseteq\prod_{\alpha=1}^{N_\zeta}\mathcal X_{\zeta-1,\alpha}.\]

A formal microstate

\[\mu_{\zeta,r} \in \Omega_\zeta\]

is one compatible joint realization of the cosmological peer boundaries.

This is the general state architecture already established in the R²D canon: at every nonprimitive boundary, formal microstates are compatible joint realizations of peer subordinate boundaries, and the enclosing boundary classifies those microstates into readable macrostates.

The cosmological boundary defines

\[\pi_\zeta:\Omega_\zeta\to\mathcal M_\zeta.\]

A cosmological macrostate

\[i \in \mathcal M_\zeta\]

is therefore a boundary-defined classification of compatible joint peer realizations.

Its multiplicity is

\[W_{\zeta,i}=\bigl|\pi_\zeta^{-1}(i)\bigr|\]

Thus Wζ,i counts something definite.

It counts the number of compatible joint realizations of the peer boundaries that Bζ classifies as the same cosmological macrostate.

Its logarithmic coordinate is

\[S_{\zeta,i}=\ln W_{\zeta,i}.\]

The total possible cosmological multiplicity is

\[W_{\zeta,\mathrm{tot}}=|\Omega_\zeta|\]

This is a pre-gravitational count architecture.

It is not a contentless one.

The cosmological content is the recursively organized peer-boundary hierarchy.

What has not yet been assigned is the later physical dictionary by which those peer boundaries become readable as masses, energies, radii, gravitational sources, fields, or spacetime structures.

Realization remains distinct from possibility.

Let

\[c_{\zeta,i}\]

denote realized occupancy of cosmological macrostate i.

Then

\[W_{\zeta,i} \ne c_{\zeta,i}\]

Let

\[\nu_{\zeta,i}\]

count realized formal microstate occurrences associated with that macrostate over a finite realization domain.

Then

\[W_{\zeta,i} \ne \nu_{\zeta,i} \ne c_{\zeta,i}\]

The structural cosmological macrostate is the occupancy mode,

\[i_\zeta^\ast=\arg\max_i c_{\zeta,i}.\]

A structural cosmological transition occurs when

\[i_\zeta^\ast \to k_\zeta^\ast\]

This transition is not yet expansion.

It is a change in the dominant realized organization of the cosmological peer boundaries.

For a readable transition

\[i \to k\]

define the oriented count distinction

\[\lambda_{\zeta,i\to k}\]

At this stage,

\[\lambda_\zeta\]

is not radius, distance, area, scale factor, mass, or physical time.

It distinguishes two different organizations of the peer-boundary count domain.

Enclosing compatibility defines possibility:

\[P_{\zeta,i}=\ln\frac{\Theta_{\star\mid\zeta,i}}{W_{\zeta,i}}\]

Enclosing constraint defines retention:

\[R_{\zeta,i\to k}=C_\star\lambda_{\zeta,i\to k}\]

The resulting asymmetry is

\[A_{\zeta,i\to k}=P_{\zeta,i\to k}-R_{\zeta,i\to k}\]

This asymmetry biases how the peer boundaries jointly realize the available cosmological state landscape.

The primitive cosmological causal relation is therefore:

Bζ → compatibility among {Bζ−1,α } → peer realization.

The peers do not independently assemble the enclosing cosmological state from below.

The enclosing boundary defines which peer realizations can jointly realize it.

Stable enclosing compatibility can therefore appear as stable deterministic relations among the peers. The frozen canon explicitly defines determinism as persistent correlation among peer realizations produced by stable enclosing compatibility.

Only afterward are the peers physically dimensionalized.

A peer boundary may later acquire a physical read such as:

\[m_\alpha , E_\alpha , r_\alpha\]

and relations among peers may acquire a gravitational representation involving

\[G\]

But these physical quantities do not define the peer boundaries.

They represent them.

The R²D causal order is therefore:

peer boundaries

1. What Is Being Counted?

This question must be answered explicitly.

At cosmological boundary

\[B_\zeta\]

Let

\[B_{\zeta-1\mid\zeta}=\{B_{\zeta-1,1},B_{\zeta-1,2},\ldots,B_{\zeta-1,N_\zeta}\}\]

These peer boundaries are the immediate subordinate constituents of the cosmological count relation.

2. Peer Boundary Does Not Mean Primitive Object

A peer boundary is not defined by an assumed material ontology.

It need not initially be:

• a particle; • a galaxy; • a halo; • a black hole; • a mass point; • a region of space.

It is a subordinate count domain.

Its later physical identity depends on the projection through which it is read.

For peer

\[B_{\zeta-1,\alpha}\]

\[\mathcal X_{\zeta-1,\alpha}\]

An element

\[x_{\zeta-1,\alpha} \in \mathcal X_{\zeta-1,\alpha}\]

Choose one realization from every peer:

\[\mathbf{x}_r=\bigl(x_{\zeta-1,1}^{(r)},x_{\zeta-1,2}^{(r)},\ldots,x_{\zeta-1,N_\zeta}^{(r)}\bigr)\]

This is a joint realization of the peer set.

The primitive cosmological count therefore already has concrete relational content.

The product

\[\prod_{\alpha=1}^{N_\zeta}\mathcal X_{\zeta-1,\alpha}\]

But not every combination need be compatible with the cosmological boundary.

Compatibility is defined from above.
