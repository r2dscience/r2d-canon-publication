Addendum 47 — Horizon Count–Clock Calibration Persists Across Boundary Replacement

Abstract

Addendum 46 established that the Nariai limit is a boundary-clock replacement problem.

As the Schwarzschild–de Sitter black-hole and cosmological horizon branches coalesce, their multiplicity gap vanishes,

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=\ln\frac{W_c}{W_b}\longrightarrow0,\]

while the ordinary static-patch clock also degenerates:

\[\omega_b^{(0)},\omega_c^{(0)}\longrightarrow0.\]

The normalization defining that clock vanishes at the same boundary,

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}\longrightarrow0.\]

The replacement Nariai landscape nevertheless possesses the finite boundary-appropriate phase-frequency read

\[\omega_N=\frac{c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi}.\]

Thus neither the old clock nor the old two-horizon multiplicity classification survives unchanged.

The present addendum asks the next R²D question:

If the physical count and physical clock both change under boundary replacement, does a deeper relation remain coherent?

Pure de Sitter provides an independent horizon calibration within the same Λ-defined gravitational family.

Its horizon radius is

\[r_{\mathrm{dS}}=\sqrt{\frac3\Lambda},\]

its horizon multiplicity coordinate is

\[S_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{3\pi}{\Lambda\ell_P^2},\]

and its Gibbons–Hawking phase-frequency read is

\[\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{c}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac\Lambda3}.\]

The replacement Nariai horizon has

\[r_N=\frac1{\sqrt\Lambda},\]

\[S_N=\frac\pi{\Lambda\ell_P^2},\]

and

\[\omega_N=\frac{c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi}.\]

Therefore,

\[S_N=\frac13S_{\mathrm{dS}},\]

while

\[\omega_N=\sqrt3\,\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

Neither physical coordinate is invariant.

But their combined relation is:

\[S_N\omega_N^2=S_{\mathrm{dS}}\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}^2.\]

Explicitly,

\[S\omega^2=\frac{c^2}{4\pi\ell_P^2}=\frac{c^5}{4\pi G\hbar}.\]

Define the dimensionless gravitational count–clock coupling

\[\alpha_G^\omega\equiv\frac{G\hbar\omega^2}{c^5}.\]

Then both horizon calibrations satisfy

\[4\pi\alpha_G^\omega S=1.\]

This relation is not introduced here as a new primitive R²D law.

It follows algebraically whenever two physical calibrations hold:

\[S=\frac{\pi r^2}{\ell_P^2},\]

and

\[\omega=\frac{c}{2\pi r}.\]

Its significance is instead relational.

Pure de Sitter and Nariai possess different radii, different horizon multiplicities, different physical clocks, and different boundary classifications. Yet both realize the same dimensionless count–clock calibration.

Thus boundary replacement need not preserve either physical coordinate separately.

It can preserve the relation connecting them.

This is the R²D meaning of replacement coherence:

state identity changes; relational organization persists.

The result is not a conservation law along the full Schwarzschild–de Sitter family.

It does not imply

\[\frac{d}{dM}(S\omega^2)=0.\]

Nor does it imply that pure de Sitter is the state immediately preceding Nariai.

Instead, the result identifies a common gravitational calibration class realized by two different horizon landscapes.

Therefore:

horizon count–clock calibration persists across boundary replacement.

1. Addendum 46 Left an Invariance Question

Addendum 46 established that the ordinary Schwarzschild–de Sitter clock does not survive the Nariai boundary unchanged.

The ordinary static-patch frequencies satisfy

\[\omega_b^{(0)},\omega_c^{(0)}\longrightarrow0.\]

The normalization defining the old clock also satisfies

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}\longrightarrow0.\]

A replacement Nariai clock is instead read as

\[\omega_N=\frac{c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi}.\]

Thus physical frequency itself is not what remains unchanged.

The next question is therefore not

Which clock is the true clock?

It is:

What relation, if any, remains coherent when the clock changes?

2. R²D Does Not Require Physical Coordinates to Survive Replacement

R²D assigns physical coordinates relative to count boundaries.

A change of boundary classification can therefore change:

\[r,\]

\[A,\]

\[S,\]

\[\omega,\]

\[T,\]

and other dimensional reads.

No contradiction follows merely because these coordinates do not remain numerically constant.

A deeper invariant, if one exists, should be sought in the relation among the reads rather than in their individual values.

3. Recursive Replacement Preserves Relation More Naturally Than Identity

The R²D architecture does not require subordinate realization identities to survive recursive reclassification.

What can survive is relational organization.

Schematically,

\[\mathcal R_{\mathrm{old}}\longrightarrow\mathcal R_{\mathrm{new}},\]

while

relational form

may remain coherent even when the individual physical realizations differ.

This is the appropriate standard for the de Sitter–Nariai comparison.

4. Pure de Sitter Provides a Reference Horizon Calibration

For pure de Sitter space,

\[M=0.\]

The cosmological horizon radius is

\[r_{\mathrm{dS}}=\sqrt{\frac3\Lambda}.\]

Its spherical boundary area is

\[A_{\mathrm{dS}}=4\pi r_{\mathrm{dS}}^2.\]

Therefore,

\[A_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{12\pi}{\Lambda}.\]

5. The de Sitter Horizon Multiplicity Coordinate

Using the horizon multiplicity calibration,

\[S=\frac A{4\ell_P^2},\]

we obtain

\[S_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{12\pi/\Lambda}{4\ell_P^2}.\]

Thus,

\[S_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{3\pi}{\Lambda\ell_P^2}.\]

And therefore,

\[W_{\mathrm{dS}}=\exp\!\left(\frac{3\pi}{\Lambda\ell_P^2}\right).\]

6. The de Sitter Horizon Has a Thermal Phase-Clock Read

Define the de Sitter Hubble scale

\[H_{\mathrm{dS}}=c\sqrt{\frac\Lambda3}.\]

The Gibbons–Hawking thermal phase-frequency read is

\[\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{H_{\mathrm{dS}}}{2\pi}.\]

Therefore,

\[\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{c}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac\Lambda3}.\]

This is a physical frequency calibration.

It is not identified with primitive R²D recurrence.

7. Radius and Clock Are Reciprocal in the de Sitter Calibration

Using

\[r_{\mathrm{dS}}=\sqrt{\frac3\Lambda},\]

we obtain

\[\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{c}{2\pi r_{\mathrm{dS}}}.\]

Equivalently,

\[2\pi r_{\mathrm{dS}}\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}=c.\]

This is the physical cycle–phase calibration of the de Sitter horizon.

8. The Replacement Nariai Horizon Has a Different Radius

At Nariai,

\[r_N=\frac1{\sqrt\Lambda}.\]

Therefore,

\[\frac{r_N}{r_{\mathrm{dS}}}=\frac{1/\sqrt\Lambda}{\sqrt{3/\Lambda}}=\frac1{\sqrt3}.\]

Hence,

\[r_N=\frac{r_{\mathrm{dS}}}{\sqrt3}.\]

The replacement horizon is smaller by a factor of 3.

9. Its Boundary Area Is Smaller by a Factor of Three

Since

\[A=4\pi r^2,\]

we obtain

\[A_N=4\pi r_N^2=\frac{4\pi}{\Lambda}.\]

Thus,

\[A_N=\frac13A_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

This is a change in physical boundary area.

10. Its Multiplicity Coordinate Changes by the Same Factor

Using

\[S=\frac A{4\ell_P^2},\]

we obtain

\[S_N=\frac\pi{\Lambda\ell_P^2}.\]

Therefore,

\[S_N=\frac13S_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

The replacement boundary does not preserve the old horizon count.

11. Its Physical Clock Changes in the Opposite Direction

Addendum 46 established the boundary-appropriate Nariai phase-frequency read

\[\omega_N=\frac{c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi}.\]

Compare this with

\[\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{c}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac\Lambda3}.\]

Therefore,

\[\omega_N=\sqrt3\,\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

The smaller replacement boundary carries the faster physical clock calibration.

12. Neither Coordinate Is Preserved

We have

\[S_N\ne S_{\mathrm{dS}},\]

and

\[\omega_N\ne\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

More specifically,

\[S_N=\frac13S_{\mathrm{dS}},\]

while

\[\omega_N=\sqrt3\,\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

Thus neither horizon multiplicity nor horizon phase frequency is individually invariant.

13. Their Changes Are Reciprocal

Square the frequency relation:

\[\omega_N^2=3\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}^2.\]

\[\omega_N=\sqrt3\,\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

Multiply by

\[S_N=\frac13S_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

Then

\[S_N\omega_N^2=\frac13S_{\mathrm{dS}}(3\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}^2).\]

Therefore,

\[S_N\omega_N^2=S_{\mathrm{dS}}\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}^2.\]

A relational quantity survives even though its individual physical coordinates do not.

14. Evaluate the Shared Relation Explicitly

For de Sitter,

\[S_{\mathrm{dS}}=\frac{3\pi}{\Lambda\ell_P^2},\]

and

\[\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}^2=\frac{c^2\Lambda}{12\pi^2},\]

Therefore,

\[S_{\mathrm{dS}}\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}^2=\frac{3\pi}{\Lambda\ell_P^2}\frac{c^2\Lambda}{12\pi^2}.\]

Thus,

\[S_{\mathrm{dS}}\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}^2=\frac{c^2}{4\pi\ell_P^2}.\]

15. The Nariai Horizon Gives the Same Result

For Nariai,

\[S_N=\frac\pi{\Lambda\ell_P^2},\]

and

\[\omega_N^2=\frac{c^2\Lambda}{4\pi^2},\]

Hence,

\[S_N\omega_N^2=\frac\pi{\Lambda\ell_P^2}\frac{c^2\Lambda}{4\pi^2}.\]

Therefore,

\[S_N\omega_N^2=\frac{c^2}{4\pi\ell_P^2}.\]

The two boundary realizations share exactly the same count–clock relation.

16. Remove the Planck-Length Calibration

Using

\[\ell_P^2=\frac{G\hbar}{c^3},\]

we obtain

\[\frac{c^2}{4\pi\ell_P^2}=\frac{c^5}{4\pi G\hbar}.\]

Therefore,

\[S\omega^2=\frac{c^5}{4\pi G\hbar}.\]

This is the common dimensional form of the calibration relation.

17. Define a Dimensionless Gravitational Clock Coupling

Define

\[\alpha_G^\omega\equiv\frac{G\hbar\omega^2}{c^5}.\]

The quantity is dimensionless.

Then the shared count–clock relation becomes

\[4\pi\alpha_G^\omega S=1.\]

Thus both pure de Sitter and Nariai satisfy

\[4\pi\alpha_{G,\mathrm{dS}}^\omega S_{\mathrm{dS}}=1,\]

and

\[4\pi\alpha_{G,N}^\omega S_N=1.\]

18. The Coupling Changes While the Product Does Not

Because

\[\omega_N^2=3\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}^2,\]

we have

\[\alpha_{G,N}^\omega=3\alpha_{G,\mathrm{dS}}^\omega.\]

At the same time,

\[S_N=\frac13S_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

Therefore,

\[\alpha_G^\omega\propto S^{-1}.\]

across these two horizon calibrations.

The dimensionless product remains fixed.

19. This Is Not Conservation of Entropy

The relation

\[S_N=\frac13S_{\mathrm{dS}}\]

already proves that horizon entropy is not conserved under this comparison.

Nor is the summed entropy of the two equal Nariai horizon components equal to the original pure de Sitter entropy.

The invariant is therefore not total entropy conservation.

It is a relation among boundary-calibrated quantities.

20. This Is Not Conservation of Frequency

Likewise,

\[\omega_N=\sqrt3\,\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

Frequency changes.

Thus no physical clock value is being transported unchanged from one landscape to the other.

21. Nor Is It Conservation of Radius or Area

We also have

\[r_N=\frac{r_{\mathrm{dS}}}{\sqrt3},\]

and

\[A_N=\frac13A_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

The physical geometry changes.

Therefore the shared relation is not hidden conservation of a dimensional spatial coordinate.

22. The Invariant Comes From the Joint Scaling Structure

For the two calibrated spherical horizons,

\[S\propto r^2,\]

while

\[\omega\propto r^{-1}.\]

Therefore,

\[\omega^2\propto r^{-2}.\]

Hence,

\[S\omega^2\propto r^2r^{-2}=1.\]

The cancellation is a direct consequence of the opposed scaling of boundary count and physical clock.

23. Boundary Projects Onto Area

Addenda 41–43 established the relevant gravitational hierarchy:

\[\text{boundary}\longrightarrow A.\]

Under spherical projection,

\[A=4\pi r^2.\]

The horizon multiplicity calibration gives

\[S=\frac A{4\ell_P^2}.\]

Therefore,

\[S\propto r^2.\]

The quadratic count scaling comes from the area projection of the boundary.

24. The Physical Clock Is Reciprocal to the Boundary Scale

For both boundary calibrations considered here,

\[\omega=\frac c{2\pi r}.\]

Therefore,

\[\omega\propto r^{-1}.\]

The physical clock scales inversely with the boundary radius.

25. The Product Removes the Physical Radius

Combining

\[S=\frac{\pi r^2}{\ell_P^2},\]

with

\[\omega=\frac c{2\pi r},\]

gives

\[S\omega^2=\frac{\pi r^2}{\ell_P^2}\frac{c^2}{4\pi^2r^2}.\]

The physical radius cancels:

\[S\omega^2=\frac{c^2}{4\pi\ell_P^2}.\]

Thus the relation removes the particular gravitational scale at which the boundary is physically realized.

26. This Is Not Entropy as Circumference

Nothing in the relation requires

\[S\propto r.\]

Nothing requires entropy to be circumferential.

The horizon multiplicity coordinate satisfies

\[S\propto r^2\]

because the boundary is physically projected through area.

The phase clock satisfies

\[\omega\propto r^{-1}.\]

The invariant product therefore arises from

\[r^2\times r^{-2}.\]

No circumferential entropy coordinate is introduced.

27. Nor Is This a Primitive \(\lambda\)–\(\tau\) Relation

The equation

\[S\omega^2=\frac{c^5}{4\pi G\hbar}.\]

is written entirely in physically calibrated quantities.

It does not imply a primitive R²D relation such as

\[\tau=f(\lambda).\]

It does not establish

\[\frac{d\tau}{d\lambda}.\]

And it does not identify physical phase frequency with the primitive occurrence coordinate.

The relation remains a physical calibration relation.

28. The Algebra Alone Does Not Establish a New Fundamental Law

Whenever both

\[S=\frac{\pi r^2}{\ell_P^2}\]

and

\[\omega=\frac c{2\pi r}\]

hold, the relation

\[4\pi\alpha_G^\omega S=1\]

follows algebraically.

Therefore this addendum does not claim that R²D independently derives the relation from primitive counting.

The novelty is not the cancellation itself.

The novelty is that distinct gravitational boundary realizations reproduce the same relation.

29. The Result Is Therefore a Calibration-Class Invariant

Define

\[I_G\equiv4\pi\alpha_G^\omega S.\]

For pure de Sitter,

\[I_{G,\mathrm{dS}}=1.\]

For the replacement Nariai horizon,

\[I_{G,N}=1.\]

Thus the two physical landscapes belong to the same count–clock calibration class.

30. This Is Replacement Coherence, Not Transport

The correct R²D statement is not

\(I_G\) was physically carried from de Sitter into Nariai.

No such transport has been demonstrated.

The correct statement is

two different boundary realizations instantiate the same relation.

This is relational coherence.

31. Pure de Sitter Is Not the State Immediately Before Nariai

The Schwarzschild–de Sitter family contains a continuous range of source masses between

\[M=0\]

and

\[M=M_N.\]

Pure de Sitter is the zero-mass endpoint.

Nariai is the maximum-mass endpoint.

Therefore,

\[\text{de Sitter}\ne\text{immediate pre-Nariai state}.\]

The comparison in this addendum is between two distinguished horizon calibrations within the same Λ- defined gravitational family.

32. The Relation Is Not Claimed to Be Constant Along the Full Family

The present result does not assert

\[\frac{d}{dM}(S_h\omega_h^2)=0.\]

For generic intermediate Schwarzschild–de Sitter states, the horizon clock normalizations are more complicated.

The simple relation

\[\omega=\frac c{2\pi r}\]

does not serve as the same horizon-clock formula for every member of the two-horizon family.

Thus the invariant identified here is not a continuous first integral in M .

33. The Replacement Statement Is Local to Nariai

As the ordinary two-horizon landscape approaches Nariai,

\[S_b,S_c\longrightarrow S_N.\]

Under the boundary-appropriate normalization,

\[\omega_b^{\mathrm{BH}},\omega_c^{\mathrm{BH}}\longrightarrow\omega_N.\]

The limiting landscape therefore possesses the definite pair

\[(S_N,\omega_N).\]

The replacement boundary is physically well calibrated even though the old static clock has disappeared.

34. The de Sitter Comparison Identifies the Shared Class

Pure de Sitter supplies the independent pair

\[(S_{\mathrm{dS}},\omega_{\mathrm{dS}}).\]

The two pairs differ:

\[(S_{\mathrm{dS}},\omega_{\mathrm{dS}})\ne(S_N,\omega_N).\quad\text{[Source glyph after relation clipped; author review required.]}\]

But both satisfy

\[I_G=1.\]

Thus the replacement landscape has changed physical coordinates without leaving the common count– clock calibration class.

35. This Is the R²D Pattern of Relation Without Identity

The two landscapes differ in:

• horizon radius; • horizon area; • horizon multiplicity; • physical phase frequency; • temperature; • and boundary classification.

Yet they share

\[4\pi\alpha_G^\omega S=1.\]

Therefore,

identity is replaced; relation remains coherent.

This is precisely the kind of structure recursive replacement is expected to preserve.

36. The Result Mirrors the General R²D Replacement Architecture

The generic R²D pattern is

subordinate realization \(\longrightarrow\) recursive reclassification \(\longrightarrow\) new count meaning.

The constituent representation need not survive.

What can survive is a relation among the newly classified counts.

The de Sitter–Nariai comparison supplies a physical example in which:

\[S\]

changes,

\[\omega\]

changes,

but

\[\alpha_G^\omega S\]

does not.

37. This Does Not Mean the Two Boundaries Are the Same Boundary

The common calibration does not imply

\[B_{\mathrm{dS}}=B_N.\]

The physical geometries differ.

Their multiplicities differ.

Their clocks differ.

Therefore the correct statement is

\[B_{\mathrm{dS}}\ne B_N,\]

while

\[I_G(B_{\mathrm{dS}})=I_G(B_N).\]

Boundary identity is unnecessary for relational coherence.

38. The Invariant Is Also Not an Energy Conservation Law

The relation

\[4\pi\alpha_G^\omega S=1\]

does not represent transport of energy.

No conserved energetic substance is being transferred from the de Sitter horizon into the Nariai horizon.

The relation is a compatibility of physical calibrations.

Thus:

replacement coherence \(\ne\) energy conservation. [PDF line clipped; author review required.]

39. Nor Is It a Statement of Total Horizon Entropy Conservation

At Nariai there are two equal horizon components.

If one defines a summed horizon entropy,

\[S_{\mathrm{tot},N}=2S_N,\]

then

\[S_{\mathrm{tot},N}=\frac23S_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

Therefore,

\[S_{\mathrm{tot},N}\ne S_{\mathrm{dS}}.\]

The count–clock invariant applies to the calibrated individual horizon relation considered here.

It is not a statement of conserved total entropy.

40. The Factor of Two Between Nariai Horizons Does Not Alter the Calibration Result

At Nariai the two limiting horizon branches possess equal area and equal multiplicity coordinate:
