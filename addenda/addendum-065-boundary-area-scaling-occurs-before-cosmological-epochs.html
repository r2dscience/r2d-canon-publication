Addendum 65 — Boundary-Area Scaling Occurs Before Cosmological Epochs

Partition Crossovers, Equality Areas, and the Physical Origin of Radiation-, Matter-, Curvature-, and Λ-Dominated Regimes

Abstract

Addenda 59–64 reconstructed cosmological dynamics in a layered R2D order.

Primitive directional asymmetry occurs before structural realization.

Recursive throughput occurs before physical rate.

Clock and geometric calibration produce the Hubble rate

\[ H\]

The first Friedmann equation then partitions the physical rate magnitude:

\[ H^2=F_\varepsilon+F_k+F_\Lambda. \]

Addenda 61–63 deprojected the individual terms.

Matter-energy is a source-energy read divided by physical boundary support.

Spatial curvature is an inverse-area geometric read of the evolving cosmological state.

A constant positive Λ is a fixed reference-geometric contribution.

Addendum 64 then showed that evolution of these already-defined physical reads produces the cosmological acceleration equation.

The next question is therefore:

Why does one cosmological history appear as successive radiation-, matter-, curvature-, and \(\Lambda\)-dominated epochs?

R2D does not require a different primitive law for each epoch.

Nor does a change of dominant Friedmann contribution necessarily imply recursive boundary replacement.

The distinction follows from the different ways in which downstream physical reads scale with the same evolving cosmological boundary area.

For a separately conserved homogeneous source component j with constant equation-of-state parameter

\[ p_j=w_j\varepsilon_j, \]

source continuity gives

\[ \varepsilon_j\propto a^{-3(1+w_j)}. \]

Since the cosmological boundary-area read satisfies

\[ A_\zeta\propto a^2, \]

the corresponding Friedmann contribution obeys

\[ F_j(A_\zeta)=B_j A_\zeta^{-s_j}, \]

with

\[ s_j=\frac{3}{2}(1+w_j). \]

Thus radiation has

\[ s_r=2, \]

pressureless matter has

\[ s_m=\frac32, \]

spatial curvature has the geometric scaling

\[ s_k=1, \]

and constant Λ has

\[ s_\Lambda=0. \]

The familiar hierarchy is therefore

\[2>\frac32>1>0\]

On an expanding branch,

Ȧζ > 0,

so contributions with larger scaling exponent decrease more rapidly.

Radiation therefore falls relative to matter.

Matter falls relative to curvature magnitude.

Matter and curvature both fall relative to a constant Λ contribution.

The cosmological history is consequently a history of changing relative weights within one physical rate partition.

Define a reference area

\[ A_0\]

and write

\[ F_j(A)=F_{j,0}\left(\frac{A}{A_0}\right)^{-s_j}\]

For two positive contributions i and j , their equality occurs at

\[ A_{i\mid j}=A_0\left(\frac{F_{i,0}}{F_{j,0}}\right)^{1/(s_i-s_j)}. \]

This defines an equality area.

Matter-radiation equality, matter-Λ equality, and analogous crossovers are therefore not intrinsically new count boundaries.

They are values of the same cosmological boundary area at which two downstream physical contributions to \(H^2\) become equal.

The normalized partition weights can be defined by

\[ \Omega_j=\frac{F_j}{H^2}, \]

with

\[\sum_j\Omega_j=1\]

For fixed area-scaling exponents,

dFj

\[\frac{dF_j}{d\ln A_\zeta}=-s_jF_j\]

Define the signed partition-weighted scaling exponent

\[\bar s=\sum_\ell s_\ell\Omega_\ell\]

Then the normalized contributions obey

\[\frac{d\Omega_j}{d\ln A_\zeta}=\Omega_j(\bar s-s_j)\]

This is a compact physical equation for the evolution of the Friedmann partition.

On an expanding branch, positive contributions with

\[ s_j > \bar s\]

lose relative weight, while those with

\[ s_j < \bar s\]

gain relative weight.

The equation is not a new primitive R2D law.

It is the downstream consequence of one evolving boundary area acting on physical reads that carry different scaling exponents.

This also separates several transitions that are often conflated.

Partition equality,

\[ F_i = F_j\]

is not the same as cosmological acceleration onset,

\[\ddot a = 0\]

which is not the same as a turning point,

\[ H = 0\]

and none is automatically primitive R2D stall,

\[ A_\zeta = 0\]

For pressureless matter plus positive constant Λ, matter-Λ equality occurs when

\[\epsilon_m = \epsilon_\Lambda\]

Acceleration begins earlier, when

\[\epsilon_m = 2\epsilon_\Lambda\]

Because

\[\epsilon_m\propto A_\zeta^{-3/2}\]

the acceleration-threshold area satisfies

\[ A_{\mathrm{acc}}=2^{-2/3}A_{m\mid\Lambda}\]

Thus accelerated expansion begins before the Λ contribution becomes larger than the matter contribution to \(H^2\).

Dominance and acceleration are different physical statements.

The central result is therefore:

cosmological epochs are not successive primitive laws or necessarily successive recursive boundaries. They are regimes in which different physical projections of one cosmological boundary dominate the Friedmann rate partition because those projections scale differently with the evolving boundary area. Equality epochs are therefore equality areas within one projected history.

And hence:

boundary-area scaling occurs before cosmological epochs.

1. Cosmological History Begins With One Evolving Boundary

Let

\[ B_\zeta\]

denote the cosmological boundary under reconstruction.

Its structural state changes within the same boundary:

\[ i_\zeta^\ast \to k_\zeta^\ast \to \cdots\]

and its physical boundary-area read changes correspondingly:

\[ A_{\zeta,1} \to A_{\zeta,2} \to \cdots\]

Nothing in this sequence requires recursive replacement of Bζ .

2. The Friedmann Partition Evolves on That Boundary

The projected physical rate satisfies

\[ H^2=\sum_j F_j\]

The relative magnitude of the terms can change as

\[ A_\zeta\]

changes.

Thus the physical interpretation of the history can change while the underlying boundary ownership remains the same.

3. Define a Projected Dominance Regime

A physical contribution j dominates over a range of area when

\[|F_j|\gg|F_\ell|\]

for the other dynamically relevant terms \(\ell\).

A cosmological “epoch” can therefore be defined as a dominance regime of the projected rate partition.

4. Epoch Does Not Mean Primitive Ontology

A radiation-dominated regime does not imply that the primitive universe has become “radiation.”

A matter-dominated regime does not imply that a new primitive matter law has replaced it.

A Λ-dominated regime does not imply that terminal R2D causality has become Λ.

The names identify which downstream physical read dominates the projected rate.

5. General Source Scaling Comes From Source Continuity

For a separately conserved homogeneous source component,

pj = wj εj .

The continuity equation is

\[\dot\epsilon_j+3H(\epsilon_j+p_j)=0\]

6. Substitute the Equation of State

Then

\[\dot\epsilon_j+3H(1+w_j)\epsilon_j=0\]

Therefore:

\[\epsilon_j\propto a^{-3(1+w_j)}\]

7. Replace Scale Factor With Boundary Area

Because

\[ A_\zeta\propto a^2,\qquad a\propto A_\zeta^{1/2}\]

Thus:

\[\epsilon_j\propto A_\zeta^{-\frac32(1+w_j)}\]

8. Define the Area-Scaling Exponent

Let

\[ s_j\equiv\frac32(1+w_j)\]

Then:

\[ F_j\propto A_\zeta^{-s_j}\]

For ordinary positive-energy source terms, the gravitational prefactor does not change the area exponent.

9. Radiation Has sr = 2

For radiation,

\[ w_r=\frac13\]

Therefore:

\[ s_r=\frac32\left(1+\frac13\right)=2\]

Hence:

\[ F_r\propto A_\zeta^{-2}\]

10. Matter Has sm = 3/2

For pressureless matter,

\[ w_m = 0\]

Therefore:

\[ s_m=\frac32\]

Thus:

\[ F_m\propto A_\zeta^{-3/2}\]

This is the result already obtained directly from source energy divided by boundary support.

11. Curvature Has Effective Scaling Exponent One

Addendum 62 gave

\[ F_k\propto A_\zeta^{-1}\]

Thus define its geometric area-scaling exponent as

\[ s_k = 1\]

This notation does not make curvature a physical fluid.

12. Constant Λ Has Scaling Exponent Zero

Addendum 63 gave

\[ F_\Lambda=\mathrm{constant}\]

Therefore:

\[ s_\Lambda = 0\]

13. The Standard Scaling Hierarchy Is

\[ s_r>s_m>s_k>s_\Lambda\]

Explicitly,

\[2>\frac32>1>0\]

14. This Hierarchy Is an Area Hierarchy

The familiar scale-factor powers

\[ a^{-4},\quad a^{-3},\quad a^{-2},\quad a^0\]

become

\[ A_\zeta^{-2},\quad A_\zeta^{-3/2},\quad A_\zeta^{-1},\quad A_\zeta^0\]

Boundary area is therefore sufficient to order the sectors.

15. Expansion Makes Large-s Terms Fade Faster

For an expanding branch,

\[\dot A_\zeta > 0\]

If

si > sj ,

then

\[\frac{|F_i|}{|F_j|}\propto A_\zeta^{-(s_i-s_j)}\]

decreases with area.

16. Radiation Falls Relative to Matter

Because

\[ s_r-s_m=\frac12,\qquad\frac{F_r}{F_m}\propto A_\zeta^{-1/2}\]

Thus radiation naturally becomes less important relative to matter as an expanding boundary grows.

17. Matter Falls Relative to Curvature Magnitude

Similarly,

\[ s_m-s_k=\frac12\]

so

\[\frac{F_m}{|F_k|}\propto A_\zeta^{-1/2}\]

Where curvature is dynamically relevant, its magnitude falls more slowly than matter.

18. Matter Falls More Rapidly Relative to Λ

Because

\[ s_m-s_\Lambda=\frac32,\qquad\frac{F_m}{F_\Lambda}\propto A_\zeta^{-3/2}\]

19. Radiation Falls Even More Rapidly Relative to Λ

Because

\[ s_r - s_\Lambda = 2\]

\[\frac{F_r}{F_\Lambda}\propto A_\zeta^{-2}\]

20. Curvature Falls Relative to a Constant Reference Contribution

Since

\[ s_k - s_\Lambda = 1\]

\[\frac{|F_k|}{F_\Lambda}\propto A_\zeta^{-1}\]

21. The Ordering Is Conditional on the Coefficients

Scaling exponents determine how relative contributions change.

They do not determine their absolute amplitudes.

A sector with a negligible coefficient may never dominate over the physical interval being considered.

22. Therefore the Standard Epoch Sequence Is Not Mathematically Mandatory

The exponent ordering permits a typical progression from rapidly diluting sectors toward slowly diluting sectors.

But it does not guarantee a distinct curvature-dominated era, for example.

Physical calibration and initial partition weights matter.

23. Define a Reference-Area Representation

Choose some physical reference state with area

\[ A_0\]

Write:

\[ F_j(A)=F_{j,0}\left(\frac{A}{A_0}\right)^{-s_j}\]

This representation makes crossover areas explicit.

24. Equality of Two Positive Contributions

Suppose i and j are positive contributions with

\[ s_i\ne s_j\]

Their equality condition is

\[ F_i = F_j\]

25. Solve for the Equality Area

Then:

\[ F_{i,0}\left(\frac{A}{A_0}\right)^{-s_i}=F_{j,0}\left(\frac{A}{A_0}\right)^{-s_j}\]

Therefore:

\[ A_{i\mid j}=A_0\left(\frac{F_{i,0}}{F_{j,0}}\right)^{1/(s_i-s_j)}\]

Once a physical clock and cosmological solution

\[ A_\zeta (t)\]

are supplied, one may assign a time

ti∣j

\[ A_{i\mid j}\]

Thus:

\[\text{equality area}\to\text{equality time read}\]

For radiation and matter,

\[ s_r-s_m=\frac12\]

Thus:

\[ A_{r\mid m}=A_0\left(\frac{F_{r,0}}{F_{m,0}}\right)^2\]

28. Nothing Recursively Closes at Matter-Radiation Equality by Definition

The condition is simply

\[ F_r = F_m\]

It does not imply

\[ B_\zeta \to B_{\zeta+1}\]

29. Nor Does It Require a Change in Primitive R2D Law

The same primitive architecture continues to apply on both sides of the crossover.

Only the dominant physical projection changes.

For matter,

\[ s_m=\frac32\]

while

\[ s_\Lambda = 0\]

Therefore:

\[ A_{m\mid\Lambda}=A_0\left(\frac{F_{m,0}}{F_\Lambda}\right)^{2/3}\]

31. The Λ Reference Scale Does Not Change at Equality

At

\[ A_{m\mid\Lambda}\]

the evolving matter contribution has fallen to the fixed Λ contribution.

The reference area

\[ A_\Lambda\]

itself has not changed.

32. Curvature Equality Requires Sign Care

The curvature contribution is

\[ F_k=-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}\]

Thus its sign depends on the curvature class.

A generic “curvature dominance” statement should therefore distinguish magnitude from signed contribution.

33. For Crossover Magnitudes Use

\[|F_k|\]

For example,

\[|F_k|=F_\Lambda\]

defines a curvature-Λ magnitude-equality area.

When

\[ k = +1\]

\[ F_k < 0\]

A negative curvature contribution can partially cancel positive source terms.

Thus the relevant event may be a rate reduction or turning point rather than a positive-sector dominance regime.

35. A Turning Point Is Defined by the Whole Partition

A cosmological turning point satisfies

\[ H = 0\]

Therefore:

\[\sum_jF_j=0\]

This is not the same condition as equality of any arbitrary pair of contributions.

36. Partition Equality Is Not a Turning Point

The condition

\[ F_i = F_j\]

usually occurs while

\[ H^2 > 0\]

Thus:

\[\text{partition equality}\ne\text{turning point}\]

37. Partition Equality Is Also Not R2D Stall

Primitive R2D stall is

\[ A_\zeta = 0\]

No derivation equates this with

\[ F_i = F_j\]

Therefore:

\[\text{equality area}\ne\text{primitive stall}\]

by definition.

38. Nor Is Equality Automatically a Structural Macrostate Transition

A change in which Friedmann term is larger does not necessarily imply that the occupancy mode

\[ i_\zeta^\ast\]

has changed.

The partition and structural-state descriptions belong to different layers.

39. Define Normalized Friedmann Contributions

For

\[ H^2 \ne 0\]

define

Fj

Then:

\[\sum_j\Omega_j=1\]

These are signed physical partition weights.

40. The Ωj Are Not Probabilities

They are not:

\[ W_j/W_{\mathrm{tot}}\]

not occupancies,

and not R2D possibility fractions.

They are normalized contributions to the projected rate constraint.

41. Differentiate One Scaling Sector

For

\[ F_j=B_jA^{-s_j}\]

\[\frac{dF_j}{d\ln A}=-s_jF_j\]

42. Differentiate the Total Rate Magnitude

Since

\[ H^2=\sum_\ell F_\ell\]

\[\frac{dH^2}{d\ln A}=-\sum_\ell s_\ell F_\ell\]

43. Define the Partition-Weighted Scaling Exponent

Let

\[\bar s=\sum_\ell s_\ell\Omega_\ell\]

Then:

\[\frac{d\ln H^2}{d\ln A}=-\bar s\]

The weighted exponent \(\bar s\) is algebraic and need not be a convex average. For signed contributions, s average.

44. Derive the Partition-Flow Equation

Differentiate

\[\Omega_j=\frac{F_j}{H^2}\]

Then:

\[\frac{d\Omega_j}{d\ln A_\zeta}=\Omega_j(\bar s-s_j)\]

45. This Describes the Evolution of Cosmological Dominance

For positive contributions on an expanding branch:

If

\[ s_j > \bar s\]

then

\[\frac{d\Omega_j}{d\ln A}<0\]

Its relative importance falls.

then

Its relative importance grows.

47. Constant Contributions Naturally Gain Relative Weight

For constant Λ,

\[ s_\Lambda = 0\]

While other positive sectors have

\[ s_j > 0\]

the Λ partition fraction tends to grow as an expanding boundary increases in area.

48. Radiation Naturally Loses Relative Weight

Radiation has the largest standard exponent,

\[ s_r = 2\]

It therefore declines most rapidly among the usual positive sectors as area grows.

49. Matter Lies Between Radiation and the Nondiluting Sector

With

\[ s_m=\frac32\]

matter decreases more slowly than radiation but more quickly than curvature magnitude or constant Λ.

Its intermediate dominance is therefore a scaling consequence where amplitudes permit it.

50. The Partition-Flow Equation Is Not a Primitive R2D Evolution Law

It follows only after:

• physical source classes have been defined; • geometric curvature has been projected; • a fixed Λ reference scale has been supplied; • the first Friedmann rate partition has been formed.

Thus it belongs entirely to the mature cosmological projection.

51. Dominance and Acceleration Must Be Separated

Addendum 64 showed that acceleration depends on

\[\epsilon + 3p\]

rather than on which term is simply largest in \(H^2\).

Therefore the onset of accelerated expansion need not coincide with a Friedmann equality event.

52. Consider Pressureless Matter Plus Constant Λ

Matter-Λ equality is

\[\epsilon_m = \epsilon_\Lambda\]

Equivalently,

\[ F_m = F_\Lambda\]

For pressureless matter plus Λ,

Using

\[\epsilon_\Lambda=\frac{\Lambda c^4}{8\pi G}\]

the acceleration threshold is

\[\epsilon_m = 2\epsilon_\Lambda\]

Matter density decreases as

\[\epsilon_m\propto A_\zeta^{-3/2}\]

Let

\[ A_{m\mid\Lambda}\]

denote matter-Λ equality.

Then:

\[\left(\frac{A_{\mathrm{acc}}}{A_{m\mid\Lambda}}\right)^{-3/2}=2\]

\[ A_{\mathrm{acc}}=2^{-2/3}A_{m\mid\Lambda}\]

55. Therefore

\[ A_{\mathrm{acc}}<A_{m\mid\Lambda}\]

on the expanding branch.

Accelerated expansion starts before \(\Lambda\) becomes the larger contribution to \(H^2\).
