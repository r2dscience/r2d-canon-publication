# Addendum 2 — Boundary Mass Occurs Before Relativity

## Landscape, Background, and the Recursive Replacement of Mass

## Abstract

Relativity ordinarily begins with an object that already possesses mass and asks how its
measured energy, momentum, length, and clock rate depend upon relative motion. Recursively
Realized Distinguishability (R²D) requires an earlier question:

                                At what boundary is mass defined?

A physical mass is not a primitive object that retains identical ontological status as the count
boundary changes. A mass belongs to a specified recursively realized boundary. That boundary
possesses a native multiplicity landscape,

$$\mathcal W_n=\{W_{n,i}\}.$$

whose possible-count architecture is defined before any particular realization occurs. The
landscape determines the boundary class to which an orientation-even physical coupling can
belong. The enclosing background does something different. Background is realized
nondirectional count, not part of the native multiplicity landscape. It supplies the recursive scale
of the units counted by that landscape.

Thus two boundaries may possess isomorphic multiplicity landscapes while representing
radically different physical mass scales. An ensemble of binary spins and an ensemble of binary
protein switches may share
$$W_i=\binom{N}{i}.$$
without possessing the same physical mass, energy, or recurrence scale. The counting
architecture is the same; the recursively realized units being counted are not.

The central proposal is therefore:

             the landscape defines mass; the background calibrates its physical size.

Let

$$\mu_n$$

denote the dimensionless orientation-even mass class defined by the native landscape of
boundary $B_n$ , and let

$$m_n^\Phi$$

denote the physical mass scale supplied by the background against which that boundary is read.
Then the boundary-relative physical mass has the form

$$m_n=m_n^\Phi\mu_n.$$

The precise functional map

$$\mathcal W_n\longrightarrow\mu_n$$

and the quantitative map

$$\Phi_n\longrightarrow m_n^\Phi$$

remain physical mapping problems. They are not derived here.

What is established is the architecture required before either map can be attempted.

Mass belongs to the landscape-bearing boundary. Background does not belong to that landscape.
It determines the recursive scale of what one count at that boundary represents. Enclosing
possibility and constraint may then change occupancy, recurrence, and motion without
reconstructing the native landscape.

A moving mass is therefore a turbine only when the mass boundary itself is the selected
boundary. If the atmosphere is selected, the atmosphere is the turbine and molecular masses are
subordinate physical realizers. If the solar system is selected, the solar-system boundary is the
turbine and planetary masses are subordinate physical coordinates. Recursive replacement
changes which mass is ontologically defined.

This addendum establishes the boundary discipline required before Lorentz geometry, $mc^2$,
Newtonian inertia, or gravitational geometry can be interpreted within R²D.

The usual relativistic problem begins with a mass $m$.

momentum, $p$,

proper time, $\tau$,

That ordering already assumes something R²D cannot assume:

                 $m$ exists independently of the count boundary used to define it.

R²D begins earlier.

Before asking how a mass moves, one must ask:

                                  Which boundary is the mass?

Before asking how its ruler transforms:

                                Which boundary owns the ruler?

Before asking how its clock transforms:

                      Which boundary owns the recurrence being clock-read?

When the enclosing relation is understood, the shorter notation

The boundary therefore comes before the coordinate.

The turbine is therefore not a universal object called “mass,” “molecule,” “planet,” or “particle.”

It is:

               the selected boundary whose landscape is being recurrently realized.

These quantities belong entirely to the boundary-defined possible-count structure. Changing the
boundary changes the count domain itself.

The landscape therefore exists logically before any particular trajectory through it.

Occurrence realizes the landscape.

Occupancy describes the realized distribution within it.

Neither creates it.

Therefore,

$$
