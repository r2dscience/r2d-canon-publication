Addendum 71 — Relative Recurrence Occurs Before the Compton Shift

Persistent Mass Closure, Radiative Recurrence, and Orientation-Dependent Recoil

Abstract

The Compton effect is conventionally presented as decisive evidence that electromagnetic radiation behaves as particles carrying energy and momentum.

An incident photon of wavelength λ scatters from an electron through angle θ , producing an outgoing wavelength

\[\lambda'\]

satisfying

\[ \lambda^\prime-\lambda=\frac h{mc}(1-\cos\theta). \]

R2D does not begin with either a photon particle or an inert material electron.

The preceding R2D development has already supplied the two physical boundary relations required by the experiment.

Blackbody radiation established a radiative recurrence with physical reads

\[ E_\gamma=hf\]

and

\[\lambda=\frac{c}{f}\]

These are later energetic and spatial calibrations of recurrent radiative count, not primitive photon ontology.

The gravitational/mass development independently introduced a persistent local mass-turbine closure class. Before physical clock calibration, its recurrence is a count relation. After clock calibration it acquires a phase-frequency read ωM , with the physical rest-energy calibration

\[ mc^2=\hbar\omega_M\]

The same recurrence has the reduced Compton spatial read

\[\bar\lambda_M=\frac{c}{\omega_M}=\frac{\hbar}{mc}\]

Thus persistent mass recurrence occurs before rest-energy and Compton coordinates.

For Compton scattering it is useful to express the same mass recurrence in cycle rather than phase units:

\[ f_M=\frac{\omega_M}{2\pi}=\frac{mc^2}{h}\]

Its corresponding full recurrence length is

\[\lambda_M=\frac{c}{f_M}=\frac{h}{mc}=2\pi\bar\lambda_M\]

The quantity conventionally called the Compton wavelength is therefore not interpreted in R2D as the physical radius or spatial size of the massive object.

It is the physical spatial place value of the persistent recurrence native to the retained mass boundary.

Compton scattering then places two different physical recurrence reads into one interaction boundary:

free radiative recurrence

and

persistent retained mass recurrence.

Let the incoming radiative recurrence have frequency and orientation

\[(f,\hat{\mathbf n})\]

and the outgoing recurrence

\[(f',\hat{\mathbf n}')\]

Define

\[\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}'=\cos\theta\]

At the mature Lorentz projection, radiative energy and momentum are

\[ E_\gamma=hf,\qquad\mathbf p_\gamma=\frac{hf}{c}\hat{\mathbf n}\]

and after scattering,

hf ′ ′

\[ E_\gamma'=hf',\qquad\mathbf p_\gamma'=\frac{hf'}{c}\hat{\mathbf n}'\]

If the massive boundary is initially at translational rest relative to the chosen enclosing background, the recoil momentum read is

\[\mathbf p_R=\frac{h}{c}(f\hat{\mathbf n}-f'\hat{\mathbf n}')\]

Therefore,

\[ p_R^2=\frac{h^2}{c^2}(f^2+f'^2-2ff'\cos\theta)\]

The persistent mass coupling is unchanged by the scattering event.

Its local mass closure remains the same recurrence class.

What changes is the translational physical state of the enclosing massive boundary.

Its final relativistic energy therefore satisfies

\[ E_R^2=m^2c^4+p_R^2c^2\]

Physical energy compatibility across the interaction boundary gives

\[ mc^2+hf=E_R+hf'\]

Eliminating ER and pR gives

\[ mc^2(f-f^\prime)=hff^\prime(1-\cos\theta). \]

Now substitute

\[ f_M=\frac{mc^2}{h}. \]

The Compton relation becomes

\[ f_M\left(\frac1{f^\prime}-\frac1f\right)=1-\cos\theta. \]

Using

\[ \lambda=\frac cf,\qquad\lambda^\prime=\frac c{f^\prime},\qquad\lambda_M=\frac c{f_M}, \]

gives

\[ \lambda^\prime-\lambda=\lambda_M(1-\cos\theta). \]

Thus:

\[ \frac{\lambda^\prime-\lambda}{\lambda_M}=1-\cos\theta. \]

The Compton shift factorizes into two physical coordinates:

mass-recurrence ruler × orientation change.

Since

\[1-\cos\theta=\frac12\left|\hat{\mathbf n}-\hat{\mathbf n}'\right|^2\]

the same result can be written

\[\frac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda_M}=\frac12\left|\hat{\mathbf n}-\hat{\mathbf n}'\right|^2\]

This is the central physical decomposition.

The spatial Compton scale does not arise because the electron possesses a primitive physical diameter.

It is the downstream spatial read of persistent mass recurrence.

The angular factor does not arise from the size of the scatterer.

It is the orientation difference between the incoming and outgoing radiative recurrence classes.

The law becomes even more primitive when all dimensional place values are removed.

Define the incoming radiative recurrence relative to the mass recurrence:

\[\chi\equiv\frac{f}{f_M}\]

Likewise,

\[\chi'\equiv\frac{f'}{f_M}\]

Because

\[ f_M=\frac{mc^2}{h}\]

one has

\[\chi=\frac{hf}{mc^2}\]

The Compton relation becomes

\[\frac1{\chi'}-\frac1\chi=1-\cos\theta\]

Solving for the outgoing relative recurrence gives

\[\chi'=\frac{\chi}{1+\chi(1-\cos\theta)}\]

This is the dimensionless R2D form of Compton scattering.

It contains only:

  1. radiative recurrence relative to persistent mass recurrence; / 2. orientation change.

The conventional classical and quantum regimes then become recurrence-separation regimes.

If

\[\chi \ll 1\]

then

\[ f\ll f_M\]

The incoming radiative recurrence is much slower than the persistent mass recurrence.

Then

\[\frac{f'}f=\frac1{1+\chi(1-\cos\theta)}\approx1\]

The scattering is approximately elastic.

This is the Thomson regime.

R2D therefore reads the classical Thomson limit as strong recursive recurrence separation:

Thomson regime = \(f\ll f_M\).

As

\[\chi\]

approaches order unity,

\[ f\sim f_M\]

or equivalently,

\[ hf \sim mc^2\]

the two physical recurrence place values become comparable.

The wavelength shift becomes strongly distinguishable.

The Compton regime is therefore not selected by a universal primitive quantum length.

It is selected by the relative recurrence of the interacting boundaries.

The actual dimensionless interaction coordinate is

\[\chi(1-\cos\theta)\]

Strong recurrence reclassification occurs when

\[\chi(1-\cos\theta)=O(1)\]

Thus both recurrence scale and orientation matter.

The experiment also produces an exact turbine partition.

The outgoing radiative fraction is

\[ q_{\mathrm{rad}}\equiv\frac{f'}f=\frac1{1+\chi(1-\cos\theta)}\]

Physical energy conservation gives recoil kinetic energy

\[ K_R=h(f-f')\]

Therefore define

KR χ(1 − cos θ)

\[ q_{\mathrm{recoil}}=\frac{\chi(1-\cos\theta)}{1+\chi(1-\cos\theta)}\]

Then:

\[ q_{\mathrm{rad}}+q_{\mathrm{recoil}}=1\]

The incoming radiative physical read is partitioned between continued radiative recurrence and mechanical recoil of the retained mass boundary.

The persistent mass recurrence itself is not consumed.

The rest-mass boundary remains in the same mass-coupling class.

The scattering changes its translational relation to the enclosing background.

Compton scattering therefore exposes, in one exact physical experiment, the interaction of the two R2D limits previously developed separately:

unretained radiative recurrence

and

retained persistent mass recurrence.

The central statement is:

the Compton shift is not primitively a collision between a photon particle and a material electron. It is the physical compatibility relation between free radiative recurrence and persistent retained mass recurrence at one enclosing interaction boundary. The mass recurrence supplies the Compton ruler \(\lambda_M=h/(mc)\); the orientation change supplies \(1-\cos\theta\); and the exact law is their product. In dimensionless form, the outgoing recurrence depends only on the incoming recurrence relative to the mass turbine and on the > orientation change.

And hence:

relative recurrence occurs before the Compton shift.
