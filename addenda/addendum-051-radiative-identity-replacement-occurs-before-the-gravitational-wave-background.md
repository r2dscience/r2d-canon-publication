# Addendum 51 — Radiative Identity Replacement Occurs Before the Gravitational-Wave Background

## Abstract

Addendum 50 identified radiation as a boundary-relative count status.

A subordinate gravitational source relation becomes radiative when changing relational organization is
recursively reclassified at an enclosing boundary as a readable occurrence,

$$\chi_R$$

Radiation is therefore not primitive source energy escaping into space.

It is the physical wave read of an enclosing recurrent occurrence.

Recursive replacement does not stop there.

A radiative boundary can distinguish individual gravitational occurrences by labels such as

> source,      ϕ,        n,    p,        f,

representing source identity, phase, propagation direction, polarization, and recurrence class.

At a still more enclosing boundary, some of those labels may cease to define distinct macrostates.

The realization remains.

Its count meaning changes.

Let

$$B_R$$

denote a gravitational radiative boundary and

$$B_B$$

a more enclosing background boundary.

Define

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1752 -->

$$\pi_{B\mid R}:\mathcal M_R\to\mathcal M_B.$$

Two radiative occurrences are background-equivalent when

$$\pi_{B\mid R}(\chi_R^{(\alpha)})=\pi_{B\mid R}(\chi_R^{(\gamma)}).$$

The distinction separating them at BR is then no longer owned by BB .

If several radiative occurrence classes map to one enclosing background class β , their realized occurrence
counts combine:

$$b_{B,\beta}=\sum_{\alpha\in\pi_{B\mid R}^{-1}(\beta)}N_\alpha.$$

The logarithmic background coordinate is

$$\Phi_{B,\beta}=\ln b_{B,\beta}.$$

Thus background formation is not count destruction.

It is not necessarily dissipation.

It is not necessarily thermalization.

It is not necessarily loss of recurrence.

It is not defined by low amplitude, large source number, long propagation distance, or cosmological
redshift.

It is a many-to-one change in the count domain:

> distinguishable radiative occurrences → background degeneracy.

Crucially, background formation does not require every radiative distinction to disappear.

A map such as

$$(s,\phi,\mathbf n,p,f)\longmapsto f$$

can replace individual source, phase, direction, and polarization identity while retaining recurrence class.

The resulting background is therefore spectrally resolved.

Likewise,

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1753 -->

$$(s,\phi,\mathbf n,p,f)\longmapsto(N,f)$$

can produce an anisotropic frequency-resolved background.

Thus:

> background $\ne$ featureless.

The same distinction is already visible in blackbody radiation.

Individual electromagnetic emission events need not remain readable at a thermal boundary, while
frequency class and occupancy remain measurable.

Blackbody radiation therefore demonstrates that

> occurrence identity can disappear while recurrence survives.

The gravitational-wave background realizes the same recursive architecture.

The complete gravitational sequence now contains two successive reclassifications:

$$\mathcal M_S\xrightarrow{\pi_{R\mid S}}\mathcal M_R\xrightarrow{\pi_{B\mid R}}\mathcal M_B$$

In words,

> source organization → radiative occurrence → background realization.

The first bridge determines which source distinctions survive as radiation.

The second determines which radiative distinctions survive as structure within background.

Conventional stochastic gravitational-wave observables such as

$$S_h(f),\quad h_c(f),\quad\Omega_{\mathrm{GW}}(f)$$

come later.

They are statistical, strain, and energetic projections of a background already defined by recursive count
replacement.

They are not the primitive background itself.

Nor does the word stochastic imply primitive randomness.

Statistical description becomes natural after individual occurrence labels have been replaced.
