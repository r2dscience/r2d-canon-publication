# Addendum 40 — Keplerian Closure Occurs Before Gravitational Force

## Abstract

Newtonian gravity is conventionally introduced through the force law

$$\mathbf F=-\frac{GMm}{r^3}\mathbf r.$$

The resulting equations of motion then produce the orbital relations discovered empirically by Kepler.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) permits the opposite explanatory order.

Addenda 35–39 established the relativistic and gravitational reference architecture before returning to the
planetary limit.

Addendum 35 showed that motion is defined relative to an enclosing rapidity reference rather than owned
intrinsically by the moving boundary.

Addendum 37 showed that free fall is zero differential proper acceleration relative to the immediate
gravitational background even while motion relative to a larger reference changes.

Addendum 38 identified differential free-fall motion across neighboring peers as the observable precursor
to curvature.

Addendum 39 then obtained the Einstein equation as the minimal covariant compatibility relation within
the specified metric projection class.

The planetary problem occupies a simpler readability regime. Orbital geometry, recurrence, and scale are
simultaneously observable. Kepler's laws therefore provide a direct empirical window into the weak-field
gravitational closure before force, field, potential, or curved spacetime need be introduced.

For a bound planar orbit, Kepler I supplies the closure geometry

$$r(\theta)=\frac{p}{1+e\cos\theta},$$

while Kepler II supplies constant areal recurrence,

$$\frac{dA_K}{dt}=\text{constant}.$$

Define

$$J_K\equiv r^2\dot\theta=2\frac{dA_K}{dt}.$$

Kepler II gives

$$J_K=\text{constant}.$$

This removes the transverse component of the relative coordinate acceleration.

Writing

$$u(\theta)=\frac1r,$$

the Kepler ellipse satisfies

$$u''+u=\frac1p.$$

Combining this closure geometry with constant areal recurrence gives

$$\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}=-\mu_G\frac{\mathbf r}{r^3},$$

$$\mu_G=\frac{J_K^2}{p}.$$

Thus the inverse-square relative acceleration law is already contained in elliptical closure plus areal
recurrence.

No gravitational force law has been assumed.

Kepler III then gives

$$\mu_G=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{T^2},$$

showing that μG is shared by a family of orbital closures belonging to the same gravitational relation.

The orbiting body's mass does not appear.

The gravitational relation therefore organizes the free-fall motion before local mass is used to construct a
Newtonian force coordinate.

Within the new R²D architecture, the quantity
