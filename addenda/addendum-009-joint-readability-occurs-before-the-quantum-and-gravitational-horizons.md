# Addendum 9 — Joint Readability Occurs Before the Quantum and Gravitational Horizons

## Biological Deprojection and the Complementary Loss of Distinction and Recurrence

## Abstract

Addenda 4–8 established recursive replacement in a regime in which several neighboring physical
boundaries can be interrogated directly.

In muscle, the enclosing mechanical state, the myosin-switch distribution, the individual switch states, and
faster subordinate dynamics can be experimentally separated.

Addendum 4 showed that the spectroscopically resolved myosin-switch asymmetry is organized by
chemical possibility and mechanical retention:

$A_M=P_M-R_M$.

Addendum 5 showed that individual switch identities are replaced by ensemble occupancy:

$$\pi_N:\{M_1,M_2\}^{N}\longrightarrow\{0,1,\ldots,N\},$$

with multiplicity

$$W_N(i)=\left|\pi_N^{-1}(i)\right|.$$

Addendum 6 generalized this result across biological scales:

$$\Gamma_n\longrightarrow\mathcal C_n\overset{\kappa_n}{\longmapsto}B_{n+1}.$$

A subordinate trajectory is physically defined at its own boundary, closes there, and is replaced before the
enclosing boundary acquires its own native state and recurrence.

Addendum 7 then showed that experimental readability is itself boundary relative. The physical system can
remain muscle while the dynamically read boundary changes with perturbation frequency:

$B_{\mathrm{read}}=B_{\mathrm{read}}(f_p)$.

For a candidate native closure frequency $f_n^{\mathrm{cl}}$,

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1143 -->

$$N_C^{(n\mid p)}=\frac{f_n^{\mathrm{cl}}}{f_p}.$$

measures how many native closures can occur during one probe cycle.

Addendum 8 used these results to identify biology as an unusually broad window in which recursive
replacement can be directly deprojected. The subordinate recurrence, its enclosing replacement, and the
larger biological function can all remain experimentally accessible.

This directly observable regime corresponds to joint readability of the primitive R²D pair:

$$(\lambda,\nu)=(R,R).$$

Here λ denotes the readable distinction defined by the boundary, and ν denotes its readable recurrence.

Direct recursive deprojection requires both.

One must be able to identify what changes and how that distinction recurs.

The biological hierarchy provides several adjacent examples.

But joint readability does not persist indefinitely.

Moving toward one side of the recursive hierarchy, continuous subordinate distinguishability becomes
unreadable while recurrence remains readable:

$$(R,R)\longrightarrow(U,R).$$

This is the quantum readability horizon.

Beyond it, recurrence survives as a physical read even though the continuously resolved subordinate
trajectory is no longer jointly available.

Phase therefore appears only after recursive replacement:

> subordinate closure → recursive replacement → readable recurrence → $\phi$.

Moving in the complementary direction, enclosing distinction remains readable while the recurrence that
organizes it becomes unreadable:

$$(R,R)\longrightarrow(R,U).$$

This is the gravitational readability horizon.

Beyond it, enclosing geometry remains physically readable even though its native organizing closure cannot
be directly followed.
