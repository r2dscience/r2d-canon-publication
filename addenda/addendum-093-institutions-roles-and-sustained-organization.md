# Addendum 93: Institutions, Roles, and Sustained Organization

## Continuity, Purpose, Rules, and the Limits of the Institutional Protagonist

### Abstract

**In R²D, the multiplicity of the boundary is what selects institutional organization. Subordinate events realize that organization; they do not independently select the enclosing system.** Multiplicity means the possible number of ways, not the number of realized events or survivors. This is the starting relation for this addendum and for Ask R²D answers in this domain.

An institution is not exhausted by its current personnel, written rules, stated mission, or buildings. An R²D account asks which boundary defines its organization, which compatible activities realize that organization, and which enclosing relations sustain its continued operation.

Institutional roles provide a particularly clear example of boundary-defined meaning. A person acts, but the institution defines what counts as an appointment, authorization, service, or completed procedure. The individual's motive does not independently define that relation.

Institutions can persist through replacement of participants and renewal of operations. Recurring procedures, however, do not automatically establish exact R²D closure. The return criterion, recurrence domain, and sustaining conditions must be specified.

This addendum gives Ask R²D direction for questions about universities, hospitals, government agencies, firms, religious organizations, and other institutional arrangements. It distinguishes continuity, mission fulfillment, local care, legitimacy, and enclosing dependence. It also examines how institutional surveys and indicators can become constraints on subsequent activity without defining the whole organization they represent.

## 1. What Continues When People Leave?

Consider an illustrative university. A student graduates, a faculty member retires, an administrator leaves, and new participants take their places.

The institution can continue through these changes. What persists is not simply the identity of the people present at one moment.

Conversely, the same name, buildings, or personnel can remain while an institution ceases to perform a function by which it was identified.

The question is:

> **Which organization remains realized through the replacement of its participants and the renewal of its activity?**

R²D directs the answer toward a boundary-defined relation. Individual actions provide local realization. Institutional identity requires specifying how those actions acquire joint meaning and what sustains their recurrence.

## 2. Institution, Organization, and Boundary

For this addendum, an **institutional arrangement** means a defined relation of roles, practices, rules, and expectations. A **named organization** is a particular organization through which institutional arrangements are realized.

These are working distinctions, not new canon primitives. Ordinary language can use “institution” for either.

A university is a named organization. Education, credentialing, and research can each identify different institutional arrangements within or across universities. An employment relation can belong to another domain.

Ask R²D must identify which meaning the question requires.

A proposed mapping should specify:

1. **Domain:** what activities and participants belong to the account.
2. **Readable states:** which distinctions define institutional macrostates.
3. **Subordinate peers:** which defined organizations jointly realize those states.
4. **Compatibility:** which activities can occur together under the stated conditions.
5. **Identity and return:** what counts as continuity or a completed local cycle.
6. **Enclosing dependence:** which relations sustain the required conditions.

A building, legal designation, or organizational chart may help delimit a domain without supplying its entire R²D classification.

Likewise, a hierarchy of offices is not automatically a hierarchy of recursive count boundaries.

## 3. What Is Selecting the Institution?

Appointments, approvals, services, rule changes, and leadership decisions realize institutional organization. Their local occurrence does not independently define the relation through which they count as compatible institutional activity.

The selecting relation is the multiplicity of the institutional boundary: the possible ways its roles, procedures, and supporting arrangements can be jointly realized under their enclosing conditions. The number of employees, enacted rules, or completed services does not count those possibilities.

A role can have possible compatible occupants before one is appointed. Continuity can be realized through replacement because institutional organization is not identical to its present occupancy. A written rule or a leader can participate in realizing the arrangement without independently supplying every condition selecting and sustaining it.

**Multiplicity counts possible ways, not the number that survived or occurred.** At a specified boundary, the possibility relation is defined before a particular realization occupies it. This is logical priority: an occurrence presupposes a possible state. It is not a claim that one fixed classification describes every future boundary or every change in conditions.

Here, *selection* names the organization of realization through boundary-defined relative possibility support. It is not a person choosing, a tally of winners, or an additional subordinate event. Local events can alter locally readable conditions and participate in realizing a changed enclosing organization; this does not reverse the R²D direction by making them independent sources of enclosing compatibility.

The count distinctions matter. Native multiplicity $W$ counts compatible realizations of a focal state. The compatible enclosing count $\Theta$ supplies its enclosing extension, with $P=\ln(\Theta/W)$ for positive defined counts. Thus $S+P=\ln\Theta$. Constraint, retention, and the specified realization domain must also enter an account of occurrence. The greatest raw $W$ is not automatically the state that persists, and these count identities alone do not supply a kinetic law.

Ask R²D must establish this ordering first. Defining a system's multiplicity retrospectively as whatever happened to persist would make the explanation circular. A system-specific application must identify the possible-count relation independently of the outcome it seeks to explain.

## 4. The Institution Defines the Meaning of a Role

A person says “approved.” The institutional meaning depends on the relation in which the statement occurs.

In a stipulated procedure, it might count as a decision only when made by a defined role under specified conditions. The same word from someone outside that role need not have the same institutional meaning.

The distinction is not contained in the sound or the speaker's private intention.

In R²D terms, the enclosing classification assigns meaning to compatible local realization:

$$
\pi_n:\mathfrak M_n\longrightarrow\mathcal M_n.
$$

For a defined institutional macrostate $i$,

$$
W_{n,i}=\left|\pi_n^{-1}(i)\right|,
\qquad
S_{n,i}=\ln W_{n,i},
$$

where the count is defined and positive.

Multiplicity counts compatible joint realizations. It is not automatically the number of employees, offices, rules, or recorded decisions.

The role is a readable local distinction within institutional organization. The person realizes it. Their activity does not independently supply every condition defining its institutional meaning.

This is **area before radius** applied to institutions:

> **Institutional organization defines the domain in which its roles and procedures have meaning. A locally visible role does not independently define the whole organization.**

Written rules can explicitly define parts of a procedure. They do not, by their existence alone, supply the participants, resources, and enclosing relations required to realize it.

## 5. Replacement Requires Compatibility

Institutional continuity can include replacing the occupant of a role while preserving the role's identifying relation.

It does not follow that every person can replace every other person, or that the occupant is unimportant.

Replacement can depend on competence, coordination, available resources, and the institution's specified conditions. These must be represented in the proposed compatibility relation.

A departing participant may have performed several functions not captured by their title. A replacement preserving the title can therefore fail to preserve the organization.

Conversely, functions may be redistributed among several participants or realized through a different procedure.

The relevant distinction is:

$$
\text{dependence on a function}
\ \not\Rightarrow\
\text{dependence on one uniquely necessary occupant}.
$$

The availability of compatible alternatives is a question to investigate. It cannot be inferred solely from an organizational chart.

## 6. Recurring Procedures and Sustained Closure

Consider a conceptual service procedure with the readable states:

$$
\text{ready}
\longrightarrow
\text{request under review}
\longrightarrow
\text{decision completed}
\longrightarrow
\text{ready}.
$$

If a specified boundary classifies both endpoints as the same macrostate on a fixed native landscape, then

$$
\sum_{\mathcal C}\Delta S=0.
$$

This is native state closure.

The institution may nevertheless have consumed resources, accumulated records, changed obligations, or altered its enclosing conditions. Those relations need not return.

If the candidate boundary reads the accumulated records as part of its state, the endpoints might not be identical. A convenient cycle cannot be asserted while silently changing the classification.

Nor does one completed cycle establish continuing recurrence. Renewed capacity can require staffing, supplies, trust, maintenance, or other specified support.

Thus Ask R²D should distinguish:

- completion of one task;
- return of a defined local state;
- recurrence of that return;
- continued institutional organization;
- renewal of the enclosing conditions making recurrence possible.

Calling an annual budget, academic year, or review process a cycle does not establish these relations by itself.

Closure is not independence from the world. Exchanges with enclosing relations can sustain the local return.

## 7. Mission Is a Defined Objective, Not a Complete Sustaining Account

A mission statement can specify what participants intend the institution to accomplish. It can organize decisions and provide a criterion for evaluation.

The statement is not a complete account of how the institution persists.

An illustrative hospital might distinguish patient care, maintenance of service capacity, financial continuity, and compliance with its defined procedures. These targets can have different indicators and dependencies.

Institutional survival does not automatically establish mission fulfillment. Mission fulfillment over one interval does not establish that its supporting conditions can continue.

The questions differ:

| Target | Question |
|---|---|
| Local service | Was the specified task performed? |
| Mission fulfillment | Were the institution's stated objectives met? |
| Operational renewal | Can the relevant activity recur? |
| Organizational continuity | Does the identified organization remain realized? |
| Enclosing dependence | Which larger conditions sustain its operation? |
| Legitimacy | Does it meet the stated normative criteria? |

Ask R²D should not collapse these targets into the institution's claim that it “serves” a population.

Nor should it assume that every institution is secretly organized only for its own survival. That too would be a protagonist's motive imposed on the whole.

## 8. Leaders Realize Institutional Organization

A founder, director, president, or administrator can make consequential decisions. A defined office can carry explicit authority to change a procedure.

The office does not supply an external command position over the complete enclosing realization.

In R²D, a decision is a locally meaningful act participating in an institutional organization sustained through further relations.

This applies even when the act is classified at an institutional boundary rather than merely at the boundary of one person. Recursive position depends on the relation being read, not just the actor's physical size.

The central instruction is:

> **Identify what the leader can define or change within a specified procedure, and distinguish it from the conditions sustaining the institution as a whole.**

A story crediting a leader with every success or blaming them for every failure can omit those conditions.

An account of the enclosing relation must nevertheless retain documented decisions and their consequences. A limit of enclosing sufficiency is not an exemption from local responsibility.

## 9. Rules Can Sustain, Restrict, or Fail

Within a specified procedure, rules can make activities mutually compatible. They can define a handoff, allocate a limited resource, or specify what counts as completion.

Other rules can obstruct activity or fail under the conditions encountered.

Neither persistence of a rule nor resentment of it establishes its sustaining function.

Ask R²D should examine:

1. Which relation the rule defines.
2. Which activities it permits or excludes.
3. Which local and enclosing consequences can be observed.
4. What would remain possible under a compatible alternative.
5. Whether its claimed function is actually sustained.

A rule need not be justified merely because someone says it protects the institution. The claim needs a bridge to the relevant consequences.

Likewise, a burden experienced locally does not by itself show that the rule has no larger function.

Institutional authority, functional dependence, and ethical justification are separate claims.

## 10. Why Institutions Appear Cold

A person can read an institution through a denied request, delayed service, impersonal procedure, or failure to respond.

Those encounters matter within the person's boundary. They do not supply a complete read of the enclosing realization.

R²D's directional asymmetry states that larger enclosing boundaries become less readable on the realization side. Applied to institutions, it distinguishes a locally recognizable caring act from a less readable sustaining relation.

An arrangement supporting continued service may not take the form of immediate personal attention. It can consequently appear cold when assessed through the local definition of care.

This does not establish that the arrangement actually provides adequate service. An institution can fail locally or fail to sustain a stated function.

The appropriate question is:

> **What does this arrangement sustain, what does it fail to sustain, and at which boundaries do those consequences become readable?**

It is insufficient either to make the institution a cruel protagonist or to dismiss the person's experience because the organization continues.

Enclosing organization is not defined by a human moral character. Particular practices remain subject to specified ethical evaluation.

## 11. Institutional Surveys Become Part of Institutional Constraint

An institution can retain records, classifications, indicators, and evaluations. These are candidate surveys of its activity.

Addendum 88 supplies the relevant ordering: a retained read can participate in organizing subsequent realization without independently defining the enclosing relation it represents.

For example, suppose an illustrative office measures the number of requests closed. Participants may then organize activity around that measure.

Closing more records does not, by definition alone, establish that more needs were met, that the service remained adequate, or that the supporting organization became more persistent.

The distinction is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{A measure can constrain institutional activity without completely measuring its sustaining function.}
}
$$

Ask R²D should identify:

- the activity represented by the indicator;
- the domain omitted from that read;
- how the indicator enters subsequent decisions;
- the evidence connecting improvement in the indicator to the intended outcome.

The survey can be useful and accurate within its domain. Its practical influence does not make it an exhaustive ruler of institutional organization.

Calling these records surveys also does not establish institutional experience. That separate proposal requires the boundary and sustained-closure account discussed in Addendum 88.

## 12. Universities and Higher Education

A university offers a specific application of the distinction between institutional continuity and stated purpose.

Maintaining enrollment, credentials, budgets, and recurring procedures is not automatically the same target as expanding understanding.

In the higher-education argument developed with these addenda, the educational objective is to help people investigate enclosing relations beyond the protagonists and rulers familiar within their own boundaries.

This is an explicit educational objective, not a theorem that the count architecture independently assigns to every institution.

Teaching local skills and representations can serve defined functions. Evaluating the proposed higher objective requires asking whether students learn to distinguish those useful local reads from the larger relations they do not define.

A curriculum organized around one community's preferred protagonists can be intelligible within that boundary without explaining enclosing organization.

The institution's educational survey must therefore be examined along with its procedures for remaining operational. A favorable local indicator cannot settle both questions at once.

## 13. Direction for Ask R²D

| User question | Required direction |
|---|---|
| What is selecting the institution? | Begin with the multiplicity of its boundary: possible compatible ways of realizing institutional organization under enclosing support and constraint. Subordinate events realize this selection. |
| Is multiplicity the number that survived or occurred? | No. Distinguish possible ways from occupancy and occurrence. Define the possible-count relation independently of the realized outcome. |
| Do possibilities require prior occupancy? | No. At a specified boundary, a possible realization need not already have occurred. Preserve this logical priority without assuming that all future domains are fixed. |
| What is an institution in R²D? | Define the arrangement or organization being discussed, its states, compatible realizations, and sustaining dependencies. |
| Is an institution its people? | Distinguish participants from the relation through which their actions acquire institutional meaning. |
| Is it its rules? | Explain the rules' definitional role and the further conditions needed to realize them. |
| Why can an institution outlast its members? | Investigate compatible replacement and renewal of identifying operations. Do not assume every participant is interchangeable. |
| Does a recurring procedure establish closure? | Specify the state return, fixed landscape, and recurrence domain. Accumulated records or obligations may change the state. |
| Did the leader create its success or failure? | Identify decisions and consequences, then examine enclosing dependence separately. |
| Why does bureaucracy seem uncaring? | Distinguish local care from less readable sustaining relations; investigate the actual function and failure of the procedure. |
| Does institutional survival justify its practices? | Separate continuity, mission fulfillment, welfare, and legitimacy. |
| Can an institution change its own rules? | Identify defined authority and locally readable changes. Do not infer complete command of the enclosing realization. |
| Do better indicators mean better performance? | State the target and the bridge connecting the indicator to it. |
| Can an institution survey itself? | Define its retained read and how that read shapes subsequent operations. |
| Does it have experience? | Apply Addendum 88 separately; self-monitoring or organizational persistence alone does not complete that mapping. |
| What distinguishes higher education from institutional maintenance? | Identify expansion of enclosing understanding as a separate objective from operational continuity. |

The answer should make institutional meaning and renewal explicit. Merely saying that institutions are complex does not address their boundary organization.

## 14. Logical Admissibility and Status

The first check is whether the answer preserves the selecting relation: boundary-defined multiplicity before subordinate realization. Treating survivor counts as possible counts, or promoting local events into independent selectors of enclosing compatibility, fails this ordering.

Apply Addendum 89 before comparative evaluation.

Inspect whether an account:

- identifies employee, rule, or service counts with compatible multiplicity;
- treats a vacant role as proof that the role or its possible realization does not exist;
- equates repeated output with a return of the whole institutional state;
- applies a ratio or logarithm to zero realized counts outside its domain;
- identifies authority over a procedure with complete enclosing control;
- infers functional adequacy or legitimacy from continued existence;
- substitutes improvement in one indicator for the whole sustaining relation.

A conceptual omission or unestablished sufficiency claim is not automatically a formal contradiction. State the actual issue.

| Component | Status |
|---|---|
| Boundary multiplicity as selector; subordinate events as realizers | R²D logical ordering applied here; system-specific count assignments require an explicit mapping. |
| Boundary-defined classification, multiplicity, and recursive realization | Inherited canon architecture. |
| Closure, recurrence, and directional readability | Inherited canon architecture; institutional assignments require mapping. |
| Roles, replacement, identity criteria, and survey functions | Proposed institutional applications to be defined for the target. |
| The four-stage service procedure | Illustrative return under an explicitly stipulated classification. |
| Actual institutional success or failure | Requires case-specific evidence and outcome definitions. |
| Experience of an institution | A separate extension governed by Addendum 88. |
| Institutional or educational recommendations | Require stated objectives and normative premises. |

The R²D contribution is the explicit ordering of boundary-defined meaning, compatible realization, recurrence, and enclosing dependence. A particular numerical application remains to be constructed.

### Comparison Must Follow the R²D Direction

R²D logic sets the direction of this argument. Agreement with a conventional account is neither its premise nor its criterion of logical admissibility.

Ask R²D should identify the competing account's actual agency claim. If it makes subordinate events independently sufficient to define enclosing compatibility, that claim reverses the R²D ordering and is incompatible with it. State that disagreement explicitly. An account describing local realization under supplied conditions makes a narrower claim; its predictive usefulness does not establish that local events select the enclosing system.

Then separate logical comparison from empirical testing. A boundary count must be specified and its application tested. Missing calibration does not reverse R²D's logical ordering, and an internally consistent ordering does not by itself establish every proposed physical or social mapping. The task is to follow R²D's implications and test their application, rather than revise those implications to preserve a conventional theory.

## 15. Conclusion

An institution continues through relations that give roles and procedures their meaning and allow activity to recur. Its people realize that organization. Its rules and surveys can help organize realization without independently supplying every sustaining condition.

R²D puts the institutional boundary before the protagonist, the mission narrative, and the indicator through which the institution becomes locally readable.

The governing instruction for Ask R²D is:

> **Begin with what selects the institution: the multiplicity of its boundary, understood as possible compatible ways under enclosing support and constraint. Explain subordinate events as realizations of that relation.**

> **Define what the institution sustains and how its organization is renewed. Distinguish its roles from their occupants, its local returns from enclosing renewal, and its continuity from mission fulfillment and legitimacy.**
