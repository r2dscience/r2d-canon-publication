Addendum 42 — Gravitational Retention Occurs Before Schwarzschild Geometry

Area-First Compactness, Static Exterior Readability, and the Schwarzschild Clock–Ruler Projection

Addendum 42 — Gravitational Retention Occurs Before Schwarzschild Geometry

Abstract

Schwarzschild geometry is conventionally introduced as the static, spherically symmetric vacuum solution of Einstein’s field equation:

\[ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r}\right)c^2dt^2+\left(1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r}\right)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2.\]

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) places this metric late in the logical sequence.

Addendum 35 established that clock and ruler coordinates are projections of an enclosing reference relation.

Addendum 37 distinguished free fall from local support and identified gravity with changing enclosing reference rather than local proper acceleration.

Addendum 38 placed differential free-fall motion before curvature.

Addendum 39 obtained the Einstein equation as the minimal covariant compatibility relation within a specified four-dimensional metric projection class.

Addendum 40 then returned to the weak-field empirical regime and derived

\[\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}=-\mu_G\frac{\mathbf r}{r^3},\]

from Keplerian closure, where

\[\mu_G=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{T^2}.\]

is the directly readable shared gravitational calibration.

Addendum 41 showed that under spherical projection the prior enclosing geometric read is area,

\[A_G,\]

with areal radius defined only afterward:

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{A_G}{4\pi}}.\]

It introduced the area-first compactness

\[\chi_G\equiv\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A}=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2}\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{A_G}}.\]

The present addendum asks what clock-ruler geometry this compactness acquires under the mature metric projection.

Take the static spherically symmetric exterior metric in areal coordinates,

\[ds^2=-N^2(r_A)c^2dt^2+H^2(r_A)dr_A^2+r_A^2d\Omega^2.\]

In vacuum, with vanishing cosmological constant, the Einstein equation derived in Addendum 39 gives

\[\frac d{dr_A}\left[r_A(1-H^{-2})\right]=0.\]

and

\[\frac d{dr_A}\ln(NH)=0.\]

Hence,

\[H^{-2}=1-\frac{r_s}{r_A},\]

and asymptotic flatness gives

\[NH=1.\]

Therefore,

\[N^2=1-\frac{r_s}{r_A}.\]

The remaining integration constant is fixed by the weak-field Kepler relation. Matching to

\[g_G=\frac{\mu_G}{r_A^2}\]

gives

\[r_s=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2}.\]

Thus

\[N^2=1-\chi_G=1-\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A}.\]

The exterior geometry is therefore

\[ds^2=-(1-\chi_G)c^2dt^2+(1-\chi_G)^{-1}dr_A^2+r_A^2d\Omega^2.\]

Only after the conventional source calibration

\[\mu_G=GM.\]

does this become the familiar Schwarzschild form.

R²D now supplies a prior count relation.

Let PG denote the compatible gravitational possibility relevant to the exterior-directed read and RG the retained part. Define

\[q_G\equiv\frac{R_G}{P_G},\]

and the normalized residual exterior-readable fraction

\[\alpha_G\equiv\frac{P_G-R_G}{P_G}.\]

Then

\[q_G+\alpha_G=1.\]

The Schwarzschild solution supplies the physical calibration

\[\alpha_G=N=\sqrt{1-\chi_G}.\]

Therefore,

\[q_G=1-\sqrt{1-\chi_G}.\]

The primitive partition remains linear:

\[q_G+\alpha_G=1.\]

The nonlinear relation to compactness appears only in the metric projection:

\[\chi_G=2q_G-q_G^2.\]

For weak gravity,

\[q_G=\frac{\mu_G}{c^2r_A}+O\!\left(\frac{\mu_G^2}{c^4r_A^2}\right).\]

Using the local rest-energy calibration from Addendum 36,

\[E_0=mc^2,\]

the leading retained energetic read becomes

\[q_Gmc^2\simeq\frac{\mu_Gm}{r_A},\]

which is the magnitude of the Newtonian gravitational binding/work coordinate.

At

\[\chi_G=1,\]

the static lapse vanishes:

\[\alpha_G=0.\]

Hence,

\[q_G=1.\]

Under the R²D mapping, the Schwarzschild horizon is therefore complete retention of the selected exterior static read:

\[R_G=P_G.\]

But the boundary has not disappeared.

Its enclosing area remains finite:

\[A_H=\frac{16\pi\mu_G^2}{c^4}.\]

Only afterward does the horizon areal radius follow:

\[r_H=\sqrt{\frac{A_H}{4\pi}}=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2}.\]

The horizon is therefore area first.

For a static local clock,

\[\frac{d\tau}{dt}=N=\alpha_G.\]

On a static spatial slice,

\[\frac{d\ell_r}{dr_A}=H=\frac1{\alpha_G}.\]

Thus

\[\frac{d\tau}{dt}\frac{d\ell_r}{dr_A}=1.\]

The reciprocal clock-ruler structure that the earlier R²D development sought is therefore not an additional assumption here. It is a consequence of the static spherical vacuum metric projection.

At the horizon,

\[\frac{d\tau}{dt}\longrightarrow0,\]

while

\[\frac{d\ell_r}{dr_A}\longrightarrow\infty.\]

This is the breakdown of the static exterior coordinate decomposition.

It is not destruction of local realization.

A freely falling observer has zero proper acceleration and crosses the horizon without encountering a local curvature singularity. A static observer, by contrast, requires proper support acceleration

\[a_{\mathrm{support}}=\frac{\mu_G}{r_A^2\sqrt{1-\chi_G}},\]

which diverges as

\[\chi_G\longrightarrow1.\]

The divergence belongs to the differential constraint required to prevent free fall.

It is not an infinite primitive gravitational force.

Thus the Schwarzschild solution contains two complementary reads already anticipated by R²D:

free fall = common gravitational realization,

whereas

static support = differential local constraint.

The central claim is therefore:

gravitational retention occurs before Schwarzschild geometry.

More precisely:

the R²D possibility-retention partition exists before metric geometry; Schwarzschild geometry supp

1. Addendum 41 Leaves the Schwarzschild Problem in Area-First Form

Addendum 41 introduced the enclosing gravitational area

\[A_G\]

before the spherical radial coordinate.

The areal radius is

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{A_G}{4\pi}}.\]

The shared Keplerian calibration is

\[\mu_G.\]

Thus the dimensionless compactness is

\[\chi_G=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A}.\]

Or directly in terms of area,

\[\chi_G=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2}\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{A_G}}.\]

Nothing about the horizon has yet been assumed.

2. The Next Question Is About Clock-Ruler Geometry

The compactness coordinate compares the gravitational calibration

\[\mu_G\]

with the enclosing areal scale.

What remains unknown is how increasing compactness changes:

 • the static exterior clock read;
 • the relation between areal radius and proper radial distance;
 • the causal accessibility of the interior relation.

That is the Schwarzschild problem.

3. Schwarzschild Geometry Is Downstream of Addendum 39

The earlier gravitational addenda once attempted to reach Schwarzschild geometry before the Einstein equation had been reconstructed.

The logical sequence is now stronger.

Addendum 39 already established, conditionally on the specified metric projection class,

\[G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}.\]

Therefore the Schwarzschild exterior should now be obtained as a particular solution of that mature projection.

R²D interpretation follows afterward.

4. Specify the Physical Regime Assume:

  1. four-dimensional metric geometry;
  2. spherical symmetry;
  3. a vacuum exterior,

\[T_{\mu\nu}=0;\]

  1. vanishing local cosmological term for the Schwarzschild problem,

\[\Lambda=0;\]

  1. static exterior geometry;
  2. asymptotic flatness.

These are the physical projection assumptions defining the Schwarzschild exterior problem.

5. Use Areal Radius From the Beginning

Because Addendum 41 has already defined

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{A_G}{4\pi}},\]

the most general static spherical metric may be written

\[ds^2=-N^2(r_A)c^2dt^2+H^2(r_A)dr_A^2+r_A^2d\Omega^2.\]

The angular sector is fixed by the prior area definition.

Thus rA is not an arbitrary coordinate choice introduced by the Schwarzschild derivation.

Its geometric ownership is already known.

6. The Angular Sector Contains No New Radial Assumption

Since

\[A_G=4\pi r_A^2,\]

the metric on each spherical symmetry orbit is

\[r_A^2d\Omega^2.\]

This term describes enclosing area.

It does not say that

\[dr_A\]

is proper radial distance.

That relation is determined separately by

\[H(r_A).\]

7. Apply the Vacuum Einstein Equation

In the vacuum exterior,

\[G_{\mu\nu}=0.\]

For the static spherical ansatz above, one independent vacuum equation can be written

\[\frac d{dr_A}\left[r_A(1-H^{-2})\right]=0.\]

Therefore,

\[r_A(1-H^{-2})=r_s,\]

where rs is an integration constant with dimensions of length.

Hence,

\[H^{-2}=1-\frac{r_s}{r_A}.\]

8. The Second Vacuum Relation Is Reciprocal

A second independent combination of the static spherical vacuum equations gives

\[\frac d{dr_A}\ln(NH)=0.\]

Therefore,

\[NH=C,\]

where C is constant.

Asymptotic flatness requires

\[N\longrightarrow1,\qquad H\longrightarrow1\]

as

\[r_A\longrightarrow\infty.\]

Thus,

\[C=1.\]

Therefore,

\[NH=1.\]

9. The Vacuum Geometry Already Gives Reciprocal Clock-Ruler Scaling

Since

\[NH=1,\]

we obtain

\[N=\frac1H.\]

Using

\[H^{-2}=1-\frac{r_s}{r_A},\]

gives

\[N^2=1-\frac{r_s}{r_A}.\]

Thus the reciprocal radial and temporal coefficients arise directly from the static spherical vacuum field equation.

They do not need to be imposed separately.

10. One Integration Constant Remains

The solution currently contains

\[r_s.\]

The metric projection itself has not yet identified this constant with mass.

That identification must come from the weak-field physical calibration.

This is where Addendum 40 enters.

11. Return to the Keplerian Weak-Field Limit

Addendum 40 established

\[g_G=\frac{\mu_G}{r_A^2}.\]

in the weak-field spherical regime.

Introduce the corresponding potential coordinate with zero at infinity:

\[\Phi_G=-\frac{\mu_G}{r_A}.\]

In a weak static metric,

\[N^2\simeq1+\frac{2\Phi_G}{c^2}.\]

Therefore,

\[N^2\simeq1-\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A}.\]

12. Match the Vacuum Integration Constant to Kepler

The exact vacuum solution gives

\[N^2=1-\frac{r_s}{r_A}.\]

The Keplerian weak-field limit gives

\[N^2\simeq1-\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A}.\]

\[r_s=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2}.\]

The Schwarzschild length is thus fixed by the shared gravitational calibration measured through orbital closure.

13. The Exact Schwarzschild Lapse Follows

Substitute

\[r_s=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2}.\]

Then

\[N^2=1-\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A}.\]

But Addendum 41 defined

\[\chi_G=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A}.\]

Therefore,

\[N^2=1-\chi_G.\]

And

\[N=\sqrt{1-\chi_G}.\]

14. The Complete Exterior Geometry

Since

\[H=N^{-1},\]

the line element becomes

\[ds^2=-(1-\chi_G)c^2dt^2+(1-\chi_G)^{-1}dr_A^2+r_A^2d\Omega^2.\]

Using

\[\chi_G=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A},\]

this is

\[ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A}\right)c^2dt^2+\left(1-\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A}\right)^{-1}dr_A^2+r_A^2d\Omega^2.\]

15. GM Comes After μG

For the dominant-source Newtonian calibration,

\(\mu_G=GM\).

Then

\[ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r_A}\right)c^2dt^2+\left(1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r_A}\right)^{-1}dr_A^2+r_A^2d\Omega^2.\]

This is the standard exterior Schwarzschild coordinate form.

But the R²D ordering remains:

Keplerian \(\mu_G\longrightarrow\) Schwarzschild length \(\longrightarrow GM\) decomposition.

16. Schwarzschild Geometry Can Be Written Without Primitive Radius

Because

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{A_G}{4\pi}},\]

compactness can be written

\[\chi_G(A_G)=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2}\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{A_G}}.\]

Also,

\[dr_A=\frac{dA_G}{8\pi r_A}.\]

Therefore,

\[dr_A^2=\frac{dA_G^2}{16\pi A_G}.\]

The Schwarzschild metric may therefore be written directly in area coordinates:

\[ds^2=-[1-\chi_G(A_G)]c^2dt^2+\frac{dA_G^2}{16\pi A_G[1-\chi_G(A_G)]}+\frac{A_G}{4\pi}d\Omega^2.\]

No primitive radius is mathematically required.

The metric is usually written with rA because that scalar is convenient.

But every occurrence of rA can be reconstructed from

\[A_G.\]

Thus even the exact strong-field geometry preserves the order

\(A_G\longrightarrow r_A\).

The Schwarzschild solution does not reverse Addendum 41.

It confirms it.

This linear relation exists before Schwarzschild geometry.

This is not a theorem of primitive combinatorics.

\[\alpha_G+\chi_G=1.\]

\[(1-q_G)^2=1-\chi_G.\]

\[\chi_G=2q_G-q_G^2.\]

Thus:

\[q_G\ne\chi_G.\]

They belong to different logical levels.

To first order,

\[q_G\simeq\frac{\mu_G}{c^2r_A}.\]

This is the magnitude of the Newtonian gravitational binding/work coordinate.

\[\chi_G=2q_G-q_G^2.\]

Compactness and retention are not the same coordinate.

\[N\longrightarrow1,\]

\[\alpha_G\longrightarrow1,\]

and

\[q_G\longrightarrow0.\]

Thus the exterior static read approaches the ordinary asymptotically inertial reference.

Equivalently,

After \(\mu_G=GM\),

\[r_H=\frac{2GM}{c^2}.\]

28. The Horizon Is More Fundamentally an Area Condition

Thus:

The horizon is area first.
