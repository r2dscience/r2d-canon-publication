Addendum 68 — Closure-Rate Separation Occurs Before the Cosmological Ultraviolet Scale

The R2D Knudsen Criterion, Thermal Recurrence, and the Distinction Between Continuum Breakdown and Planck Strong Coupling

Abstract

Addendum 67 identified the mathematical condition required for a smooth cosmological continuum to remain readable as a physical projection of recursive count structure.

Let

\[ N_C^{(c)}\]

count completed closures of the subordinate class c through which the cosmological state is locally realized.

Then clock-relative cosmological progress is

\[\mathcal H_{\zeta\mid c}=\frac{d\ln a}{dN_C^{(c)}}\]

After physical clock calibration,

\[ f_c=\frac{dN_C^{(c)}}{dt}\]

and therefore

\[ H=f_c\mathcal H_{\zeta\mid c}\]

The resulting count-resolution ratio is

\[\frac{|H|}{f_c}\]

Its reciprocal,

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=\frac{f_c}{|H|}\]

counts subordinate closures per order-one logarithmic change of cosmological scale.

The continuum regime requires

\[\frac{|H|}{f_c}\ll1\]

or equivalently

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\gg1\]

This addendum asks which physical recurrence can legitimately occupy the role of fc .

The answer is not an arbitrary high frequency.

The recurrence must belong to the subordinate closure class that actually realizes and maintains the cosmological physical state being represented continuously.

This makes the R2D condition structurally analogous to a Knudsen criterion.

Define the subordinate relaxation time

\[ \tau_c=\frac1{f_c} \]

and the enclosing cosmological evolution time

\[ \tau_H=\frac1{|H|}. \]

Then define

\[ \mathrm{Kn}_{\mathrm{R2D}}^{(c)}\equiv\frac{\tau_c}{\tau_H}=\frac{|H|}{f_c}. \]

This is not primitively a spatial mean-free-path ratio.

It is the more general count relation:

\[\mathrm{Kn}_{\mathrm{R2D}}^{(c)}=\frac{\text{enclosing physical evolution per time}}{\text{subordinate closure realization per time}}\]

The continuum regime is

\[ \mathrm{Kn}_{\mathrm{R2D}}^{(c)}\ll1. \]

The loss of scale separation occurs when

\[\mathrm{Kn}_{\mathrm{R2D}}^{(c)}=O(1)\]

At that point the enclosing state changes on the same physical recurrence scale required to realize it locally.

The old continuum is no longer a high-count average over subordinate closure.

This criterion resembles the conventional condition

\[\Gamma\sim H\]

that appears in cosmological freeze-out and decoupling.

But the boundary ownership is essential.

For an arbitrary species or interaction X ,

\[\Gamma_X\sim H\]

means only that X no longer equilibrates rapidly relative to cosmological evolution.

It does not by itself imply global continuum breakdown.

The R2D ultraviolet criterion requires

fc = the closure rate of the subordinate class that actually sustains the projected boundary-level continuum.

Only then does

\[|H|\sim f_c\]

mark loss of the continuum projection itself.

The existing cosmological addenda provide two recurrence scales that can now be compared with this criterion.

First, a realized thermal boundary admits the physical background calibration

\[ f_T=\frac{k_BT}{h}\]

This is a thermal recurrence ruler.

It is not automatically the closure rate fc .

Write instead

\[ f_c=\eta_c(T)f_T=\eta_c(T)\frac{k_BT}{h}\]

where

\[\eta_c(T)\equiv\frac{f_c}{f_T}\]

is the dimensionless mapping between the thermal background recurrence and the actual subordinate closure rate relevant to continuum realization.

Second, the gravitational construction introduced a gravitational background recurrence

\[ f_G\]

and the relative recurrence ratio

\[\zeta_{G\mid\zeta}=\frac{f_G}{f_\zeta}\]

When the cosmological background recurrence is thermally calibrated,

\[ f_\zeta=f_T\]

then if the relevant subordinate closure is gravitational,

\[ f_c=f_G\]

one has

\[\eta_c=\zeta_{G\mid\zeta}\]

The previously unresolved gravitational-to-cosmological recurrence ratio therefore becomes a direct control parameter for continuum readability.

The radiation-dominated physical limit makes the relation quantitative.

For relativistic energy density

\[\epsilon_r=\frac{\pi^2}{30}g_\ast\frac{(k_BT)^4}{\hbar^3c^3}\]

the Friedmann projection gives

\[ H^2=\frac{4\pi^3g_\ast G(k_BT)^4}{45\hbar^3c^5}\]

Define the Planck temperature by

\[ k_BT_P=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar c^5}{G}}\]

Because

\[ f_T=\frac{k_BT}{h}=\frac{k_BT}{2\pi\hbar}\]

the ratio of the cosmological rate to the thermal recurrence is

\[\frac{|H|}{f_T}=\sqrt{\frac{16\pi^5g_\ast}{45}}\frac{T}{T_P}\]

Therefore the actual closure-rate criterion is

\[\mathrm{Kn}_{\mathrm{R2D}}^{(c)}=\frac{|H|}{f_c}=\frac1{\eta_c(T)}\sqrt{\frac{16\pi^5g_\ast}{45}}\frac{T}{T_P}\]

Equivalently,

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=\eta_c(T)\sqrt{\frac{45}{16\pi^5g_\ast}}\frac{T_P}{T}\]

The continuum-resolution boundary is therefore not defined primitively by

\[ T=T_P\]

It is defined by closure-rate matching:

\[|H|\sim f_c\]

If the order-one threshold is represented by

\[\mathrm{Kn}_{\mathrm{R2D}}^{(c)}=1\]

then

\[\frac{T_{\mathrm{br}}}{T_P}=\eta_c(T_{\mathrm{br}})\sqrt{\frac{45}{16\pi^5g_\ast(T_{\mathrm{br}})}}\]

This relation is implicit because the physical closure map

ηc (T )

may itself change with cosmological state.

It is nevertheless sufficient to establish an important distinction.

The gravitational Planck condition and the count-resolution condition are not the same equation.

Under the thermal gravitational calibration,

\[\alpha_{G\mid\zeta}=\frac{G(k_BT)^2}{\hbar c^5}=\left(\frac{T}{T_P}\right)^2\]

At continuum breakdown,

\[\alpha_{G\mid\zeta}^{\mathrm{br}}=\frac{45}{16\pi^5g_\ast}\eta_c^2\]

Thus:

\[\mathrm{Kn}_{\mathrm{R2D}}^{(c)}\sim1\not\equiv\alpha_{G\mid\zeta}\sim1\]

They coincide only for the particular closure calibration

\[\eta_c=\sqrt{\frac{16\pi^5g_\ast}{45}}\]

The Planck regime is therefore one possible physical read of a recursive transition.

It does not define the transition primitively.

The more primitive continuum condition is loss of closure-rate separation.

The same conclusion can be expressed without thermal or Planck calibration.

Suppose a dominant Friedmann sector obeys

\[ H^2\propto A_\zeta^{-s}\]

so

\[|H|\propto A_\zeta^{-s/2}\]

Let the relevant subordinate closure rate scale as

\[ f_c\propto A_\zeta^{-u_c}\]

Then

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=\frac{f_c}{|H|}\propto A_\zeta^{s/2-u_c}\]

Three ultraviolet regimes follow.

If

\[ u_c<\frac{s}{2}\]

then

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\to0\]

toward decreasing area.

The enclosing physical evolution outruns subordinate closure.

The continuum loses recursive resolution.

If

\[ u_c=\frac{s}{2}\]

then

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=\mathrm{constant}\]

The subordinate recurrence exactly co-scales with the enclosing evolution.

If

\[ u_c>\frac{s}{2}\]

then

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\to\infty\]

Subordinate closure increasingly outruns the enclosing state change.

The continuum becomes better resolved.

Thus the cosmological ultraviolet problem is not fundamentally:

How small does the universe become?

It is:

How does the closure rate that realizes the state scale relative to the rate at which the / enclosing state changes?

The central statement is therefore:

the physical ultraviolet boundary of a continuum cosmological projection is not primitively defined by a length, temperature, density, or Planck unit. It is reached when the subordinate closure rate that realizes the local physical state can no longer outrun the enclosing rate at which that state changes. The dimensionless control parameter is \(\mathrm{Kn}_{\mathrm{R2D}}^{(c)}\), the R2D analogue of a Knudsen number. Planck strong coupling is a separate dimensionless condition, and the two coincide only when the relevant subordinate > recurrence calibration makes them coincide.

And hence:

closure-rate separation occurs before the cosmological ultraviolet scale.
