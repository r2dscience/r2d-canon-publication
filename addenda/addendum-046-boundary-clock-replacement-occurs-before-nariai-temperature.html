Addendum 46 — Boundary-Clock Replacement Occurs Before Nariai Temperature

Abstract

Addendum 45 established that the Schwarzschild–de Sitter black-hole and cosmological horizon multiplicity classes lose their distinction at the Nariai limit.

Their multiplicity gap,

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=S_c-S_b=\ln\frac{W_c}{W_b},\]

satisfies

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}>0\]

throughout the ordinary two-horizon regime and approaches

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}\longrightarrow0\]

when

\[r_b\longrightarrow r_c.\]

At that same physical boundary, the ordinary Schwarzschild–de Sitter horizon surface gravities vanish:

\[\kappa_b^{(0)}\longrightarrow0,\qquad\kappa_c^{(0)}\longrightarrow0.\]

Consequently the corresponding static-patch phase-frequency reads,

\[\omega_h^{(0)}=\frac{\kappa_h^{(0)}}{2\pi c},\]

also vanish.

Read without regard to boundary ownership, this might appear to imply that recurrence itself stops at Nariai.

R²D does not permit that inference.

A physical clock is a calibrated read belonging to a specified count boundary. When the distinction defining that boundary disappears, the physical clock belonging to that classification may itself become degenerate.

Schwarzschild–de Sitter provides an exact example.

The ordinary two-horizon static clock is normalized relative to a reference relation that also degenerates as the two horizon branches coalesce.

Let

\[r_g\]

denote the geodesic static reference radius satisfying

\[f'(r_g)=0.\]

Define the corresponding static normalization factor

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}\equiv\sqrt{f(r_g)}.\]

As Nariai is approached,

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}\longrightarrow0.\]

More strongly, with

\[q=\frac{r_b}{r_c}\]

and

\[\epsilon\equiv1-q,\]

the multiplicity gap, both ordinary surface gravities, and the clock normalization all vanish linearly:

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=O(\epsilon),\]

\[\kappa_b^{(0)}=O(\epsilon),\]

\[\kappa_c^{(0)}=O(\epsilon),\]

and

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}=O(\epsilon).\]

The zero of the old clock therefore occurs at the same boundary at which the count distinction defining the old two-horizon landscape disappears.

But the ratio

\[\frac{\kappa_h^{(0)}}{N_{\mathrm{stat}}}\]

has a finite limiting value.

Under the Bousso–Hawking normalization,

\[\kappa_h^{\mathrm{BH}}=\frac{\kappa_h^{(0)}}{N_{\mathrm{stat}}},\]

and at Nariai,

\[\kappa_N=c^2\sqrt{\Lambda}.\]

The corresponding phase-frequency read is

\[\omega_N=\frac{c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi}.\]

Only afterward does thermal dimensionalization give

\[k_BT_N=\hbar\omega_N=\frac{\hbar c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi}.\]

The finite quantity \(\omega_N\) should not be interpreted as the old static-patch clock recovering from zero.

The old clock satisfies

\[\omega_b^{(0)},\omega_c^{(0)}\longrightarrow0.\]

The finite quantity

\[\omega_N\]

belongs to the replacement Nariai landscape.

The old and new clock reads have different boundary owners.

Thus the correct R²D order is

two-horizon count distinction

\(\Downarrow\)

two-horizon clock calibration

\(\Downarrow\)

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}\longrightarrow0\]

\(\Downarrow\)

\[\kappa_b^{(0)},\kappa_c^{(0)},N_{\mathrm{stat}}\longrightarrow0\]

\(\Downarrow\)

old clock classification becomes degenerate

\(\Downarrow\)

Nariai replacement landscape

\(\Downarrow\)

\[\omega_N=\frac{c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi}\]

\(\Downarrow\)

\[T_N.\]

Temperature comes last.

Therefore:

boundary-clock replacement occurs before Nariai temperature.

More precisely:

the vanishing Schwarzschild–de Sitter static-patch temperature is the degeneration of a boundary-s

1. Addendum 45 Established the Boundary-Replacement Point

For the ordinary Schwarzschild–de Sitter two-horizon regime,

\[r_b<r_c.\]

The corresponding horizon multiplicities satisfy

\[W_b<W_c.\]

Their logarithmic gap is

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=\ln\frac{W_c}{W_b}.\]

Addendum 45 showed

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}>0\]

for

\[r_b<r_c,\]

while at the Nariai limit,

\[r_b=r_c,\]

we obtain

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=0.\]

The old two-horizon count classification therefore becomes marginal at Nariai.

2. The Next Question Is Not Another Area Question

Addendum 45 resolved the distinguishability side of the transition.

The horizon multiplicity distinction disappears.

But R²D keeps distinguishability and recurrence conceptually separate.

The next question is therefore:

What happens to the physical clock associated with the old two-horizon boundary when that boundary classification becomes degenerate?

This is the subject of the present addendum.

3. A Physical Clock Has a Boundary Owner

R²D does not treat physical time as a primitive universal coordinate existing independently of count boundaries.

A physical clock is a later read of recurrence under a specified boundary relation and calibration.

Therefore every physical clock must answer:

Which boundary defines this clock?

A clock cannot automatically be extrapolated unchanged after the boundary relation supplying its calibration has disappeared.

4. Begin With the Schwarzschild–de Sitter Static Patch

The static Schwarzschild–de Sitter metric is

\[ds^2=-f(r)c^2dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2d\Omega^2,\]

with

\[f(r)=1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r}-\frac{\Lambda r^2}{3}.\]

The coordinate t is the static-patch time coordinate.

It belongs to the physical classification in which the black-hole and cosmological horizons remain distinct.

5. The Ordinary Horizon Surface Gravities

For the black-hole horizon,

\[\kappa_b^{(0)}=\frac{c^2}{2}\left(\frac1{r_b}-\Lambda r_b\right).\]

For the cosmological horizon, taking the positive magnitude,

\[\kappa_c^{(0)}=\frac{c^2}{2}\left(\Lambda r_c-\frac1{r_c}\right).\]

These are the ordinary static-patch surface-gravity reads.

6. Surface Gravity Is Already a Physical Clock Coordinate

Associate the phase-frequency read

\[\omega_h^{(0)}=\frac{\kappa_h^{(0)}}{2\pi c}.\]

Thus

\[\omega_b^{(0)}=\frac{\kappa_b^{(0)}}{2\pi c},\]

and

\[\omega_c^{(0)}=\frac{\kappa_c^{(0)}}{2\pi c}.\]

These are physical frequency reads.

They are not themselves primitive R²D recurrence counts.

7. The Ordinary Frequencies Vanish at Nariai

At Nariai,

\[r_b,r_c\longrightarrow\frac1{\sqrt\Lambda}.\]

Therefore,

\[\frac1{r_b}-\Lambda r_b\longrightarrow0,\]

and

\[\Lambda r_c-\frac1{r_c}\longrightarrow0.\]

Hence,

\[\kappa_b^{(0)},\kappa_c^{(0)}\longrightarrow0.\]

Consequently,

\[\omega_b^{(0)},\omega_c^{(0)}\longrightarrow0.\]

8. The Conventional Static Temperature Therefore Also Vanishes

Using the thermal calibration

\[k_BT_h^{(0)}=\frac{\hbar\kappa_h^{(0)}}{2\pi c},\]

or equivalently,

\[k_BT_h^{(0)}=\hbar\omega_h^{(0)},\]

we obtain

\[T_b^{(0)},T_c^{(0)}\longrightarrow0.\]

This is the familiar zero-temperature static-patch Nariai limit under this normalization.

9. Zero Physical Frequency Does Not Yet Establish Zero Primitive Recurrence

The inference

\[\omega_h^{(0)}\longrightarrow0\]

therefore establishes only

this specified physical frequency read vanishes.

It does not by itself establish

all recurrence has vanished. [Source rejects this inference.]

The distinction is essential because the clock is boundary calibrated.

10. The Old Clock Belongs to the Two-Horizon Classification

The ordinary static-patch coordinate is defined while

\[r_b<r_c.\]

That same regime possesses

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}>0.\]

Thus the ordinary static clock belongs to the physical landscape in which the two horizon classes remain distinguishable.

Schematically,

\(\Delta S_{c\mid b}>0\longrightarrow\) two-horizon static clock.

The clock should therefore not be assumed to remain primitive after

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=0.\]

11. Schwarzschild–de Sitter Has No Unique Asymptotic Clock

Unlike asymptotically flat Schwarzschild geometry, Schwarzschild–de Sitter possesses no spatial infinity at which the timelike Killing vector can be assigned a unique asymptotic normalization.

Therefore surface gravity and horizon temperature require an explicit clock normalization.

This is not a minor technicality.

It identifies the reference relation through which the physical clock is being read.

12. Introduce the Geodesic Static Reference

Between the black-hole and cosmological horizons there is a static radius at which

\[f'(r_g)=0.\]

From

\[f'(r)=\frac{2GM}{c^2r^2}-\frac{2\Lambda r}{3},\]

the reference radius satisfies

\[\frac{GM}{c^2}=\frac{\Lambda r_g^3}{3}.\]

Thus

\[r_g^3=\frac{3GM}{\Lambda c^2}.\]

13. Express the Reference Radius Through the Two Horizons

The Schwarzschild–de Sitter root relations give

\[r_g^3=\frac12r_br_c(r_b+r_c).\]

Using

\[q\equiv\frac{r_b}{r_c},\]

we obtain

\[r_g=r_c\left[\frac{q(1+q)}2\right]^{1/3}.\]

The clock reference therefore changes as the two horizon branches reorganize.

It is not a fixed external observer independent of the gravitational landscape.

14. Define the Static Clock Normalization

Define

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}\equiv\sqrt{f(r_g)}.\]

Because

\[f'(r_g)=0,\]

we have

\[\frac{2GM}{c^2r_g}=\frac{2\Lambda r_g^2}{3}.\]

Hence,

\[f(r_g)=1-\Lambda r_g^2.\]

Therefore,

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}^2=1-\Lambda r_g^2.\]

15. Express the Normalization in Terms of q

Addendum 45 gave

\[r_c^2=\frac3{\Lambda(1+q+q^2)}.\]

Therefore,

\[\Lambda r_g^2=\frac3{1+q+q^2}\left[\frac{q(1+q)}2\right]^{2/3}.\]

Thus,

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}^2=1-\frac3{1+q+q^2}\left[\frac{q(1+q)}2\right]^{2/3}.\]

This is the normalization of the old static clock.

16. The Old Clock Reference Vanishes at Nariai

At Nariai,

\[q\longrightarrow1.\]

Then

\[\left[\frac{q(1+q)}2\right]^{2/3}\longrightarrow1,\]

and

\[1+q+q^2\longrightarrow3.\]

Therefore,

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}^2\longrightarrow1-\frac33=0.\]

Hence,

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}\longrightarrow0.\]

The static frequencies vanish at the same time that the reference normalization defining their clock also vanishes.

17. This Creates a 0/0 Structure

At Nariai,

\[\kappa_h^{(0)}\longrightarrow0,\]

while

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}\longrightarrow0.\]

Thus a boundary-normalized surface gravity has the form

\[\frac{\kappa_h^{(0)}}{N_{\mathrm{stat}}}\longrightarrow\frac00.\]

The numerator alone therefore cannot determine the limiting physical clock.

The normalization is disappearing with it.

18. Parameterize the Boundary Separation Define

\[\epsilon\equiv1-q.\]

The Nariai limit is

\[\epsilon\longrightarrow0.\]

This parameter directly measures the remaining separation of the two horizon-radius reads.

19. The Static Normalization Vanishes Linearly

Near Nariai,

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}=\frac\epsilon2+O(\epsilon^2).\]

Thus the reference supporting the ordinary static clock disappears linearly with the horizon-branch distinction.

20. The Black-Hole Surface Gravity Vanishes at the Same Rate

Near Nariai,

\[\kappa_b^{(0)}=\frac{c^2\sqrt\Lambda}{2}\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2).\]

Therefore,

\[\kappa_b^{(0)}=O(\epsilon).\]

21. The Cosmological Surface Gravity Does Likewise

Similarly,

\[\kappa_c^{(0)}=\frac{c^2\sqrt\Lambda}{2}\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2).\]

Thus,

\[\kappa_c^{(0)}=O(\epsilon).\]

The two ordinary horizon clock reads vanish together.

22. The Multiplicity Gap Also Vanishes Linearly

From Addendum 45,

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=\frac{3\pi}{\Lambda\ell_P^2}\frac{1-q^2}{1+q+q^2}.\]

Near

\[q=1,\]

this becomes

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=\frac{2\pi}{\Lambda\ell_P^2}\epsilon+O(\epsilon^2).\]

Therefore,

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=O(\epsilon).\]

23. Four Apparently Different Quantities Share One Boundary Zero

As

\[\epsilon\longrightarrow0,\]

we have

\(\Delta S_{c\mid b},\ \kappa_b^{(0)},\ \kappa_c^{(0)},\ N_{\mathrm{stat}}=O(\epsilon).\)

Thus:

multiplicity distinction \(\longrightarrow0\),

black-hole static clock read \(\longrightarrow0\),

cosmological static clock read \(\longrightarrow0\),

and

static clock normalization \(\longrightarrow0\)

all at the same boundary.

This is more than an isolated temperature zero.

The entire two-horizon clock architecture becomes degenerate together.

24. The Clock Degeneracy Tracks the Count-Boundary Degeneracy

The simultaneous scaling gives the physical sequence

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}\longrightarrow0\]

together with

\[N_{\mathrm{stat}}\longrightarrow0\]

and

\[\omega_b^{(0)},\omega_c^{(0)}\longrightarrow0.\]

The old horizon distinction and the physical clock belonging to that distinction disappear together.

That is the R²D-relevant observation.

25. The Appropriate Question Is No Longer “Why Does Recurrence Stop?”

Nothing in the calculation establishes that primitive recurrence stops.

What it establishes is

the old boundary can no longer support the same clock read.

The question therefore changes from

Why does time stop at Nariai?

to

What physical clock, if any, belongs to the replacement boundary classification?

26. This Is a Clock-Version of the Gibbs Problem

Addenda 44 and 45 identified a Gibbs-type classification error:

remove the defining multiplicity distinction + retain the old state classification.

The clock analogue is

remove the clock-defining boundary + retain its clock unchanged.

Once

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=0,\]

the old two-horizon clock should not automatically be extrapolated beyond the classification that defined it.

27. A Replacement Landscape Is Required

The exact Nariai limit is not most naturally represented by merely setting

\[r_b=r_c\]

inside an otherwise unchanged static coordinate system.

A rescaled limiting geometry gives the nontrivial Nariai spacetime.

Conventionally this has the product structure

\[\mathrm{dS}_2\times S^2.\]

The old static-patch coordinate degenerates while a finite limiting geometry remains.

This is precisely what boundary replacement should look like physically.

28. Replacement Does Not Restore the Old Horizon Distinction

The Nariai landscape does not recreate

\[r_b<r_c.\]

The old multiplicity gap remains closed.

Thus replacement does not mean

recover old two-horizon classification.

It means

new readable organization after old classification becomes marginal.

29. Bousso–Hawking Normalization Supplies a Different Clock Read

Normalize the static Killing field relative to the geodesic static reference:

\[\xi_{\mathrm{BH}}=\frac1{N_{\mathrm{stat}}}\partial_t.\]

Surface gravity rescales accordingly:

\[\kappa_h^{\mathrm{BH}}=\frac{\kappa_h^{(0)}}{N_{\mathrm{stat}}}.\]

This is not the same clock normalization as the unnormalized static-patch read.

30. The Normalized Ratio Has a Finite Limit

Since both numerator and denominator vanish linearly,

\[\lim_{q\to1}\frac{\kappa_b^{(0)}}{N_{\mathrm{stat}}}\]

exists.

Likewise for the cosmological horizon.

Using the leading-order forms,

\[\frac{(c^2\sqrt\Lambda/2)\epsilon}{\epsilon/2}=c^2\sqrt\Lambda.\]

Therefore,

\[\lim_{q\to1}\kappa_b^{\mathrm{BH}}=\lim_{q\to1}\kappa_c^{\mathrm{BH}}=c^2\sqrt\Lambda.\]

31. Define the Replacement Nariai Surface Gravity

The common finite limiting value is

\[\kappa_N=c^2\sqrt{\Lambda}.\]

This belongs to the boundary-normalized Nariai read.

It is not equal to either old unnormalized surface gravity at the limiting point, because those satisfy

\[\kappa_b^{(0)},\kappa_c^{(0)}\longrightarrow0.\]

32. The Replacement Phase-Frequency Read Is Finite

Convert the normalized surface gravity to a phase-frequency read:

\[\omega_N=\frac{\kappa_N}{2\pi c}.\]

Therefore,

\[\omega_N=\frac{c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi}.\]

This is the finite physical recurrence calibration of the replacement Nariai landscape.

33. The New Clock Is Not the Old Clock Rescued From Zero

The old clock satisfies

\[\omega_b^{(0)}\longrightarrow0,\qquad\omega_c^{(0)}\longrightarrow0.\]

The replacement read satisfies

\[\omega_N=\frac{c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi}\ne0.\]

There is no contradiction because the clock reads have different boundary owners.

Thus:

\[\omega_b^{(0)}\ne\omega_N\]

and

\[\omega_c^{(0)}\ne\omega_N\]

as boundary-defined physical quantities.

34. A Limiting Ratio Does Not Imply Continuity of Clock Identity

The mathematical relation

\[\omega_N=\lim\frac{\omega_h^{(0)}}{N_{\mathrm{stat}}}\]

does not mean that the unnormalized old clock remains the same physical object throughout the boundary replacement.

The ratio defines the limiting clock under a different normalization.

R²D therefore distinguishes:

continuity of a limiting relation

from

identity of the boundary-defined physical coordinate.

35. The Replacement Geometry Has Its Own Scale

At Nariai,

\[r_N=\frac1{\sqrt\Lambda}.\]

The replacement clock is

\[\omega_N=\frac{c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi}.\]

Therefore,

\[2\pi r_N\omega_N=c.\]

Equivalently,

\[r_N=\frac{c}{2\pi\omega_N}.\]

The replacement geometry and its physical phase-clock calibration are mutually consistent.

36. This Is a Physical Cycle–Phase Relation, Not a New Primitive R²D Law

The equation

\[2\pi r_N\omega_N=c\]

should not be reinterpreted as a primitive relation between R²D distinguishability and occurrence count.

It is already written in physical variables:

\[r_N,\quad\omega_N,\quad c.\]

It therefore belongs to the physical projection.

This is especially important because R²D does not contain a primitive law of the form

\[dr/d\lambda=2\pi\lambda.\]

No such relation is introduced here.

37. Nor Is This the Planck Radial-Distinction Relation

Addendum 43 proposed

\[\Delta r=\ell_P\]

as the physical calibration of one elementary radial macrostate distinction.

The Nariai relation

\[r_N=\frac{c}{2\pi\omega_N}\]

has a different meaning.

It relates the entire replacement horizon scale to a boundary-specific phase-frequency read.

Thus:

\[\Delta r=\ell_P\]

and

\[r_N=\frac{c}{2\pi\omega_N}\]

must remain distinct.

38. Temperature Is a Later Calibration Still

Only after the finite phase-frequency read is identified do we write

\[k_BT_N=\hbar\omega_N.\]

Therefore,

\[k_BT_N=\frac{\hbar c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi}.\]

And

\[T_N=\frac{\hbar c\sqrt\Lambda}{2\pi k_B}.\]

Temperature is the dimensional thermal read of the replacement clock.

It is not the primitive source of that clock.

39. Zero Old Temperature and Finite New Temperature Are Compatible

We therefore have

\[T_b^{(0)},T_c^{(0)}\longrightarrow0\]

while

\[T_N\ne0.\quad\text{[PDF callout clipped; author review required.]}\]

These statements belong to different physical clock normalizations and different boundary classifications.

Thus:

\[T^{(0)}\longrightarrow0\]

does not imply

\[T_N=0.\]
