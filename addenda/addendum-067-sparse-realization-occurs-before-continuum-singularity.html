Addendum 67 — Sparse Realization Occurs Before Continuum Singularity

Occurrence Resolution, Discrete Geometric Steps, and the Mathematical Signature of Continuum Breakdown

Abstract

Addendum 66 established that R2D denies the singular Big Bang as the primitive origin of reality.

The largest realized boundary is recursively realized through an infinitude of fundamental distinctions.

Physical area, volume, density, temperature, curved spacetime, and cosmological clock time arise only afterward as boundary-defined physical projections.

The FLRW singularity is therefore not the origin of recursive reality.

It is the singular endpoint obtained by extrapolating a later continuum cosmological projection toward

\[ A_\zeta \to 0\]

This leaves a constructive question:

What mathematical condition marks the point at which the smooth cosmological projection ceases to resolve the recursively realized count structure from which it is constructed?

R2D already contains the required quantities.

Addendum 58 defined clock-relative cosmological progress:

\[\mathcal H_{\zeta\mid c}=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dN_C^{(c)}}=\frac{d\ln a}{dN_C^{(c)}}\]

Here

\[ N_C^{(c)}\]

is the completed recurrence count of an admissible subordinate clock class.

Addendum 59 then showed, for a dominant coherent cosmological transition class χ,

\[\mathcal H_{\zeta\mid c}=\delta_{A,\chi}\rho_{\zeta\mid\zeta-1,c}\]

where

\[\delta_{A,\chi}=\frac12\ln\frac{A_{\zeta,k}}{A_{\zeta,i}}=\ln\frac{a_k}{a_i}\]

is the physical logarithmic scale step produced by one enclosing cosmological occurrence, and

\[\rho_{\zeta\mid\zeta-1,c}=\frac{N_\zeta^\chi}{N_C^{(c)}}\]

is the enclosing-occurrence/subordinate-recurrence ratio.

These quantities define a natural R2D continuum-resolution parameter:

\[ \epsilon_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\equiv\left|\frac{d\ln a}{dN_C^{(c)}}\right|=\left|\mathcal H_{\zeta\mid c}\right|. \]

For a single dominant coherent transition class,

\[ \epsilon_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=|\delta_{A,\chi}|\,\rho_{\zeta\mid\zeta-1,c}. \]

Its reciprocal is

\[ \mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\equiv\left|\frac{dN_C^{(c)}}{d\ln a}\right|=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}}. \]

The quantity

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\]

has a direct count interpretation.

It is the number of completed subordinate clock closures through which one order-one logarithmic change of projected cosmological scale is realized.

A smooth continuum approximation requires

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\gg1\]

or equivalently,

\[\epsilon_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\ll1\]

Many subordinate realizations then occur within one macroscopic geometric interval.

The individual count steps are unreadable in the physical continuum coordinate.

When instead

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=O(1)\]

the continuum interval is supported by only order-one subordinate closures.

The discrete realization path can no longer be replaced by a smooth differential trajectory without losing the count structure that generates it.

This is the R2D mathematical signature of continuum breakdown.

The resulting state is not one with few fundamental distinctions.

The largest gear remains recursively realized through an infinitude of fundamental distinctions.

The sparsity is resolution relative:

sparse realization = too few realized count updates per projected continuum interval.

It is the realized sampling of the multiplicity landscape that becomes sparse relative to the physical field coordinate.

The possible multiplicity

\[ W\]

need not become small.

Nor need primitive reality become spatially granular.

The continuum loses readability because its smooth coordinate now attempts to interpolate across distinguishable count steps rather than average over many of them.

Physical clock calibration makes the same criterion immediately visible.

Addendum 58 gave

\[ dt=T_c\,dN_C^{(c)}\]

with

\[ f_c=\frac1{T_c}=\frac{dN_C^{(c)}}{dt}\]

Therefore:

\[ H=f_c\mathcal H_{\zeta\mid c}\]

Hence:

\[ \epsilon_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=\frac{|H|}{f_c}. \]

And:

\[ \mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=\frac{f_c}{|H|}. \]

The continuum cosmological regime therefore requires

\[|H|\ll f_c\]

The cosmological boundary must evolve slowly relative to the subordinate recurrence through which that evolution is physically resolved.

Now compare this with the fixed FLRW singular extrapolation.

For a dominant positive sector with

\[ H^2=BA_\zeta^{-s},\qquad s>0\]

one has

\[|H|=\sqrt B\,A_\zeta^{-s/2}\]

If the subordinate clock calibration remains finite over the same count domain,

\[ f_c < \infty\]

then

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=\frac{f_c}{\sqrt B}A_\zeta^{s/2}\]

Therefore:

\[ A_\zeta\to0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\to0\]

The fixed continuum projection necessarily passes through

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\sim1\]

before it can reach

\[ A_\zeta = 0\]

Thus, for any finite subordinate recurrence calibration, continuum readability fails at finite projected area before the formal FLRW singularity.

There is only one way for the fixed continuum description to avoid this result.

The subordinate physical recurrence must itself increase without bound:

\[ f_c\to\infty\]

fast enough to maintain

\[|H|/f_c\ll1\]

But then the fixed clock calibration has itself failed.

The continuum has not been preserved by the same physical ruler.

It has been preserved only by moving to progressively faster subordinate recurrence structure.

That is precisely a recursive-boundary problem.

Therefore the singular extrapolation presents an R2D dichotomy:

\[|H|\to\infty\]

requires either

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=O(1)\]

and loss of continuum readability,

or

\[ f_c\to\infty\]

and loss of the fixed physical clock calibration.

In neither case does the same smooth physical projection remain unchanged all the way to the singularity.

This supplies the mathematical bridge sought in Addendum 66.

The continuum does not become primitive reality at its singular endpoint.

Its own count-resolution parameter announces the failure of the approximation first.

The central result is:

a continuum physical trajectory is readable only while many subordinate recurrent realizations occur within each macroscopic projected change. R2D quantifies this by \(\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\). When this count falls from \(\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\gg1\) to \(\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=O(1)\), the smooth field trajectory ceases to be a high-count approximation and becomes a sparsely realized multiplicity path. For any finite subordinate clock calibration, the fixed FLRW singular extrapolation necessarily crosses this discrete-resolution threshold before reaching zero area.

And hence:

sparse realization occurs before continuum singularity.

1. A Continuum Is a Read of Many Count Realizations

R2D does not take mathematical continuity as primitive.

A smooth physical trajectory represents recursively realized count change only when sufficiently many subordinate realizations occur below the physical resolution of the trajectory.

Thus:

many unresolved count steps → smooth physical read.

2. Continuity Is Therefore Resolution Relative

A trajectory can be continuous at one physical boundary while remaining discretely realized at a subordinate boundary.

There is no contradiction.

The enclosing boundary does not resolve every subordinate count step.

3. The Cosmological Continuum Must Obey the Same Rule

The FLRW trajectory

\[ a(t)\]

is a smooth physical read.

R2D therefore asks how many subordinate recurrent realizations support a distinguishable change in

\[ a\]

4. Begin With Clock-Relative Progress

Addendum 58 defined

\[\mathcal H_{\zeta\mid c}=\frac{d\ln a}{dN_C^{(c)}}\]

This quantity is dimensionless.

It exists before seconds are assigned to the subordinate recurrence.

5. It Measures Geometric Progress Per Subordinate Closure

If

\[|\mathcal H_{\zeta\mid c}|\ll1\]

then one subordinate closure changes the projected logarithmic cosmological scale only slightly.

Many closures are required for a macroscopic geometric change.

6. Define the Continuum-Resolution Parameter

Let

\[\epsilon_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\equiv|\mathcal H_{\zeta\mid c}|\]

This is the projected geometric change per subordinate recurrent realization.

7. Define the Continuum-Support Count

Its reciprocal is

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\equiv\frac1{\epsilon_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}}=\left|\frac{dN_C^{(c)}}{d\ln a}\right|\]

8. Ncont Is a Count, Not a Density

It does not count objects per physical volume.

It counts realized subordinate recurrences per logarithmic physical-scale change.

Thus it remains consistent with the R2D distinction between primitive counting and downstream spatial density.

9. The Continuum Regime Is

\[\mathcal N_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\gg1\]

Many subordinate realizations then contribute to one resolved geometric change.

10. Equivalently

\[\epsilon_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\ll1\]

The projected trajectory changes only slightly per subordinate count update.

11. The Sparse Realization Regime Begins When

Now one order-one physical scale change is supported by only order-one subordinate recurrent closures.

12. Equivalently

\[\epsilon_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}=O(1)\]

The physical trajectory can no longer hide its discrete realization behind a large-count average.

13. This Is Not Fundamental Sparsity

R2D does not say that only a few fundamental distinctions now exist.

The largest realized gear remains supported by an infinitude of fundamental distinctions.

The sparse quantity is the number of realized transitions participating in the specified projected interval.

For one cosmological transition class,

\[\delta_{A,\chi}=\ln\frac{a_k}{a_i}\]

\[\epsilon_{\mathrm{cont}}^{(c)}\ll1\]
