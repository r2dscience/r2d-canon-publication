Canonical basis: R²D Parts I–IV (September 25, 2026); Addenda 35–36, 105, 131–134, 139–142.

Abstract

Velocity, invariant mass, electromagnetic propagation speed, rest energy, and kinetic energy are often introduced as properties of particles or as independent mechanical primitives. Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) distinguishes their logical owners. Native distinguishability and complete compatibility belong to enclosing count boundaries. A background supplies the physical reference, ruler, and clock; a mass-bearing boundary has an orientation-even coupling read; and the relative motion of that boundary is classified through the enclosing spatial–recurrence relationship. A symmetric background may sustain positive reciprocal activity with zero net directed output. This does not make the sum of reciprocal turns an invariant mass or make background recurrence identical to electromagnetic radiation.

This addendum consolidates the physical mapping. The propagation speed of light is a background-calibrated read of an electromagnetic propagation class; its universal dimensional value is not derived from bare counts. The physical invariant coupling of a mass-bearing boundary acquires the zero-momentum energetic read \(E_0=mc^2\). Changing the background-relative motion read changes total energy but not invariant mass, giving \(K=(\gamma-1)mc^2\) and the nonrelativistic limit \(K\simeq mv^2/2\). For a common enclosing response owner, the native vector of distinguishable oriented incidences is exactly additive under disjoint partition. A selected additive reciprocal response valuation has a nonadditive paired norm, reproducing the algebra of composite invariant mass under independently stated physical calibration. The addendum distinguishes this exact count mathematics from the remaining physical problem of selecting and realizing a unique weighted response valuation.

143.1. The question and the order of definition

R²D Part I defines a formal microstate as one compatible joint realization of subordinate peers under an enclosing boundary. Its native multiplicity \(W_{B,i}\) and entropy \(S_{B,i}=\ln W_{B,i}\) are count structures, not intrinsically measured distances, times, energies, or masses. Complete enclosing compatibility establishes which joint distinctions and recurrences can be compared. Subordinate events realize those relations; they do not independently define the multiplicity landscape.

Part IV distinguishes a local orientation-even boundary coupling from a background-relative direction and motion read. Addendum 142 makes this separation explicit for velocity. The present purpose is to identify the corresponding positions of \(m\), \(c\), \(mc^2\), and kinetic energy in one coherent physical representation. It is not to re-derive the complete Lorentz theory or to postulate a previously undiscovered microscopic mechanism.

The organizing order is

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered} \text{Enclosing compatible distinction and symmetric recurrence}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Boundary coupling and background reference}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Relative-motion and propagation reads}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Calibrated energetic reads: }mc^2\text{ and }K. \end{gathered}} \tag{143.1} \]

143.2. Symmetric recurrence is activity without directed output

A complete realized record \(h\) for one turbine may admit two oppositely oriented completed-return counts, \(N_+(h)\) and \(N_-(h)\). Addendum 131 defines

\[ N_{\mathrm{even}}=N_++N_-,\qquad N_{\mathrm{odd}}=N_+-N_-. \tag{143.2} \]

Under orientation reversal \(\sigma\), the former is invariant and the latter reverses sign. An admitted, reciprocally realized record may satisfy

\[ N_+=N_->0,\qquad N_{\mathrm{odd}}=0,\qquad N_{\mathrm{even}}>0. \tag{143.3} \]

Thus zero directed output does not imply zero completed recurrence or absence of a realized background. These are counts of completed turbine returns: they are not native possible multiplicities \(W\), state occurrence counts \(\nu\), the realized scalar background \(b\), or the independently specified background recurrence \(N_J^\Phi\). Nor are they electromagnetic photon counts or observed spectral frequencies.

The term background radiation in the reciprocal thermal-turbine discussion means reversible absorption and emission of background-native recurrence. Electromagnetic radiation is a particular later physical projection, not the primitive definition of that background.

The gravitational-horizon question concerns which relation remains readable when enclosing organizing recurrence does not. It does not follow that the surviving mass coupling equals \(N_{\mathrm{even}}\) or any prescribed function of those turn counts.

143.3. Mass: an orientation-even coupling of a selected boundary

Part IV assigns a selected physical boundary \(B\) a generic coupling magnitude \(\kappa_B\). When the enclosing read identifies two opposed subordinate orientations, the corresponding orientation index is replaced while an orientation-even coupling class may remain distinguishable:

\[ \pi_{+}(\mathcal C^+)=\pi_{+}(\mathcal C^-) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{orientation is not retained in that read}. \tag{143.4} \]

The proposed mass projection is the physical magnitude read of such an orientation-even boundary coupling:

\[ \boxed{\kappa_B\ \overset{\text{physical mass read}}{\longmapsto}\ m_B.} \tag{143.5} \]

Equation (143.5) is not the assertion that the dimensionless native count \(\kappa_B\) has already been uniquely selected or numerically calibrated. Mass is an invariant mechanical coupling of a specified complete physical system. It is not an independently conserved substance copied from subordinate boundaries. A new enclosing boundary can have a mass different from the arithmetic sum of subordinate rest masses.

For a calibrated physical energy–momentum read in flat spacetime,

\[ \boxed{m_B^2c^4=E_B^2-c^2|\mathbf p_B|^2.} \tag{143.6} \]

This equation is the conventional measurement of the invariant coupling, not the primitive R²D definition of its native count magnitude. The physical mass read is available wherever an appropriate complete-system coupling is defined; one need not be at an astrophysical event horizon to measure mass.

143.4. The speed of light: background-owned propagation calibration

Addendum 141 defines a complete enclosing spatial–recurrence comparison by dimensionless signed spatial distinction \(\ell_J\) and positive recurrence comparison count \(n_J\). Its physical motion projection is

\[ v_J=\frac{L_J}{\tau_J}\frac{\ell_J}{n_J}, \tag{143.7} \]

where \(L_J\) and \(\tau_J\) are supplied by the observing background. Neither originates in a subordinate moving object.

For a physical electromagnetic propagation class whose corresponding dimensionless quotient is \(q_{Q,\mathrm{null}}\), the analogous background calibration reads

\[ \boxed{c_\Phi=\frac{L_\Phi}{\tau_\Phi}q_{Q,\mathrm{null}}.} \tag{143.8} \]

The designation null describes the independently specified physical class of electromagnetic propagation. Merely defining the quotient does not prove nullity or identify the physical electromagnetic field. The familiar wave relation \(c=\lambda f\) is a downstream physical representation involving a measured wavelength and frequency; it is not an equality of an R²D turbine-return count with a photon count.

In the R²D interpretation, \(c\) is a propagation-speed read of the enclosing spatial–recurrence relation, not the intrinsic velocity of a primitive traveling photon and not a material-medium velocity relative to an ether. The observed invariant vacuum speed and its value are supplied by independently established electromagnetic and inertial-frame calibrations. Primitive dimensionless compatibility alone does not determine meters per second or the universal physical calibration.

143.5. Velocity: motion relative to a background

Addendum 142 distinguishes ordinal peer comparison from the enclosing magnitude landscape. If \(r_a\) denotes a selected peer’s relative rank and \(g_\Phi\) is the dimensionless motion-class quantile supplied by complete compatibility, its physical one-axis velocity read is

\[ \boxed{v_{a|\Phi}=u_\Phi^{\mathrm{ref}}+ \frac{L_\Phi}{\tau_\Phi}g_\Phi(r_a).} \tag{143.9} \]

A finite observed rank must not be equated automatically with a possible-count percentile; the appropriate realization relation is required. The essential point is that the reference, physical scale, and compatible magnitude classification all belong to the enclosure. Velocity does not originate as an isolated molecule’s background-independent property.

In the separately calibrated relativistic physical representation of Addenda 35–36, background-relative velocity is written using rapidity \(\xi\):

\[ \boxed{\beta=\frac vc=\tanh\xi,\qquad \gamma=\cosh\xi=(1-\beta^2)^{-1/2}.} \tag{143.10} \]

Equation (143.10) is not asserted to follow from the uncalibrated rank function \(g_\Phi\) for every physical background. It is the relativistic motion read to which a valid R²D propagation/reference mapping must correspond.

143.6. One reciprocal physical representation yields \(mc^2\)

Let a mass-bearing physical boundary have positive reciprocal mechanical response coordinates \(\Pi_+,\Pi_-\) under a background-relative rapidity read. Addendum 36 considers

\[ \Pi_\pm=\Pi_0e^{\pm a\xi},\qquad a>0, \tag{143.11} \]

and defines orientation-odd and orientation-even mechanical coordinates

\[ p=\frac{\Pi_+-\Pi_-}{2},\qquad Q=\frac{\Pi_++\Pi_-}{2}. \tag{143.12} \]

With the independent physical calibrations \(m=(dp/dv)_{v=0}\), \(E=bcQ\), and \(dE=v\,dp\), Addendum 36 obtains \(a=b=1\) and \(\Pi_0=mc\). Hence

\[ \boxed{\Pi_\pm=mc\,e^{\pm\xi},\qquad p=mc\sinh\xi,\qquad E=mc^2\cosh\xi.} \tag{143.13} \]

The reciprocal norm does not depend on the motion reference:

\[ \boxed{\Pi_+\Pi_-=m^2c^2,\qquad E^2-p^2c^2=m^2c^4.} \tag{143.14} \]

At zero background-relative rapidity \(\xi=0\), momentum vanishes but the invariant coupling remains positive:

\[ \boxed{p=0,\qquad E_0=mc^2.} \tag{143.15} \]

Thus \(mc^2\) is the zero-momentum energetic representation of the invariant mass-bearing boundary coupling. It is not proof that a stationary object has no recurrence or that its entire rest energy is externally frame-dependent kinetic energy. The two physical calibrations of Addendum 36 are explicit premises, not consequences of bare native counts.

143.7. Kinetic energy is the additional relative-motion energetic read

For the same complete mass-bearing physical system, observed from a reference with background-relative speed \(v\), Equation (143.13) gives

\[ E=\gamma mc^2. \]

Define the difference from the zero-momentum energetic read:

\[ \boxed{K_\Phi(v)=E-E_0=(\gamma-1)mc^2.} \tag{143.16} \]

For \(|v|\ll c\),

\[ \gamma=1+\frac12\frac{v^2}{c^2}+O(v^4/c^4), \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{K_\Phi(v)=\tfrac12mv^2+O(mv^4/c^2).} \tag{143.17} \]

This result gives kinetic energy its R²D physical meaning: an energetic difference associated with a change in the motion read relative to a specified background, rather than an additional primitive energy substance inside the object.

At a jointly readable thermal boundary, the corresponding classical molecular fluctuation read is

\[ K_{a|\Phi}^{\mathrm{thermal}}=\tfrac12m_a|\mathbf v_a-\mathbf u_\Phi|^2, \qquad \langle K^{\mathrm{thermal}}\rangle =\tfrac12m_a\langle|\mathbf v_a-\mathbf u_\Phi|^2\rangle. \tag{143.18} \]

This is a physical, ensemble-relative kinetic-energy read. Its distribution and thermal calibration belong to the complete enclosing statistical organization; its existence does not imply that independently energetic microscopic trajectories generate the background. In particular, the classical thermal expression and the relativistic bulk kinetic-energy difference refer to different specified physical comparisons.

143.8. Composition: additive response, nonadditive mass

The new native mathematical contribution is a composition distinction. Fix one admitted complete enclosing record \(h\). Let \(\mathcal I_J(h)\) be its finite set of distinguishable response incidences, each classified by a native type \(t\) and orientation \(s=\pm\). Define the exact count vector

\[ \mathbf n_J(h)=(n_{t,s}(h))_{t,s},\qquad n_{t,s}=|\mathcal I_{t,s}(h)|. \tag{143.19} \]

Theorem 143.1 — Additivity of native response incidence. If one complete admitted record has a disjoint, exhaustive incidence partition \(\mathcal I_J=\mathcal I_A\sqcup\mathcal I_B\), then

\[ \boxed{\mathbf n_J=\mathbf n_A+\mathbf n_B.} \tag{143.20} \]

Proof. Every incidence of each type and orientation belongs to exactly one partition class. Its cardinalities therefore add coordinatewise. \(\square\)

The theorem does not require the separately possible records of \(A\) and \(B\) to be independent, and it does not imply a dynamical conservation law across different occurrences. It concerns one valid complete comparison. To turn native incidences into a magnitude-bearing response, one may select common coefficients \(w_{t,s}\) and define \(R_{J,s}=\sum_t w_{t,s}n_{t,s}\). This response is also additive across the same partition. The bare R²D compatibility relation does not uniquely select those physical weights; even extension-count weights are only candidate valuations until their physical read is justified.

For a selected nonnegative additive two-orientation response \(R_\pm\), define the orientation-even paired norm

\[ \mu_J=2\sqrt{R_{J,+}R_{J,-}}. \tag{143.21} \]

Theorem 143.2 — Nonadditive paired composition. If \(R_{J,\pm}=R_{A,\pm}+R_{B,\pm}\), then

\[ \boxed{\mu_J^2=\mu_A^2+\mu_B^2+ 4\bigl(R_{A,+}R_{B,-}+R_{B,+}R_{A,-}\bigr).} \tag{143.22} \]

Proof. Expand \(4(R_{A,+}+R_{B,+})(R_{A,-}+R_{B,-})\) and group the two within-subdomain products. \(\square\)

The cross terms reflect the complete enclosing norm of the combined response, not necessarily a new physical force or binding energy. Two one-sided null responses \(R_A=(r,0)\) and \(R_B=(0,s)\) each have \(\mu=0\), yet their complete enclosure has \(\mu_J=2\sqrt{rs}>0\).

Under a common, independently established one-axis physical response calibration

\[ E_J=\varepsilon_\Phi(R_{J,+}+R_{J,-}),\qquad cp_J=\varepsilon_\Phi(R_{J,+}-R_{J,-}), \tag{143.23} \]

both energy and momentum add for that complete partition, while

\[ \boxed{M_J=\frac{\varepsilon_\Phi}{c^2}\mu_J} \tag{143.24} \]

is generally nonadditive. In more than one spatial dimension, the conventional complete-system relation becomes \(M_J^2c^4=E_J^2-c^2|\mathbf p_J|^2\); one pair of collinear response coordinates does not by itself derive its full angular structure.

Importantly, binding, emission, and changing enclosure require a fresh complete physical response accounting, including field and outgoing radiation contributions. Additivity within one admitted partition is not conservation through a transformation, and a bound subsystem need not retain the invariant mass of the larger isolated system.

143.9. Complementary readability and the limits of identification

At the quantum-side readability horizon, a spectral or propagation recurrence can remain readable after its subordinate continuously resolved distinctions are lost. At the gravitational-side readability horizon, a mass-bearing or geometric distinction can remain readable after the selected enclosing organizing recurrence is no longer jointly accessible. Neither horizon implies that all physical clocks or motions stop.

If \(q_G:\mathcal H_J\to\mathcal G\) is a physically specified gravitational read and \(\kappa_J\) is a proposed native coupling invariant of complete records, it survives the receiving read only when

\[ \boxed{q_G(h)=q_G(h')\ \Longrightarrow\ \kappa_J(h)=\kappa_J(h').} \tag{143.25} \]

To claim that the same nontrivial invariant survives both quantum and gravitational reads, the corresponding fiber condition must also hold for the quantum map \(q_Q\), as established in Addendum 132. A readable mass classification does not uniquely reveal the complete reciprocal recurrence history.

The exact mathematical results of this addendum are (i) additive incidence composition for one complete admitted record and (ii) nonadditivity of a selected paired norm. Existing physical results establish the mapping among \(c\), \(mc^2\), relativistic kinetic energy, and composite invariant mass under independently specified physical calibrations.

The following are not primitive-count theorems established here: that symmetric recurrence uniquely fixes a nonzero gross recurrence rate; that an unweighted \(N_+N_-\) is universally mass; that a chosen extension-weight valuation uniquely reads physical energy; that one background count ratio by itself fixes the universal dimensional value of \(c\); or that the full Lorentz and gravitational dynamics arise from native counts without physical input. The null propagation class, reciprocal mechanical representation, and energetic mass calibration remain explicit physical mapping conditions.

143.10. Conclusion

R²D assigns three familiar physical quantities to distinct but compatible relational roles:

\[ \boxed{\begin{aligned} \text{Mass }m &: \text{orientation-even coupling read of a complete boundary},\\ \text{Light speed }c &: \text{background-calibrated electromagnetic propagation read},\\ \text{Kinetic energy }K &: \text{additional energetic read of relative motion}. \end{aligned}} \tag{143.26} \]

The zero-momentum energy \(mc^2\) is the invariant coupling’s energetic calibration; the relativistic kinetic-energy difference \(K=(\gamma-1)mc^2\) and its low-speed limit \(mv^2/2\) follow when that same system is compared under a nonzero motion read. Positive symmetric recurrence may sustain a complete organization with no net directional output. A null one-sided propagation read may have zero individual invariant mass while two appropriately composed null responses have positive enclosing mass.

The enclosing relationship defines the invariant coupling and the allowed relative comparisons; the background supplies the physical ruler, clock, and reference; subordinate realizations make those relationships observable. Additive native incidence composition and nonadditive invariant norms are compatible parts of this architecture. This addendum consolidates their correspondence with successful conventional physics without converting the still-open native physical-calibration problem into an assumed proof.
