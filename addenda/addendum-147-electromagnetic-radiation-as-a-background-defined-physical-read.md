## Abstract

Electromagnetic radiation is conventionally described using propagating electric and magnetic fields, electromagnetic waves, or photons. These descriptions correctly reproduce measured physical relationships, but they begin downstream of the dimensionless count architecture developed in Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D).

Addendum 145 establishes propagation as an enclosing compatibility relation among recurring distinctions. Addendum 146 introduces the primitive quantum-background hypothesis: a medium of binary distinctions whose compatible local recurrence can be symmetric, with possibility and retention changing reciprocally through completed closure. Enclosing possibility defines the admissible continuation of that recurrence before physical space, time, fields, or particles are introduced.

The present addendum maps that architecture onto electromagnetic radiation.

The organizing sequence begins with binary directional distinguishability. The multiplicity of distinctions defines the native directional class, while the enclosing background determines its magnitude. A compatible possibility is realized as an occurrence within that background. Physical velocity is a subsequent measurement of this background-defined relationship, rather than a primitive quotient of distance and elapsed time.

A complete source–background–receiver compatibility provides the corresponding observational construction. A source oscillator admits reciprocal local recurrence. The enclosing quantum background defines a coherent propagation class. A receiving oscillator can realize a compatible recurrence with a source-relative phase relationship. More generally, a detector can register a new readable macrostate without resolving the faster or smaller subordinate recurrence.

The source and receiver need not share identical native oscillation rates. Their relative phases and frequencies may change while the complete enclosing propagation classification remains unchanged.

The electromagnetic physical mapping identifies a selected quantum-background propagation class with the vacuum null class. Under the appropriate calibration, its characteristic speed is $c$, and admissible inertial reference changes preserve that physical propagation relationship. Wavelength and frequency are complementary downstream measurements satisfying $\lambda f=c$.

The cyclic recurrence admits the phase representation developed in Addendum 72. Locally differing phase references require the comparison connection of Addendum 78, whose curvature is developed in Addendum 79. Under the source-free electromagnetic physical mapping, the curvature possesses transverse electric and magnetic readings satisfying Maxwell's vacuum propagation equations.

Detector realization remains a separate count relation. The incoming recurrence changes the compatible possibility and retention landscape of the detector boundary. The detector realizes a new readable distinction, rather than reconstructing an invisible particle trajectory.

These correspondences demonstrate that the primitive R²D organization of Addendum 146 admits a coherent physical mapping onto electromagnetic propagation, source–receiver recurrence, polarization, and detection. The mapping does not derive every electromagnetic constitutive or calibration law from primitive counting alone.
