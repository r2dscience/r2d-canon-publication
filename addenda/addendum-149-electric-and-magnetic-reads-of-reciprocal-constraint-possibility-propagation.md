## Abstract

R²D Addendum 145 constructs a propagating count distinction whose complete multiplicity remains unchanged. Addendum 146 establishes reciprocal local possibility–retention closure with zero accumulated asymmetry. Addendum 147 exhibits an admissible electromagnetic phase-connection and transverse plane-wave physical projection. Their remaining point of contact is precise: can *canonical enclosing possibility and retention*, rather than a retrospectively assumed electromagnetic potential, supply a common primitive response whose temporal and spatial comparisons admit electric and magnetic readings?

We give an explicit finite consistency construction. Five binary peer regions belong to one enclosing quantum-background owner admitting exactly one singleton classification. Its eighty native realizations determine local compatible extension counts, hence canonical possibility. A specified positive enclosing productive constraint makes the local possibility and retention changes reciprocal. An admitted cyclic continuation moves the singleton classification through the five regions while every local state returns and its complete closure has zero net asymmetry. The retained-response contrast satisfies the exact native identity

$$\Delta_nP_j=\nabla_-R_j.$$

A common transverse response valuation has two derived differences, $e_j=-\Delta_nR_j$ and $b_j=\nabla_-R_j$. Under the traveling compatibility they are equal and obey paired, source-free, one-dimensional *finite-difference* propagation identities. Their positive quadratic norm is constant under the enclosing continuation and equals $2(\ln2)^2$ in the five-region system. Its two nonconstant Fourier harmonics give an exact decomposition of this invariant.

A selected downstream transverse connection read $\mathcal A_{\mathrm{EM}}=g_QR\,dy$ maps these count differences to electric and magnetic field components. When the receiving background assigns ruler and clock calibrations and the vacuum electromagnetic response relation, the two fields have one common propagation amplitude, equal calibrated field-energy contributions, and positive forward energy flux. A monochromatic harmonic and its quantum $hf$ energetic calibration require an explicitly selected physical mode and normalization; an uncalibrated singleton is not automatically a photon. The construction establishes a concrete bridge between canonical R²D counting and a compatible electromagnetic plane-wave read, not a universal identification of $R$ with vector potential or a derivation of the complete Maxwell system from binary counting alone.

## 149.5. A native traveling compatibility identity

Define the singleton indicator and its retention valuation:

$$
\rho_j(n)=\begin{cases}1,&j=n\pmod5,\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
\qquad
R_j(n)=d\rho_j(n),
\qquad
P_j(n)=\ln32-R_j(n).
\tag{149.11}
$$

Here $n$ is a native comparison index, **not physical elapsed time**. The admitted positive traversal satisfies

$$
\boxed{\rho_j(n+1)=\rho_{j-1}(n),}
\tag{149.12}
$$

with cyclic indices. Define finite differences on the complete comparison record:

$$
\Delta_n f_j(n)=f_j(n+1)-f_j(n),
\qquad
\nabla_- f_j(n)=f_j(n)-f_{j-1}(n).
\tag{149.13}
$$

Then, exactly,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_nP_j(n)
&=-\big(R_j(n+1)-R_j(n)\big)\\
&=R_j(n)-R_{j-1}(n)\\
&=\nabla_-R_j(n).
\end{aligned}
$$

**Theorem 149.1 — Canonical possibility–retention propagation identity.** For the specified complete five-region compatibility with possibility (149.5), retention (149.8), and one-step traveling continuation (149.12),

$$
\boxed{\Delta_nP_j=\nabla_-R_j.}
\tag{149.14}
$$

**Proof.** The fixed local sum (149.9) gives $\Delta_nP_j=-\Delta_nR_j$. The admitted traveling relation gives $\Delta_nR_j=R_{j-1}-R_j=-\nabla_-R_j$. Combine them. $\square$

This is the added mathematical bridge. It relates *canonical across-boundary possibility* to a *spatial comparison of canonical retained response*, before assigning electromagnetic physical coordinates.

## 149.6. Exact transverse-response difference identities

Define two dimensionless **comparison reads** of the same retained coordinate:

$$
\boxed{e_j(n)=-\Delta_nR_j(n)=\Delta_nP_j(n),
\qquad b_j(n)=\nabla_-R_j(n).}
\tag{149.15}

By Theorem 149.1,

$$
\boxed{e_j(n)=b_j(n).}
\tag{149.16}
$$

$$
\Delta_nb_j=-\nabla_-e_j.
\tag{149.17}

Moreover, because $b_j(n+1)=b_{j-1}(n)$ and $e=b$,

$$
\Delta_ne_j=-\nabla_-b_j.
\tag{149.18}

**Theorem 149.2 — Paired source-free transverse difference relations.** The complete traveling count compatibility admits a pair of response reads satisfying

$$
\boxed{\Delta_nb+\nabla_-e=0,
\qquad \Delta_ne+\nabla_-b=0.}
\tag{149.19}
$$

**Proof.** The first equality follows from the common scalar response and commuting differences. The second follows from the exact one-sided translation of the response together with $e=b$. $\square$

These are **dimensionless one-direction finite-difference identities**. Their electromagnetic interpretation requires a transverse component assignment and physical calibration. They are not the full three-dimensional Maxwell equations or a universal consequence of any arbitrary $P$–$R$ cycle.

## 149.7. A positive quadratic count invariant

The local retained coordinate is reference-dependent up to an additive constant. Its differences are not. Define

$$
\boxed{\mathcal N_{J_Q}(n)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z_5}\big(\nabla_-R_j(n)\big)^2.}
\tag{149.20}
$$

In the one-singleton profile, the differences have one $+d$, one $-d$, and three zeros. Thus

$$
\boxed{\mathcal N_{J_Q}=2d^2=2(\ln2)^2>0.}
\tag{149.21}
$$

**Theorem 149.3 — Positive conserved transverse-response norm.** Under the cyclic traveling compatibility, $\mathcal N_{J_Q}(n+1)=\mathcal N_{J_Q}(n)$; it is strictly positive for the nonconstant one-singleton record and unchanged under adding a constant reference to $R$.

**Proof.** The traveling map cyclically permutes the five differences, preserving the sum of their squares. The selected difference vector has two nonzero entries $+d,-d$. A common constant added to $R$ cancels under $\nabla_-$. $\square$

This quadratic invariant is distinct from the conserved total multiplicity deficit of Addendum 145:

$$
\mathcal D_{J_Q}=\sum_j\ln\frac{2}{W(x_j)}=d.
\tag{149.22}
$$

They agree neither in units nor in general functional form: $\mathcal D_{J_Q}=d$, while $\mathcal N_{J_Q}=2d^2$. The latter resolves the *interfaces* of the constrained pattern and is a useful candidate for a quadratic field-response read. Their conservation is conditional on the same specified enclosing traveling organization.

An exact local continuity law also exists at the integer-incidence level. With $z_{j+1/2}(n)=\rho_j(n)\in\{0,1\}$,

\boxed{\rho_j(n+1)-\rho_j(n)=z_{j-1/2}(n)-z_{j+1/2}(n).}
\tag{149.23}
$$

Multiplying by $d$ gives the corresponding logarithmic retention valuation. Thus integer completed interface incidence and its real-valued response calibration remain distinct count types.

## 149.8. The pulse is not yet a monochromatic plane wave

The traveling singleton is a finite, periodic propagating **pulse-like count profile**. Its native five-comparison period does not by itself make it a pure sinusoidal mode.

Its exact cyclic Fourier decomposition is

$$
\boxed{
R_j(n)=\frac d5\left[
1+2\cos\frac{2\pi(j-n)}5+2\cos\frac{4\pi(j-n)}5
\right].}
\tag{149.24}
$$

The constant component contributes no spatial gradient. Denote the two nonconstant harmonics by $R^{(1)}$ and $R^{(2)}$. Orthogonality and periodic translation give

$$
\mathcal N_{J_Q}=\mathcal N_1+\mathcal N_2,
\tag{149.25}
$$

where

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal N_s=\frac85d^2\sin^2\frac{\pi s}{5},\quad s=1,2,
\qquad
\mathcal N_1+\mathcal N_2=2d^2.}
\tag{149.26}
$$

**Proposition 149.4 — Conserved harmonic decomposition.** The positive quadratic invariant of the five-region pulse decomposes into two positive, separately translation-invariant harmonic gradient norms, with no cross term in their complete cyclic sum.

**Proof.** The two nonconstant discrete Fourier modes are orthogonal on $\mathbb Z_5$. For $R^{(s)}_j=(2d/5)\cos(2\pi s(j-n)/5)$, evaluating the squared backward differences gives $(8/5)d^2\sin^2(\pi s/5)$. The identity $\sin^2(\pi/5)+\sin^2(2\pi/5)=5/4$ gives the stated sum. $\square$

This identifies which additional selection is needed for a **plane-wave** read: the enclosing preparation or receiving projection must distinguish a selected harmonic mode. Fourier projection is a *read of the already admitted complete count pattern*, not the assertion that its signed harmonic values are themselves the native logarithmic possibilities of a new unconstrained binary landscape.

## 149.9. The transverse electromagnetic connection as a downstream read

Consider one selected physical transverse polarization, with propagation along $x$ and the connection component along $y$. In a selected scalar-potential-free radiation gauge, use the **physical-read ansatz**

$$
\boxed{\mathcal A_{\mathrm{EM}}=g_Q R\,dy,\qquad \varphi_{\mathrm{EM}}=0.}
\tag{149.27}
$$

Here $R$ is the selected compatible response profile or its harmonic projection. The coefficient $g_Q$ carries the dimensions and amplitude calibration required by the quantum background. Neither a physical vector potential nor a spatial direction was primitive in Sections 149.2–149.8.

Let the background assign address separation $L_Q>0$ and recurrence duration $\tau_Q>0$. The cell-comparison field reads are

$$
\boxed{
E_{y,j}=\frac{g_Q}{\tau_Q}\Delta_nP_j
=-\frac{g_Q}{\tau_Q}\Delta_nR_j,
\qquad
B_{z,j}=\frac{g_Q}{L_Q}\nabla_-R_j.}
\tag{149.28}
$$

These finite differences are *cell-comparison readings*, conventionally staggered across address and recurrence boundaries; they are not claimed to be identical point samples at one continuous spacetime coordinate before interpolation.

By Theorem 149.1,

$$
\boxed{E_{y,j}=c_QB_{z,j},\qquad c_Q\equiv L_Q/\tau_Q.}
\tag{149.29}
$$

The common $g_Q$ fixes the relative amplitude; the background's spatial and recurrence calibrations fix the physical ratio. The transverse $y$ and $z$ directions are supplied by the selected electromagnetic spatial projection, not by interpreting $P$ and $R$ as two perpendicular native axes.

The physical electromagnetic curvature is

$$
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{EM}}=d\mathcal A_{\mathrm{EM}}.
\tag{149.30}
$$

For a smooth selected harmonic representation $R(x,t)=r_0+a\cos(kx-\omega t)$, the same ansatz gives

$$
E_y=-g_Q a\omega\sin(kx-\omega t),
\qquad
B_z=-g_Q ak\sin(kx-\omega t).
\tag{149.31}
$$

If the calibrated traveling class obeys $\omega=c_Qk$, then $E_y=c_QB_z$. The two radiative field components are **in phase**, even though the underlying native $P$ and $R$ coordinates change reciprocally: the field components read *differences* of those coordinates, not their absolute levels.

The smooth curvature automatically satisfies $d\mathcal F_{\mathrm{EM}}=0$. The complementary source-free Maxwell relation follows for the selected harmonic when the vacuum physical response and null propagation conditions are applied, as in Addendum 147. An additive reference shift $R\mapsto R+r_*$ leaves these differences unchanged. That limited reference invariance does not replace the full local $U(1)$ phase-connection transformations of Addenda 78–79.

Thus the equal electric and magnetic energetic contributions follow from **one common response**, its two propagation comparisons, and the specified vacuum calibration. No primitive fifty–fifty energetic partition is imposed.

In particular, the single constrained binary singleton is **not automatically one photon**; a number-state energy calibration does not imply a nonzero classical mean electric field in a one-quantum state.

$$

=C_J\lambda_JN_{\chi_J}.

Our finite positive quadratic invariant concerns spatial comparison within $J_Q$; it does not repeal or bypass (149.39).

Likewise, conservation of the selected positive response under an admitted complete record is not universal energy conservation across arbitrary changes of boundary. The relevant energetic closure requires the chosen electromagnetic response and calibration.
