Addendum 45 — Multiplicity-Gap Closure Occurs Before the Nariai Limit

Abstract

Addendum 44 proposed a generic R²D condition for gravitational saturation.

Let a subordinate gravitational boundary have multiplicity

\[W_b,\]

and let its enclosing gravitational boundary have multiplicity

\[W_c.\]

Define their logarithmic multiplicity gap

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=S_c-S_b=\ln\frac{W_c}{W_b}.\]

The nested classification remains multiplicatively distinct while

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}>0.\]

Its marginal condition is

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=0.\]

Then

\[W_b=W_c.\]

If both boundaries share the same horizon-area calibration,

\[S=\frac A{4\ell_P^2},\]

the same condition is physically read as

\[A_b=A_c.\]

Addendum 44 did not identify a physical system that satisfies this relation. It deliberately stopped before assigning a maximum mass.

Schwarzschild–de Sitter geometry provides an exact independent test.

For positive cosmological constant Λ, the static projection is

\[ds^2=-f(r)c^2dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2d\Omega^2,\]

where

\[f(r)=1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r}-\frac{\Lambda r^2}{3}.\]

Within its nonextremal two-horizon regime, the equation

\[f(r)=0\]

has two positive roots,

\[r_b<r_c,\]

representing the black-hole and cosmological horizon reads.

They are not independent surfaces inserted into a fixed container. They are two roots of one shared gravitational equation.

Their exact coupling implies

\[r_b^2+r_br_c+r_c^2=\frac3\Lambda.\]

Define

\[q\equiv\frac{r_b}{r_c},\qquad0\le q\le1.\]

Then

\[r_c^2=\frac3{\Lambda(1+q+q^2)},\]

and

\[r_b^2=\frac{3q^2}{\Lambda(1+q+q^2)}.\]

The corresponding areas are

\[A_c=\frac{12\pi}{\Lambda(1+q+q^2)},\]

and

\[A_b=\frac{12\pi q^2}{\Lambda(1+q+q^2)}.\]

Therefore,

\[\frac{A_b}{A_c}=q^2.\]

Because both horizons share the same Bekenstein–Hawking calibration,

\[S_b=\ln W_b=\frac{A_b}{4\ell_P^2},\]

and

\[S_c=\ln W_c=\frac{A_c}{4\ell_P^2}.\]

Their multiplicity gap is therefore

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=\frac{3\pi}{\Lambda\ell_P^2}\frac{1-q^2}{1+q+q^2}.\]

For

\[0\le q<1,\]

this quantity is positive:

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}>0.\]

Thus throughout the ordinary two-horizon Schwarzschild–de Sitter regime,

\[W_c>W_b.\]

The physical solution independently reaches its limiting mass when the two positive roots become a double root:

\[f(r_N)=0,\qquad f'(r_N)=0.\]

These conditions give

\[r_N=\frac1{\sqrt\Lambda},\]

and

\[M_N=\frac{c^2}{3G\sqrt\Lambda}.\]

This is the Nariai limit.

At that independently derived maximum mass,

\[r_b=r_c=r_N.\]

Therefore,

\[q=1,\]

and hence

\[A_b=A_c=\frac{4\pi}{\Lambda}.\]

Consequently,

\[S_b=S_c,\]

and

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=\ln\frac{W_c}{W_b}=0.\]

The exact known maximum-mass condition therefore occurs precisely where the independently proposed R²D subordinate–enclosing multiplicity gap closes.

The result is stronger than area equality alone.

At Nariai:

\[W_b=W_c,\]

\[S_b=S_c,\]

\[A_b=A_c,\]

\[r_b=r_c,\]

and the two previously distinct positive roots of the same gravitational equation coalesce into one double root.

Thus the old two-horizon classification becomes mathematically marginal at exactly the same point at which the R²D count distinction vanishes.

The enclosing cosmological boundary is not fixed during this process.

At zero black-hole mass,

1. Addendum 44 Made a Prediction Before Choosing a Physical Solution

Addendum 44 considered a generic subordinate gravitational multiplicity well.

Its multiplicity was

\[W_H,\]

and the multiplicity of its enclosing gravitational count boundary was

\[W_E.\]

The remaining count distinction was defined as

\[\Delta S_{E\mid H}=\ln\frac{W_E}{W_H}.\]

The proposed marginal condition was

\[\Delta S_{E\mid H}=0.\]

Nothing in that construction assumed Schwarzschild–de Sitter geometry.

Nothing assumed a cosmological constant.

Nothing assumed the Nariai mass.

Therefore the physical solution can now be used as an independent test.

2. The Test Requires Two Coupled Gravitational Boundaries

A useful test system must contain:

  1. a subordinate gravitational horizon;
  2. an enclosing gravitational horizon;
  3. a continuously varying gravitational source coordinate;
  4. a regime in which both boundaries remain separately readable;
  5. and a known limiting mass at which the two-horizon classification becomes marginal.

Schwarzschild–de Sitter possesses exactly this architecture.

3. Begin With Schwarzschild–de Sitter Geometry

For positive cosmological constant,

\[\Lambda>0,\]

the static Schwarzschild–de Sitter metric is

\[ds^2=-f(r)c^2dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2d\Omega^2,\]

where

\[f(r)=1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r}-\frac{\Lambda r^2}{3}.\]

Horizons occur where

\[f(r)=0.\]

4. The Ordinary Static Regime Contains Two Positive Horizon Roots

For an allowed nonextremal source mass,

\[f(r)=0\]

contains two positive roots:

\[r_b<r_c.\]

The smaller root,

\[r_b,\]

is the black-hole horizon.

The larger root,

\[r_c,\]

is the cosmological horizon.

There is also one negative algebraic root that does not represent a positive horizon radius.

5. The Two Horizons Belong to One Equation

Both

\[r_b\]

and

\[r_c\]

satisfy

\[f(r)=0.\]

Therefore changing M changes both roots.

The black-hole and cosmological horizon reads are not independently variable physical surfaces.

They are coupled solutions of one gravitational relation.

This already matches the structural requirement from Addendum 44:

\[W_H=W_H(M),\]

while

\[W_E=W_E(M,\ldots).\]

The enclosing boundary need not remain fixed while the subordinate well changes.

6. Multiply the Horizon Equation by r

From

\[1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r}-\frac{\Lambda r^2}{3}=0,\]

multiply by r :

\[r-\frac{2GM}{c^2}-\frac{\Lambda r^3}{3}=0.\]

Rearrange:

\[r^3-\frac3\Lambda r+\frac{6GM}{\Lambda c^2}=0.\]

This cubic makes the coupling among the roots explicit.

7. Introduce the Third Root

Let the third root be

\[r_n<0.\]

The cubic contains no r 2 term.

Therefore the sum of its roots vanishes:

\[r_b+r_c+r_n=0.\]

Hence,

\[r_n=-(r_b+r_c).\]

8. The Pairwise Root Relation Gives the Coupled Horizon Identity

For the cubic,

\[r_br_c+r_br_n+r_cr_n=-\frac3\Lambda.\]

Substitute

\[r_n=-(r_b+r_c).\]

Then

\[r_br_c-r_b(r_b+r_c)-r_c(r_b+r_c)=-\frac3\Lambda.\]

Simplifying gives

\[-r_b^2-r_br_c-r_c^2=-\frac3\Lambda.\]

Therefore,

\[r_b^2+r_br_c+r_c^2=\frac3\Lambda.\]

This is the exact coupled horizon relation.

9. The Enclosing Horizon Is Not a Fixed Box

The equation

\[r_b^2+r_br_c+r_c^2=\frac3\Lambda\]

shows immediately that increasing one positive root changes the allowed value of the other.

Thus the cosmological horizon cannot remain fixed while the black-hole horizon changes.

The exact physical problem is a coupled boundary realization.

10. Define a Convenient Relative Radius Coordinate

Define

\[q\equiv\frac{r_b}{r_c}.\]

Throughout the ordinary two-horizon regime,

\[0\le q<1.\]

At horizon coalescence,

\[q\longrightarrow1.\]

This ratio is a convenient physical coordinate.

It is not introduced as a primitive R²D quantity.

11. Solve the Coupled Horizon Identity

Using q

Write

\[r_b=qr_c.\]

Then

\[q^2r_c^2+qr_c^2+r_c^2=\frac3\Lambda.\]

Therefore,

\[r_c^2(1+q+q^2)=\frac3\Lambda.\]

Hence,

\[r_c^2=\frac3{\Lambda(1+q+q^2)}.\]

And

\[r_b^2=\frac{3q^2}{\Lambda(1+q+q^2)}.\]

12. Project the Two Boundaries Onto Area

For spherical horizon projection,

\[A_b=4\pi r_b^2\]

and

\[A_c=4\pi r_c^2.\]

Therefore,

\[A_b(q)=\frac{12\pi q^2}{\Lambda(1+q+q^2)},\]

and

\[A_c(q)=\frac{12\pi}{\Lambda(1+q+q^2)}.\]

The two areas are therefore explicitly coupled.

13. Their Area Ratio Is Simple

Take the ratio:

\[\frac{A_b}{A_c}=q^2.\]

Define

\[\sigma_{b\mid c}\equiv\frac{A_b}{A_c}.\]

Then

\[\sigma_{b\mid c}=q^2.\]

For

\[0\le q<1,\]

we have

\[0\le\sigma_{b\mid c}<1.\]

At equality,

\[\sigma_{b\mid c}=1.\]

14. The Area Ratio Is Not Yet the Primitive R²D Gap

The ratio

\[\sigma_{b\mid c}\]

is physically convenient.

But Addendum 44 identified a more primitive count comparison:

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=\ln\frac{W_c}{W_b}.\]

The present addendum should therefore test that quantity directly.

15. Apply the Same Horizon Multiplicity Calibration to Both Boundaries

For the black-hole horizon,

\[S_b=\ln W_b=\frac{A_b}{4\ell_P^2}.\]

For the cosmological horizon,

\[S_c=\ln W_c=\frac{A_c}{4\ell_P^2}.\]

The common calibration allows their logarithmic multiplicities to be compared directly.

16. The Logarithmic Capacity Ratio Equals the Area Ratio

Because both use the same coefficient,

\[\frac{S_b}{S_c}=\frac{A_b}{A_c}=q^2.\]

Thus

\[\frac{S_b}{S_c}=\sigma_{b\mid c}.\]

This is a ratio of logarithmic multiplicity coordinates.

It is not the same as the ratio

\[W_b/W_c.\]

17. Define the Exact Multiplicity Gap

The R²D saturation coordinate from Addendum 44 is

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}\equiv S_c-S_b.\]

Since

\[S=\ln W,\]

we have

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=\ln\frac{W_c}{W_b}.\]

This is the actual multiplicative distinction between the two horizon classes.

Using

\[A_c=\frac{12\pi}{\Lambda(1+q+q^2)},\]

\[A_b=\frac{12\pi q^2}{\Lambda(1+q+q^2)},\]

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}=\frac{3\pi}{\Lambda\ell_P^2}\frac{1-q^2}{1+q+q^2}.\]

Equivalently,

\[\ln\frac{W_c}{W_b}=\frac{3\pi}{\Lambda\ell_P^2}\frac{1-q^2}{1+q+q^2}.\]

For

\[0\le q<1,\]

\[1-q^2>0.\]

\[1+q+q^2\]

is positive.

Therefore,

\[\Delta S_{c\mid b}>0.\]

Hence,

\[W_c>W_b.\]

The enclosing cosmological horizon remains multiplicatively distinguishable from the subordinate black- hole horizon throughout the ordinary two-horizon regime.
