Binary Distinguishability, Complete Closure, Traveling and Standing Recurrence

Status: Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026
Canonical basis: R²D Parts I–IV; Addenda 35–36, 105, 107, 112, 115–120, 122, 131–144.

Abstract

Propagation is conventionally described as the movement of a disturbance through space. In mechanics, a wave propagates through material whose local constituents undergo recurring changes. In electromagnetism, a wave propagates without requiring a material medium. These descriptions commonly begin with a physical wave equation, a velocity, or a propagating object.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) places propagation within a more primitive mathematical architecture. An enclosing boundary defines distinguishable alternatives, their compatible multiplicities, and the constraints under which possibilities are realized. Completed local responses acquire enclosing occurrence meaning through recursive replacement. Propagation occurs when the complete enclosing compatibility admits a continued directional relationship across receiving boundaries.

The organizing causality belongs to the larger enclosing boundary. Possibilities pull; subordinate occurrences realize compatible change. An occurrence at one boundary does not independently create the possibilities of the next boundary.

The dimensionless construction begins with binary directional distinguishability. Its native multiplicity landscape is

\[ W_N(k)=\binom Nk. \]

The singleton endpoints represent complete directional classifications, while maximum multiplicity occurs at the most balanced classification. An enclosing asymmetric constraint can distinguish directional input and organize a complete local response. The local state may return exactly while the enclosing occurrence retains a nonzero directional output.

Using the interfacial counting and conservation results of Addendum 116, we construct a finite system in which a singleton asymmetric classification can be replaced by the maximum-multiplicity classification at one region while the neighboring region acquires the asymmetric classification. This redistribution preserves the complete count organization and does not require transport of a persistent subordinate identity.

Two enclosing outcomes must be distinguished. When compatible outward continuation is admitted and realized, the recurring relationship propagates. When outward continuation is prevented but compatible reciprocal local recurrence remains sustained, a spatially retained recurrence can be realized. Under an enclosingly compatible fixed spatial recurrence pattern, this is the dimensionless counterpart of a standing wave. Preventing outward propagation does not independently guarantee that such a recurring mode remains admitted.

For an explicitly unrestricted reciprocal binary propagation-history domain, the coherent one-sided alternatives are singleton macrostates, each with multiplicity \(W_{\mathrm{prop}}=1\). Under bounded enclosing spatial–recurrence compatibility, these singleton alternatives are the unique extrema of the signed propagation ratio. Their admissibility does not guarantee that either possibility is realized.

The theory distinguishes the direction of recursive organization, which belongs to the larger enclosing boundary, from positive and negative spatial orientation relative to an observer. It introduces neither primitive physical velocity nor a wave equation.

Physical wavelength, frequency, sound speed, electromagnetic propagation, pressure, and energetic transport are downstream mappings of the dimensionless compatibility and recurrence relationships. The present addendum establishes the count architecture and its conditional finite propagation results. It does not uniquely select the enclosing compatibility of a particular physical wave.

145.1. The question after Addendum 144

Addenda 141–144 developed the distinction between scale-free entropic organization and the physical rulers, clocks, velocities, and energetic readings supplied by enclosing backgrounds.

The same native entropic landscape can be realized under different background calibrations.

A molecule moving relative to an observer, a cylinder containing a thermal gas, a traveling acoustic disturbance, and a propagating electromagnetic relationship therefore need not possess different primitive count architectures merely because their physical representations differ.

The question addressed here precedes those physical mappings:

What constitutes propagation in dimensionless R²D?

A moving container is not equivalent to a propagating mechanical or electromagnetic wave. The former provides a useful illustration of observer-relative directional distinguishability. The latter requires a complete enclosing relationship among spatially separated recurring distinctions.

The essential mathematical problem is not how rapidly a wave moves. It is how an enclosing compatibility permits a constrained local response to become a distinguishable occurrence at a larger boundary.

Propagation is not automatic.

A symmetric background may sustain reciprocal recurrence without directional output. An asymmetric enclosing relation may admit directional continuation. Another enclosing relation may prevent outward continuation while sustaining a bounded reciprocal recurrence.

The task is to distinguish these possibilities before assigning any physical coordinates.

145.2. Possibilities pull: the direction of recursive organization

Part I establishes that the enclosing boundary defines the compatible realizations of subordinate distinctions.

Addendum 107 summarizes the organizing principle:

Possibilities pull; probabilities do not push.

For a local macrostate \(i\) at boundary \(B\), its native multiplicity is

\[ \boxed{ W_B(i)=|\pi_B^{-1}(i)|. } \tag{145.1} \]

For a specified enclosing boundary \(J\), let \(\Theta_{J\mid B,i}\) count compatible enclosing extensions of that local classification.

The canonical possibility coordinate is

\[ \boxed{ P_{B,i} = \ln\frac{\Theta_{J\mid B,i}}{W_B(i)}. } \tag{145.2} \]

The directional asymmetry of a local transition is

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A=\Delta P-\Delta R. } \tag{145.3} \]

The enclosing relation therefore determines the directional support of compatible realization, including the effect of retained constraint.

Subordinate events realize possibilities. They do not create the enclosing multiplicity landscape or determine its causal direction.

This is especially important for propagation.

A completed local response does not push a new possibility into existence at a larger boundary. The larger enclosing compatibility already defines the relationship between the local completion and the next readable occurrence.

The organizing direction is therefore

\[ \boxed{ \text{Enclosing compatibility} \Longrightarrow \text{compatible subordinate realization}. } \tag{145.4} \]

The corresponding recursive readout proceeds from local completion to enclosing occurrence.

These are complementary descriptions, not opposed causal directions.

Recursive direction and spatial orientation

Two directional classifications must remain distinct.

Recursive direction concerns the relationship between a subordinate count boundary and the larger boundary that defines its compatible realization.

Spatial orientation distinguishes positive and negative relations with respect to a selected enclosing observer.

Outward recursive continuation need not correspond to one universal positive spatial direction. A larger enclosing relation may organize more than one spatial branch.

The direction toward larger enclosing organization is logically prior to the physical spatial orientation assigned to its realization.

145.3. Binary distinguishability before velocity

One-dimensional relative motion begins with a binary directional comparison.

For two distinguishable participants belonging to one enclosing comparison, the selected observer can classify nonzero relative motion as

\[ \boxed{ s_{i\mid O}\in\{-1,+1\}. } \tag{145.5} \]

The positive and negative labels specify opposite orientations relative to the observer.

An equality of the two relative motion readings is a separate unresolved or zero distinction, not a third direction.

Neither orientation supplies a physical velocity magnitude.

The enclosing spatial–recurrence relation determines the compatible magnitude classification. Its receiving background subsequently supplies the physical ruler and clock.

The moving-cylinder illustration makes this distinction explicit.

Consider a cylinder containing a thermal gas, observed relative to a specified enclosing reference. Each molecular velocity component is classified as positive or negative relative to that reference.

Changing the cylinder’s relative-motion classification can change the number of positive and negative readings without requiring any change in the cylinder’s internal thermodynamic multiplicity landscape.

In conventional physical terms, this is represented by translating the Maxwell–Boltzmann velocity-component distribution relative to the observer.

In R²D, the more primitive distinction is that the observer-relative directional classification and the internal thermodynamic landscape belong to different enclosing comparisons.

A moving cylinder is therefore a useful illustration of a completely one-sided directional classification.

It also illustrates the conventional picture of a photon as a propagating individual object. But transporting a container or an individual object is not the primitive R²D definition of electromagnetic propagation.

The wave requires a separate enclosing spatial–recurrence relationship.

145.4. The binary multiplicity landscape

Consider \(N\) distinguishable binary directional alternatives.

Let \(k\) be the number classified positive.

The native multiplicity is

\[ \boxed{ W_N(k)=\binom Nk. } \tag{145.6} \]

Its logarithmic entropic coordinate is

\[ \boxed{ S_N(k)=\ln\binom Nk. } \tag{145.7} \]

At the two endpoints,

\[ \boxed{ W_N(0)=W_N(N)=1. } \tag{145.8} \]

The maximum multiplicity is

\[ W_{\max} = \binom N{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}. \]

Define the dimensionless signed directional count

\[ \boxed{ d_N(k)=\frac{2k-N}{N}. } \tag{145.9} \]

Then

\[ -1\le d_N(k)\le1. \]

The endpoints have complete directional classification:

\[ d_N(0)=-1,\qquad d_N(N)=+1. \]

The balanced classification has zero signed directional count when \(N\) is even.

The entropic slope

The exact native entropic increment is

\[ \boxed{ S_N(k+1)-S_N(k) = \ln\frac{N-k}{k+1}. } \tag{145.10} \]

This is a dimensionless relationship between neighboring classifications.

The entropic landscape does not possess an intrinsic spatial scale, recurrence duration, or physical velocity.

Multiplicity deficit

For the selected binary landscape, define

\[ \boxed{ D_N(k)=S_{\max}-S_N(k) = \ln\frac{W_{\max}}{W_N(k)}. } \tag{145.11} \]

At a singleton endpoint,

\[ D_N=\ln W_{\max}. \]

At maximum multiplicity,

\[ D_N=0. \]

Thus the progression

\[ \boxed{ W=1\longrightarrow W_{\max} } \tag{145.12} \]

is also a progression from maximum multiplicity deficit to zero deficit.

This provides a count-level representation of a constrained directional classification returning toward its symmetric reference.

The multiplicity deficit is not automatically identical to canonical retained constraint \(C_J^{out}\). That relationship depends on the enclosing possibility and retention classifications.

The important primitive result is the native landscape and its directional count structure.

145.5. What closes?

Closure requires a specified count boundary.

A local macrostate return is not the movement of a persistent object around a geometrical loop. It is a completed sequence of compatible local distinctions returning to the same readable macrostate.

For a binary local boundary \(B\), the simplest return is

\[ +\longrightarrow-\longrightarrow+. \]

The return establishes

\[ \boxed{ \Delta S_{B,\mathcal C}=0. } \tag{145.13} \]

The native multiplicity returns exactly.

But enclosing possibility and retention need not return to their initial values.

Thus a completed local closure can satisfy

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{\mathcal C} = \Delta P_{\mathcal C} - \Delta R_{\mathcal C} \ne0. } \tag{145.14} \]

The two local directional states do not, by themselves, define the orientation of propagation. That orientation belongs to a larger comparison.

The enclosing occurrence

At the next recursive boundary, a completed local closure is classified as a realization of an enclosing occurrence.

The causal relation is

\[ \boxed{ \chi_{n+1}^{(r)} \Longrightarrow \mathcal C_{n,a}. } \tag{145.15} \]

The corresponding recursive carry is

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal C_{n,a} \overset{\kappa_n}{\mapsto} \chi_{n+1}^{(r)}. } \tag{145.16} \]

These expressions distinguish causal ownership from count reclassification.

The completed local return does not create the enclosing occurrence. Its completion acquires an enclosing count identity under the compatibility already defined by the larger boundary.

The canonical recurrence law

Over a common recurrence-comparison domain \(\mathcal D_R\), the canonical R²D law is

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{\mathcal C} N_{\mathcal D_R}(\mathcal C) = C_J\lambda_J N_{\mathcal D_R}(\chi_J). } \tag{145.17} \]

The local completed recurrence and the enclosing occurrence recurrence are different count types.

Their ratio is

\[ \boxed{ \rho_{J\mid B} = \frac{N_{\mathcal D_R}(\chi_J)} {N_{\mathcal D_R}(\mathcal C_B)}. } \tag{145.18} \]

The local closure can recur even when a selected enclosing transition class has zero recurrence.

That distinction is fundamental to the separation of traveling and standing recurrence.

145.6. The propagation boundary

A propagating wave requires more than one locally returning state.

The complete enclosing boundary must distinguish how successive local completions relate to neighboring spatial comparisons.

Consider a finite collection of peer regions

\[ B_1,B_2,\ldots,B_m \]

belonging to one common enclosing owner \(J\).

The ordering specifies native spatial-comparison positions. It does not yet define physical distance.

For each region, distinguish:

  • an incoming asymmetric constraint classification;
  • a compatible local response;
  • a completed local closure;
  • an outgoing interface-occurrence classification.

A complete propagation relation requires compatibility among these readings.

Write

\[ \boxed{ \Gamma_J^{\mathrm{relay}} \subseteq \mathcal C^{in} \times \mathcal C^{local} \times \mathcal X^{out} \times \mathcal C^{next}. } \tag{145.19} \]

A member of this relation specifies a compatible input, local completion, output, and subsequent receiving classification.

The complete relation belongs to \(J\).

For successive spatial peers \(B_j\) and \(B_{j+1}\), an outgoing occurrence at one interface and an incoming constraint at the next are compatible when their joint classification is admitted by \(J\).

Symbolically,

\[ \boxed{ \chi_j^{out} \sim_J C_{j+1}^{in}. } \tag{145.20} \]

The symbol \(\sim_J\) denotes enclosing compatibility. It does not assert numerical equality between an occurrence count and a constraint coordinate.

Nor does it assert that \(B_j\) and \(B_{j+1}\) are successive recursive levels. They may be spatial peers inside one larger enclosure.

Recursive carry occurs when their completed local realizations receive enclosing occurrence identities at \(J\).

Propagation is the continued realization of an admitted enclosing relationship across successive receiving classifications.

No persistent subordinate microstate is required.

145.7. Finite binary interface compatibility

Addendum 116 establishes the exact count relation for two adjacent regions.

Let their positive and negative directional counts be

\[ a_+,\ a_-, \qquad b_+,\ b_-. \]

The possible oriented interface-pair counts are

\[ \boxed{ F_+=a_+b_-, \qquad F_-=a_-b_+. } \tag{145.21} \]

For inventories

\[ q_A=a_++a_-, \qquad q_B=b_++b_-, \]

and signed classifications

\[ k_A=a_+-a_-, \qquad k_B=b_+-b_-, \]

the exact imbalance is

\[ \boxed{ F_+-F_- = \frac{q_Bk_A-q_Ak_B}{2}. } \tag{145.22} \]

These are possible pairing counts, not completed interface-occurrence counts.

The enclosing boundary determines which compatible possibilities are realized.

The smallest binary example

Take two regions, each containing two directional distinctions.

The local native landscape is

\[ W(x)=\binom2x. \]

Select the classifications

\[ x=2:\quad (+,+),\qquad W=1, \]

and

\[ x=1:\quad (+,-),\qquad W=2. \]

Now consider

\[ (x_A,x_B)=(2,1). \]

The possible positive and negative interface-pair counts are

\[ F_+=2,\qquad F_-=0. \]

The enclosing boundary can admit a reciprocal positive exchange

\[ \boxed{ (2,1)\longrightarrow(1,2). } \tag{145.23} \]

At region \(A\),

\[ W_A:1\longrightarrow2. \]

At region \(B\),

\[ W_B:2\longrightarrow1. \]

The combined product multiplicity is unchanged:

\[ \boxed{ W_AW_B=2. } \tag{145.24} \]

The asymmetric classification has changed its spatial location without creating or destroying the complete product multiplicity.

This is a dimensionless redistribution of a compatible directional distinction.

The local multiplicity deficit decreases at \(A\) and increases at \(B\).

Neither region requires a persistent identified subordinate constituent to pass between them.

The construction proves that the redistribution is compatible with the selected binary count architecture.

It does not establish that the exchange must be realized.

145.8. Propagation from one common enclosing compatibility

Extend the preceding construction to \(m\ge3\) regions arranged in a periodic spatial comparison network.

Let each region possess the selected local classification

\[ x_j\in\{1,2\}. \]

Require the enclosing conservation relation

\[ \boxed{ \sum_{j=1}^{m}x_j=m+1. } \tag{145.25} \]

Exactly one region therefore occupies the asymmetric singleton classification \(x_j=2\). All other regions occupy \(x_j=1\).

The complete spatial possibilities are

\[ (2,1,1,\ldots,1), \]

\[ (1,2,1,\ldots,1), \]

and their remaining cyclic placements.

Under the selected product-compatible construction, all these configurations have equal multiplicity

\[ \boxed{ W_{\mathrm{complete}}=2^{m-1}. } \tag{145.26} \]

Compatible continuation

At a boundary between the singleton region and a symmetric neighboring region, the exact binary interface counts admit reciprocal exchange.

The common enclosing owner can therefore admit a sequence of completed neighboring exchanges.

For four regions, one compatible positive sequence is

\[ \begin{aligned} (2,1,1,1) &\longrightarrow(1,2,1,1)\\ &\longrightarrow(1,1,2,1)\\ &\longrightarrow(1,1,1,2)\\ &\longrightarrow(2,1,1,1). \end{aligned} \tag{145.27} \]

The regional classification returns exactly after four comparisons.

Nevertheless, the complete enclosing relation distinguishes four positive interface completions.

This is a propagating count distinction.

Proposition 145.1 — Compatible propagation without subordinate transport

For the specified finite binary network, an enclosing compatibility admitting successive oriented exchanges of the singleton classification supports nonzero spatial propagation while preserving the complete product multiplicity and total directional inventory.

Proof.

Each admitted exchange replaces one regional \((2,1)\) pairing with \((1,2)\). The sum of the regional positive counts and the product of their native multiplicities are unchanged. Repeated compatible exchanges can place the singleton classification successively at different spatial comparison positions. The regional classifications need not preserve any subordinate formal microstate identity across those exchanges. \(\square\)

The proposition establishes an admitted propagation class.

145.18. Established results and mathematical limits

The investigation establishes the following results.

Result 1 — Propagation is boundary-defined

A propagating relationship is an enclosing spatial–recurrence organization, not the transport of a persistent subordinate microstate.

Result 2 — Binary directional distinguishability is sufficient

Result 4 — Closure does not prohibit propagation

An enclosing occurrence can retain nonzero spatial propagation or circulation even when every local readable state returns.

Exact local return and enclosing directional output are different classifications.

Result 5 — Singleton extrema characterize coherent one-sided propagation

For an unrestricted reciprocal binary propagation-history domain, the all-positive and all-negative histories have multiplicity one.

The multiplicity result does not guarantee occurrence realization.

For the specified periodic nearest-neighbor construction, requiring complete spatial participation and return in the minimum number of exchanges leaves only coherent one-sided traversal.

This result is conditional on the declared enclosing completeness requirement.

Result 7 — Propagation is not guaranteed

Enclosing compatibility may exclude the selected outward continuation even while permitting local recurrence.

A blocked outward propagation class can coexist with sustained reciprocal local activity.

Result 8 — Standing recurrence is a distinct compatible class

If the enclosing boundary admits a spatially retained, recurring response with zero net outward propagation, that possibility can be realized as a standing-mode occurrence.

Blocking alone does not guarantee sustained standing recurrence. The corresponding enclosing compatibility must admit it.
