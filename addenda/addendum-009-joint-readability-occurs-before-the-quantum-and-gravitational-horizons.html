Biological Deprojection and the Complementary Loss of Distinction and Recurrence

Abstract

Addenda 4–8 established recursive replacement in a regime in which several neighboring physical boundaries can be interrogated directly.

In muscle, the enclosing mechanical state, the myosin-switch distribution, the individual switch states, and faster subordinate dynamics can be experimentally separated.

Addendum 4 showed that the spectroscopically resolved myosin-switch asymmetry is organized by chemical possibility and mechanical retention:

\(A_M=P_M-R_M\).

Addendum 5 showed that individual switch identities are replaced by ensemble occupancy:

\[\pi_N:\{M_1,M_2\}^{N}\longrightarrow\{0,1,\ldots,N\},\]

with multiplicity

\[W_N(i)=\left|\pi_N^{-1}(i)\right|.\]

Addendum 6 generalized this result across biological scales:

\[\Gamma_n\longrightarrow\mathcal C_n\overset{\kappa_n}{\longmapsto}B_{n+1}.\]

A subordinate trajectory is physically defined at its own boundary, closes there, and is replaced before the enclosing boundary acquires its own native state and recurrence.

Addendum 7 then showed that experimental readability is itself boundary relative. The physical system can remain muscle while the dynamically read boundary changes with perturbation frequency:

\(B_{\mathrm{read}}=B_{\mathrm{read}}(f_p)\).

For a candidate native closure frequency \(f_n^{\mathrm{cl}}\),

\[N_C^{(n\mid p)}=\frac{f_n^{\mathrm{cl}}}{f_p}.\]

measures how many native closures can occur during one probe cycle.

Addendum 8 used these results to identify biology as an unusually broad window in which recursive replacement can be directly deprojected. The subordinate recurrence, its enclosing replacement, and the larger biological function can all remain experimentally accessible.

This directly observable regime corresponds to joint readability of the primitive R²D pair:

\[(\lambda,\nu)=(R,R).\]

Here λ denotes the readable distinction defined by the boundary, and ν denotes its readable recurrence.

Direct recursive deprojection requires both.

One must be able to identify what changes and how that distinction recurs.

The biological hierarchy provides several adjacent examples.

But joint readability does not persist indefinitely.

Moving toward one side of the recursive hierarchy, continuous subordinate distinguishability becomes unreadable while recurrence remains readable:

\[(R,R)\longrightarrow(U,R).\]

This is the quantum readability horizon.

Beyond it, recurrence survives as a physical read even though the continuously resolved subordinate trajectory is no longer jointly available.

Phase therefore appears only after recursive replacement:

subordinate closure → recursive replacement → readable recurrence → \(\phi\).

Moving in the complementary direction, enclosing distinction remains readable while the recurrence that organizes it becomes unreadable:

\[(R,R)\longrightarrow(R,U).\]

This is the gravitational readability horizon.

Beyond it, enclosing geometry remains physically readable even though its native organizing closure cannot be directly followed.

The corresponding physical sequence is:

recursive enclosing relation → \(\mathcal A\) → \(r_A\).

Thus the quantum and gravitational regimes are complementary one-sided projections of a recursive architecture whose two-sided operation can be observed directly in biology.

The central statement is:

Recursive replacement can be directly deprojected only while both native distinction and native recurrence…

Hence:

Joint readability occurs before the quantum and gravitational horizons.

1. Addenda 4–8 Established the Jointly Readable Middle

The preceding addenda developed one experimental sequence.

First, an enclosing chemical-mechanical relation was shown to organize a local molecular state:

\[A_M=P_M-R_M.\]

Second, the identities of individual switches were shown to be replaced by an enclosing ensemble state:

\[\mathbf x\overset{\pi_N}{\longmapsto}i.\]

Third, this replacement was generalized recursively:

\[\Gamma_n\longrightarrow\mathcal C_n\overset{\kappa_n}{\longmapsto}B_{n+1}.\]

Fourth, the experimental read was shown to move between boundaries as the probe clock changed:

\[B_{\mathrm{read}}=B_{\mathrm{read}}(f_p).\]

Together, these results establish a physical regime in which recursive replacement can be observed rather than merely inferred.

2. Direct Replacement Requires Two Readable Primitive Coordinates

A boundary must first define a distinction.

Let the native distinguishable interval at \(B_n\) be

\[\lambda_n.\]

That distinction must then recur.

Let its native recurrence count be

\[\nu_n.\]

The pair

\[(\lambda_n,\nu_n)\]

therefore describes two complementary aspects of boundary-readable closure:

  • what distinction is being realized;
  • how that distinction recurs.

When both are readable:

\[(\lambda_n,\nu_n)=(R,R).\]

The native state and its recurrence can then be jointly interrogated.

3. Joint Readability Makes Direct Recursive Deprojection Possible

If both \(\lambda_n\) and \(\nu_n\) are readable, an experiment can in principle determine:

what changes,

how it recurs,

what enclosing state follows after replacement.

Thus the sequence

\[\Gamma_n\longrightarrow\mathcal C_n\overset{\kappa_n}{\longmapsto}B_{n+1}\]

can be physically deprojected.

This is the regime exposed by biological systems.

4. Define the Joint-Readability Window

Let

\[\mathcal W_J=\{B_n:(\lambda_n,\nu_n)=(R,R)\}.\]

Within

\[\mathcal W_J,\]

both the boundary-native distinction and its recurrence remain available to physical measurement.

This does not require every instrument to read both simultaneously.

It requires that both be physically accessible in the regime.

Recursive replacement can therefore be reconstructed from both sides.

5. Biology Occupies an Unusually Broad Joint-Readability Window

The biological hierarchy considered in Addenda 4–8 includes schematically:

muscle-scale function

⇕

ensemble occupancy

⇕

myosin switching

⇕

μs-scale protein dynamics

⇕

molecular recurrence.

Several neighboring state domains remain experimentally accessible.

The replacement between them can therefore be deprojected directly.

6. Biological Function Anchors the Enclosing Side

Muscle force provides an independently identifiable muscle-scale state.

The myosin-switch states provide independently identifiable molecular distinctions.

Their ensemble occupancy provides another independently measurable state classification.

The boundary assignments do not therefore depend on frequency alone.

Biological function supplies an external anchor for enclosing statehood.

Thus:

state/function → boundary ownership,

while

frequency → dynamic readability.

7. Probe Frequency Moves the Read Within the Jointly Readable Window

Addendum 7 defined

\[N_C^{(n\mid p)}=\frac{f_n^{\mathrm{cl}}}{f_p}.\]

When

\[N_C^{(n\mid p)}\gg1,\]

the subordinate recurrence repeatedly closes before one probe cycle is completed.

When

\[N_C^{(n\mid p)}\sim1,\]

the probe begins to resolve the native dynamics of \(B_n\).

Thus changing

\[f_p\]

can shift

\[B_{\mathrm{read}}\]

while the physical specimen remains unchanged.

Within the joint-readability window, recursive depth can therefore be experimentally scanned.

8. The Experimental Horizon of Addendum 7 Is Not Yet a Fundamental Physical Horizon

This distinction is essential.

A probe-defined readability transition occurs when

\[N_C^{(n\mid p)}\sim1.\]

Changing the experimental clock can move this transition.

It therefore reflects the relation between the probe and a readable physical recurrence.

A fundamental R²D readability horizon is different.

At such a horizon, one member of the primitive pair

\[(\lambda,\nu)\]

is no longer jointly readable as a native coordinate of that physical boundary.

This loss cannot be removed merely by increasing instrumental bandwidth.

9. Define the Physical Readability Horizon

A physical readability horizon occurs when the recursively realized state remains physically projectable but one member of

\[(\lambda,\nu)\]

ceases to be directly readable at that boundary.

Two complementary cases follow:

\[(U,R)\]

and

\[(R,U).\]

These are not merely limits of experimental technology.

They define different physical projections of recursive replacement.

10. Moving Toward the Quantum Side

Within the jointly readable biological regime, faster probes can expose progressively faster subordinate recurrence.

Schematically:

s → ms → μs → molecular recurrence.

As long as both the native distinction and its recurrence remain readable, the experiment remains inside

\(\mathcal W_J\).

But R²D proposes that this direct deprojection eventually terminates.

The subordinate distinction ceases to remain jointly readable even though recurrence survives.

Thus:

\[(R,R)\longrightarrow(U,R).\]

11. Definition — The Quantum Readability Horizon

The quantum readability horizon is the recursive transition at which

\(\lambda_Q=U\)

while

\(\nu_Q=R\).

Thus:

\[(\lambda,\nu)_Q=(U,R).\]

The native recurrence remains physically readable.

The continuously resolved subordinate distinction does not.

12. The Quantum Horizon Is Not Merely a Faster Biological Boundary

The transition

\[(R,R)\longrightarrow(U,R)\]

should not be interpreted as a biological recurrence becoming too fast for a particular instrument.

The physical statement is stronger.

At the quantum-side boundary, continuous subordinate distinguishability is no longer jointly available as a native physical coordinate.

Thus a still faster experiment does not simply recover the missing trajectory as though nothing had changed.

The count domain has changed.

13. Quantum Recurrence Survives Recursive Replacement

Although continuous subordinate geometry is unreadable, recurrence remains:

\[\nu_Q=R.\]

This surviving recurrence can acquire a physical phase representation.

Thus:

subordinate closure

⇓

recursive replacement

⇓

readable recurrence

⇓

\(\phi\).

Phase is therefore downstream of replacement.

14. Phase Is Not the Primitive Quantum Object

The cyclic phase coordinate

\[\phi\]

is a physical representation of readable recurrence after continuous subordinate distinguishability has crossed the readability horizon.

Therefore:

closure → recurrence → \(\phi\).

Not:

\(\phi\) → closure.

This ordering is essential to the later quantum program.

15. Energy Calibration Follows the Recurrence Read

Once recurrence is represented physically as frequency,

\[f,\]

the familiar energy calibration is

\(E=hf\).

Equivalently,

\[E=\hbar\omega.\]

R²D does not require energy to be primitive beneath recurrence.

The physical quantum coordinate follows the surviving readable side of recursive replacement.

16. The Quantum Horizon Does Not Eliminate All Spatial Description

The statement

\[\lambda_Q=U.\]

should not be interpreted to mean that spatial coordinates can never appear in quantum experiments.

Spatial coordinates can be defined at enclosing measurement boundaries.

Detector position can be readable.

Interference patterns can be spatially distributed.

What is lost is the jointly readable continuous subordinate trajectory that would permit direct deprojection of the quantum recurrence into a persistent native path.

Thus:

enclosing spatial read is not a persistent subordinate quantum trajectory.

17. This Is the Quantum-Side Limit of the Biological Deprojection Procedure

Inside biology, moving the read downward can expose:

fluctuation → native recurrence.

At the quantum horizon, this process no longer continues as direct trajectory recovery.

Recurrence remains.

The subordinate continuous path does not.

Thus:

direct recursive deprojection becomes one-sided.

The experiment now reads recurrence without jointly readable subordinate geometry.

18. Moving Toward the Enclosing Side Produces the Complementary Limit

Now move in the opposite recursive direction.

Enclosing boundaries generally possess slower native closure.

Schematically:

\[f_{n+1}^{\mathrm{cl}}<f_n^{\mathrm{cl}}\]

for adjacent physical clock calibrations satisfying the recursive-clock relation.

As progressively larger enclosing boundaries are considered, their native closure becomes increasingly difficult to complete within the available physical read.

Eventually the recurrence that organizes the enclosing distinction becomes unreadable.

Yet the distinction itself remains readable.

Thus:

\[(R,R)\longrightarrow(R,U).\]

19. Definition — The Gravitational Readability Horizon

The gravitational readability horizon is the recursive transition at which

\[\lambda_G=R\]

while

\[\nu_G=U.\]

Thus:

\[(\lambda,\nu)_G=(R,U).\]

The enclosing distinction remains physically readable.

Its organizing native recurrence does not.

20. The Gravity Horizon Does Not Mean That No Clocks Exist

The statement

\[\nu_G=U\]

does not mean that physical clocks cease to operate in gravitational systems.

Local recurrence remains measurable.

Orbital periods can be measured.

Atomic clocks can be compared.

The unread recurrence is specifically the native organizing closure of the enclosing gravitational boundary whose geometry is being read.

Thus:

local readable clocks are not the native enclosing closure.

This distinction is essential.

21. Geometry Survives on the Gravitational Side

At the gravitational horizon, enclosing distinction remains readable.

The physical read can therefore be expressed geometrically.

Addendum 3 established the ordering

\[\text{recursive enclosing relation}\longrightarrow\mathcal A\longrightarrow r_A,\]

where

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal A}{4\pi}}.\]

Area precedes radius.

The radius is a downstream physical coordinate of an already realized enclosing relation.

22. Radius Is Therefore Complementary to Quantum Phase

The quantum projection preserves recurrence and produces phase:

\[(U,R)\longrightarrow\phi.\]

The gravitational projection preserves enclosing distinction and produces geometry:

\[(R,U)\longrightarrow\mathcal A\longrightarrow r.\]

Thus the structural parallel is:

\[\phi\longleftrightarrow r.\]

This is not an equality.

It is a correspondence between complementary downstream coordinates produced after opposite losses of joint readability.

23. Biology Supplies the Missing Middle of the Phase–Radius Duality

The two projections can now be placed around the directly observable regime:

\[\phi\longleftarrow(U,R)\longleftarrow(R,R)\longrightarrow(R,U)\longrightarrow r.\]

The middle term

\[(R,R)\]

is not merely a theoretical interpolation.

It is the regime in which Addenda 4–8 directly expose recursive replacement across biological boundaries.

This gives the phase–radius duality an empirical center.

24. The Complementary Readability Table Is Therefore Physically Ordered

The three regimes are:

\[\boxed{\begin{array}{c|cc} & \lambda & \nu \\ \hline \text{Quantum} & U & R \\ \text{Biology/Thermodynamics} & R & R \\ \text{Gravity} & R & U \end{array}}\]

The center is jointly readable.

The two sides lose complementary coordinates.

Thus the table is not merely classificatory.

It describes the progression from directly deprojectable recursive replacement to one-sided physical projection.

25. Biology/Thermodynamics Is the Two-Sided Regime

Within the biological and thermodynamic window:

\[\lambda=R\]

and

\[\nu=R.\]

The native distinction can be observed.

Its recurrence can be observed.

Replacement can therefore be followed.

This makes the middle regime structurally different from either horizon.

26. Quantum Mechanics Begins After One Side Becomes Unreadable

Beyond the quantum horizon:

\[\lambda=U,\qquad\nu=R.\]

The surviving recurrence is represented through phase, frequency, and quantum state coordinates.

The missing continuous subordinate geometry should not be restored by assumption.

Thus:

surviving recurrence does not imply a hidden persistent trajectory.

This is the quantum projection error R²D seeks to avoid.

27. Gravity Begins After the Complementary Side Becomes Unreadable

Beyond the gravitational horizon:

\[\lambda=R,\qquad\nu=U.\]

The surviving enclosing distinction is represented geometrically.

The missing native organizing recurrence should not be replaced by the assumption that geometry itself is primitive.

Thus:

surviving geometry does not imply that geometry is the primitive cause of its own organization.

This is the complementary gravitational projection error.

28. The Two Projection Errors Are Mirror Images

On the quantum side, one is tempted to project a missing trajectory backward beneath recurrence.

On the gravitational side, one is tempted to project surviving geometry backward beneath the recursive organization that produced it.

R²D rejects both moves.

Quantum:

readable recurrence does not imply that distinguishability secretly remains primitive.

Gravity:

readable distinguishability does not imply that recurrence was never required.

Both errors result from promoting the surviving projection coordinate to primitive ontology.

29. Joint Readability Must Therefore Come Before Either Projection

The biological window establishes the architecture in which both coordinates coexist:

\[(\lambda,\nu)=(R,R).\]

Only after recursive replacement moves beyond this jointly readable regime do the two one-sided projections emerge.

Therefore the causal order is:

joint recursive readability

⇓

recursive replacement

⇓

complementary one-sided projection.

This is the central ordering of Addendum 9.

30. The h/G Conversion Duality Follows the Same Separation

Addendum 3 identified a structural parallel between

\[h,\hbar\]

and

\[G.\]

The biological middle clarifies why.

On the quantum side, recurrence survives and is calibrated into energy:

\[E=hf\]

or

\[E=\hbar\omega.\]

On the gravitational side, mass-energy is calibrated into geometry:

\[\ell_G=\frac{Gm}{c^2}=\frac{GE}{c^4}.\]

Thus the conversion roles arise on opposite sides of the joint-readability window.

31. The Conversion Constants Do Not Cause Replacement Neither

\[h\]

nor

\[G\]

creates the recursive boundary transition.

Replacement occurs first.

The constants calibrate the physical coordinate that remains readable afterward.

Thus:

recursive replacement → surviving readability → physical calibration.

This preserves the ontology-first ordering.

32. The Horizons Are Not Defined by Fixed Frequency Bands

The biological hierarchy can be associated experimentally with seconds, milliseconds, microseconds, and spectroscopic recurrence.

But the primitive R²D horizons should not be identified with a particular numerical frequency band.

The quantum horizon is defined by:

\[(R,R)\longrightarrow(U,R).\]

The gravitational horizon is defined by:

\[(R,R)\longrightarrow(R,U).\]

Specific frequencies belong to particular physical realizations.

The readability transition is more primitive than the numerical clock coordinate.

33. Infrared Is Therefore Not the Definition of the Quantum Horizon

Infrared spectroscopy may lie near an experimentally important region of biological molecular recurrence.

It can help extend direct deprojection toward faster boundaries.

But:

infrared is not the quantum horizon

as a primitive statement.

The horizon is reached when continuous subordinate distinguishability is no longer jointly readable with recurrence.

That must be established structurally, not assigned from the name of a spectral band.

34. Microwave Is Likewise Not the Definition of the Gravitational Horizon

A microwave background or another long-wavelength physical read may participate in a particular cosmological realization.

But:

microwave is not the gravitational horizon

as a primitive definition.

The gravitational horizon is the point at which the organizing recurrence of an enclosing geometrical distinction is no longer jointly readable.

Again, boundary ownership precedes spectral labeling.

35. Recursive Scale Is Not Metric Size

The transition toward the quantum horizon should not be reduced to:

smaller and smaller objects.

Nor should the transition toward gravity be reduced to:

larger and larger objects.

Recursive scale is defined by nesting of count boundaries, closure, and replacement.

Thus:

recursive scale is not metric size.

Metric size is itself a downstream physical coordinate.

36. Faster and Slower Recurrence Are Physical Implementations, Not Primitive Definitions

In biological systems, deeper subordinate boundaries often recur faster.

Larger functional boundaries often recur more slowly.

This creates the useful experimental hierarchy:

IR → μs → ms → s.

But R²D does not define recursive depth by clock speed alone.

The boundary must be established by state replacement.

Frequency then becomes a physical calibration of native recurrence.

37. Direct Deprojection Stops at Both Horizons Within

\[\mathcal W_J,\]

the experiment can read both sides of replacement.

At the quantum horizon:

\[(U,R),\]

the subordinate distinction required for two-sided deprojection is unavailable.

At the gravitational horizon:

\[(R,U),\]

the native organizing recurrence required for two-sided deprojection is unavailable.

Thus:

direct recursive deprojection

terminates in complementary ways.

38. Quantum Mechanics and Gravity Are Therefore One-Sided Readability Regimes

The later mathematical theories remain physically powerful because they describe the coordinate that survives.

Quantum mechanics develops the consequences of readable recurrence after continuous subordinate geometry is unavailable.

Gravity develops the consequences of readable enclosing geometry after its organizing recurrence is unavailable.

Thus:

quantum = recurrence-side projection,

and

gravity = geometry-side projection

within the R²D interpretation.

39. This Does Not Make Quantum Mechanics or Gravity Incomplete as Predictive Theories

A one-sided projection can remain mathematically complete within the boundary on which it is defined.

The R²D claim concerns ontology and boundary ownership.

A physical law can be exact at its projected boundary without supplying the primitive recursive architecture beneath that projection.

Thus:

predictive completeness at \(B_n\) does not imply ontological primitiveness.
