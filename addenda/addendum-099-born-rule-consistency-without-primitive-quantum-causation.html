Addendum 99 — Born-Rule Consistency Without Primitive Quantum Causation

Boundary Ownership, Observable Realization Counts, and Quantum-Horizon Readability

Abstract

The Born rule describes the observed statistical relationships among quantum preparation, measurement, and detector outcomes. Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) must account for these relationships without treating a quantum state vector, measurement operator, elementary particle, or other projected mathematical representation as the primitive cause of the measurement.

This addendum establishes that the observable Born-rule family admits a coherent R²D boundary-indexed count representation. The construction accommodates normalized detector outcomes, mutually exclusive outcome grouping, preparation mixtures, operational preparation equivalence, sequential measurements, joint quantum correlations, and no-signaling. It distinguishes experimental record counts from native multiplicity, microstate occurrence, realized occupancy, and completed-closure recurrence, preserving their respective boundary ownership.

The quantum state, measurement effect, and measurement transformation enter as calibrated mathematical representations of readable physical relationships. They are not used to construct the primitive compatibility domain whose realization they represent.

The quantum readability horizon imposes an additional requirement. Recurrence remains readable after its complementary subordinate distinguishability has become inaccessible to joint observation. Consequently, the Born representation cannot be required to reconstruct that unreadable subordinate structure or uniquely establish the causal organization of both sides of the recursive turbine. This is not an incomplete application of R²D. It is a direct consequence of the horizon architecture.

The result is a consistency theorem, not a new derivation of the Born rule from primitive counting alone. It establishes that one coherent Born-rule observable family can satisfy R²D’s requirements of boundary ownership, count-type discipline, recursive replacement, and one-sided readability without assigning primitive causal priority to the fitted quantum representation.

And hence: Born-rule consistency occurs without primitive quantum causation.

1. The Question

The purpose of this addendum is to answer one question:

Can the observable Born-rule relationships among preparation, measurement, and detector outcomes be represented as one coherent family of R²D boundary-defined count relations without treating the fitted quantum representation as the cause of those relations?

The answer established here is yes.

This is not the assertion that arbitrary probability assignments constitute an explanation of quantum physics. Nor is it the assertion that the primitive R²D axioms uniquely determine all quantum outcome probabilities.

The claim is more precise.

The experimentally successful Born-rule representation can be retained as a quantitative physical read while the R²D definitions determine what owns the relevant states, compatible realizations, occurrences, and measurements.

The investigation must therefore separate three mathematical roles:

  1. Primitive R²D architecture: the boundaries and count relations defining what can be realized.
  2. Observable physical realization: the preparation procedures, measurement arrangements, and detector outcomes that can be distinguished and counted.
  3. Projected quantum representation: the state vectors, density operators, measurement effects, and transformations used to represent those observable relationships.

A successful quantum representation does not thereby become a primitive cause.

This distinction becomes especially important at the quantum readability horizon.

2. Canonical R²D Begins With Boundary Ownership

At boundary \(B_n\), R²D defines a domain of compatible formal microstates:

\[ \mathfrak M_n \subseteq \prod_{a\in I_{n-1}}\mathfrak X_{n-1,a}. \]

The boundary defines its own readable macrostate classification:

\[ \pi_n:\mathfrak M_n\rightarrow\mathcal M_n. \]

For macrostate \(i\), native multiplicity is

\[ W_{n,i} = \left|\pi_n^{-1}(i)\right|. \]

The fundamental direction of definition is:

\[ \boxed{ B_n \longrightarrow \mathfrak M_n \longrightarrow \pi_n \longrightarrow \{W_{n,i}\}. } \]

Subordinate realizations do not independently assemble the enclosing compatibility domain.

Recursive replacement strengthens this statement. A state, transition, source, or path defined at one boundary does not automatically retain the same primitive identity when the count boundary changes.

For an enclosing occurrence,

\[ \chi_{n+1}^{(r)}, \]

the causal realization relation is

\[ \boxed{ \chi_{n+1}^{(r)} \Longrightarrow (\mathcal C_{n,a})_{a\in I_n} \in\mathfrak D_{n+1,\chi}. } \]

A compatible subordinate closure realizes the enclosing occurrence. Its later classification at the enclosing boundary is recursive carry, not a reversal of causal priority.

These are the controlling R²D premises.

Quantum measurement does not create an exception to them.

3. The Quantum Horizon Is a Requirement of Readability

Part III establishes that a recursively realized observer occupies a finite window of joint readability.

At the quantum horizon, recurrence remains readable while the associated subordinate distinguishability becomes unreadable:

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal R_{B_O}(\rho)=1, \qquad \mathcal R_{B_O}(\lambda_{\mathrm{sub}})=0. } \]

The physical recurrence remains real. Its subordinate realization remains part of the recursively organized structure.

What has changed is the observer’s ability to read the complementary relation from the same boundary.

The unreadable side has not disappeared, and its absence from the experimental record is not evidence that R²D has failed to define it.

This establishes an important distinction between formal existence and physical readability.

A subordinate compatibility domain may be mathematically defined relative to its native boundary without being reconstructible from an enclosing quantum measurement.

The same architecture has an opposite gravitational counterpart: distinguishability can remain readable when the corresponding enclosing directed recurrence cannot be jointly read.

Thus neither horizon requires reconstruction of its complementary side.

The quantum consistency test must preserve the horizon, not attempt to eliminate it.

4. Measurement Defines a New Enclosing Relation

Let \(P\) denote a physically specified preparation procedure, and \(M\) a physically specified measurement arrangement.

The complete measurement boundary is denoted

\[ B_{P,M}. \]

It encloses the relevant preparation, detector, and environmental realizations.

Its possible compatible joint realizations belong to

\[ \mathfrak M_{P,M}, \]

and its readable detector outcomes are classified by

\[ \pi_{P,M}: \mathfrak M_{P,M}\rightarrow\mathcal D_M. \]

For a binary detector,

\[ \mathcal D_M=\{D_+,D_-\}. \]

The compatible realization domain belongs to \(B_{P,M}\).

The state of the detector is not the state of an independently complete quantum particle subsequently observed from outside.

Nor does a fitted quantum state vector independently construct the physical compatibility relation.

The R²D order is

\[ \boxed{ \text{enclosing measurement compatibility} \Longrightarrow \text{compatible preparation–detector realization} \Longrightarrow \text{readable detector outcome}. } \]

This is the measurement architecture already developed in Part III and Addendum 83.

The purpose here is to establish consistency across the complete family of experimentally readable quantum outcomes.

5. Experimental Records Have Their Own Count Boundary

A measurement experiment generally consists of repeated preparation and measurement procedures.

Let \(\kappa\) denote a complete experimental context, including preparation, measurement setting, relevant interventions, and record-acceptance rules.

Suppose \(N_\kappa\) completed, accepted records are retained.

Each record is classified by one of the readable outcome labels

\[ i\in\mathcal D_\kappa. \]

An enclosing record-ensemble boundary \(B_{R,\kappa}\) defines compatible joint realizations of these subordinate records.

A realized record configuration may be represented by

\[ \mathbf r_\kappa = (r_1,r_2,\ldots,r_{N_\kappa}), \]

where each \(r_s\) is a readable record label.

The observed count associated with outcome \(i\) is

\[ c^{R}_{\kappa,i} = \sum_{s=1}^{N_\kappa} \mathbf 1(r_s=i). \]

Therefore,

\[ \sum_i c^{R}_{\kappa,i} = N_\kappa. \]

The record-ensemble boundary classifies the realized configuration by its complete outcome-count vector.

Its normalized observable read is

\[ \boxed{ \widehat\mu_\kappa(i) = \frac{c^{R}_{\kappa,i}}{N_\kappa}. } \]

This is a normalized realized record count.

It is not automatically the native multiplicity of the measurement boundary, the occurrence count of an unreadable radiative microstate, or the recurrence count of a completed detector closure.

Those distinctions remain essential.

The experimental record boundary defines what is actually counted.

This preserves the R²D distinction between possible structure, occurrence, occupancy, and recurrence.

7. Theorem — Finite Observable Representation of the Born Family

Theorem 99.1 — Finite Born-Rule Outcome Counts Admit a Boundary-Indexed Realization

Let \(\mathcal K\) be any finite set of experimentally specified measurement contexts.

For each context \(\kappa\), let \(\mathcal D_\kappa\) be a finite set of mutually exclusive readable outcomes.

Suppose a calibrated Born representation assigns normalized outcome weights

\[ b_\kappa(i)\ge0, \qquad \sum_{i\in\mathcal D_\kappa}b_\kappa(i)=1. \]

Then, for every desired finite precision \(\epsilon>0\), there exists a sufficiently large finite record-ensemble configuration with normalized outcome counts satisfying

\[ \boxed{ \left| \widehat\mu_\kappa(i)-b_\kappa(i) \right|<\epsilon } \]

for every tested context and outcome.

Proof

For one context with \(d\) outcome classes, choose a sufficiently large integer \(N\).

For the first \(d-1\) outcomes, choose

\[ c_i=\lfloor Nb_\kappa(i)\rfloor. \]

Assign the remaining count to the final outcome:

\[ c_d=N-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}c_i. \]

Every \(c_i\) is a nonnegative integer, and

\[ \sum_i c_i=N. \]

The normalized count differences can be made arbitrarily small by increasing \(N\).

Indeed, for the first \(d-1\) classes, each difference is less than \(1/N\). For the final class, the difference is less than \((d-1)/N\).

Thus a sufficiently large \(N\) satisfies the required tolerance.

The realized record configuration is classified by its enclosing record boundary.

For mutually exclusive outcome classes, count addition is exact.

For experiments involving joint outcomes and reported marginals, construct the finite joint record counts first and obtain the marginal records by grouping those same joint outcomes. Their empirical marginal identities then hold exactly.

The construction may be repeated for any finite collection of experimentally specified contexts.

Therefore the finite observable Born family admits an R²D-compatible record-count representation to arbitrary precision.

Q.E.D.

Logical significance

This theorem concerns readable record configurations.

It does not infer the compatible subordinate quantum microstates from the observed weights.

Nor does it assert that independently sampled records construct the enclosing physical measurement boundary.

The record boundary defines its formal joint domain; the observed data occupy a configuration within it.

The theorem establishes observational count consistency, not a primitive derivation of Born statistics.

8. The Born Rule Is a Calibrated Observable Representation

The conventional quantum representation assigns a preparation coordinate

\[ \sigma_P, \]

where \(\sigma_P\) denotes a positive, normalized density operator.

Let

\[ \{E_{M,i}\} \]

represent the experimentally calibrated measurement effects.

The Born representation is

\[ \boxed{ b(i\mid P,M) = \operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_PE_{M,i}). } \]

With a complete measurement,

\[ E_{M,i}\ge0, \qquad \sum_iE_{M,i}=I. \]

Hence

\[ b(i\mid P,M)\ge0, \qquad \sum_ib(i\mid P,M)=1. \]

This representation supplies a coherent set of experimentally testable outcome relationships.

Under R²D, the operators do not define the primitive measurement compatibility domain.

Instead,

\[ \boxed{ \text{boundary-defined physical realization} \rightarrow \text{readable outcome relations} \rightarrow \text{calibrated quantum representation}. } \]

The reverse direction must not be mistaken for primitive causal construction.

A fitted density operator can represent many preparation–measurement relationships without being the source of the compatibility that organizes them.

The experimentally calibrated Born response for a two-outcome equatorial measurement is

\[ \boxed{ b_\pm(\theta,\alpha) = \frac{ 1\pm V\cos(\theta-\alpha) }{2}, } \]

where \(V\) is the calibrated visibility.

The phase coordinates are observable relational parameters.

The cosine dependence is the fitted physical read. It is not inserted into R²D as the primitive definition of an unreadable subordinate recurrence character.

For \(V=1/2\) and \(\theta=0\), the following idealized finite record configurations reproduce selected Born fractions exactly:

where both counts are positive.

This measured contrast must not automatically be identified with the primitive enclosing possibility difference \(\Delta P\) or net asymmetry \(\Delta A\).

Those quantities belong to their own R²D definitions and boundary assignments.

The physical response can be calibrated without reconstructing them across the quantum horizon.

10. Preparation Mixtures Preserve Count Additivity

Suppose preparation \(P_a\) is selected through a physically specified randomization protocol with fraction \(t_a\), where

\[ t_a\ge0, \qquad \sum_at_a=1. \]

The pooled record counts obey ordinary count addition.

For the corresponding observable response,

\[ \boxed{ b(i\mid P_{\mathrm{mix}},M) = \sum_at_ab(i\mid P_a,M). } \]

The fitted quantum representation expresses the same relation through

\[ \sigma_{\mathrm{mix}} = \sum_at_a\sigma_{P_a}. \]

Consequently,

\[ \operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{\mathrm{mix}}E_{M,i}) = \sum_at_a \operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_{P_a}E_{M,i}). \]

The equality reflects one readable mixture protocol and its observable consequences.

It does not establish that the distinct subordinate preparation histories possess the same primitive state identities.

Principle — Operationally Equivalent Preparations Need Not Have Identical Subordinate Realizations

Two preparation procedures are operationally equivalent over a specified family of measurements when

\[ b(i\mid P,M) = b(i\mid P',M) \]

for every admissible measurement and outcome in that family.

This equivalence describes the surviving physical read.

Recursive replacement does not require the same subordinate path or source identity to persist beneath both preparations.

Thus operational preparation equivalence is consistent with R²D boundary-relative statehood.

11. Measurement Changes the Compatible Realization

A quantum measurement is not merely an observer updating an account of an independently complete physical object.

The measurement apparatus participates in a new enclosing realization relation.

If a selected measurement occurrence is

\[ \chi_M^{(r)}, \]

then R²D requires compatible joint subordinate realizations:

\[ \boxed{ \chi_M^{(r)} \Longrightarrow (\mathcal C_Q,\mathcal C_D,\ldots) \in\mathfrak D_{M,\chi}. } \]

The detector can acquire a newly readable macrostate.

Depending on the measurement compatibility, the associated subordinate realization may also change its readable state classification.

For a selected event class whose compatible realizations all satisfy

\[ i_Q^{\mathrm{final}} \neq i_Q^{\mathrm{initial}}, \]

the change in that subordinate macrostate is a necessary consequence of the selected enclosing event.

This does not imply that every quantum measurement changes every subordinate macrostate.

The physical requirement is more general: measurement defines a new joint compatibility relation, whose realized consequences depend on the boundary and selected occurrence.

The quantum state-update equation represents that change at the level of the fitted physical read.

It is not the primitive cause.

12. Sequential Measurements Remain Consistent

Consider two measurements performed in sequence:

\[ P\rightarrow M_1\rightarrow M_2. \]

The second measurement is conditioned by the physical realization of the first.

The fitted quantum representation expresses the joint outcome relationship as

\[ \boxed{ b(i,j\mid P,M_1,M_2) = \operatorname{Tr} \left[ \mathcal I_{M_2,j} \left( \mathcal I_{M_1,i}(\sigma_P) \right) \right], } \]

where the instruments \(\mathcal I\) represent calibrated measurement transformations.

For a normalized subsequent instrument,

\[ \boxed{ \sum_j b(i,j\mid P,M_1,M_2) = b(i\mid P,M_1). } \]

This is consistency under grouping of mutually exclusive complete outcome histories.

But the marginal outcome of \(M_2\) after \(M_1\) need not equal the outcome of \(M_2\) when \(M_1\) was never performed.

The measurement histories correspond to different physical contexts.

R²D places those contexts within different enclosing measurement relations.

The mathematical transformation

\[ \mathcal I_{M_1,i} \]

is a calibrated read of their relationship.

It is not required to be a primitive operation performed upon an elementary particle.

Principle — Sequential Quantum State Updates Are Reads of Changing Measurement Compatibility

A subsequent quantum state representation may differ because the previous measurement has changed the physically realized and readable relation.

This change need not be attributed to ontological collapse of a primitive wavefunction.

13. Joint Quantum Measurements Preserve Enclosing Statehood

Consider a joint preparation whose separately readable outcomes occur at detector boundaries \(B_A\) and \(B_B\).

R²D assigns primitive joint compatibility to the enclosing relation.

For measurement settings \(x\) and \(y\), the fitted Born distribution is

\[ \boxed{ b(a,b\mid x,y) = \operatorname{Tr} \left[ \sigma_{AB} (E_{a\mid x}\otimes F_{b\mid y}) \right]. } \]

This joint distribution is normalized and nonnegative.

When the local effects are complete,

\[ \sum_bF_{b\mid y}=I_B, \]

so

\[ \sum_b b(a,b\mid x,y) = \operatorname{Tr} \left[ \sigma_{AB}(E_{a\mid x}\otimes I_B) \right]. \]

The resulting local marginal is independent of the remote measurement setting \(y\).

Thus the fitted observable family obeys no-signaling.

The joint correlation does not require direct peer-to-peer causal signaling.

Nor does it require independently predetermined local values for all incompatible measurement settings.

Different settings define different physically realized measurement contexts.

The quantum representation assigns them consistently to one operational family without requiring their alternatives to coexist as one boundary-independent collection of primitive outcomes.

This is compatible with the enclosing joint-state architecture developed in Addendum 82.

Principle — Joint Born Correlations Can Be Counted Without Primitive Local Particle Completeness

The local detector outcomes are real.

Their joint organization belongs to an enclosing relation.

The tensor-product quantum representation is a successful coordinate system for that relationship, not its primitive source.

14. Primitive R²D Asymmetry Remains Boundary-Specific

Part I distinguishes native multiplicity, occurrence, possibility, retention, and realized occupancy.

For a specified readable transition

\[ i\rightarrow k \]

at a fixed boundary \(B_n\), the canonical occupancy-realization postulate states

\[ \boxed{ \Delta G_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \Delta\tau_{n,i\rightarrow k} + \Delta P_{n,i\rightarrow k} - \Delta R_{n,i\rightarrow k}. } \]

The possibility difference depends on enclosing compatible extension multiplicity:

\[ \Delta P_{n,i\rightarrow k} = \ln \frac{K_{n+1\mid n,k}} {K_{n+1\mid n,i}}. \]

Retention is

\[ \Delta R_{n,i\rightarrow k} = C_{n+1}^{out}\lambda_{n,i\rightarrow k}. \]

These are R²D quantities defined by their respective boundaries.

A Born detector histogram does not automatically determine their individual values.

Nor may a comparison of two outcome labels be inserted into the transition postulate without specifying the relevant readable transition and count domain.

At the quantum horizon, the complementary subordinate compatibility is not available as an independently readable experimental reconstruction.

Consequently, the proper application of R²D does not demand that the fitted quantum outcome representation supply those inaccessible counts.

This is not an unsatisfied law.

It is adherence to the boundary-specific meaning of the law and to the quantum readability horizon.

15. The Recurrence-Weighted R²D Law Does Not Require Cross-Horizon Reconstruction

The canonical recurrence-weighted law is

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{n,a,\mathcal C} N_{\mathcal D_R}(\mathcal C_{n,a}) = C_{n+1}\lambda_{n+1} N_{\mathcal D_R}(\chi_{n+1}). } \]

The recurrence counts refer to qualified completed closures and enclosing occurrences in a specified common recurrence-comparison domain.

They are not interchangeable with the number of recorded detector clicks.

Nor does the Born probability determine them.

The law is formally defined for the relevant recursively adjacent closure relations.

At the quantum readability horizon, the complete complementary physical read is not jointly accessible.

The Born observable family therefore does not supply a direct measurement of every term of the recurrence-weighted equality.

But the horizon does not require such a measurement.

The absence of a jointly readable cross-horizon comparison is the expected physical condition, not a violation of R²D.

When adjacent closure counts are independently readable in another physical regime, they can be investigated directly.

That is part of the significance of thermodynamics as R²D’s window of joint readability.

At the quantum horizon, the admissible experiment instead measures the relationships that survive the read.

16. Principle — The Readable Projection Does Not Invert the Horizon

Let \(\mathcal R\) denote a complete recursively organized realization.

Let

\[ \mathcal O_{B_O}(\mathcal R) \]

denote the physical information readable from observation boundary \(B_O\).

The quantum horizon restricts the complementary subordinate structure recoverable through this observational relation.

Different complete realizations may therefore share the same readable projection:

\[ \mathcal O_{B_O}(\mathcal R_1) = \mathcal O_{B_O}(\mathcal R_2) \]

without
