Addendum 37 — Differential Free-Fall Rapidity Occurs Before Curvature

Finite Reference Change, Common-Mode Gravity, and the Tidal Read of the Enclosure

Addendum 37 — Differential Free-Fall Rapidity Occurs Before Curvature

Abstract

General relativity conventionally describes gravity through curved spacetime. Freely falling bodies follow geodesics, supported bodies possess proper acceleration, and neighboring geodesics exhibit tidal deviation determined by curvature.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) asks what must exist before that continuous geometric description can be assigned.

Addendum 35 established that physical motion is defined relative to an enclosing rapidity reference. A local boundary does not define its own zero of motion. The enclosure defines the rapidity landscape within which subordinate motion is realized.

Addendum 36 then showed that mass is a separate local boundary coupling represented mechanically within that already-existing reference geometry.

Gravity therefore should not begin with mass bending spacetime or with force accelerating a body.

It should begin with a change in the enclosing reference relation.

Consider

\[B_a\subset\Phi_{G,a}\subset\Phi_E,\]

where Ba is a selected local boundary, ΦG,a is its immediate freely falling gravitational reference, and ΦE is a more enclosing physical reference.

Define

\[\eta_a\equiv\xi_{B_a\mid\Phi_{G,a}},\]

as the local differential rapidity of the selected boundary relative to its immediate background, and

\[\bar\Xi_{G,a}\equiv\xi_{\Phi_{G,a}\mid\Phi_E},\]

as the rapidity of that immediate gravitational background relative to the larger enclosure.

Then

\[\xi_{a\mid E}=\eta_a+\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

Before introducing acceleration, take a finite realization step j → j + 1:

\[\Delta_j\xi_{a\mid E}=\Delta_j\eta_a+\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

For free fall,

\[\Delta_j\eta_a=0.\]

The local boundary remains comoving with its immediate gravitational background even while that background changes relative to a larger enclosure:

\[\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}\ne0.\]

Thus free fall is zero local differential rapidity change, not zero change relative to every enclosing boundary.

For support that holds the local boundary fixed relative to the larger reference,

\[\Delta_j\xi_{a\mid E}=0,\]

so

\[\Delta_j\eta_a=-\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

The support produces the local differential relation required to oppose common-mode gravitational realization.

Gravity becomes locally distinguishable only when neighboring free-fall references cease to share the same enclosing rapidity change.

For neighboring peers a and b, define

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\equiv\delta_{ab}(\Delta_j\bar\Xi_G).\]

If

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}=0,\]

the gravitational change is locally common mode.

If

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\ne0,\]

neighboring free-fall boundaries acquire different enclosing motion changes.

This is the finite R²D precursor to tidal gravity.

Only afterward is a continuous clock supplied:

\[a_a^{\mathrm{prop}}=c\frac{d\eta_a}{d\tau_a},\]

\[g_{G,a}=c\frac{d\bar\Xi_{G,a}}{d\tau_a}.\]

And only after neighboring boundaries receive continuous separation coordinates does differential free-fall acceleration acquire the geometric representation of geodesic deviation and curvature.

Thus curvature is not produced merely because an enclosing rapidity coordinate varies with position. Accelerated coordinates can do that even in flat spacetime.

The stronger condition is:

neighboring freely falling boundaries acquire different enclosing rapidity changes.

The central claim is therefore:

differential free-fall rapidity occurs before curvature.

1. Gravity Begins After the Reference Boundary

Addendum 35 established that velocity is not owned by one boundary alone.

For a local boundary Ba ,

\[v_{a\mid\Phi}\]

is defined relative to an enclosing reference Φ.

Likewise, rapidity is relational:

\[\xi_{a\mid\Phi}.\]

The background defines the rapidity zero.

The local boundary realizes a position within that rapidity landscape.

Gravity must therefore begin by asking:

What happens when the enclosing reference relation itself changes?

2. Mass Is Not Required to Define the Gravitational Reference

Addendum 36 introduced mass as a local orientation-even coupling:

\[m=m[B_m].\]

That coupling can be mechanically represented within an already-defined rapidity geometry.

But the rapidity geometry does not originate from mass.

Gravity should therefore not begin with

m → curvature.

The prior problem is

how does the enclosing reference relation vary among local boundaries?

Mass may later determine how stress-energy is physically read.

It does not define the motion reference itself.

3. Introduce the Local Gravitational Reference

Let

\[B_a\]

be one selected local boundary.

Let

\[\Phi_{G,a}\]

be the immediate physical background relative to which local free fall is nondifferential.

Let

\[\Phi_E\]

be a more enclosing reference.

The relational hierarchy is

\[B_a\subset\Phi_{G,a}\subset\Phi_E.\]

These symbols identify nested physical reference relations.

They need not denote literal concentric material shells.

4. Define the Local Differential Rapidity

Define

\[\eta_a\equiv\xi_{B_a\mid\Phi_{G,a}},\]

This coordinate asks how the selected local boundary moves relative to its immediate gravitational background.

If

\[\eta_a=\text{constant},\]

the selected local boundary has no changing differential motion relative to that local background.

5. Define the Enclosing Gravitational Rapidity

Now define

\[\bar\Xi_{G,a}\equiv\xi_{\Phi_{G,a}\mid\Phi_E},\]

This coordinate describes the relation of the immediate gravitational background to the more enclosing reference.

It is not the same quantity as

\[\eta_a.\]

The two belong to different boundary relations.

6. The Total Rapidity Is Their Reference Composition

For the local collinear comparison,

\[\xi_{a\mid E}=\eta_a+\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

The total motion read relative to the larger enclosure therefore contains:

local differential relation

plus

enclosing background relation.

These contributions should not be collapsed into one primitive acceleration.

7. This Is Not Recursive Carry

The equation

\[\xi_{a\mid E}=\eta_a+\bar\Xi_{G,a}\]

does not represent

\[B_n\longrightarrow B_{n+1}\]

recursive replacement.

The selected local boundary remains the same boundary.

The relation compares nested physical motion references.

Thus:

rapidity composition ≠ recursive carry.

Nor has this development established

\[\xi=\Gamma,\]

where Γ denotes the canonical recurrence ladder.

8. Difference Must Come Before Derivative

A continuous derivative already assumes:

• a continuous parameter; • a clock coordinate; • differentiability; • and a geometric interpolation among realized states.

R²D should begin earlier.

Take two successive distinguishable realizations,

\[j\longrightarrow j+1.\]

For any relational coordinate X , define the finite change

\[\Delta_jX\equiv X_{j+1}-X_j.\]

Thus,

\[\Delta_j\xi_{a\mid E}=\Delta_j\eta_a+\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

This finite relation exists before an acceleration rate is assigned.

9. Inertia Is Persistence of the Reference Relation

For inertial persistence relative to one fixed immediate background,

\[\Delta_j\eta_a=0.\]

No new local differential motion distinction has appeared.

The boundary can remain at rest or move uniformly relative to the selected reference.

The primitive distinction is therefore not

rest versus motion.

It is

persistent rapidity relation versus changed rapidity relation.

10. Applied Acceleration Is a Change in the Local Differential Term

Suppose a local constraint acts specifically on Ba .

Then the local rapidity relation may change:

\[\Delta_j\eta_a\ne0.\]

This is a change of the selected local boundary relative to its immediate background.

It is therefore the finite precursor of locally measurable proper acceleration.

No gravitational relation is required.

Applied acceleration and gravity remain distinct.

11. Gravity Belongs to a Different Term

A gravitational realization can instead change the immediate background relative to the larger enclosure:

\[\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}\ne0.\]

The selected local boundary can participate in that change without developing a differential relation relative to its immediate background.

Thus gravity occupies the enclosing term.

Applied acceleration occupies the local term.

12. Principle — Free Fall Is Local Rapidity Persistence

For free fall, Ba remains locally comoving with its immediate gravitational reference.

Therefore,

\[\Delta_j\eta_a=0.\]

But the gravitational reference may continue changing relative to ΦE :

\[\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}\ne0.\]

Hence,

\[\Delta_j\xi_{a\mid E}=\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

Free fall is therefore not zero change.

It is zero differential local change relative to the immediate gravitational background.

13. Free Fall Can Change Velocity Relative to a Larger Boundary

Because

\[\Delta_j\xi_{a\mid E}\ne0.\]

may hold during free fall, the physical velocity of Ba relative to ΦE can change.

Yet locally,

\[\Delta_j\eta_a=0.\]

There is no contradiction.

The two reads belong to different boundary relations.

Thus:

zero local proper acceleration ≠ zero change relative to every enclosing boundary. [PDF text clipped at right edge; wording requires author review.]

14. The Accelerometer Belongs to the Local Differential Relation

An accelerometer carried by Ba compares its own local boundary with its immediate physical environment.

It therefore detects the differential relation

\[\eta_a,\]

not the common enclosing term by itself.

During ideal free fall,

\[\Delta_j\eta_a=0.\]

Thus the accelerometer reads no local proper acceleration even while

\[\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}\ne0.\]

15. Support Prevents Common-Mode Participation

Now impose a local support constraint.

Suppose the constraint holds the selected boundary fixed relative to the larger reference over the comparison interval:

\[\Delta_j\xi_{a\mid E}=0.\]

But the immediate gravitational background still changes:

\[\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}\ne0.\]

Therefore,

\[0=\Delta_j\eta_a+\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

Hence,

\[\Delta_j\eta_a=-\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

Support creates the local differential relation required to oppose gravitational common-mode participation.

16. Weight Is Therefore a Constraint Read

The supported object does not experience a new primitive gravitational push that was absent during free fall.

Instead, support prevents it from following the gravitational background relation.

The resulting locally readable distinction is

\[\Delta_j\eta_a.\]

Thus:

weight = local differential constraint required to oppose gravitational common mode.

Mass enters when that differential acceleration is mechanically calibrated.

It does not create the gravitational common mode.

17. Free Fall and Support Are Complementary Branches

The same enclosing gravitational change

\[\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}\]

can appear in two distinct local realizations.

Free fall

\[\Delta_j\eta_a=0,\]

so

\[\Delta_j\xi_{a\mid E}=\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

Supported rest relative to the larger reference

\[\Delta_j\xi_{a\mid E}=0,\]

so

\[\Delta_j\eta_a=-\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

The common gravitational relation is the same.

What changes is whether the selected local boundary participates in it.

18. Introduce a Neighboring Free-Fall Boundary

Now consider two neighboring local boundaries,

\[B_a,\qquad B_b.\]

Each has an immediate gravitational reference:

\[\Phi_{G,a},\qquad\Phi_{G,b}.\]

Both participate in the larger enclosing relation ΦE .

Their total rapidities are

\[\xi_{a\mid E}=\eta_a+\bar\Xi_{G,a},\]

\[\xi_{b\mid E}=\eta_b+\bar\Xi_{G,b}.\]

19. Define the Peer-Difference Operator

For any quantity X defined on the neighboring pair, let

\[\delta_{ab}X\equiv X_b-X_a.\]

Then

\[\delta_{ab}\xi_E=\delta_{ab}\eta+\delta_{ab}\bar\Xi_G.\]

The peer comparison removes anything that is exactly common to both.

20. Common-Mode Gravity Cancels From the Peer Difference

Suppose the enclosing gravitational relation is locally common mode:

\[\bar\Xi_{G,a}=\bar\Xi_{G,b}.\]

Then

\[\delta_{ab}\bar\Xi_G=0.\]

For neighboring free-fall peers,

\[\Delta_j\eta_a=\Delta_j\eta_b=0.\]

Thus a common gravitational contribution produces no new local differential motion between them.

The enclosing relation remains real.

It is simply not distinguished by that local peer comparison.

21. Principle — Uniform Shared Gravity Becomes Local Background

A contribution identical across all local peers in the comparison domain does not classify those peers differently.

It therefore becomes background-like at that boundary.

Thus:

shared gravitational realization → local common mode.

This is the R²D basis of the local equivalence principle.

22. Common Mode Is Not Absence

If

\[\delta_{ab}\bar\Xi_G=0,\]

the gravitational relation has not vanished.

Only its distinction between a and b has vanished.

Therefore:

locally unreadable difference ≠ absence of gravitational relation. [PDF text clipped at right edge; wording requires author review.]

This is the same boundary-relative readability distinction used throughout R²D.

23. A Local Inertial Frame Comes After Common-Mode Identification

Once the shared gravitational relation has become locally common mode, the free-fall background can be assigned a local inertial coordinate frame.

The logical order is therefore

shared gravitational realization

⇓

common-mode local background

⇓

free-fall reference

⇓

local inertial coordinates.

The coordinate frame does not make gravity disappear.

It represents the prior common-mode cancellation.

24. The Equivalence Principle Is a Boundary Statement

A sufficiently uniform gravitational contribution is shared by:

• the selected free-falling boundary; • neighboring free-falling peers; • and the local measuring apparatus.

Because that contribution is common, local comparison does not distinguish it.

A supported apparatus instead introduces an equal-and-opposite local differential relation.

Thus:

equivalence = local projection equivalence after common-mode quotienting.

Gravity and applied acceleration are not the same primitive relation.

Their local observable consequences can coincide after the common part has been removed.

25. A Static Peer Rapidity Difference Is Not Yet Curvature

Suppose

\[\bar\Xi_{G,a}\ne\bar\Xi_{G,b}.\]

This alone does not prove curvature.

Different coordinate assignments can produce different rapidity values even in flat spacetime.

A genuinely gravitational tidal distinction requires more.

The neighboring free-fall references must acquire different changes in their enclosing rapidity relations.

Therefore the relevant primitive object is not merely

\[\delta_{ab}\bar\Xi_G.\]

It is its finite change.

26. Define the Finite Tidal Rapidity

For the realization step j → j + 1, define

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\equiv\delta_{ab}(\Delta_j\bar\Xi_G).\]

Equivalently,

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}=\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,b}-\Delta_j\bar\Xi_{G,a}.\]

Because finite difference and peer subtraction commute,

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}=\Delta_j(\delta_{ab}\bar\Xi_G).\]

This quantity is dimensionless.

No continuous spacetime geometry has been introduced.

27. The Tidal Quantity Has a Direct Physical Meaning

The question encoded by

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\]

is:

Do neighboring freely falling backgrounds acquire the same enclosing rapidity change?

If

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}=0,\]

their gravitational change remains common mode.

If

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\ne0,\]

their relative free-fall relation changes.

Thus:

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\ne0\longrightarrow\text{tidal distinguishability}.\]

28. Principle — Tidal Gravity Is Common-Mode Failure Across Peers

A gravitational relation can be quotiented out locally only insofar as it remains common to the local comparison domain.

Therefore:

tidal gravity is the finite failure of enclosing rapidity change to remain common mode across neighbo

This relation is prior to curvature notation.

29. Difference Before Gradient

The quantity

\[\mathcal T_{ab,j}^{(\xi)}\]

compares two boundaries.

It does not require a spatial derivative.

Only after those boundaries are assigned a continuous separation coordinate can one write a gradient.

Therefore the logical order is

peer difference → separation coordinate → gradient.

A derivative must not be used to define the distinction from which the derivative is constructed.

30. Area Before Radius Applies to the Gravitational Domain

No primitive radial coordinate has been required so far.

The neighboring labels

\[a,\qquad b\]

identify peer boundaries.

They are not assumed to be points on an already-existing Euclidean radius.

For a spherical gravitational projection, let the enclosing area be

\[\mathcal A_G.\]

Only afterward define the areal coordinate

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal A_G}{4\pi}}.\]

Thus:

\[\mathcal A_G\longrightarrow r_A.\]

Any later derivative such as

\[\frac{\partial\bar\Xi_G}{\partial r_A}\]

is therefore a projected coordinate representation of a prior enclosing relation.

31. Gravity Is Not Initially a Field Over Primitive Radius

A conventional gravitational field may later be written

\[g(r).\]

R²D should not interpret this notation as proving that a primitive radius existed first and that gravity was subsequently painted onto it.

The prior structure is

enclosing gravitational relation

⇓

peer differential

⇓

area/radius mapping

⇓

\(g(r)\).

Thus:

gravitational enclosure occurs before radial field coordinates.

32. Only Now Introduce a Continuous Proper Clock

Once the finite realization sequence is represented by a differentiable local clock coordinate, define

\[\tau_a\]

as the proper clock read of Ba .

Then the local differential proper-acceleration read is

\[a_a^{\mathrm{prop}}=c\frac{d\eta_a}{d\tau_a}.\]

This is the continuous physical dimensionalization of the finite relation

\[\Delta_j\eta_a.\]

Proper acceleration is therefore downstream of rapidity change.

33. Define the Continuous Gravitational Background Change

Along the same local realization define

\[g_{G,a}=c\frac{d\bar\Xi_{G,a}}{d\tau_a}.\]

This quantity describes the changing immediate gravitational reference relative to the larger enclosure as sampled along Ba .

It is not yet a universal gravitational field.

It is a local continuous read of the enclosing reference change.

34. The Larger-Background Acceleration Decomposes

Differentiate

\[\xi_{a\mid E}=\eta_a+\bar\Xi_{G,a}\]

along the proper clock of Ba .

Then

\[c\frac{d\xi_{a\mid E}}{d\tau_a}=c\frac{d\eta_a}{d\tau_a}+c\frac{d\bar\Xi_{G,a}}{d\tau_a}.\]

Define

\[A_{a\mid E}\equiv c\frac{d\xi_{a\mid E}}{d\tau_a}.\]

Therefore,

\[A_{a\mid E}=a_a^{\mathrm{prop}}+g_{G,a}.\]

The terms remain physically distinct.

35. Free Fall in the Continuous Limit

For free fall,

\[\frac{d\eta_a}{d\tau_a}=0.\]

Thus,

\[a_a^{\mathrm{prop}}=0.\]

Yet generally,

\[g_{G,a}\ne0.\]

Therefore,

\[A_{a\mid E}=g_{G,a}.\]

A freely falling boundary changes its motion relative to the larger enclosure while remaining unaccelerated relative to its immediate gravitational reference.

36. Support in the Continuous Limit

For idealized support holding the selected boundary fixed relative to the larger reference,

\[A_{a\mid E}=0.\]

Therefore,

\[0=a_a^{\mathrm{prop}}+g_{G,a}.\]

Hence,

\[a_a^{\mathrm{prop}}=-g_{G,a}.\]

The accelerometer measures this differential local term.

37. Weight Enters Only After Mass Calibration

Addendum 36 established the local mass coupling and its mechanical calibration.

For the supported local boundary,

\[F_{\mathrm{support}}=m a_a^{\mathrm{prop}}.\]

Therefore,

\[F_{\mathrm{support}}=-mg_{G,a}.\]

In magnitude,

\[|F_{\mathrm{support}}|=m|g_{G,a}|.\]

The mass determines the mechanical support-force read.

It does not define the gravitational rapidity relation.

38. Free Fall Is Therefore Mass Independent at the Reference Level

The condition for local free fall is

\[\frac{d\eta_a}{d\tau_a}=0.\]

No mass parameter appears.

Thus the shared gravitational reference relation can be defined before the mass of the freely falling test boundary is introduced.

Mass enters later when a differential constraint such as support is mechanically calibrated.

This is the R²D ordering beneath universality of free fall.

39. Define Continuous Tidal Acceleration
