R²D Addendum 139 — Observational Coherence Across Recursive Backgrounds

Complete Compatibility, Surviving Relationships, and the Replacement of Motion Identity

Status: Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026
Canonical basis: R²D Part I (authoritative September 25, 2026 revision), especially boundary-native statehood, complete enclosing compatibility, recurrence, and recursive carry; Parts II–IV on joint readability, complementary horizons, and downstream physical projection; Addenda 120, 122, 131–132, and 138.
Purpose: Establish exact unitless conditions under which a background-indexed observational sequence supports a coherent moving or recurring relational read without persistent subordinate microstate identity; show why complete coherence is not reconstructable from instantaneous or adjacent comparisons; and derive the most informative classification common to distinct background-native reads without choosing an invariant in advance.

Abstract

A physical trajectory, recurrent turbine, emitted pulse, or apparently persistent velocity is ordinarily described as successive states of a continuing object. R²D instead assigns primitive count identity and compatibility to enclosing boundaries. A succession of observations may remain coherently identifiable even when the subordinate microstate distinctions through which the observations are realized do not possess persistent identity across comparisons. This addendum isolates that mathematical claim from its physical interpretation.

An enclosing owner specifies finite complete comparison records and background-indexed readable classifications. We distinguish complete sequence admissibility, unique relational identification from an observation, and persistence of a selected relation across multiple background comparisons. Exact fiber and factorization theorems characterize the first two; an intersection-of-compatibility-sets theorem characterizes persistence at fixed comparison domain. A finite pair of symmetric enclosing backgrounds is then constructed with the same instantaneous native landscape, the same single-snapshot extension counts, and exactly the same adjacent two-snapshot extension counts. Yet one admits persistent translation through four background comparisons while the other admits no such persistence through three. This proves that lower-order statistical reads do not determine complete observational coherence.

A background-resolution change may further identify translated relations according to an exact modular aliasing law, although temporal subsampling is not itself recursive replacement. For genuine changes of native background, an enclosing comparison must specify the old and new read maps without positing persistent subordinate microstates. The maximal classification independently readable from both backgrounds is derived—not selected in advance—as the quotient by the equivalence relation generated by their read fibers, equivalently the connected components of their cross-background compatibility graph. The construction extends to any finite coherent family of background reads. It does not select which native comparison maps nature realizes, determine physical coherence time, equate possible record fractions with probabilities, or derive quantum, gravitational, acoustic, or electromagnetic laws.

139.1. Position within the canon

Part I defines a boundary-relative formal microstate as a compatible joint peer realization and a readable macrostate as its enclosing-owned classification. Recurrence counts, native multiplicity, realized occurrence, and occupancy are different count types. Recursive carry reclassifies a completed local realization by its enclosing occurrence; it does not transport an unchanged subordinate object through successive boundaries [1].

Addendum 120 makes the distinction between presentations and native realizations explicit. In particular, a readable property of a complete comparison descends through a read map exactly when it is constant on that map’s fibers [2]. Addendum 138 applies that distinction to enclosing-native symmetric recurrence without requiring that an enclosing recurrence be decomposable into independently admitted subordinate cycles [3]. Addendum 131 distinguishes orientation-even completed recurrence from net oriented output and demonstrates that a symmetric enclosing background does not, by itself, select a universal numerical recurrence ratio [4]. Addendum 132 identifies the common-read factorization problem at two complementary readability horizons [5].

This addendum changes the target. It does not seek a universal whole-closure count or a physical speed. It asks which complete relationships among successive background-indexed observations remain compatible, identifiable, and readable after a change of background comparison or native boundary.

The word coherence requires care. Here observational coherence means persistence of a specified relational classification across successive background-indexed reads within a declared complete comparison domain. Part IV also uses coherence for the persistence of an organized relation across recursive boundaries. The two notions may be related, but are not identified by definition.

139.2. Complete background-indexed observations

Let \(J\) own a finite domain \(H_J\) of admitted complete possible comparison records. Specify an ordered family of background comparison indices \(r_0,\ldots,r_k\). These are count labels, not elapsed seconds. The background’s realized nondirectional count \(b_J\), its logarithmic coordinate \(\Phi_J=\ln b_J\), and its separately counted completed background-reference recurrence \(N_J^\Phi\) are not identified with \(k+1\).

Let \(Y_t\) be the observing boundary’s readable classification set at index \(r_t\), and supply complete-record read maps

\[ q_t:H_J\longrightarrow Y_t. \tag{139.1} \]

Their joint read is

\[ Q_J(h)=\big(q_0(h),\ldots,q_k(h)\big), \qquad \Gamma_J^{(k)}=Q_J(H_J). \tag{139.2} \]

\(\Gamma_J^{(k)}\) is the complete admitted sequence relation. It need not equal the join of separately known adjacent pair projections. Nothing in (139.1)–(139.2) assumes that the same subordinate formal microstate persists across \(r_t\).

For an observed sequence \(y=(y_0,\ldots,y_k)\) define the compatible complete-record fiber

\[ F_J(y)=\{h\in H_J:Q_J(h)=y\}. \tag{139.3} \]

Definition 139.1 (sequence admissibility). The sequence \(y\) is admitted by \(J\) precisely when \(F_J(y)\neq\varnothing\). This is an existence claim about complete possible records, not a claim that \(y\) was realized.

139.3. Theorem 1 — Relational identification without subordinate persistence

Let

\[ I_J:H_J\longrightarrow\mathcal S_J \tag{139.4} \]

be an enclosing-owned complete relational response classification. Its values may distinguish translated patterns, complete returns, or other identified relational organizations. Its status as a native readable class or a specified compatibility-response class must be declared; assigning the same descriptive name to unrelated possibilities is not sufficient.

Theorem 1 (complete observational identification). An admitted observation sequence \(y\) determines one definite relational class \(I_J\) if and only if

\[ \boxed{F_J(y)\ne\varnothing,\qquad |I_J(F_J(y))|=1.} \tag{139.5} \]

Proof. The first condition ensures at least one complete compatible record. The second states that all records compatible with the observation share exactly one \(I_J\) value. If two such records have different values, the observed sequence cannot determine which class applies. \(\square\)

The condition does not require \(|F_J(y)|=1\). Multiple distinct fine realizations can remain possible while their enclosing relational classification is uniquely identified.

Corollary 1.1 (universal observational factorization). The classification is determined from every admitted complete observation sequence if and only if

\[ Q_J(h)=Q_J(h')\Longrightarrow I_J(h)=I_J(h') \quad\text{for all }h,h'\in H_J. \tag{139.6} \]

Equivalently, there is a unique map on the image \(\overline I_J:\Gamma_J^{(k)}\to\mathcal S_J\) with \(I_J=\overline I_J\circ Q_J\).

Proof. This is the finite fiber-constancy criterion of Addendum 120, applied to \(Q_J\). \(\square\)

Scope. The theorem establishes compatibility-based identification. It does not say that \(I_J\) is itself a persistent physical object or determine which member of \(F_J(y)\) occurs.

139.4. Theorem 2 — Persistence of a selected observational relationship

For a fixed declared complete domain \(H_J\), let \(T_{t,L}\subseteq Y_t\times Y_{t+1}\) specify the pair responses that count as the same selected relationship \(L\) at comparison \(t\). These relations belong to the owner’s admitted comparison scheme. Their being individually satisfied is not permission to join separately specified candidate pairs into a new enclosing record.

Define

\[ A_{t,L}=\{h\in H_J:(q_t(h),q_{t+1}(h))\in T_{t,L}\}, \tag{139.7} \]

\[ H_J^{\mathrm{coh}}(j,L)=\bigcap_{t=0}^{j-1}A_{t,L}, \qquad K_J(j,L)=|H_J^{\mathrm{coh}}(j,L)|. \tag{139.8} \]

Theorem 2 (complete compatibility survival). The relationship \(L\) remains admitted through the first \(j\) background comparisons if and only if \(K_J(j,L)>0\). Moreover,

\[ \boxed{H_J^{\mathrm{coh}}(j+1,L)\subseteq H_J^{\mathrm{coh}}(j,L),\qquad K_J(j+1,L)\le K_J(j,L).} \tag{139.9} \]

Proof. A complete record persists under \(L\) precisely when it satisfies all \(j\) required comparisons. The intersection defines those records. One additional requirement can only remove members from that fixed set. \(\square\)

A normalized fraction

\[ C_J(j,L)=\frac{K_J(j,L)}{|H_J|} \tag{139.10} \]

is defined when \(|H_J|>0\) and counts the fraction of possible complete records satisfying this persistence requirement. It is neither a realized occurrence probability nor a measured physical autocorrelation. The monotonicity in (139.9) concerns a fixed complete domain and nested requirements, not a general law of coherence loss across changing native boundaries. The possibility count may change with the comparison domain, and further measurements may simultaneously improve identification of a completed relational class while its earlier two-point physical correlation decreases.

139.5. Finite counterexample — Equal local reads, different complete coherence

Choose four readable position classes \(X=\mathbb Z_4=\{0,1,2,3\}\) and five background comparison indices \(r_0,\ldots,r_4\). Specify a common abstract record-index set for two alternative enclosing owners,

\[ D=\mathbb Z_4\times\{-1,+1\}\times\{-1,+1\}, \qquad h=(s,\sigma,\varepsilon), \qquad |D|=16. \tag{139.11} \]

The owners declare these as 16 distinct counted complete comparison possibilities; the third coordinate is a genuine admitted complete-context distinction, not an invented duplicate presentation. It need not remain readable in the observable position sequence. The two owners may also share any fixed instantaneous native local multiplicity landscape, for example \(W_{B,x}=2\) for every \(x\in X\). These local native counts are separate from the 16 complete comparison counts.

Owner \(J_P\): Persistent translation. Define

\[ x_t^P(h)=s+t\sigma\pmod4, \qquad t=0,\ldots,4. \tag{139.12} \]

The finer alternative \(\varepsilon\) is compatible context that does not affect this particular position read. Every admitted sequence has increments \((\sigma,\sigma,\sigma,\sigma)\).

Owner \(J_F\): Changing translation. Define the complete increments

\[ (d_0,d_1,d_2,d_3)=(\sigma,\varepsilon,-\sigma,-\varepsilon) \tag{139.13} \]

and

\[ x_0^F=s,\qquad x_{t+1}^F=x_t^F+d_t\pmod4. \tag{139.14} \]

Thus its complete read is

\[ (x_0^F,\ldots,x_4^F) =(s,s+\sigma,s+\sigma+\varepsilon,s+\varepsilon,s)\pmod4. \tag{139.15} \]

Both owners admit all \(s,\sigma,\varepsilon\) choices and are symmetric under simultaneous reversal \((s,\sigma,\varepsilon)\mapsto(-s,-\sigma,-\varepsilon)\) of the position read. Both return to their initial position class after four background comparisons. Neither stipulates a stochastic transition law or a physically realized trajectory.

Theorem 3 (failure of lower-order coherence reconstruction). These two owners have equal complete-record single-position projection counts and equal adjacent-pair projection counts for every position and every comparison stage, but differ in the existence and counts of persistent constant-translation relationships across three and four steps.

Proof. For either owner, fixed \((\sigma,\varepsilon)\) makes \(x_t\) a translation of \(s\), so for each \(t\) and \(x\in X\),

\[ |\{h\in D:x_t^P(h)=x\}|=|\{h\in D:x_t^F(h)=x\}|=4. \tag{139.16} \]

At any stage, the adjacent displacement is \(+1\) or \(-1\). For each given initial position and displacement, exactly two of the four choices of \((\sigma,\varepsilon)\) realize that displacement. Hence for either owner and every \(t=0,1,2,3\),

\[ K_{J,t}(x,x')= \begin{cases} 2,&x'-x\equiv 1\ \text{or}\ -1\pmod4,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \tag{139.17} \]

The persistent-translation criterion requires one \(\delta\in\{-1,+1\}\) with \(x_{t+1}-x_t\equiv\delta\pmod4\) at every one of the first \(j\) comparisons. All 16 \(J_P\) records satisfy it for \(j=1,2,3,4\). For \(J_F\), the increments \((\sigma,\varepsilon,-\sigma,-\varepsilon)\) give 16 admissible records at \(j=1\), eight at \(j=2\) (only \(\varepsilon=\sigma\)), and none at \(j\ge3\) (since \(-\sigma\ne\sigma\) in \(\mathbb Z_4\) for \(\sigma=\pm1\)). Thus:

Background comparisons \(j\) \(K_{J_P}(j,\mathrm{translation})\) \(K_{J_F}(j,\mathrm{translation})\)
1 16 16
2 16 8
3 16 0
4 16 0

The entire one-time and adjacent two-time possible-count data are equal, but the complete persistence classifications differ. \(\square\)

Corollary 3.1. Exact lower-order marginal counts do not determine the complete background-indexed coherent correspondence. Independently multiplying or joining those margins may introduce complete records excluded by the owner.

Interpretation. \(J_F\) lacks the selected constant-translation property through three steps; it is not generally devoid of all possible organized complete recurrence. Both owners close in position at step four, so return of readable occupancy is not equivalent to persistence of one complete motion relationship.

139.6. Changes of observation resolution inside one comparison domain

A fixed owner with an admitted translated read

\[ x_t=x_0+t\delta\pmod m, \tag{139.18} \]

may be inspected only at every \(k\)-th background comparison. The reduced read satisfies

\[ x_{(j+1)k}-x_{jk}\equiv k\delta\pmod m. \tag{139.19} \]

Theorem 4 (modular aliasing). Two fine translation classes \(\delta,\delta'\) are indistinguishable through this reduced increment read if and only if

\[ \boxed{k(\delta-\delta')\equiv0\pmod m.} \tag{139.20} \]

If all \(m\) translation classes are admitted, their distinct reduced increments number

\[ \boxed{m/\gcd(m,k).} \tag{139.21} \]

Proof. Their coarse increments agree precisely under (139.20). Multiplication by \(k\) on \(\mathbb Z_m\) has image of cardinality \(m/\gcd(m,k)\). \(\square\)

For \(m=4\), reads at stride one distinguish all four translations, stride two distinguishes two, and stride four distinguishes only one. On the restricted subset \(\delta\in\{-1,0,+1\}\), stride two identifies forward with reverse while retaining stationarity; stride four identifies all three.

The complete enclosing relationship need not change when the observer’s retained comparisons change. Moreover a coarse apparent return or displacement need not establish persistent fine translation. Temporal thinning of one read is not, by itself, recursive replacement, because it leaves the original native count domain fixed.

139.7. Genuine native-background replacement

Now let \(B\) and \(B'\) be distinct native background boundaries with different admitted native realization domains \(\mathfrak M_B\) and \(\mathfrak M_{B'}\). A larger comparison owner \(L\) supplies a finite complete domain \(D_L\) and read maps

\[ a:D_L\to\mathfrak M_B, \qquad b:D_L\to\mathfrak M_{B'}, \qquad u:D_L\to U_L, \tag{139.22} \]

where \(u\) records any enclosing context needed for correct compatibility. This is Addendum 120’s general replacement architecture. It does not assume a function transporting one native microstate to another. Nor are the two native domains equal by definition.

Let the original native background distinguish a selected observational relationship \(I_B:\mathfrak M_B\to V\). When does that relationship have a well-defined surviving read on \(B'\)?

Theorem 5 (cross-native preservation). On the reached image \(b(D_L)\), there exists a unique \(I_{B'}\) such that

\[ I_B\circ a=I_{B'}\circ b \tag{139.23} \]

if and only if

\[ \boxed{b(d)=b(d')\Longrightarrow I_B(a(d))=I_B(a(d'))} \tag{139.24} \]

for all \(d,d'\in D_L\).

Proof. Necessity follows from the proposed equality. For sufficiency define \(I_{B'}(b(d))=I_B(a(d))\); fiber constancy makes it well-defined, and surjectivity onto \(b(D_L)\) gives uniqueness. \(\square\)

The theorem preserves the selected classification, not necessarily the source’s native microstate identity or multiplicity. If \(I_B\) is defined only on a set of complete sequence records rather than individual native microstates, that declared sequence-response domain must replace \(\mathfrak M_B\) in (139.22), or be included as part of the complete read from \(D_L\); it must not be silently smuggled into an instantaneous state label.

139.8. Theorem 6 — Derive the maximal common invariant without selecting one

Theorem 5 tests a selected invariant. We now derive the complete surviving classification from the enclosing correspondence without choosing any \(I\) beforehand.

Let \(q_B:D_L\to Y_B\) and \(q_{B'}:D_L\to Y_{B'}\) be the specified native or complete-sequence response reads from the same admitted comparison domain. Define

\[ \boxed{ \approx_L=\operatorname{Eq}\big(\ker q_B\cup\ker q_{B'}\big), \qquad \mathcal I_{BB'}=D_L/\!\approx_L. } \tag{139.25} \]

Here \(\operatorname{Eq}\) is the equivalence closure generated by equality under either read.

Theorem 6 (maximal common background classification). The quotient \(\mathcal I_{BB'}\) is the most informative classification that can be recovered separately from each of the two background reads. Every other common invariant is a function of it.

Proof. A common invariant must be constant on each \(q_B\) fiber and on each \(q_{B'}\) fiber, hence on every finite chain generated by those identifications. It therefore factors through \(\mathcal I_{BB'}\). Conversely the quotient projection is constant on each read fiber and so factors through both reads. \(\square\)

Equivalently form the bipartite graph with reached \(Y_B\) and \(Y_{B'}\) classes as vertices and an edge \((q_B(d),q_{B'}(d))\) for each admitted complete comparison record. Its connected components are the quotient classes, counting multiplicities separately. This graph merely represents the compatibility relation; it is not a primitive propagation model.

For example, if four complete comparison records have respective fine-sequence reads

\[ 000,\quad010,\quad012,\quad032 \tag{139.26} \]

and coarser endpoint reads

\[ 00,\quad00,\quad02,\quad02, \tag{139.27} \]

then the common quotient has two classes, \(\{000,010\}\) and \(\{012,032\}\). Only afterward can they be interpreted as return versus displacement. The distinctions stationary versus excursion and return, and which intermediate route yielded displacement, do not survive the endpoint read. The owner supplied the complete records and the two native reads; the partition was computed from them without predetermining which invariant should survive.

Limit. The quotient can be trivial. If the compatibility bipartite graph is connected, every commonly readable scalar invariant is constant. The existing R²D axioms do not guarantee multiple components or require any particular physical coherence observable to span them.

139.9. Theorem 7 — Multiple backgrounds and a hierarchy of common reads

Let one specified comparison owner supply \(n+1\) coherent background read maps \(q_0,\ldots,q_n\) on the same finite domain \(D_L\). Define

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal I_{0:n}= D_L/\operatorname{Eq}\left(\bigcup_{i=0}^{n}\ker q_i\right). } \tag{139.28} \]

Theorem 7 (finite common-read hierarchy). \(\mathcal I_{0:n}\) is the most informative classification readable independently from every \(q_i\), and

\[ \boxed{|\mathcal I_{0:n+1}|\le|\mathcal I_{0:n}|.} \tag{139.29} \]

Proof. Theorem 6 applies to the family of all fiber equivalences. Adding another read can only enlarge the generated equivalence relation, thus merge quotient classes, never split them. \(\square\)

This monotonicity concerns the common classification on a fixed comparison domain as additional readability constraints are imposed. It is not a theorem that each successive native boundary has fewer macrostates or smaller possible multiplicity. Different backgrounds may retain complementary distinctions and need not lie on one linear observational-resolution axis.

The existence of a coherent common comparison is a substantive premise. Independently specified owners do not automatically supply a common \(D_L\) or complete admissible cross-background pairings. Addendum 138’s spurious-join objection remains operative.

139.10. Possible counts, native multiplicity, and realization

Three counts must remain separate:

  1. \(|F_J(y)|\) or \(K_J(j,L)\) counts admitted complete possible comparison records within a declared domain; its cardinality depends on genuine native identification in that domain.
  2. \(W_B(s)=|\pi_B^{-1}(s)|\) is the native possible multiplicity of a readable state at a particular boundary. It need not equal a marginal count of complete comparison records, and it does not automatically persist as the native multiplicity of \(B'\).
  3. \(\nu\), \(N_{\mathcal D_R}(\mathcal C)\), and corresponding complete realized records are realized occurrence/recurrence counts in their specified realization domains. They are not derived by equating compatible possible records with observed events.

If multiple presentations identify one native comparison realization, count the distinct native images once, as in Addenda 120 and 138. When a fixed earlier comparison count is regrouped by a read map \(q\), exact aggregation retains the relevant fiber weights. A newly defined native boundary receives its own count specification.

Orientation symmetry of an admitted possible domain does not by itself impose reciprocal realized occurrence counts. Nor does persistence of a compatibility class determine a physical coherence duration or emitted/absorbed background pulse rate.

139.11. Downstream physical readings: turbine, gas, and the two horizons

The mathematics applies first to finite enclosing compatible observations. The following are proposed downstream physical interpretations, not theorems of this addendum.

Gas and thermal motion. A sequence of readable spatial classifications can support a persistent translated-response class, giving a downstream moving-molecule description without requiring persistent identity of the subordinate formal microstates defining each count. Its physical relation to measured velocity autocorrelation, momentum exchange, and energy balance must be independently established. R²D’s possible-count survival fraction is not a measured decay law.

Acoustic absorption and emission. One turbine can have a locally distinguishable closure compatible with an enclosing pressure disturbance, while another turbine has a responsive closure within the same air background. Physical sound propagation and acoustic power transfer are modeled downstream through the equations of acoustics. The sound propagation speed is not the pulse frequency and does not specify a universal count of turbine returns.

Electromagnetic recurrence. The physical projection may preserve a recurrent spectral response after continuous subordinate geometry becomes unreadable. This does not derive a wave equation, identify one completed local turbine return with one photon, or equate the speed of light with an intrinsic background fluctuation frequency. Part IV treats electromagnetic propagation as a within-boundary physical read distinct from recursive carry.

Gravitational and inertial motion. A readable spatial or areal relationship may survive while its organizing enclosing recurrence is unavailable to the chosen observer. That is a candidate interpretation of persistent motion, not yet a derivation of inertial motion or gravitational dynamics. Observed forces, friction, and energy–momentum transfer remain quantitatively real physical relations that any R²D projection must reproduce.

Part III locates the thermodynamic regime where distinguishability and recurrence can be jointly interrogated between two complementary readability limits: a subordinate recurrence-side (quantum) horizon and an enclosing distinction-side (gravitational) horizon. The present mathematical results do not identify those physical horizons with a particular finite \(q_i\), nor require one common physical background scale.

139.12. Results, limitations, and next target

Established relative to specified finite enclosing owners and read maps:

  1. Complete observational admissibility requires a nonempty enclosing record fiber (Definition 139.1).
  2. Unique relational identification requires one enclosing response class across that fiber, not a unique subordinate realization (Theorem 1).
  3. Persistence of a selected relation is equivalent to the nonemptiness of its complete compatible-record intersection; its possible count is nonincreasing under nested requirements on a fixed domain (Theorem 2).
  4. Equal instantaneous and adjacent two-snapshot possible counts can coexist with different complete relational coherence (Theorem 3).
  5. Changes of comparison resolution can alias distinct translations according to an exact modular condition (Theorem 4).
  6. Genuine cross-native replacement preserves a selected relational invariant precisely under a complete correspondence-fiber condition (Theorem 5).
  7. A maximal common classification follows directly from the complete enclosing compatibility and its two native read maps, without choosing an invariant first (Theorem 6).
  8. Common classifications across multiple coherent reads form a descending chain of quotient resolutions on one fixed comparison domain (Theorem 7).

Not established: A uniquely selected complete coherence relation from Part I alone; a stochastic or nonstochastic realization-selection rule; universal physical coherence time; a universal numeric turbine-to-background recurrence ratio; a physical identification of acoustic, thermal, electromagnetic, or gravitational clocks; a derivation of Maxwell propagation, inertia, \(c\), or \(mc^2\).

Next foundational question: Do the native background and recursive-carry axioms independently constrain the admitted cross-background compatibility and read maps enough to force a nontrivial common observational-coherence class, without separately stipulating the complete relation? This is stronger than deriving a quotient once its data are supplied. The physical test would then ask whether that constrained class explains measured correlation persistence and complementary quantum/gravitational readability.

Conclusion

The mathematical identity of a coherent moving or recurring physical read need not be supplied by a continuously persistent subordinate microstate. An enclosing boundary can define complete compatible relationships among successive background-indexed observations, and those relationships can be identifiable even when multiple fine realizations remain admitted.

The most consequential finite result is

\[ \boxed{\text{Equal instantaneous and adjacent counts}\not\Rightarrow\text{equal complete observational coherence}.} \tag{139.30} \]

For a change between two native observing backgrounds, the complete classification of relationships readable from both is derived, not selected in advance:

\[ \boxed{\mathcal I_{BB'}=D_L/\operatorname{Eq}(\ker q_B\cup\ker q_{B'}).} \tag{139.31} \]

Thus observational coherence can survive native replacement without persistence of subordinate identity or invariance of physical frequency. Whether the common quotient is nontrivial and physically realized depends on the enclosing compatibility and its independently established projection. The remaining task is to determine how much of that relation the primitive background and recursive-carry architecture actually selects.
