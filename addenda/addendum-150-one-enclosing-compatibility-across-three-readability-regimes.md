# R²D Addendum 150 — One Enclosing Compatibility Across Three Readability Regimes

*Finite Excitation Exchange, Reciprocal Incidence, and the Survival and Erasure of a Common Count Distinction*

**Status:** Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026  
**Canonical basis:** R²D Parts I–IV (September 25, 2026); Addenda 105, 112, 116, 120, 122, 125, 131–132, 135–141, and 143–149.

## Abstract

Thermodynamic, quantum, and gravitational quantities have been reconstructed within R²D as different physical readings of enclosing compatibility, retained distinction, reciprocal recurrence, and background calibration. A further consistency question is whether one *complete, physically interpretable but dimensionless* count domain supports a nonconstant classification readable in all three modes, without inventing three independent models or installing temperature, photon energy, or mass as primitive causes.

We construct an enclosing owner containing a two-class molecular subsystem and six binary reservoir sites, with exactly three jointly retained excitation distinctions. The owner's thirty-five admitted native records split into classes of twenty and fifteen. From these same records, canonical molecular possibility has contrast $\Delta P=-\ln(4/3)$. The exact interfacial incidence graph has sixty possible excitation-exchange relations, each with a forward and reverse orientation, although the molecular possibility contrast is nonzero. A selected productive constraint can provide an exactly reciprocal possibility–retention cycle with zero complete-cycle asymmetry.

Three receiving count reads are then defined on this **one** complete domain: the complementary reservoir-excitation class, the molecular two-level class, and a locally resolved, orientation-even coupling class. Their common quotient contains exactly two classes, with cardinalities twenty and fifteen. The common nonconstant read is the logarithm of the compatible reservoir multiplicity. Its between-class contrast is $\ln(4/3)$; the *contrast* is a fixed property of the complete compatibility, while the two-valued *read* changes across allowed excitation-exchange relations. A coarser whole-boundary coupling read merges the classes, making the maximal three-read quotient trivial. This supplies an exact finite example of cross-domain relational readability and its boundary-dependent erasure.

The construction also proves a necessary distinction: a nonconstant classification can survive three simultaneous reads without being conserved along a recurring exchange. The exchange graph here is connected; any scalar constant along **every** exchange edge is therefore constant on the whole domain. Physical identifications with thermodynamic temperature, quantum spectral frequency, and gravitational mass or flux are intentionally reserved for a subsequent calibration study.

## 150.1. The question and the prior canon

Part I supplies the native count relationships

$$
W_{B,i}=|\pi_B^{-1}(i)|,\qquad
P_{B,i}=\ln\frac{\Theta_{J\mid B,i}}{W_{B,i}},\qquad
\Delta R=C_J^{out}\lambda,\qquad
\Delta A=\Delta P-\Delta R.
\tag{150.1}
$$

These are boundary-indexed definitions. Neither physical temperature nor electromagnetic energy nor gravitational mass belongs to them primitively. Part II introduces domain-specific energetic reads; Part IV distinguishes orientation-even mass coupling from orientation-retaining charge coupling. Addenda 132 and 139 prove the fiber criterion and common-quotient construction for relationships simultaneously readable across different receiving boundaries. Addenda 143–149 develop scale and electromagnetic examples.

The present purpose is not to reprove that criterion. It is to supply a **single finite common owner with an independently stated joint compatibility**, calculate its exact extension counts and exchange incidences, and exhibit both a successful three-read quotient and an explicit coarsening that erases it.

We distinguish throughout (i) a possible native record, (ii) a possible complete exchange record, (iii) a realized occurrence or completed recurrence, and (iv) a physical calibration. None is silently substituted for another.

## 150.2. A finite molecule–background owner

Let a selected two-class molecular boundary $M$ have native label $q\in\{0,1\}$. Let a finite background $B$ have six distinguishable binary site labels $b_i\in\{0,1\}$, $i=1,\ldots,6$. These labels are *distinguishable excitation classifications*; they are not primitive particles or physical energy quanta.

Specify the complete enclosing owner $J$ by the joint condition

$$
\boxed{q+\sum_{i=1}^{6}b_i=3.}
\tag{150.2}
$$

Its admitted complete native domain is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal H_J=\left\{(q,b_1,\ldots,b_6)\in\{0,1\}^{7}:q+\sum_i b_i=3\right\}.}
\tag{150.3}
$$

This is a single compatibility, not a Cartesian product of independently chosen molecular and background states. A local molecular classification has one native alternative in either state, so $W_M(0)=W_M(1)=1$ in the selected two-class presentation. The reservoir at fixed excitation count $b=\sum_i b_i$ has native multiplicity $W_B(b)=\binom6b$.

If $q=0$, the reservoir must have $b=3$:

$$\Theta_{J\mid M,0}=\binom63=20.$$

If $q=1$, it must have $b=2$:

$$\Theta_{J\mid M,1}=\binom62=15.$$

Consequently

$$
\boxed{|\mathcal H_J|=20+15=35=\binom73.}
\tag{150.4}
$$

Let $H_0$ and $H_1$ denote these two molecular-class fibers. Their possible cardinalities are $|H_0|=20$ and $|H_1|=15$. Neither cardinality is an observed occupancy frequency.

## 150.3. The canonical molecular possibility and the reservoir relation

The local molecule has $W_M(q)=1$, so the canonical possibility is calculated from the enclosing extension count:

$$
\boxed{P_M(0)=\ln20,\qquad P_M(1)=\ln15.}
\tag{150.5}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{\Delta P_{0\to1}=P_M(1)-P_M(0)=\ln\frac34=-\ln\frac43.}
\tag{150.6}
$$

Meanwhile the reservoir-native entropy at fixed $b$ is $S_B(b)=\ln\binom6b$. Thus, on the complete admitted domain,

$$
\boxed{P_M(q)=S_B(3-q),}
\tag{150.7}
$$

and its contrast is

$$
\boxed{\mathscr I_J=S_B(3)-S_B(2)=\ln\frac43\approx0.287682072.}
\tag{150.8}
$$

This is a positive **between-class compatibility contrast**, not a primitive energy gap or a realized entropy-production increment. It is fixed by the count organization. A particular complete record still belongs to one of the two different classes.

The equality (150.7) uses the selected nondegenerate molecular label and the complete $q+b=3$ restriction. It is not a universal equality of molecular possibility and reservoir entropy in systems with different degeneracies or compatibility.

## 150.4. Reciprocal exchange incidences: sixty in each orientation

A possible exchange incidence changes the molecular classification $0\to1$ while replacing exactly one reservoir label $b_i=1$ by $b_i=0$, leaving the complete total equal to three. Every record in $H_0$ has exactly three possible outgoing exchange incidences. Therefore

$$
\boxed{E_{0\to1}^{possible}=20\cdot3=60.}
\tag{150.9}
$$

The inverse incidence $1\to0$ changes one previously empty reservoir site to occupied. Every record in $H_1$ has four such possible incidences, giving

$$
\boxed{E_{1\to0}^{possible}=15\cdot4=60.}
\tag{150.10}
$$

Every forward incidence has an exact inverse, because it connects the same pair of complete native records. The undirected exchange graph therefore has **60 edges**; the two orientations of those edges produce 60 possible directed incidences each, not 120 distinct undirected relationships.

**Proposition 150.1 — Reciprocal incidence with unequal state multiplicity.** One finite enclosing compatibility can have unequal conditional native counts, $|H_0|\ne|H_1|$, nonzero canonical possibility contrast, and exactly paired forward/reverse *possible* exchange incidences.

**Proof.** Equations (150.4)–(150.6) give the unequal counts and nonzero contrast. Equations (150.9)–(150.10) count the two orientations of the same sixty exchange edges. $\square$

This is the count architecture of reciprocal exchange. It does **not** establish that the sixty available edges occur equally often in a physical realization, or derive a kinetic law or stochastic detailed balance.

## 150.5. A selected productive-retention comparison

Canonical possibility has already been fixed by the complete possible counts. To exhibit a compatible *nonzero* reciprocal retention response, select for this owner

$$
\boxed{C_J^{out}=d:=\ln\frac43>0,\qquad
\lambda_{0\to1}=+1,\quad\lambda_{1\to0}=-1.}
\tag{150.11}
$$

This is an explicitly specified positive productive constraint equal numerically to the calculated compatibility contrast. The equality is a *selection for this model*, not a rule identifying $C_J^{out}$ universally with an entropy contrast. Choose the reference $R(0)=0$; then $R(1)=d$, and

| Molecular leg | $\Delta P$ | $\Delta R$ | $\Delta A=\Delta P-\Delta R$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $0\to1$ | $-d$ | $+d$ | $-2d$ |
| $1\to0$ | $+d$ | $-d$ | $+2d$ |
| Complete return | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ |

Thus an admitted complete return $0\to1\to0$ satisfies

$$
\boxed{\Delta_{\mathcal C}P=\Delta_{\mathcal C}R=\Delta A_{\mathcal C}=0.}
\tag{150.12}
$$

Both oriented branches can have nonzero asymmetry although the whole return is reciprocal. An *admitted* return is a possible complete comparison; a positive *realized* $N_{\mathcal C}$ requires realization of such a return in a separately specified recurrence-comparison domain.

The recurrence-weighted R²D law retains its canonical form. When $\Delta A_{\mathcal C}=0$ and its corresponding **positively constrained enclosing transition** has $C_J\lambda_J>0$, that specified transition has zero recurrence. Possible exchange incidences, realized local returns, and this particular directed enclosing-transition recurrence remain different count classes; positive local reciprocity is not evidence of positive recurrence for the prohibited class.

## 150.6. Three distinct receiving count reads, one owner

Define three maps on exactly the same thirty-five records:

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_T(h)&=b(h)=\sum_i b_i\in\{2,3\},\\
q_Q(h)&=q(h)\in\{0,1\},\\
q_G^{loc}(h)&=\kappa_{q(h)}\in\{\kappa_0,\kappa_1\},\quad \kappa_0\ne\kappa_1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{150.13}
$$

The subscript $T$ indicates a reservoir-configuration **candidate thermodynamic read**; $Q$ a molecular-level **candidate quantum read**; $G^{loc}$ a **locally resolved orientation-even coupling read** that might subsequently admit gravitational calibration. The $\kappa_q$ are merely distinct count-level coupling labels here. No gravitational mass or flux is assigned, and the canonical count relation does not itself force $\kappa_0\ne\kappa_1$ to remain readable in every gravitational experiment.

The compatibility (150.2) makes the three labels jointly constrained:

$$
\boxed{q_T=3-q_Q,\qquad q_G^{loc}=\kappa_{q_Q}.}
\tag{150.14}
$$

Thus these are different *presentations* of the one shared two-class relation, not three independent physical causes. Every map identifies exactly the records belonging to $H_0$ or $H_1$, while suppressing the remaining reservoir-site details.

## 150.7. The nonconstant common invariant and its exact quotient

Define one nonconstant enclosing read on the same complete domain:

$$
\boxed{I_J(h)=\ln\binom6{3-q(h)}.}
\tag{150.15}
$$

It takes two distinct values,

$$
I_J(h)=\begin{cases}\ln20,&h\in H_0,\\\ln15,&h\in H_1.\end{cases}
\tag{150.16}
$$

By (150.14), $I_J$ is determined separately by each of the three receiving reads. Explicitly, there are functions $I_T,I_Q,I_G$ on their respective reached images such that

$$
\boxed{I_J=I_T\circ q_T=I_Q\circ q_Q=I_G\circ q_G^{loc}.}
\tag{150.17}
$$

**Theorem 150.2 — Exact nontrivial three-read classification.** For the selected common owner and three read maps (150.13), the most informative common classification has precisely two classes, $H_0,H_1$, with sizes twenty and fifteen. Its nonconstant scalar read $I_J$ is given by (150.15), and its between-class contrast is $\mathscr I_J=\ln(4/3)$.

**Proof.** By (150.14), every $q_T$ fiber, every $q_Q$ fiber, and every $q_G^{loc}$ fiber is either $H_0$ or $H_1$. Thus the equivalence closure generated by their kernels is exactly the two-class partition. By the common-read theorem of Addenda 132 and 139, the quotient

$$
\boxed{\mathcal I_{TQG}=\mathcal H_J/\operatorname{Eq}(\ker q_T\cup\ker q_Q\cup\ker q_G^{loc})=\{H_0,H_1\}}
\tag{150.18}
$$

is the most informative classification recoverable independently from every read. Equation (150.16) proves it is nonconstant. Equation (150.8) gives its contrast. $\square$

The *value* of $\mathscr I_J$ is constant for the selected owner because it compares two complete classes. The *value* of $I_J(h)$ depends on the class of the particular record. They must not be conflated.

## 150.8. Coarser whole-boundary coupling erases the distinction

Consider a different, larger-boundary read that retains only the complete excitation inventory:

$$
\boxed{q_G^{tot}(h)=q(h)+b(h)=3\qquad\forall h\in\mathcal H_J.}
\tag{150.19}
$$

This is a count-level **whole-system inventory read**, proposed as the appropriate abstract comparison to a coarser orientation-even total-coupling or gravitational-monopole read. It is *not yet* a theorem that measured gravitational mass is literally $q+b$.

Because $q_G^{tot}$ is constant, its sole fiber is the entire thirty-five-record domain. The three-read quotient with this coarser read is

$$
\boxed{\mathcal I_{TQG}^{tot}=\{\mathcal H_J\},\qquad|\mathcal I_{TQG}^{tot}|=1.}
\tag{150.20}
$$

**Theorem 150.3 — Boundary-dependent erasure.** Replacing $q_G^{loc}$ by the whole-boundary read $q_G^{tot}$ makes every nonconstant common scalar invariant unreadable from all three receiving read maps jointly, although the native complete domain and its twenty/fifteen possible-count partition have not changed.

**Proof.** Every two records have the same $q_G^{tot}$ value. A scalar factoring through this constant map is constant on all of $\mathcal H_J$. The generated common-read equivalence relation therefore has one class. Equations (150.4)–(150.8) still hold at the underlying owner, because the owner has not changed. $\square$

Loss of this distinction from the coarser read does not destroy the molecular or reservoir organization. Nor does it imply that no more discriminating gravitational observable could retain it. The theorem concerns the specified single total-inventory class.

## 150.9. A different sense of invariance: recurrence conservation

A potential ambiguity is especially important here. The two-class read $I_J(h)$ is an **invariant across receiving descriptions of the same complete record**. It is **not conserved across the admitted molecular exchange**: $0\leftrightarrow1$ changes its value between $\ln20$ and $\ln15$.

In fact, the undirected sixty-edge exchange graph on $\mathcal H_J$ is connected. To see this, any two three-excitation reservoir subsets can be related by successive replacements of one excited site with one unexcited site. Each such replacement factors through a two-excitation reservoir subset at $q=1$. Every $q=1$ record is adjacent to at least one $q=0$ record. Hence every native record lies in one connected exchange component.

**Theorem 150.4 — No nonconstant state invariant on every exchange edge.** If a scalar $F:\mathcal H_J\to V$ satisfies $F(h)=F(h')$ whenever $h,h'$ are joined by one of the sixty possible exchange edges, then $F$ is constant on $\mathcal H_J$.

**Proof.** Equality along edges propagates along every finite graph path. Connectivity implies equality between every pair of vertices. $\square$

Thus:

- The conserved total excitation classification $K=q+b=3$ is constant on all possible records.
- The nonconstant $I_J(h)$ is independently readable in three differently labeled receiving classifications, but changes when an exchange occurs.
- The count contrast $\mathscr I_J=\ln(4/3)$ is a fixed, nonzero property of the complete *relation between the two classes*, not a nonconstant scalar carried unchanged by an individual exchanging record.
- Possible reciprocal edge counts $60=60$ do not specify realized cycling rates.

These distinctions prevent the words *shared invariant*, *conserved quantity*, and *unchanging energetic content* from being substituted for one another.

## 150.10. General finite theorem: $N$ sites and $K$ retained distinctions

The construction is a member of an exact finite family. Let $N\ge1$ binary background sites, a two-class local subsystem $q\in\{0,1\}$, and a selected complete count $1\le K\le N$:

$$
\mathcal H_{N,K}=\{(q,b_1,\ldots,b_N):q+\sum_i b_i=K\}.
\tag{150.21}
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
|H_0|&=\binom NK,\\
|H_1|&=\binom N{K-1},\\
|\mathcal H_{N,K}|&=\binom{N+1}K,\\
\Delta P_{0\to1}&=\ln\frac{K}{N-K+1}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{150.22}
$$

Every $q=0$ record has $K$ possible forward exchanges; every $q=1$ record has $N-K+1$ reverse exchanges. Hence

$$
\boxed{\binom NK K=\binom N{K-1}(N-K+1)}
\tag{150.23}
$$

counts the same unoriented exchange edges from both endpoint classes.

**Theorem 150.5 — General finite exchange and read criterion.** Under (150.21), with local native multiplicities $W_M(0)=W_M(1)=1$:

1. The canonical possibility contrast is the last expression in (150.22).
2. Every admitted forward exchange has a unique reverse incidence, and the two possible directed-incidence totals agree as in (150.23).
3. If three selected receiving read maps independently distinguish the two local classes, their maximal common quotient has two classes. If any one receiving read merges them, and the remaining maps distinguish no finer common class, the quotient has one class.
4. The count contrast is nonzero precisely when $K\ne(N+1)/2$. For $K<(N+1)/2$, the contrast $-\Delta P$ is positive.

**Proof.** The first two claims follow by binomial enumeration and double counting of each edge. The third is the common-read fiber criterion of Addenda 132 and 139. For the fourth, $\Delta P=0$ iff $K=N-K+1$. $\square$

The six-site, three-excitation system is the special case $(N,K)=(6,3)$, with unequal twenty/fifteen class sizes and a positive $\ln(4/3)$ contrast.

## 150.11. Background, recurrence, and physical calibration remain distinct

In the present construction, the finite reservoir is a specified *subordinate background-supporting count domain*. Its combinatorial entropy $\ln\binom6b$ is not automatically Part I's realized nondirectional background $\Phi_J=\ln b_J$. The sixty possible exchange edges are not whole-closure recurrence counts $N_{\mathcal C}$, nor are they the native background recurrence count $N_J^\Phi$. If a realized complete return has reciprocally equal opposed-leg occurrence counts, its corresponding background *increment* may vanish even while the enclosing background and recurrence remain positive.

Similarly, the labels $T$, $Q$, and $G$ in (150.13) specify candidate **physical readability roles**. Their common quotient is an exact unitless consistency result. To promote the count labels to actual thermodynamic, quantum, and gravitational measurements, one must provide a common physical preparation and calibration. The native result does not already assert:

$$\epsilon=k_BT\mathscr I_J,\qquad \epsilon=hf,\qquad\epsilon=c^2\Delta m$$

as primitive equalities. Such formulas are possible *downstream* physical maps for a selected excitation contrast, not definitions used to count $35$, $20$, $15$, or $60$.

Nor does the abstract total-inventory read (150.19) automatically imply that every gravitational field observable is constant when excitation is redistributed. Only the specifically chosen coarse total class has been shown to erase the distinction.

## 150.12. Results, scope, and continuation

The new finite **witness**, rather than the pre-existing common-read abstract theorem, is the contribution:

1. One joint, explicitly molecular/background-compatible domain has exactly thirty-five native possible records, partitioned twenty/fifteen.
2. Those counts produce the canonical molecular possibility difference $-\ln(4/3)$ and the identical reservoir multiplicity contrast of opposite sign.
3. Exactly sixty undirected exchange incidences admit paired possible orientations, notwithstanding unequal state-class cardinalities.
4. A selected productive constraint supplies a nonzero reciprocal $P$–$R$ exchange with zero complete-cycle asymmetry.
5. Three separately defined receiving reads can share an exact nonconstant two-class quotient, while one coarser whole-boundary read collapses that quotient to one class.
6. The exchange graph is connected, proving that cross-read preservation of a nonconstant classification is not the same as conservation of that classification through every possible exchange.
7. The formulas hold for the general finite $(N,K)$ family under the stated constraints.

This is a **combinatorial existence and consistency result**. It does not require uniqueness of the finite owner or disproof of other valid physical descriptions. Its physical novelty will depend on connecting the chosen local orientation-even coupling read and reservoir/molecular level reads to one experimentally grounded joint preparation—not on repeating the finite counting proof.

The next investigation should hold the complete count owner fixed while specifying how independently readable physical instruments calibrate the same two-class distinction as a thermal contrast, a quantum spectral transition, or a local mass-bearing read; then compare that to a complete-system gravitational measurement that retains only the total response. The experimental read maps must be established rather than chosen solely to preserve a desired invariant.

## Conclusion

One complete, physically interpretable finite compatibility can support **three common, mutually constrained readable classifications** before temperature, electromagnetic energy, or gravitational mass is introduced:

$$
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
|\mathcal H_J|=35,\quad |H_0|=20,\quad |H_1|=15,\\
P_M(0)-P_M(1)=\mathscr I_J=\ln\frac43>0,\\
E_{0\to1}^{possible}=E_{1\to0}^{possible}=60,\\
|\mathcal I_{TQG}^{loc}|=2,\qquad
|\mathcal I_{TQG}^{tot}|=1.
\end{gathered}}
$$

The enclosing owner defines the possibilities and the exact reciprocal incidence relationship. Selected receiving boundaries retain or erase the two-class distinction according to their own read maps. **What persists across descriptions is a relation belonging to the enclosing compatibility—not a persistent subordinate excitation or an energy packet carried from one primitive domain into another.**

The distinction between **cross-read invariance** and **exchange conservation** is essential: the common nonconstant class can change during an admitted reciprocal occurrence while remaining recoverable from every sufficiently discriminating read of each complete record. That prepares the physical comparison without predetermining its outcome.
