# Addendum 44 — Entropic-Well Depth Occurs Before Maximum Gravitational Mass

## Abstract

A maximum gravitational mass is ordinarily posed as a property of matter or of a gravitational solution.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) begins earlier.

Addendum 43 established that a Schwarzschild horizon can be represented as a boundary-defined count
domain containing radially distinguishable macrostates. Their multiplicities are

$$W_{H,i},$$

with logarithmic coordinates

$$S_{H,i}=\ln W_{H,i}.$$

The horizon boundary is physically projected through area,

$$B_H\longrightarrow A_H,$$

while distinguishability within the boundary is projected through radius,

$$\lambda_H\longrightarrow r_H.$$

Under the Bekenstein–Hawking physical calibration,

$$S_H=\frac{A_H}{4\ell_P^2},$$

and under the candidate Planck radial calibration,

$$r_i=i\ell_P.$$

The present addendum asks what it means for such a gravitational multiplicity landscape to become deep,
and whether that depth can possess an intrinsic maximum.

Define the depth of a gravitational multiplicity well relative to a reference macrostate by

$$D_H\equiv S_H-S_0=\ln\frac{W_H}{W_0}.$$

For the convenient reference

$$W_0=1,$$

this becomes

$$D_H=S_H.$$

This does not identify horizon entropy with gravitational retention.

Retention and multiplicity remain distinct:

$$R_H\ne S_H.$$

The well depth measures separation in logarithmic multiplicity, not retained possibility.

For the Planck-distinguished radial horizon construction,

$$D_H=\pi N^2,$$

where

$$N=\frac{r_H}{\ell_P}.$$

Under the Schwarzschild mapping

$$r_H=\frac{2GM}{c^2},$$

this becomes

$$D_H=\frac{4\pi GM^2}{\hbar c}.$$

Thus physical gravitational mass is a downstream coordinate of multiplicity-well depth:

$$M=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar c}{4\pi G}}\sqrt{D_H}.$$

Nothing in this relation imposes a maximum value of DH or M .

A maximum gravitational mass therefore cannot be intrinsic to the subordinate well alone.

It requires an enclosing boundary.

Let the subordinate gravitational boundary have multiplicity

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1517 -->

$$W_H$$

and let an enclosing gravitational count boundary have multiplicity

$$W_E.$$

Define the subordinate–enclosing multiplicity gap

$$\Delta S_{E\mid H}=S_E-S_H=\ln\frac{W_E}{W_H}.$$

As long as

$$\Delta S_{E\mid H}>0,$$

the subordinate and enclosing count classes remain distinguishable under the nested classification
considered here.

The marginal condition is

$$\Delta S_{E\mid H}=0.$$

Then

$$S_E=S_H$$

and therefore

$$W_E=W_H.$$

The multiplicity distinction defining the subordinate/enclosing relation has vanished.

If both gravitational boundaries are read through the same areal multiplicity calibration,

$$S=\frac A{4\ell_P^2},$$

then

$$\Delta S_{E\mid H}=\frac{A_E-A_H}{4\ell_P^2}.$$

Therefore the count-level marginal condition projects physically as

$$A_E=A_H.$$

Maximum gravitational mass is then the later physical source coordinate assigned to this prior loss of
subordinate–enclosing multiplicity distinction.

<!-- source_pdf_page: 1518 -->

The logical order is therefore

multiplicity well → well depth → enclosing multiplicity gap → gap closure → areal equality → maximum mass.

A formal continuation of the original subordinate/enclosing classification after

$$W_E=W_H$$

would preserve a distinction no longer supported by the defining count relation.

This is the gravitational analogue of a Gibbs-type boundary-classification failure.

The present addendum does not identify the physical solution that realizes this marginal condition.

That is the purpose of Addendum 45.

Its central claim is narrower:

> a maximum gravitational mass is not an intrinsic property of a local mass.

Rather,

gravitational mass saturation is the projected physical read of a prior enclosing multiplicity limit.

Thus:

> entropic-well depth occurs before maximum gravitational mass.
