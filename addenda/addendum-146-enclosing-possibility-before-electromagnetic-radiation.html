The Primitive Quantum Medium, Reciprocal Closure, and Propagation Without a Primitive Space

R²D Addendum 146 — Enclosing Possibility Before Electromagnetic Radiation

The Primitive Quantum Medium, Reciprocal Closure, and Propagation Without a Primitive Space

Status: Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026
Canonical basis: R²D Parts I–IV; Addenda 72, 78–80, 105, 107, 112, 120, 122, 131–140, and 145.

Abstract

Electromagnetic radiation is ordinarily described as a propagating electromagnetic field, a collection of photons, or an excitation of a quantum field. Each description begins with physical objects or coordinates whose mathematical relationships are already defined.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) begins earlier, with boundary-indexed distinguishability, enclosing-compatible possibility, retention, recurrence, closure, and recursive replacement.

Addendum 145 establishes a dimensionless propagation architecture. A completed local recurrence can belong to a larger enclosing relationship that admits directional continuation without requiring persistent transport of subordinate microstate identities. Propagation is not guaranteed; its compatibility and realization belong to the enclosing boundary.

The present addendum investigates the corresponding primitive architecture at the quantum readability horizon.

We explicitly assume that the quantum-side background admits a medium of binary distinctions. This medium is a boundary-defined compatibility and recurrence organization, not an ensemble of hidden particles, a material ether, or a primitive electromagnetic field. The distinctions need not remain individually readable at the quantum horizon.

Within a selected local boundary, possibility and retention can change reciprocally on one entropic landscape. Increasing retention accompanies decreasing possibility; decreasing retention accompanies increasing possibility. A complete reciprocal cycle returns both coordinates and has zero net closure asymmetry, although its individual branches can possess nonzero opposing asymmetries. Positive completed recurrence remains compatible with this exact return.

The local boundary defines the completed cycle but does not independently define possibilities beyond itself. Those possibilities belong to a larger enclosing boundary. When the larger boundary admits a compatible extension of the completed local relationship, the possibility of continued realization is established. When such a possibility is realized, its complete organization acquires an enclosing occurrence classification through recursive carry.

The resulting causal interpretation is boundary-first: radiation is not pushed out of a source into an independently existing space. The larger enclosing possibilities organize the recurrence relationship whose downstream physical reading is radiation propagating through space.

This addendum formalizes that primitive interpretation and distinguishes it from the stronger claim that every reciprocal closure must propagate. It preserves the canonical separation between state occurrences, whole-closure recurrence, and constrained enclosing-transition recurrence. It does not derive physical electromagnetic fields, photons, frequency, wavelength, propagation speed, polarization, or Maxwell’s equations. Those are subsequent physical mappings.

146.1. The question after Addendum 145

Addendum 145 establishes the dimensionless architecture of propagation.

An enclosing boundary defines complete compatible relationships among recurring subordinate distinctions. A local state can return exactly while a larger enclosing relationship retains a distinguishable continuation.

The important causal distinction is that propagation is not produced by subordinate components pushing one another into successive physical positions.

The enclosing relation defines the possibilities under which subordinate changes can be realized.

The present question is:

What primitive count organization can support the physical observation called electromagnetic radiation before the introduction of electromagnetic fields, photons, or physical space?

The objective is a consistency construction.

We do not require the primitive R²D axioms to exclude all other compatible propagation classes or uniquely select the electromagnetic physical representation.

We require a dimensionless organization that admits the relevant recurring and propagating relationships without contradicting the primitive count laws.

146.2. The quantum-background assumption

Assume that an enclosing quantum-side background \(J_Q\) admits a medium of binary distinctions.

At a selected local boundary \(B\), the readable alternatives are

\[ q_B\in\{+,-\}. \]

These are boundary-defined distinctions.

They do not identify persistent particles, material constituents, primitive fields, or two physically separated locations.

A complete enclosing relation determines which joint binary realizations are compatible.

For \(N\) distinguishable binary alternatives, a selected native landscape may be represented by

\[ \boxed{ W_B(k)=\binom Nk. } \tag{146.1} \]

The logarithmic entropic coordinate is

\[ \boxed{ S_B(k)=\ln W_B(k). } \tag{146.2} \]

The landscape possesses neither intrinsic physical spatial extent nor intrinsic recurrence duration.

The quantum-background assumption introduces a compatible medium of distinctions, not physical objects hidden from observation.

At the quantum readability horizon, the underlying subordinate distinctions may no longer be individually recoverable. Their enclosing recurrence or spectral relationship can nevertheless remain readable.

This is the primitive medium considered in this addendum.

The existence of such a physical quantum medium is an explicit hypothesis. Its definition is compatible with R²D’s boundary-indexed architecture, but its physical identification is not established by binary counting alone.

146.3. Possibility and retention belong to the same landscape

Let \(B\) possess native macrostate classification \(i\).

Its multiplicity is

\[ W_{B,i}=|\pi_B^{-1}(i)|. \]

Let \(J_Q\) define the compatible enclosing extension multiplicity

\[ \Theta_{J_Q\mid B,i}. \]

The canonical possibility coordinate is

\[ \boxed{ P_{B,i} = \ln \frac{\Theta_{J_Q\mid B,i}}{W_{B,i}}. } \tag{146.3} \]

Retention is defined through the enclosing constraint relation.

For a selected local distinction,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta R=C_{J_Q}^{out}\lambda. } \tag{146.4} \]

The resulting local asymmetry is

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A=\Delta P-\Delta R. } \tag{146.5} \]

Possibility and retention are not separate physical directions.

Both concern the compatibility of realized distinctions within the same recursively organized count landscape.

The entropic landscape itself precedes any physical spatial read.

Thus the primitive relations are changes in compatible count organization, not motion along geometric axes.

146.4. The reciprocal possibility–retention cycle

Consider two readable local classifications \(i\) and \(k\), with a complete compatible return

\[ \mathcal C_B: i\longrightarrow k\longrightarrow i. \]

Suppose the enclosing compatibility admits reciprocal changes in possibility and retention.

On one branch,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta P<0, \qquad \Delta R>0. } \tag{146.6} \]

On the opposed branch,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta P>0, \qquad \Delta R<0. } \tag{146.7} \]

The two changes belong to one entropic landscape.

Exact reciprocal closure

For a fixed enclosing-compatible relation in which both coordinates return exactly,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta_{\mathcal C}P=0, \qquad \Delta_{\mathcal C}R=0. } \tag{146.8} \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{\mathcal C} = \Delta_{\mathcal C}P - \Delta_{\mathcal C}R =0. } \tag{146.9} \]

The completed cycle has zero net closure asymmetry.

Its separate branches need not have zero asymmetry. If possibility and retention change in opposite directions, their branch asymmetries can be nonzero and reciprocal.

This is not asymmetric closure.

The enclosing possibility and retention coordinates return after the completed cycle.

Proposition 146.1 — Reciprocal local closure

Let one enclosing-compatible local cycle admit two opposed branches whose possibility and retention increments are exact inverses.

Then the complete cycle has zero accumulated possibility, zero accumulated retention, and zero net asymmetry.

Proof.

The increments of each coordinate cancel over the completed return. Since asymmetry is their difference, its complete-cycle increment also vanishes. \(\square\)

The result concerns exact count closure.

It does not require a physical oscillation, field amplitude, wavelength, or spatial trajectory.

146.5. Positive recurrence under zero net asymmetry

The complete reciprocal cycle can recur.

Let

\[ N_{\mathcal D_R}(\mathcal C_B) \]

count completed local returns within a specified recurrence-comparison domain.

A compatible realization may satisfy

\[ \boxed{ N_{\mathcal D_R}(\mathcal C_B)>0 } \tag{146.10} \]

while retaining

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{\mathcal C_B}=0. } \tag{146.11} \]

These are different count statements.

The first counts completed recurrence. The second states that the complete local possibility–retention relationship returns without net asymmetry.

Under reciprocal opposed-leg realization,

\[ \nu_H=\nu_L, \]

and the corresponding R²D background increment is

\[ \boxed{ \delta_{\mathcal C}\Phi_{J_Q} = \ln\frac{\nu_H}{\nu_L} =0. } \tag{146.12} \]

Zero background increment does not mean zero background organization or zero completed recurrence.

Thus reciprocal local activity can remain positive without a net asymmetric output.

This is the symmetric-recurrence architecture established in Addendum 131.

The quantum background may organize such recurrence even though its subordinate local distinctions cannot be individually reconstructed at the receiving readability horizon.

146.6. The local boundary does not define what lies beyond it

A count boundary defines its own native distinctions, compatible realizations, and completed closures.

It does not independently define the complete possible organization of a larger boundary.

Thus a completed local cycle

\[ \mathcal C_B \]

does not determine what can occur beyond its native count domain.

The phrase outside the boundary does not yet mean a physical region of space.

It refers to distinctions and compatibility relationships belonging to a larger enclosing count domain.

Let \(J_Q\) own a complete admitted possible-record domain

\[ \mathcal H_{J_Q}. \]

Define the subset of records compatible with the completed local cycle:

\[ \boxed{ E_{J_Q}(\mathcal C_B) = \left\{ h\in\mathcal H_{J_Q}: h\text{ admits }\mathcal C_B \right\}. } \tag{146.13} \]

Its extension count is

\[ \boxed{ \Theta_{J_Q\mid B}(\mathcal C_B) = |E_{J_Q}(\mathcal C_B)|. } \tag{146.14} \]

When

\[ \Theta_{J_Q\mid B}(\mathcal C_B)>0, \]

the local closure has at least one compatible enclosing extension.

This count establishes possibility, not occurrence.

Whether that possibility is realized is a separate R²D question.

The enclosing extension count is not automatically identical to the canonical local macrostate possibility coordinate \(P_{B,i}\), because the complete closure and the native macrostate belong to different specified classifications.

146.7. Enclosing possibility precedes propagation

The completed local cycle does not produce the possibilities of the larger boundary.

Those possibilities are defined by the enclosing compatibility.

This is the causal priority established in Addendum 105 and expressed in Addendum 107:

Possibilities pull; probabilities do not push.

For propagation, the larger boundary may admit a complete relationship in which a recurring local distinction has compatible continuation beyond the selected local closure.

The local cycle cannot define that continuation independently.

The enclosing boundary defines it.

When a compatible possibility is realized, the complete relationship acquires an occurrence classification at the enclosing boundary.

The conceptual order is

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{Enclosing compatible possibilities}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Admitted reciprocal local recurrence}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Completed local closure}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{Enclosing occurrence reading}. \end{gathered} } \tag{146.15} \]

The arrows summarize organizing dependence and boundary-relative readout. They are not a claim that subordinate changes generate the enclosing possibility domain.

The complete relation is defined by the larger boundary before the subordinate realization is classified.

146.8. Recursive replacement and enclosing occurrence

Part I distinguishes causal realization from recursive carry.

An enclosing occurrence determines the compatible subordinate closures through which it can be realized:

\[ \boxed{ \chi_{J_Q}^{(r)} \Longrightarrow (\mathcal C_{B,a})_a \in\mathfrak D_{J_Q,\chi}. } \tag{146.16} \]

Under a change of count boundary, a completed local realization acquires an enclosing identity:

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal C_{B,a} \overset{\kappa_B}{\mapsto} \chi_{J_Q}^{(r)}. } \tag{146.17} \]

These are two boundary-specific descriptions of one compatible realization.

Recursive carry does not transport the local microstate into the larger boundary intact.

Nor does it construct an enclosing occurrence by adding independently acting subordinate cycles.

The completed local cycle is reclassified according to the enclosing occurrence to which its realization belongs.

What the larger boundary distinguishes

The local cycle can close exactly while the larger boundary distinguishes the complete ordering and compatibility of multiple recurring local distinctions.

Two compatible complete records may therefore have the same local reciprocal-cycle classification but different enclosing propagation classifications.

One may admit coherent directional continuation.

Another may admit only bounded reciprocal recurrence.

The native local closure does not distinguish between these enclosing alternatives by itself.

This is consistent with the complete-context compatibility and readability results of Addenda 139–140.

146.9. A dimensionless propagation compatibility construction

Let \(J_Q\) contain a finite ordered family of native comparison positions

\[ j\in\mathbb Z_m. \]

These are labels of distinguishable relations within one complete enclosing count domain. They do not yet denote calibrated physical locations.

Let the local readable distinction be binary:

\[ q_j(n)\in\{+,-\}, \]

where \(n\) indexes a background recurrence comparison, not elapsed physical time.

For a periodic pattern \(F\), the enclosing owner may admit a complete relationship

\[ \boxed{ q_j(n)=F(j-n\pmod m). } \tag{146.18} \]

This is one compatible directional propagation class from Addendum 145.

After one complete comparison period,

\[ \boxed{ q_j(n+m)=q_j(n). } \tag{146.19} \]

Each local classification returns.

Meanwhile, the complete enclosing relation distinguishes an unwrapped propagation count

\[ \boxed{ X_{n+m}-X_n=m. } \tag{146.20} \]

Thus local return and enclosing directional classification are compatible.

This construction demonstrates possible organization, not guaranteed realization.

A positive count of compatible complete records permits a propagation possibility. A positive occurrence count requires that a possibility in the selected class actually be realized.

The direction belongs to the enclosing spatial–recurrence compatibility, not to the local net asymmetry.

The local recurrence may remain symmetric throughout.

146.10. The canonical zero-asymmetry restriction

The recurrence-weighted R²D law must remain intact.

For a selected representative local closure and its corresponding constrained enclosing transition,

\[ \boxed{ \Delta A_{\mathcal C_B} N_{\mathcal D_R}(\mathcal C_B) = C_{J_Q}\lambda_{J_Q} N_{\mathcal D_R}(\chi_{J_Q}). } \tag{146.21} \]

For the exact reciprocal local cycle,

\[ \Delta A_{\mathcal C_B}=0. \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{ C_{J_Q}\lambda_{J_Q} N_{\mathcal D_R}(\chi_{J_Q})=0. } \tag{146.22} \]

If the selected enclosing transition has

\[ C_{J_Q}\lambda_{J_Q}>0, \]

its corresponding completed recurrence count must vanish.

Consequently, the zero-asymmetry local closure cannot be assigned positive recurrence of that same positively constrained enclosing transition.

This does not erase the local recurrence or the larger boundary’s possible propagation classifications.

It requires exact separation of:

  1. completed local closure recurrence;
  2. possible complete enclosing propagation records;
  3. realized occurrences classified by the enclosing boundary;
  4. completed recurrence of a specified constrained enclosing transition.

A nonzero propagation occurrence must be assigned to its correct native count class and supported by the complete enclosing realization relation. It cannot be produced merely by relabeling the constrained transition count in Equation (146.21).

This is the outstanding recurrence-accounting condition for any stronger identification of the propagating relationship with a particular enclosing transition.

The primitive compatibility construction does not require us to assume that such an identification has already been proved.

146.11. Why propagation is not a push

The primitive interpretation now follows from the causal order.

The local cycle is complete within its own boundary.

The larger boundary defines possibilities that the local boundary cannot define.

If the larger compatibility admits a continuing propagation relationship, that possibility is available for realization.

No local microstate independently creates the next boundary or its compatible distinctions.

No primitive object must be pushed across successive physical positions.

The enclosing compatibility defines what continuation means before it acquires a physical spatial representation.

Under the proposed electromagnetic interpretation:

The possibilities whose downstream physical reading is the space into which light propagates organize the propagation. Light is not pushed into that space by a primitive source object.

This is the proposed R²D organizing-causality explanation, not a claim that physical radiation can appear without compatible source, detector, or enclosing preparation conditions.

The meaning of pull is count-level directional compatibility, not a physical force acting backward from an empty future region.

The physical narrative of a source emitting light remains an admissible downstream account of realized relationships. It is not assigned primitive causal ownership of the enclosing possibilities.

146.12. Propagation and bounded reciprocal recurrence

A completed reciprocal local cycle need not possess compatible onward propagation.

The enclosing boundary may admit continued directional recurrence or may constrain that continuation.

If directional propagation is admitted and a corresponding possibility is realized, the complete relationship acquires the enclosing propagation read.

If directional continuation is prevented but a compatible spatially bounded reciprocal recurrence remains admitted and is realized, the organization can acquire a standing-mode reading.

Blocking outward propagation does not guarantee that a standing mode remains compatible.

The distinction belongs to the larger enclosing boundary.

The local reciprocal cycle may be common to both types of organization.

Thus the difference between traveling and standing radiation does not require two primitive kinds of local binary distinction.

It requires different enclosing compatibility and realization relationships.

146.13. What is primitive and what is downstream

The primitive construction contains:

  • boundary-defined binary distinguishability;
  • native multiplicity and enclosing-compatible extension counts;
  • possibility and retention on a common entropic landscape;
  • reciprocal local closure with zero net asymmetry;
  • positive completed recurrence where compatible and realized;
  • enclosingly admitted continuation;
  • boundary-relative occurrence classification and recursive carry.

It does not contain a primitive electromagnetic field, photon, physical vacuum geometry, spatial distance, wavelength, frequency, velocity, polarization, or energy.

These are not missing primitive causes.

They are possible physical descriptions of the underlying compatible recurrence and propagation organization.

The present addendum therefore stops before assigning electromagnetic physical coordinates.

The earlier quantum and electromagnetic addenda provide downstream phase, connection, curvature, and energetic mappings that can subsequently be compared with this construction.

No such mapping is assumed in the primitive definitions here.

146.14. Established mathematical results and scope

Result 1 — Reciprocal closure

A complete reciprocal possibility–retention cycle can return both coordinates exactly, yielding zero accumulated closure asymmetry.

This is an exact count result under the specified reciprocal compatibility.

Result 2 — Positive recurrence without net asymmetry

Positive completed local recurrence is compatible with zero net cycle asymmetry and reciprocal opposed-leg occurrence realization.

Zero net asymmetric output is not cessation of recurrence.

Result 3 — Enclosing possibility is not defined locally

A local count boundary does not determine its own compatible enclosing extensions.

The existence and count of those extensions require a larger enclosing owner.

Result 4 — Propagation compatibility precedes spatial projection

A larger enclosing boundary can admit a coherent continuing relationship among recurring binary distinctions.

That relation is dimensionless and does not require primitive physical space.

Result 5 — Local closure and enclosing propagation are distinct classifications

Exact local return does not, by itself, determine the enclosing propagation class.

The larger boundary can distinguish complete relations that have identical local closure signatures.

Result 6 — Recursive carry changes count identity

A completed local realization receives an enclosing occurrence meaning under the compatibility defined by the larger boundary.

No persistent subordinate microstate identity is required across that change of boundary.

Result 7 — Propagation is not guaranteed

The larger enclosing relation may admit, constrain, or exclude continued propagation.

Positive compatible extension counts do not independently determine realized occurrence counts.
