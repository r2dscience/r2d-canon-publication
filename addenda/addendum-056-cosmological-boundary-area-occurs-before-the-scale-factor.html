Addendum 56 — Cosmological Boundary Area Occurs Before the Scale Factor

Macrostate Factorization, Areal Place Value, and the Geometric Read of Cosmological Peer Reorganization

Addendum 56 — Cosmological Boundary Area Occurs Before the Scale Factor

Abstract

Addendum 54 defined the primitive cosmological state space.

A cosmological boundary

\[B_\zeta\]

contains subordinate peer boundaries whose compatible joint realizations form the formal microstate domain

\[\Omega_\zeta\]

The cosmological boundary classifies those realizations through

\[\pi_\zeta:\Omega_\zeta\to\mathcal M_\zeta.\]

A cosmological macrostate

\[i \in \mathcal M_\zeta\]

is therefore a boundary-readable classification of compatible peer realization.

Its structural state is determined by occupancy,

\[i_\zeta^\ast=\arg\max_i c_{\zeta,i}.\]

\[i\]

A structural cosmological transition is

\[i_\zeta^\ast \to k_\zeta^\ast\]

Addendum 55 then introduced the gravitational projection

\[\Pi_G:\Omega_\zeta\to\Gamma_G.\]

through which an already-defined peer-boundary relation may acquire mass-energy and geometric coordinates.

The next question is now precise:

Which gravitational observable faithfully reads the cosmological macrostate transition?

R²D cannot answer this merely by identifying a conventional cosmological variable that changes monotonically with time.

A physical quantity can represent a cosmological macrostate only if it is insensitive to the unresolved formal microstate distinctions contained within that macrostate.

Let

\[Q:\Gamma_G\to\mathcal Q\]

be a candidate physical observable.

For Q to be a physical read of the R²D macrostate classification, there must exist a macrostate-level map

\[\bar Q_\zeta:\mathcal M_\zeta\to\mathcal Q\]

such that

\[Q\circ\Pi_G=\bar Q_\zeta\circ\pi_\zeta\]

Equivalently,

\[\pi_\zeta(\mu_\alpha)=\pi_\zeta(\mu_\beta)\Longrightarrow Q[\Pi_G(\mu_\alpha)]=Q[\Pi_G(\mu_\beta)]\]

The physical observable must therefore be constant over each cosmological macrostate fiber.

This is the macrostate physical-read factorization condition.

The gravitational development preceding this addendum provides a natural first candidate.

R²D maps a gravitational boundary onto physical area:

\[B_G \to A_G\]

Apply this to the cosmological boundary.

If physical area satisfies the factorization condition,

\[A\circ\Pi_G=\bar A_\zeta\circ\pi_\zeta\]

then every cosmological macrostate i possesses a well-defined areal read

\[A_{\zeta,i}=\bar A_\zeta(i)\]

A structural cosmological transition

\[i_\zeta^\ast \to k_\zeta^\ast\]

then acquires the geometric projection

\[A_{\zeta,i} \to A_{\zeta,k}\]

Area therefore becomes the first candidate gravitational read of cosmological peer reorganization.

Radius enters only afterward.

For an isotropic spherical area read,

\[r_{\zeta,i}=\sqrt{\frac{A_{\zeta,i}}{4\pi}}\]

Thus:

\[B_\zeta \to A_\zeta \to r\zeta\]

This preserves the R²D result that area occurs before radius.

The FLRW scale factor enters one step later.

For a fixed comoving spherical label χ, the conventional FLRW metric may be written

\[ds^2=-c^2dt^2+a^2(t)[d\chi^2+S_k^2(\chi)d\Omega^2]\]

The physical area of that fixed comoving two-sphere is

\[A_\chi(t)=4\pi a^2(t)S_k^2(\chi)\]

For the same comoving boundary at two physical epochs,

\[\frac{A_\chi(t_2)}{A_\chi(t_1)}=\frac{a^2(t_2)}{a^2(t_1)}\]

Therefore,

\[\frac{a(t_2)}{a(t_1)}=\sqrt{\frac{A_\chi(t_2)}{A_\chi(t_1)}}\]

If the cosmological R²D boundary area is identified with this isotropic geometric read, then

\[\frac{a_k}{a_i}=\sqrt{\frac{A_{\zeta,k}}{A_{\zeta,i}}}\]

The scale factor is therefore not the primitive cosmological state.

It is the normalized linear geometric coordinate of a change in the projected area of that state.

No G is required for this geometric bridge.

The relation

\[A \propto a^2\]

is geometric.

The gravitational constant enters later, when the theory asks why the cosmological boundary-area read evolves as it does under the physical source projection of the peer organization.

The causal sequence is therefore

\[i_\zeta^\ast \to k_\zeta^\ast\]

↓

\[A_{\zeta,i} \to A_{\zeta,k}\]

↓

\[\frac{a_k}{a_i}=\sqrt{\frac{A_{\zeta,k}}{A_{\zeta,i}}}\]

and only after a physical time coordinate is introduced,

\[H=\frac{\dot a}{a}=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\]

Thus

\[H\]

is a physical-time rate of projected boundary-area change.

It is not primitive cosmological asymmetry:

\[H\ne A_\zeta=P_\zeta-R_\zeta\]

Nor is it the terminal orientation:

\[H\ne \Xi_\star\]

The central statement is therefore:

the cosmological scale factor does not define the cosmological state. Under an isotropic gravitational

And hence:

cosmological boundary area occurs before the scale factor.

1. The Projection Problem Is Now Well Defined

Addendum 54 established the cosmological structural transition

\[i_\zeta^\ast \to k_\zeta^\ast\]

Addendum 55 established that this transition may acquire gravitational physical coordinates.

The unresolved problem is therefore not whether a physical projection exists.

It is which observable faithfully represents the R²D macrostate.

2. A Physical Observable Cannot Be Chosen by Association Alone

Suppose some gravitational quantity

\[Q\]

changes during cosmological evolution.

That correlation does not establish that Q represents the cosmological macrostate.

The quantity may instead depend on subordinate physical distinctions that remain unresolved by

\[\pi_\zeta\]

A stronger criterion is required.

3. Define the Physical Projection

Let

\(\Pi_G:\Omega_\zeta\to\Gamma_G\) maps compatible joint peer realizations into their gravitational physical representation.

An element

\[\mu \in \Omega_\zeta\]

may therefore acquire physical coordinates

\[\Pi_G (\mu) \in \Gamma_G\]

4. Define a Candidate Physical Observable

Let

\[Q:\Gamma_G\to\mathcal Q\]

The composite

\[Q ∘ \Pi_G\]

assigns a physical observable to every formal cosmological microstate.

5. The Observable Must Respect the Macrostate Classification

Suppose

\[\mu_\alpha , \mu_\beta \in \Omega_\zeta\]

are different compatible joint peer realizations but satisfy

\[\pi_\zeta (\mu_\alpha ) = \pi_\zeta (\mu_\beta ) = i\]

If Q is to represent macrostate i, then it must satisfy

\[Q[\Pi_G (\mu_\alpha )] = Q[\Pi_G (\mu_\beta )]\]

Otherwise Q distinguishes physical information that Bζ does not classify as a macrostate distinction.

6. Principle — Macrostate Physical-Read Factorization

A physical observable Q is a valid physical coordinate of the R²D macrostate classification only if there exists

\[\bar Q_\zeta:\mathcal M_\zeta\to\mathcal Q\]

such that

\[Q\circ\Pi_G=\bar Q_\zeta\circ\pi_\zeta\]

This is the factorization condition.

7. The Factorization Condition Can Be Written as a Commuting Diagram

The required relation is

\[\begin{array}{ccc}\Omega_\zeta&\xrightarrow{\Pi_G}&\Gamma_G\\\pi_\zeta\downarrow&&\downarrow Q\\\mathcal M_\zeta&\xrightarrow{\bar Q_\zeta}&\mathcal Q\end{array}\]

Both routes must give the same physical read.

8. This Is Stronger Than Correlation

A variable may correlate statistically with macrostate classification without satisfying

\[Q\circ\Pi_G=\bar Q_\zeta\circ\pi_\zeta\]

Such a variable is not yet a faithful macrostate coordinate.

Factorization requires invariance across the complete macrostate fiber.

9. This Provides a General Test for R²D Projection

The criterion is not unique to cosmology.

Any proposed physical reading of an R²D macrostate should satisfy the same condition.

Thus the addendum introduces a general projection test, specialized here to cosmology.

10. Gravitational Area Is the Natural First Candidate

Earlier gravitational development established the mapping

\[B_G \to A_G\]

The boundary itself therefore has an established candidate spatial read.

This makes area a natural observable to test against the cosmological macrostate classification.

11. Define the Cosmological Area Observable

Let

\[A:\Gamma_G\to\mathbb R_{>0}\]

assign the relevant gravitational boundary area to a projected peer realization.

For area to represent the cosmological macrostate,

\[A\circ\Pi_G=\bar A_\zeta\circ\pi_\zeta\]

must hold.

12. Area Must Be Constant Across a Macrostate Fiber

If

\[\pi_\zeta(\mu_\alpha)=\pi_\zeta(\mu_\beta)=i\]

then the area criterion requires

\[A[\Pi_G(\mu_\alpha)]=A[\Pi_G(\mu_\beta)]\]

Only then does macrostate i possess one well-defined area read.

13. Define the Macrostate Area

When factorization holds, define

\[A_{\zeta,i}=\bar A_\zeta(i)\]

The physical area is now indexed to the R²D cosmological macrostate rather than to an unresolved subordinate realization.

14. The Structural State Acquires a Boundary Area

For the realized structural macrostate

\[i_\zeta^\ast\]

define

\[A_\zeta^\ast=A_{\zeta,i_\zeta^\ast}\]

This is the area read of the currently realized cosmological organization.

15. Structural Change Can Now Acquire a Geometric Read

If

\[i_\zeta^\ast \to k_\zeta^\ast\]

then

\[A_{\zeta,i} \to A_{\zeta,k}\]

The R²D structural transition has now been physically projected.

16. Area Change Is Not the Primitive Transition

The primitive transition remains

\[i_\zeta^\ast \to k_\zeta^\ast\]

The area change is its geometric read.

Thus:

structural change → area change,

not the reverse.

17. Area Does Not Cause the Cosmological Reorganization

The physical quantity

\[A_\zeta\]

does not generate

\[A_\zeta=P_\zeta-R_\zeta.\]

The primitive direction remains upstream in the count architecture.

18. The Boundary Owns the Area Read

The area belongs to the projected enclosing boundary.

It should not be interpreted as the sum of independently primitive subordinate peer areas unless such a mapping is separately established.

Thus:

Aζ = boundary-level physical read.

19. Area Comes Before Radius

For an isotropic spherical projection,

\[r_{\zeta,i}=\sqrt{\frac{A_{\zeta,i}}{4\pi}}\]

Therefore:

\[A_{\zeta,i} \to r_{\zeta,i}\]

The radius is derived from the boundary area.

20. The Radius Is Not a Primitive Container

R²D does not begin with a pre-existing sphere of radius r containing the peer boundaries.

It begins with the count boundary and its peer realization domain.

The radius appears only after gravitational projection.

21. Cosmological Distinguishability Can Then Acquire a Radial Read

A primitive count distinction

\[\lambda_{\zeta,i\to k}\]

may now acquire the physical read

\[\lambda_{\zeta,i\to k} \longrightarrow r_{\zeta,k} - r_{\zeta,i}\]

This is a candidate projection relation, not an identity of primitives.

22. This Preserves the Existing Gravity Dictionary

The resulting hierarchy remains

boundary → area,

and

distinguishability → radial distinction.

Cosmology does not require a different gravitational ontology.

23. The Scale Factor Must Come After the Area Read

The cosmological scale factor is already a geometric coordinate.

Therefore it cannot define the primitive R²D macrostate if the aim is to derive cosmological geometry from R²D.

It must enter after

\[A_{\zeta,i}\]

For a homogeneous and isotropic FLRW projection,

\[ds^2=-c^2dt^2+a^2(t)[d\chi^2+S_k^2(\chi)d\Omega^2]\]

It is not derived here from primitive R²D counting.

\[\chi\]

the associated physical two-sphere has area

\[A_\chi(t)=4\pi a^2(t)S_k^2(\chi)\]

The factor

\[S_k(\chi)\]

is constant when the same comoving boundary is compared at different epochs.

26. Area Ratios Therefore Determine Scale-Factor Ratios

For the same comoving boundary,

\[\frac{A_\chi(t_2)}{A_\chi(t_1)}=\frac{a^2(t_2)}{a^2(t_1)}\]

Therefore:

\[\frac{a(t_2)}{a(t_1)}=\sqrt{\frac{A_\chi(t_2)}{A_\chi(t_1)}}\]

27. This Is the Candidate R²D Scale-Factor Bridge

If the R²D cosmological area read

\[A_{\zeta,i}\]

maps onto the corresponding FLRW boundary area, then

\[\frac{a_k}{a_i}=\sqrt{\frac{A_{\zeta,k}}{A_{\zeta,i}}}\]

This is a physical mapping.

\[a(t_0)=1\]

No absolute primitive value of a is needed.

\[A \propto a^2\]

the scale factor is proportional to the square root of the projected boundary area:

\[a\propto\sqrt{A_\zeta}\]

Thus the scalar spatial place value introduced previously can be written

\[L_\zeta\propto\sqrt{A_\zeta}\]

\[L_\zeta\propto r_\zeta\propto\sqrt{A_\zeta}\]

\[B_\zeta\]

It may project as cosmological expansion.

This defines a new state domain and a new classification.

It is not equivalent to

\[a_i \to a_k\]

Even very large growth in

\[a\]

need not imply a change in recursive boundary index.

Thus:

\[\ln\frac{a_k}{a_i}\ne\text{recursive depth}\]

by definition.

This is a logarithmic geometric ratio.

It is not automatically a count of recursive closures.

macrostate → area → a

\[H\]

The CMB records the cosmological geometric evolution.

It does not define the primitive cosmological state.

No claim that the comoving sphere is an ontological terminal boundary is required.

observational horizon ≠ Bζ

by definition.

\[a(t)\]

R²D does not derive isotropy merely by defining a cosmological macrostate.

53. Without Isotropy, One Scalar Area May Be Insufficient

If the gravitational projection is anisotropic, then the structural state may require multiple directional scale reads or a tensorial spatial description.

In that case,

\[a(t)\]

would not fully represent the projected macrostate transition.

The statement

\[\frac{a_k}{a_i}=\sqrt{\frac{A_{\zeta,k}}{A_{\zeta,i}}}\]

is a conditional projection theorem requiring:

  1. a valid gravitational projection; 2. area factorization through the cosmological macrostate classification; 3. comparison of the corresponding boundary read;
  2. isotropic FLRW-type spatial scaling.

55. Principle — Physical Macrostate Read

A physical variable can represent an R²D macrostate only when it factors through the macrostate classification:
