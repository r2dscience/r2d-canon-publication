# R²D Addendum 144 — From Relative Distinguishability to Energetic Readability

*Directional Multiplicity, the Moving Container, and the Energy Dictionary*

**Status:** Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026  
**Canonical basis:** R²D Parts I–IV; Addenda 35–36, 105, 120, 122, 131–132, 139–143.

## Abstract

A container of gas can maintain an unchanged internal thermodynamic state while moving relative to an external observer. In the container's own background, its thermally realized subordinate motion distinctions may be symmetrically organized. In an observer-inclusive comparison, increasingly many of those distinctions can acquire a positive directional classification as the relative-motion relationship changes. The observer consequently reads an increasingly asymmetric directional multiplicity landscape without requiring a change in the container's internally defined landscape.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) supplies an order of explanation for this observation. The enclosing boundary first defines compatible subordinate distinctions, their multiplicities, and their relative ordering. An observer-inclusive comparison defines which distinctions are positive and negative. Complete spatial–recurrence compatibility supplies their dimensionless magnitude relationships. Only subsequently does a physical background assign a ruler, clock, velocity, mass coupling, and energetic calibration.

This addendum establishes a monotone directional-reclassification theorem, a motion-sufficiency theorem, and a cross-domain energetic-readability criterion. It shows why directional counts alone do not determine the magnitude of motion, why changing an observer's directional classification does not necessarily change the container's internal thermodynamic state, and how invariant rest energy and observer-relative kinetic energy can coexist as different physical readings of the same complete boundary.

The energetic dictionary distinguishes internal energy $U_B$, relative kinetic energy $K_{B|O}$, and invariant rest energy $E_{0,B}=M_Bc^2$. Their distinction does not require different primitive kinds of energy. Each is a specified physical read of an enclosing relationship.

The all-positive and all-negative singleton endpoints of the reciprocal binary landscape are retained as exact combinatorial results. Whether those endpoints determine maximum spatial–recurrence magnitude and a background-defined propagation speed is reserved for Addendum 145.

## 144.1. The question and the order of definition

Consider a container maintained at a fixed internal thermodynamic state. Its gas, walls, and material interactions participate in one complete physical organization.

The container may be observed from different external references. As the relative-motion relationship changes, the observer can classify increasingly many subordinate motion distinctions as positive rather than negative.

Conventionally, the observer's additional energetic read is described as kinetic energy.

The R²D question is prior:

**What change in the enclosing count relationship makes the additional energetic reading possible, and what part of that relationship remains invariant?**

We must not begin by assigning independent velocities and energies to molecules and then treating their sum as the origin of the complete container's energy.

The required sequence is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{Enclosing compatibility and distinguishability}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{Native multiplicity and relative orientation}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{Observer-inclusive directional classification}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{Complete spatial–recurrence magnitude}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{Physical velocity and coupling calibration}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{Thermodynamic and mechanical energetic reads}.
\end{gathered}
}
\tag{144.1}
$$

Nothing in the primitive construction requires molecules, masses, temperatures, physical velocities, or energy units.

These enter only when the count relationships acquire their respective physical readings.

## 144.2. Complete boundaries and their count domains

Part I defines a formal microstate at boundary $B$ as one compatible joint realization of immediately subordinate peers.

Let its formal microstate domain be

$$
\mathfrak M_B
$$

and its macrostate classification be

$$
\pi_B:\mathfrak M_B\longrightarrow\mathcal M_B.
$$

For macrostate $i$,

$$
\boxed{
W_B(i)=|\pi_B^{-1}(i)|,
\qquad
S_B(i)=\ln W_B(i).
}
\tag{144.2}
$$

The native multiplicity landscape is

$$
\mathcal W_B=\{W_B(i)\}_{i\in\mathcal M_B}.
$$

These counts specify the compatible realizations of the system before any particular realization occurs.

Part I separately distinguishes native multiplicity $W_B$, occurrence count $\nu_{B,i}$, realized occupancy $c_{B,i}$, and completed closure recurrence $N_{C,B}$.

The distinction remains essential:

$$
W_B\neq\nu_B\neq c_B\neq N_{C,B}
$$

as count types.

### The observer-inclusive boundary

Introduce a complete enclosing comparison $J$ containing the selected container boundary $B$, the external observing reference $O$, and the relationships necessary to compare them.

Let

$$
\mathcal H_J
$$

be its domain of complete admitted comparison records.

The container's internal state and its observer-relative directional classification are distinct reads of that common domain:

$$
q_B:\mathcal H_J\longrightarrow\mathcal T_B,
$$

$$
q_{O,\pm}:\mathcal H_J\longrightarrow\mathcal D_O.
$$

The first reads the selected internal organization. The second classifies a complete comparison according to observer-relative directional distinctions.

The existence of the common owner $J$ is important. We must not construct a purportedly complete state by freely combining independently admitted internal and external possibilities.

A comparison is admissible only when the enclosing compatibility admits the corresponding complete record.

The observer is therefore not an external agent examining an independently defined world. The observer participates in the boundary defining the comparison.

Nevertheless, the container's selected internal boundary and its larger observer-inclusive boundary remain distinct count domains.

## 144.3. Reciprocal distinctions and the binary multiplicity landscape

Consider a specified observer-inclusive comparison that admits $N$ distinguishable binary directional alternatives:

$$
s_a\in\{+1,-1\},
\qquad a=1,\ldots,N.
$$

Here $+$ and $-$ represent orientations of relative distinctions. They are not names for intrinsically moving molecular objects.

For the unrestricted binary comparison, define

$$
\mathfrak M_O^{\mathrm{bin}}=\{+1,-1\}^{N}.
$$

Its readable macrostate is the number of positive distinctions:

$$
k=N_+,
\qquad
N_-=N-k.
$$

The native multiplicity of macrostate $k$ is

$$
\boxed{
W_O^{\mathrm{bin}}(k)=\binom Nk.
}
\tag{144.3}
$$

Its logarithmic entropic coordinate is

$$
\boxed{
S_O^{\mathrm{bin}}(k)=\ln\binom Nk.
}
\tag{144.4}
$$

Define the normalized directional distinction:

$$
\boxed{
D_O(k)=
\frac{N_+-N_-}{N}
=\frac{2k-N}{N}.
}
\tag{144.5}
$$

The balanced macrostate, when $N$ is even, has

$$
D_O=0.
$$

At the two extremes,

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
k=0 &: \quad W_O^{\mathrm{bin}}=1,\quad D_O=-1,\\
k=N &: \quad W_O^{\mathrm{bin}}=1,\quad D_O=+1.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{144.6}
$$

The singleton endpoints are independent of $N$.

They are universal features of the unrestricted reciprocal binary landscape.

**The endpoint $W=1$ refers to the multiplicity of the specified binary macrostate at the specified receiving boundary.** It does not assert that all internal thermodynamic distinctions of the container have disappeared.

Nor does it establish a physical propagation speed. That requires the additional spatial–recurrence relationship developed below and investigated further in Addendum 145.

If the enclosing owner excludes some binary assignments or distinguishes additional information within them, its native multiplicity must be computed from its actual compatibility fibers rather than assumed to equal the unrestricted binomial count.

## 144.4. The moving-container construction

Now consider the container as one selected complete physical system.

Suppose its internal organization is fixed while its relationship to an external observing reference changes.

The container's internal reciprocal response landscape may remain symmetric. Its observer-relative response classification need not.

To establish this without physical velocities, consider one fixed complete enclosing comparison domain $J$.

Let

$$
\mathcal I_J
$$

be a finite collection of tagged compatible comparison incidences supplied by $J$.

Each incidence $a\in\mathcal I_J$ has an ordered, dimensionless comparison label

$$
u_a.
$$

The order is established by enclosing compatibility. These labels need not yet be spatial displacements or velocities.

Let the observer supply a reference cut $t$.

Define

$$
s_t(a)=
\begin{cases}
+1,&u_a>t,\\
0,&u_a=t,\\
-1,&u_a<t.
\end{cases}
\tag{144.7}
$$

The possible comparison-incidence counts are

$$
\Theta_+(t)=
|\{a\in\mathcal I_J:u_a>t\}|,
$$

$$
\Theta_-(t)=
|\{a\in\mathcal I_J:u_a<t\}|,
$$

with a separate tie count $\Theta_0(t)$.

These incidence counts are not automatically the native multiplicities $W_O^{\mathrm{bin}}(k)$ of Equation (144.3). The former count tagged compatible comparisons; the latter counts complete binary assignments classified by their positive total.

### Theorem 144.1 — Monotone observer-relative reclassification

Let $t'<t$ be two ordered observer references applied to the same complete admitted comparison-incidence domain.

Then

$$
\boxed{
\Theta_+(t')-\Theta_+(t)
=
|\{a\in\mathcal I_J:t'<u_a\le t\}|
\ge0.
}
\tag{144.8}
$$

Similarly,

$$
\boxed{
\Theta_-(t')\le\Theta_-(t).
}
\tag{144.9}
$$

**Proof.** Every comparison satisfying $u_a>t$ also satisfies $u_a>t'$. Therefore the positive comparison set at $t$ is contained in the positive comparison set at $t'$. The newly positive incidences are exactly those whose labels lie in $(t',t]$. Their cardinality gives Equation (144.8). The negative-set inclusion is reversed, establishing Equation (144.9). $\square$

The theorem is an exact count result.

It establishes that moving the observer's reference monotonically through a fixed ordered compatibility domain can increase the number of positive distinctions without changing the underlying comparison domain.

### Physical interpretation

In the moving-container example, this corresponds to the observer reading increasingly many subordinate relative movements as positive.

The internally organized container need not have changed its own reciprocal structure.

The changing $+/-$ classification belongs to the enclosing observer–container relationship.

The theorem does not assume that all compatible distinctions occur in proportions equal to their possible counts. Observed occurrence and occupancy require the realization relations of Part I.

## 144.5. An observer-relative entropic change is not automatically an internal thermodynamic change

The observer-inclusive binary landscape and the container's native internal landscape answer different questions.

The container boundary $B$ defines

$$
\mathcal W_B.
$$

The observer-inclusive comparison defines

$$
\mathcal W_O^{\mathrm{bin}}
$$

or its more detailed admitted counterpart.

When the observer-relative positive count changes, the selected observer macrostate can move from a high-multiplicity binary classification toward a lower-multiplicity extreme.

For example, with ten binary distinctions,

$$
W_O^{\mathrm{bin}}(5)=252,
$$

whereas

$$
W_O^{\mathrm{bin}}(9)=10,
\qquad
W_O^{\mathrm{bin}}(10)=1.
$$

Thus the observer can classify a realized comparison into a macrostate of smaller binary multiplicity.

But this does not imply

$$
\Delta S_B<0
$$

for the container's native internal entropy.

The selected observer-relative macrostate has changed. The internally defined landscape need not have changed.

Moreover, a change in the observed macrostate is not itself a change in the landscape $\mathcal W_O^{\mathrm{bin}}$. The entire possible-count landscape may remain fixed while different macrostates are realized or selected for comparison.

This distinction is already essential to Part II:

**Native multiplicity defines a landscape; occurrence and occupancy express realizations within it.**

Consequently, an observer-relative reduction in the multiplicity of the selected binary macrostate does not by itself establish thermodynamic entropy production, heat exchange, or a Gibbs free-energy penalty.

The energetic meaning of the change must be supplied by the complete physical relationship, not inferred from the sign-count ratio alone.

## 144.6. Directional multiplicity does not uniquely determine motion magnitude

A directional classification distinguishes orientation but does not necessarily distinguish the magnitude of a relative response.

Consider two complete comparison records, each containing three positive and two negative dimensionless response values.

The first contains

$$
(+1,+1,+1,-100,-100).
$$

Its total signed response is

$$
-197.
$$

The second contains

$$
(+100,+100,+100,-1,-1).
$$

Its total signed response is

$$
+298.
$$

Both have the same sign counts:

$$
N_+=3,\qquad N_-=2.
$$

Both therefore have

$$
D_O=\frac15.
$$

Yet their signed response magnitudes differ, even in sign.

The example demonstrates an exact limitation of binary classification.

A positive majority does not independently determine the direction of a magnitude-weighted aggregate response.

Nor does the number of positive subordinate distinctions necessarily equal the motion of the complete container.

A whole-boundary composition relationship is required.

### The finer spatial–recurrence structure

Addendum 141 introduces complete enclosing comparison records with

$$
\ell_J(h)\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad
n_J(h)\in\mathbb N_{>0},
$$

and defines

$$
\boxed{
r_J(h)=\frac{\ell_J(h)}{n_J(h)}.
}
\tag{144.10}
$$

The signed spatial count $\ell_J$ and recurrence comparison count $n_J$ belong to the same admitted complete relation.

They are not independent possibilities that can be combined at will.

A sign classification may be obtained by projecting this finer relationship:

$$
s_J(h)=\operatorname{sgn}r_J(h).
$$

But the reverse operation is generally impossible.

Many distinct magnitude-bearing relationships can have the same sign.

### Theorem 144.2 — Directional-count sufficiency for motion

Let

$$
q_{\pm}:\mathcal H_J\longrightarrow\mathcal D_J
$$

be a complete observer-relative count read and let

$$
r_B:\mathcal H_J\longrightarrow\mathcal R_B
$$

be a specified dimensionless motion classification of the complete container.

There exists a unique function on the admitted count image,

$$
\widehat r_B:q_{\pm}(\mathcal H_J)\longrightarrow\mathcal R_B,
$$

such that

$$
r_B=\widehat r_B\circ q_{\pm}
$$

if and only if

$$
\boxed{
q_{\pm}(h)=q_{\pm}(h')
\Longrightarrow
r_B(h)=r_B(h')
}
\tag{144.11}
$$

for all $h,h'\in\mathcal H_J$.

**Proof.** If $r_B$ factors through $q_{\pm}$, records with equal count reads necessarily have equal motion classifications. Conversely, if $r_B$ is constant on every $q_{\pm}$ fiber, define $\widehat r_B(d)$ as the common value on that fiber. The value is independent of the chosen representative, establishing existence and uniqueness on the image. $\square$

This is the existing R²D fiber-factorization criterion applied to observer-relative motion.

The theorem establishes the exact distinction:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Directional counts}
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{unique whole-boundary motion magnitude}
}
\tag{144.12}
$$

without the necessary enclosing compatibility.

The required magnitude may nevertheless be determined if the complete owner restricts each count fiber to one motion class.

## 144.7. Velocity enters only after background calibration

A complete spatial–recurrence classification is dimensionless.

To acquire a physical velocity reading, the background supplies the spatial scale $L_\Phi$ and recurrence duration $\tau_\Phi$.

For an admitted complete motion relationship,

$$
\boxed{
v_{B|\Phi}=
\frac{L_\Phi}{\tau_\Phi}\,r_B.
}
\tag{144.13}
$$

This is a physical projection.

It is not a primitive definition of motion from an isolated molecule.

Nor does it assign a physical distance to every arbitrary binary distinction.

The order is:

$$
\text{relative distinction}
\rightarrow
\text{ordered compatible comparison}
\rightarrow
\text{spatial–recurrence magnitude}
\rightarrow
\text{physical velocity}.
$$

At this point the moving-container construction has established two conceptually distinct physical relations.

First, the container has an internal organization, which can remain unchanged.

Second, the complete container has a changing motion relationship relative to the selected observer.

The first is the appropriate domain for an internal thermodynamic energetic read.

The second is the appropriate domain for a relative mechanical energetic read.

We can now introduce energy without making it a primitive cause of the preceding count relationships.

## 144.8. Internal energy is a thermodynamic state read

Part II defines internal energy $U_B$ as a state coordinate of a thermodynamic boundary.

Its definition belongs to the complete thermodynamic organization of $B$:

$$
U_B=U_B(\Sigma_B,V_B,N_{1,B},\ldots).
$$

R²D does not identify $U_B$ with the energy of independently persistent subordinate configurations.

The native multiplicity landscape and the enclosing thermodynamic compatibility precede the energetic read.

Where a thermodynamic mapping is valid, Part II introduces the dimensional read operator

$$
\boxed{
d\mathcal E_B[X]=k_BT_B\,dX.
}
\tag{144.14}
$$

For entropy,

$$
\Sigma_B=k_BS_B=k_B\ln W_B.
$$

The familiar energetic contribution is therefore

$$
T_B\,d\Sigma_B=k_BT_B\,dS_B.
$$

But $k_BT_B$ is a physical energetic calibration, not a primitive count.

Part II also distinguishes internal energy from the available-direction coordinate:

$$
A_B=P_B-R_B.
$$

In general,

$$
A_B\neq U_B.
$$

The energetic reading of a selected count difference is not automatically the complete system's internal energy.

### Consequence for the moving container

If the container's complete internal thermodynamic state remains unchanged while its observer-relative motion changes, then its internal-energy state coordinate remains unchanged:

$$
\boxed{\Delta U_B=0.}
\tag{144.15}
$$

The observer's changing directional count classification does not contradict this result.

It belongs to a different comparison.

If the container is physically heated, compressed, chemically transformed, or otherwise reorganized, $U_B$ may change. Those are changes of thermodynamic state, not merely changes in the observing reference.

## 144.9. Invariant energetic coupling and observer-relative asymmetry

Part IV and Addenda 36 and 143 identify mass as an orientation-even physical coupling of a specified complete boundary.

The coupling is not a primitive energetic substance inside the container's subordinate realizations.

Addendum 143 also supplies a reciprocal physical-response representation.

Let

$$
\Pi_+>0,\qquad\Pi_->0
$$

be two calibrated reciprocal response coordinates associated with the same complete physical system.

Define their orientation-even and orientation-odd combinations:

$$
Q_O=\frac{\Pi_++\Pi_-}{2},
$$

$$
P_O=\frac{\Pi_+-\Pi_-}{2}.
$$

Suppose the complete response compatibility preserves their product under the relevant change of observer:

$$
\Pi_+\Pi_-=I_B^2.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
Q_O^2-P_O^2=I_B^2.
}
\tag{144.16}
$$

The geometric mean is invariant:

$$
I_B=\sqrt{\Pi_+\Pi_-}.
$$

The arithmetic mean varies with the reciprocal asymmetry:

$$
Q_O=\frac{\Pi_++\Pi_-}{2}.
$$

### Proposition 144.3 — Reciprocal energetic excess

For positive reciprocal responses with fixed product $I_B^2$,

$$
\boxed{
Q_O-I_B
=
\frac12
\left(\sqrt{\Pi_+}-\sqrt{\Pi_-}\right)^2
\ge0.
}
\tag{144.17}
$$

**Proof.** Expand the square:

$$
\frac12(\Pi_++\Pi_--2\sqrt{\Pi_+\Pi_-})
=Q_O-I_B.
$$

Nonnegativity follows from the square. Equality holds precisely when $\Pi_+=\Pi_-$. $\square$

This is an exact algebraic relationship.

At fixed reciprocal invariant, the symmetric response minimizes the arithmetic mean. An asymmetric response has a larger arithmetic mean.

However, the $\Pi_\pm$ are not automatically the raw positive and negative molecular comparison counts.

A simple reclassification of $N$ binary distinctions preserves

$$
N_++N_-=N,
$$

whereas the reciprocal mechanical representation preserves

$$
\Pi_+\Pi_-=I_B^2.
$$

Those are different constraints.

R²D must not identify the two merely because both involve positive and negative labels.

The complete enclosing spatial–recurrence compatibility must provide any mapping between raw count classifications and reciprocal magnitude-bearing responses.

### Physical energy calibration

Under the independently specified relativistic mechanical calibration of Addenda 35–36 and 143,

$$
I_B=M_Bc,
$$

$$
E_{B|O}=cQ_O,
$$

$$
E_{0,B}=cI_B=M_Bc^2.
$$

The difference is

$$
\boxed{
K_{B|O}=E_{B|O}-E_{0,B}
=c(Q_O-I_B).
}
\tag{144.18}
$$

Using Equation (144.17),

$$
\boxed{
K_{B|O}
=
\frac c2
\left(\sqrt{\Pi_+}-\sqrt{\Pi_-}\right)^2.
}
\tag{144.19}
$$

This is a physical reading of the reciprocal representation, not an independent derivation of that representation from raw directional multiplicity.

Under its established rapidity calibration,

$$
\Pi_\pm=M_Bc\,e^{\pm\xi_O},
$$

so

$$
E_{B|O}=M_Bc^2\cosh\xi_O,
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
K_{B|O}
=M_Bc^2(\cosh\xi_O-1).
}
\tag{144.20}
$$

The important distinction is now visible.

The invariant response $I_B$ belongs to the preserved complete boundary coupling.

The observer-relative response $Q_O$ changes with its reciprocal directional resolution.

Kinetic energy is the calibrated difference between those two readings.

Neither requires the observer's changing positive/negative classification to alter the container's native internal thermodynamic state.

## 144.10. The complete energy dictionary

We can now classify the energetic readings without treating any of them as a primitive R²D quantity.

| Physical quantity | Organizing relationship | Dependence |
|---|---|---|
| $U_B$, internal energy | Complete thermodynamic state | Internal state and thermodynamic calibration |
| $E_{0,B}=M_Bc^2$, invariant rest energy | Complete orientation-even mass-bearing coupling | Complete physical state and relativistic calibration |
| $E_{B\vert O}$, observer-relative total energy | Calibrated complete response relative to observer $O$ | Physical state and observing reference |
| $K_{B\vert O}=E_{B\vert O}-E_{0,B}$, kinetic energy | Relative energetic excess | Observer-relative motion at fixed invariant state |
| $k_BT_B\,dS_B$, entropy-associated energetic read | Thermodynamic multiplicity difference | Thermodynamic mapping and specified controls |
| $-k_BT_B(\Delta S+\Delta P)$, Gibbs read | Pre-constraint count direction | Restricted Gibbs mapping at specified $T,p$ |

The entries are related but not interchangeable.

In particular, the Gibbs directional read does not identify the general enclosing possibility coordinate $P$ with total internal energy or total mass-energy.

### The accounting relationship

For a complete physical system admitting both a thermodynamic internal-energy read and a relativistic zero-momentum energy read, define

$$
\boxed{
Z_B=E_{0,B}-U_B.
}
\tag{144.21}
$$

Here $Z_B$ is an accounting difference between two specified physical energy conventions. It is not a primitive R²D variable or an additional physical energy substance.

It can include contributions or reference baselines omitted from the particular thermodynamic definition of $U_B$.

For two states of the same specified complete system, compared using a common physical calibration,

$$
\Delta E_{0,B}=\Delta U_B+\Delta Z_B.
$$

Since

$$
E_{0,B}=M_Bc^2,
$$

we obtain

$$
\boxed{
c^2\Delta M_B
=
\Delta U_B+\Delta Z_B.
}
\tag{144.22}
$$

Consequently,

$$
\boxed{
\Delta M_B=\frac{\Delta U_B}{c^2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Delta Z_B=0.
}
\tag{144.23}
$$

This identifies the exact accounting condition under which a thermodynamic internal-energy change determines an invariant mass change.

It does not require the two energetic reads to be different primitive kinds of energy.

Nor does it establish that every definition of internal energy contains every energetic contribution to the complete system.

The selected boundary and its physical energy convention must remain fixed throughout the comparison.

### Theorem 144.4 — Thermodynamic sufficiency for invariant energetic readability

Let $J$ own a complete admitted physical comparison domain $\mathcal H_J$.

Let

$$
q_T:\mathcal H_J\longrightarrow\mathcal T_B
$$

be the selected thermodynamic state read and

$$
E_{0,B}:\mathcal H_J\longrightarrow\mathbb R_{\ge0}
$$

the calibrated complete zero-momentum energy read.

Then the thermodynamic state read uniquely determines the invariant energy if and only if

$$
\boxed{
q_T(h)=q_T(h')
\Longrightarrow
E_{0,B}(h)=E_{0,B}(h')
}
\tag{144.24}
$$

for every pair of admitted complete records.

**Proof.** If invariant energy is a function of the thermodynamic read, it is constant on every thermodynamic read fiber. Conversely, fiber constancy defines a unique invariant-energy function on the admitted thermodynamic state image. $\square$

This theorem does not require the thermodynamic and mechanical descriptions to resolve the same subordinate details.

They need only belong to one admitted complete system and satisfy the stated sufficiency condition.

Mere compatibility of two energetic reads is weaker than the ability to infer one uniquely from the other.

## 144.11. Two ways of changing the observer-relative energy

The moving-container construction admits two physically distinct scenarios.

### Case A — Change of observing reference

The complete container retains its internal physical state and invariant coupling.

A different inertial observer assigns different directional classifications and a different total energy.

For the two readings,

$$
\Delta U_B=0,
$$

$$
\Delta E_{0,B}=0,
$$

while the observer-relative energy difference can be nonzero.

No energy exchange with the container is required merely because a different reference is used.

The difference is in the observer-inclusive physical read.

### Case B — Physical acceleration of the container

Fix the external observer and change the physical motion relation of the complete container.

The observer's directional classification and kinetic-energy reading can change.

In a physical realization, external work or another specified energy exchange may be required to establish that motion change.

Nevertheless, the container's internal thermodynamic state and invariant mass can remain unchanged under suitable conditions.

Thus

$$
\Delta K_{B|O}\neq0
$$

does not by itself imply

$$
\Delta U_B\neq0
$$

or

$$
\Delta M_B\neq0.
$$

The two scenarios share a relative-motion comparison but differ in physical process history.

R²D distinguishes the organization of a physical read from a claim about which energy exchange produced a particular realized change.

## 144.12. The singleton endpoint and the scope of this addendum

The unrestricted reciprocal binary landscape has two singleton endpoints:

$$
W_O^{\mathrm{bin}}(0)=W_O^{\mathrm{bin}}(N)=1.
$$

These are exact count statements.

They identify maximal directional classifications within that binary landscape.

However, identifying an all-positive binary configuration with a maximum magnitude of motion requires the complete spatial–recurrence relationship.

A sufficient construction would be an enclosing compatibility in which every reciprocal distinction contributes a fixed positive magnitude under the positive orientation and the corresponding negative magnitude under reversal, with a common recurrence denominator.

More general positive, monotone response weights can also make the all-positive assignment maximize the signed spatial–recurrence relationship.

Whether recursive replacement itself selects that compatibility for every relevant physical background has not been established by this addendum.

Nor does the present analysis impose the conventional relativistic condition that a particular physical limiting-speed class must be unattainable.

Those questions concern the relation between **singleton multiplicity, maximal spatial–recurrence compatibility, and background-defined propagation**, and belong to Addendum 145.

Importantly, the endpoint $W=1$ of the observer's binary comparison is not equivalent to vanishing internal thermodynamic multiplicity or vanishing invariant mass.

A complete container can possess a rich internal thermodynamic organization even when its selected external directional read belongs to a singleton macrostate.

## 144.13. Established results and remaining physical mappings

The present construction establishes four mathematical results.

**First:** A fixed ordered comparison domain undergoes monotone observer-relative directional reclassification when the reference crosses admitted comparison classes.

**Second:** Directional-count information determines complete-boundary motion magnitude exactly when the latter is constant on the count-read fibers.

**Third:** Positive reciprocal response coordinates with a preserved geometric mean possess a nonnegative arithmetic-mean excess, vanishing in the symmetric configuration.

**Fourth:** A complete thermodynamic state read determines invariant rest energy exactly when the invariant energetic read is constant on its fibers.
