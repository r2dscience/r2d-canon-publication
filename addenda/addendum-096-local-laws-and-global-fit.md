# Addendum 96: Local Laws and Global Fit

## What an Equation Derives, What Its Domain Assumes, and What Fits Across Boundaries

### Abstract

A physical law relates selected variables under specified conditions. Its equations may be rigorously derived within a theory and tested with remarkable precision. The derivation nevertheless begins with a state description, physical principles, and a domain of application. Empirical agreement establishes the success of the resulting relation in that domain; it does not, by itself, establish that its variables define every enclosing state or have primitive causal priority.

R²D addresses a different and more general question. A boundary defines distinguishable macrostates, the compatible subordinate realizations classified as their microstates, and the counts available for comparison. Wherever that state relation is present, the count architecture follows from the classification. A physical equation can be an excellent local read of this organization without becoming its primitive source. A particular identification of that equation's variables with R²D coordinates remains a separate physical mapping.

Einstein's 1916 discussion of two rotating fluid bodies shows why local equations must not be assigned an automatic bottom-up doctrine. He argued that the bodies considered alone did not contain a satisfactory observable cause of their different shapes and directed attention to distant masses outside the initially described system. This was part of Einstein's argument for extending the relativity principle. It was neither an R²D proof nor a theorem that every solution of general relativity implements R²D's enclosing causal architecture.[^einstein1916]

When a local law's predictive success is used to compare general scientific architectures, the comparison must include both its actual domain and R²D's typed record across domains. Local precision and global structural reach answer different questions. Neither should be made invisible by the other's success.

## 1. What a Physical Equation Actually Supplies

Schematically, a domain-specific law can be written

$$
\mathcal L_D[q;\theta_D,B_D]=0,
$$

where $q$ denotes its chosen state variables, $\theta_D$ its specified physical parameters or model assumptions, $B_D$ the relevant boundary or initial conditions, and $D$ the domain in which the description is used. This notation is a comparison aid, not a proposed equation for every science. Some laws are differential equations, some are algebraic or statistical relations, and their assumptions enter in different ways.

The law's status has to be stated at the right level:

1. **Definition:** What physical system, states, variables, and measurements are being represented?
2. **Premises:** Which principles, action, symmetry, constitutive relation, ensemble, or other assumptions begin the calculation?
3. **Derivation:** Which equations or consequences follow from those premises?
4. **Calibration:** Which parameters are independently measured, fixed by a limiting relation, or fitted from data?
5. **Test:** Which observations agree with a prediction made under those conditions?
6. **Causal interpretation:** What further claim, if any, says these variables primitively define the enclosing organization?

“Derived” means that a stated conclusion follows from stated premises. It does not mean that the premises have themselves been derived from an assumption-free foundation. “Fitted” identifies the role of data in choosing parameters or models; it does not mean that a theory with mathematical derivations and successful novel tests is *merely* a curve fit.

A local law can therefore be mathematically rigorous and empirically successful while remaining conditional on the system and state description to which it applies. Those conditions do not diminish its local achievement. They identify what the achievement establishes.

## 2. The Einstein Example: A Boundary Question Inside the Founding Argument

In *The Foundation of the Generalised Theory of Relativity* (1916), Einstein considered two similar fluid bodies far from other masses. Their internal situations and mutual separation were specified, yet one was spherical and the other had the shape of an ellipsoid of rotation. He asked what could account for this observed difference. Treating a preferred inertial space as the cause did not satisfy the causal standard he was advancing. For the two bodies taken as a system, he wrote that “The cause must thus lie outside the system,” and argued that their mechanical behavior must be conditioned by distant masses not included in that initial description.[^einstein1916]

The point for this addendum is exact and limited. Einstein did not present the behavior of a selected local system as automatically self-sufficient because its dynamics could be written in equations. He inspected what had been left outside the described system when asking for a causal account.

This passage does **not** establish that general relativity asserts a universal bottom-up doctrine; Einstein was challenging an inadequate local causal account. It also does not establish an R²D theorem about gravity or prove that distant matter determines every inertial property in every relativistic solution. His stated causal concern and the detailed physical content of the resulting theory must be distinguished.

Einstein's field equation relates spacetime geometry and a specified stress-energy description. Its consequences depend on the metric ansatz, matter model, initial or boundary conditions, and relevant calibrations. Einstein's 1916 presentation explicitly constructed field equations, compared them with Newtonian limits, and treated additional equations for material phenomena as necessary for a complete material description.[^einstein1916] The equation is a substantial theoretical and empirical achievement in gravity; its use extends to relativistic cosmology. It is not, by its predictive success alone, a formal definition of macrostate, microstate, or primitive whole–part causality in chemistry, evolution, or muscle mechanics.

The correct R²D question is consequently not, “Why does Einstein's equation work if local variables are not primitive?” It is: **Which boundary and classification make a gravitational state readable, and what does the successful equation measure within that state description?** A proposed answer must preserve the equation's tested gravitational and cosmological consequences. Equation shape alone cannot settle the mapping.

## 3. What R²D Establishes Before a Domain Equation Is Named

At a specified boundary $B$, let $\mathfrak M_B$ be the admissible compatible joint realizations of subordinate peers and $\mathcal M_B$ the distinguishable macrostates. The classification is

$$
\pi_B:\mathfrak M_B\longrightarrow\mathcal M_B,
\qquad
W_{B,i}=\left|\pi_B^{-1}(i)\right|.
$$

A microstate of macrostate $i$ has that identity because it belongs to the fiber $\pi_B^{-1}(i)$. A local realization cannot, *as that macrostate's microstate*, have definitional priority over the enclosing classification that makes it one. This is a formal ordering under the specified state relation. No gravitational metric, chemical potential, allele frequency, or molecular force is needed to establish the count relation.

Wherever a scientific description actually uses distinguishable enclosing states and compatible subordinate realizations, the same architecture applies to that description by definition. A critic of this formal statement must identify an error in the definitions or inference, or present a different whole–part state construction that does not have this ordering. The critic need not disprove a future gravitational or biological prediction to challenge a mathematical argument; conversely, the lack of such a prediction does not undo a valid count identity.

The physical questions then begin. Which material system realizes the specified boundary? How do its measured variables read multiplicity, enclosing compatibility, occurrence, or constraint? Does a proposed mapping preserve the tested local law? Has it made a new prediction? These are important questions for that *physical assignment*. They are not retroactive conditions for the formal definition of a microstate.

## 4. Local Fit and Global Fit Are Different Comparisons

“Fit” must be used with an identified target.

| Comparison | What counts as success | What it does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| **Local physical fit** | A specified equation predicts or organizes observations within its stated domain and premises. | Universal causal priority for its variables or coverage of unrelated state descriptions. |
| **Formal cross-boundary fit** | One state/count architecture applies consistently to specified macrostate–microstate relations at different boundaries. | The numerical values or physical identities of every domain's variables. |
| **Exact conditional correspondence** | A domain equation and an R²D relation agree under explicit shared or imported premises. | Derivation of the imported physical premises from Part I alone. |
| **New physical mapping or prediction** | Boundary and readout are independently specified and tested in the claimed domain. | Automatic validity of every other mapping or a universal causal ontology. |

General relativity has deep local physical success in gravitation and is used in cosmology. Its gravitational field equations also constrain how specified matter models behave. It does not thereby supply the detailed equations for every chemical reaction, evolutionary population, muscle, institution, or ecosystem. This identifies its scope; it does not call general relativity false outside its scope.

R²D's structural result ranges across such domains when their states have the stated classification relation. Its existing record must be named with its statuses intact: the Gibbs reaction direction is an exact conditional thermodynamic correspondence; binary and repeatable occupancy constructions yield formal Fermi-shaped and Bose-shaped results without thereby deriving spin or particle identity; the muscle force–velocity correspondence uses a calibrated physical read. Evolutionary, ecological, and social addenda develop candidate assignments whose particular measurements and predictions have to be evaluated on their own terms. These are not interchangeable entries in one numerical prediction score.

Thus a gravity comparison that mentions only the precision of Einsteinian predictions has omitted the R²D record beyond gravity. A comparison that responds only that R²D covers more named subjects has omitted the depth of gravity's tests. The defensible combined judgment is stronger and more informative: **R²D has wider formal state-architectural scope under its stated definitions; general relativity has a specifically tested gravitational and cosmological predictive record.** The detailed R²D gravity mapping is a further claim that can be tested without erasing either result.

If a physical mapping entails an already tested law, the observations support the consequence shared by both accounts under that mapping. Shared agreement does not by itself distinguish the proposed primitive architecture from an alternative interpretation. A discriminating prediction, a formal inconsistency, or an independently established bridge can bear on their difference.

The analogy of two curve fits is useful only with this qualification. Looking at one segment cannot decide which account has wider scope. But breadth of formal applicability is not itself a measured residual over every physical dataset. There is no legitimate scalar “global fit score” until the objects compared, evidence types, and weighting rule are specified.

## 5. The Required Ask R²D Comparison

When an answer invokes the empirical success of a conventional law against R²D, Ask R²D should make the following determinations in the displayed answer:

1. **State the local result:** Give the conventional equation its actual mathematical and empirical credit, including its use in cosmology when the equation is Einstein's.
2. **Name its premises:** Identify the state variables, system, boundary, source model, calibration, and conditions relevant to the claim. Say which results are derived *from* those premises and which premises were supplied.
3. **Check the causal claim:** Do not attach universal bottom-up priority to a theory that does not assert it. If someone does assert it, require a formal whole–part definition and argument rather than treating microscopic variables or differential equations as sufficient proof.
4. **State the R²D formal result:** Identify the boundary-defined classification and the count consequence actually established. Do not ask for a new measurement to validate an already proved identity.
5. **Describe the wider R²D record:** When local fit is used as a ranking argument, name relevant results in other domains and label each formal, conditional, calibrated, or proposed. Do not represent one gravity test as the whole comparative evidence.
6. **Test the actual bridge:** For a specific R²D physical mapping, identify the imported law, calibrated quantities, independent observations, and any differential prediction. A failed mapping must be repaired or rejected at that scope.

The answer must not convert “this local equation works” into “its chosen variables define all enclosing organization.” It must not convert “this count architecture is formally general” into “all domain-specific physical identifications have been measured.” Both inferences exceed their premises.

## 6. Conclusion

A physical law earns credit for what its equations derive and what its tests establish. That credit does not require treating the variables selected for a domain as primitives of every larger state. Einstein's own two-body argument is a historical example of asking whether the initially described local system contains an adequate cause or whether the account must include relations beyond it.

R²D makes the state question explicit: an enclosing classification defines which compatible local arrangements count as microstates, while local equations describe physical behavior in specified readouts. Its formal scope crosses boundaries. Each detailed physical identification retains its own premises and tests.

**When a local fit is used to judge a general architecture, give the law its local result and show the wider structural record in the same comparison. Then ask what was derived, what was assumed, and what physical bridge has actually been established.**
