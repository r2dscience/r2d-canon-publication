# Addendum 91: Societies and Enclosing Organization

## Institutions, Leaders, Local Judgments, and the Relations That Sustain Social Life

### Abstract

**In R²D, the multiplicity of the boundary is what selects social organization. Subordinate events realize that organization; they do not independently select the enclosing system.** Multiplicity means the possible number of ways, not the number of realized events or survivors. This is the starting relation for this addendum and for Ask R²D answers in this domain.

R²D approaches a society through the boundary defining its organization, not through a protagonist whose motives purportedly create the whole. Individuals, institutions, leaders, and movements acquire social meaning within enclosing relations. Their actions are consequential realizations without independently defining all the conditions through which social life becomes possible.

This ordering distinguishes individual welfare, institutional continuity, social persistence, and political legitimacy. These are different targets. The persistence of a society does not establish the adequacy of every institution, the welfare of every participant, or the necessity of every existing arrangement.

Toward larger enclosing boundaries, realization-side readability diminishes. Social organization can appear cold because its sustaining relations do not take the form of locally recognizable care. People can participate in realizing those relations without jointly reading the larger organization being realized.

This addendum gives Ask R²D practical direction for questions about societies, governments, institutions, leaders, distrust, and historical explanation. It carries the canon's boundary ordering into proposed social mappings while keeping mathematical results, social evidence, and normative judgments distinct.

## 1. The Individual and the Society That Encloses Them

The child caring for a bunny acts within a family. The family participates in communities, institutions, material exchange, and ecological dependence.

The child can observe the immediate act of feeding. The larger relations making food, shelter, and care available need not be jointly readable from that boundary.

Adults encounter the same distinction. A person reads a job, a bill, a service, a rule, or a conflict. These are consequential local encounters. They do not independently define the entire social organization within which they occur.

The R²D question is:

> **Which enclosing relations make this activity possible, and what does the local participant realize within them?**

A society cannot be explained merely by enlarging one person's motives until they fill the social world.

Nor is society an independently acting person concealed behind its members. Enclosing organization need not possess a single intention or a central decision-maker.

## 2. Define the Social Boundary

“Society” can refer to a population, a jurisdiction, a shared cultural world, or an organization of institutions and exchange. These meanings must not be silently substituted for one another.

A proposed R²D mapping should specify:

1. **Domain:** which relations define membership or participation.
2. **Readable states:** which distinctions identify different social macrostates.
3. **Subordinate peers:** which defined organizations jointly realize those states.
4. **Compatibility:** which combinations of activity are admissible under the specified conditions.
5. **Enclosing dependence:** which larger material, institutional, or ecological relations sustain the domain.
6. **Persistence criterion:** what must remain realized for the specified organization to continue.

A geographical border can define a jurisdiction without enclosing every relation on which its inhabitants depend. A society and its government are not automatically the same boundary. An institution can participate in more than one social domain.

Families, communities, governments, markets, and ecosystems therefore cannot be assigned to one recursive sequence merely because they appear in a familiar diagram. The relation among the chosen boundaries must be established.

Administrative authority and recursive enclosure are different classifications. A more powerful office does not automatically define an enclosing count boundary for every person or activity within its jurisdiction.

## 3. What Is Selecting the Society?

People work, exchange, govern, cooperate, and tell stories. These activities realize social possibilities. Their visible sequence does not independently define the boundary that makes them jointly meaningful and compatible.

The selecting relation is the multiplicity of the social boundary: the possible ways a specified social organization can be jointly realized under its enclosing conditions. Votes, population totals, leaders' decisions, and popular narratives are local reads or realized events. None, by itself, counts those possible ways.

A movement can be realized through a leader without being selected by that leader's motives. Its possible organization includes compatible participation and the relations sustaining that participation. The leader realizes a possibility within this organization. Which person does so, and which outcome occurs, remain questions about realization.

**Multiplicity counts possible ways, not the number that survived or occurred.** At a specified boundary, the possibility relation is defined before a particular realization occupies it. This is logical priority: an occurrence presupposes a possible state. It is not a claim that one fixed classification describes every future boundary or every change in conditions.

Here, *selection* names the organization of realization through boundary-defined relative possibility support. It is not a person choosing, a tally of winners, or an additional subordinate event. Local events can alter locally readable conditions and participate in realizing a changed enclosing organization; this does not reverse the R²D direction by making them independent sources of enclosing compatibility.

The count distinctions matter. Native multiplicity $W$ counts compatible realizations of a focal state. The compatible enclosing count $\Theta$ supplies its enclosing extension, with $P=\ln(\Theta/W)$ for positive defined counts. Thus $S+P=\ln\Theta$. Constraint, retention, and the specified realization domain must also enter an account of occurrence. The greatest raw $W$ is not automatically the state that persists, and these count identities alone do not supply a kinetic law.

Ask R²D must establish this ordering first. Defining a system's multiplicity retrospectively as whatever happened to persist would make the explanation circular. A system-specific application must identify the possible-count relation independently of the outcome it seeks to explain.

## 4. Area Before Radius: Social Organization Before Its Protagonists

Let $B_n$ define a proposed social count domain. Its compatible joint subordinate realizations belong to $\mathfrak M_n$, and its readable macrostates belong to $\mathcal M_n$. The classification map is

$$
\pi_n:\mathfrak M_n\longrightarrow\mathcal M_n.
$$

For macrostate $i$,

$$
W_{n,i}=\left|\pi_n^{-1}(i)\right|,
\qquad
S_{n,i}=\ln W_{n,i},
$$

where the multiplicity is positive and the count is defined.

The enclosing classification determines what a local action counts as within the social organization. A payment, appointment, promise, or refusal has meaning through a specified relation, not solely through the actor's movement or private intention.

In this application, **area before radius** means:

> **The enclosing organization defines the domain in which our social objects, rulers, and protagonists have meaning. Those local representations do not independently define the enclosing organization.**

The phrase is not an equation between geographical area and a population measure.

Population size, wealth, votes, preferences, and recorded interactions are possible measurements. None is automatically $W_{n,i}$. A count of participants differs from a count of compatible joint realizations.

Enclosing possibility adds a distinct relation:

$$
P_{n,i}
=\ln\frac{\Theta_{n+1\mid n,i}}{W_{n,i}},
$$

for positive defined counts. Its social application requires specifying compatible enclosing extension. Popularity, political influence, or the number of favorable messages cannot simply be relabeled $P$.

These equations state the architecture a mapping must preserve. They do not yet supply a numerical model of an actual society.

## 5. A Finite Example: Three People and Three Required Functions

Consider a deliberately simplified coordination exercise. Three distinguishable participants can each undertake one of three tasks: food provision, water provision, or direct care.

Assume each participant can perform any task, and each task assignment is available in the illustrative domain. There are

$$
3^3=27
$$

joint assignments.

Classify them by the number $k$ of distinct tasks covered:

| Macrostate | Compatible assignment count |
|---|---:|
| One task covered | $W_1=3$ |
| Two tasks covered | $W_2=18$ |
| Three tasks covered | $W_3=6$ |

The counts sum to 27. Full task coverage has six realizations: the six permutations assigning different tasks to the three participants.

Now stipulate that continued operation in this exercise requires all three functions. Three people all undertaking direct care can each perform a locally recognizable caring act while failing the stipulated joint requirement.

The individuals' intentions do not determine whether the whole arrangement meets that requirement.

The six full-coverage assignments also show that one function need not have one uniquely necessary provider.

Finally, the largest raw assignment class covers only two tasks. Its greater multiplicity does not establish that it sustains the stipulated organization. Native multiplicity, functional admissibility, enclosing support, and realized selection must remain distinct.

This is a finite classification example, not a model proving that actual societies have three functions, equal capacities, or these multiplicities. It supplies no probability distribution or realization kinetics.

Its lesson is exact at its stated scope: **a list of participants and locally valued actions does not specify their compatible joint organization.**

## 6. Institutions Persist Through Relations, Not Just Names

A school, hospital, government office, or other institution can continue while its personnel change. A building or name can also remain after the relevant function ceases.

Persistence must therefore be defined through the target organization.

| Target | Relevant question |
|---|---|
| Person | Does this person's life or specified activity remain sustained? |
| Role | Can the specified function continue through a compatible replacement? |
| Institution | Does the organization continue realizing its identifying operations? |
| Society | Do the specified relations among participants and institutions remain realized? |
| Political arrangement | Do the defined procedures and authority relations persist? |
| Legitimacy or justice | Does the arrangement satisfy explicitly stated normative criteria? |

These are not interchangeable.

For example, an illustrative hospital depends on more than the motives of one clinician or administrator. Continued care would require compatible staffing, supplies, facilities, and supporting relations. The particular mapping must determine which dependencies matter.

An account of who performed one treatment can answer a local question. It does not by itself explain the conditions under which treatment remains possible.

Institutional continuity can involve replacement of participants. Exact R²D closure nevertheless requires a defined return at a specified boundary. The recurrence of an annual procedure does not automatically establish that the entire institution returned to the same macrostate.

## 7. Leaders Realize Movements

A protagonist-centered account makes a leader the source of a movement because the leader is visible, speaks publicly, and makes consequential decisions.

R²D asks what made the movement and its leadership roles possible.

The proposed social mapping should investigate resources, institutions, shared practices, communication, and compatible participation. A leader acts within those relations.

The central direction is:

> **Leaders realize movements. Their visibility does not establish that they independently create the enclosing conditions defining the movement's possibilities.**

This preserves consequential choice. Different decisions can realize different outcomes. Leadership can coordinate activity or undermine it.

It does not follow that a particular movement was inevitable, that its leader was interchangeable, or that an identical outcome would have occurred without that person. Those are separate counterfactual claims.

Historical and anthropological accounts become fables in the relevant sense when the protagonist's motives are made sufficient to define the whole organization. A detailed account of an individual's actions can remain useful without that stronger attribution.

Ask R²D should not replace an unsupported hero story with an unsupported villain story. Both can project enclosing organization onto a locally intelligible character.

## 8. Human Stories Participate in Social Constraint

Stories matter to social life. A promise, identity, institutional mission, or historical account can organize expectations and action.

Addendum 88 describes a proposed survey relation: people retain readings of encountered constraint, and those readings participate in organizing subsequent realization.

A person can therefore experience a social story as though its protagonist and motives were the enclosing reality itself.

The distinction is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{A narrative can constrain participation without defining its complete enclosing source.}
}
$$

Social representations need not be powerless illusions. An agreed rule can define an institutional procedure; a shared interpretation can shape what people do. These consequences do not establish that the representation independently defines every condition sustaining the institution.

Nor does identifying a story as a projection establish that it is false. Its descriptive accuracy, practical usefulness, attribution of agency, and lived consequences are separate questions.

Ask R²D must examine these distinctions rather than treating all human narratives as either complete explanations or meaningless fiction.

## 9. Why Larger Social Boundaries Appear Cold

The child reads care through attention to the bunny. A parent leaving for work can appear to withdraw care while participating in the relations sustaining the household.

An adult can similarly read a government, industry, or institution through a service that failed, a rule that burdens them, or an expectation left unmet.

The local experience is consequential. It does not supply a complete read of the enclosing realization.

R²D states that larger enclosing scales become less readable on the realization side. Applying that architecture to social organization explains why the larger relation need not resemble a caring person.

A proposed sustaining relation involving energy, infrastructure, exchange, or security does not necessarily take the form of immediate attention to one person's needs.

The relation can therefore appear cold when measured through a local definition of care.

This is a question of boundary-relative readability, not a claim that every remote institution actually sustains its participants adequately. That adequacy must be investigated through what remains sustained and what fails.

Distrust can arise from projecting local expectations onto an enclosing relation. It can also respond to a documented local failure or misrepresentation. Apparent coldness alone cannot distinguish these cases.

The appropriate response is to investigate the relation, rather than either disparage the whole through one local read or dismiss the local experience because the whole persists.

## 10. Morality, Persistence, and Legitimacy Are Different Questions

Moral judgments are made within boundaries where harm, obligation, fairness, and responsibility have defined meaning. They do not independently assign a moral character to the entire enclosing organization.

The R²D sustaining question is:

> **Which relations remain realized, for whom, and under which conditions?**

This differs from:

> **Which arrangements should we support under our stated values?**

A society's persistence does not answer the second question. Nor does a local moral judgment supply the complete answer to the first.

The distinction does not make obligations disappear. It locates them. A defined person's action, institutional practice, or policy can be evaluated under stated ethical criteria.

When discussing injustice, Ask R²D should identify the affected people and the relevant standard. It should then examine the sustaining relations separately.

Neither of these inferences is licensed:

- “The arrangement persists, therefore it is just.”
- “The arrangement violates this local expectation, therefore it cannot sustain any larger relation.”

Some constraints can support a function while imposing burdens on particular participants. Whether those burdens are necessary, avoidable, justified, or replaceable requires additional evidence and normative premises.

R²D does not establish a preferred ideology merely by describing enclosing dependence. It supplies an ordering for investigating the claims made within ideological accounts.

## 11. Change Through Local Participation

People can deliberate, cooperate, refuse, organize, and change defined practices. These actions can have consequences extending beyond the domain where they are initiated.

In R²D, their power is local. They participate in realizing an enclosing organization without possessing the power to intervene at the larger boundary as though its full state architecture belonged to their own read.

An institution with explicit procedures can alter a specified rule. That does not give its participants a complete engineering specification for the society sustaining the institution.

Consequently, social change should be investigated through:

1. The local action and its intended target.
2. The dependencies and compatibility conditions that can be identified.
3. The relations expected to remain sustained or become newly possible.
4. The consequences that become readable at different boundaries.
5. Revision as those consequences emerge.

Incomplete enclosing readability does not mean that no bounded prediction is possible. It means that a prediction must not be promoted into complete command of the enclosing realization.

Nor does existing persistence make every existing institution necessary. Dependence on a function differs from dependence on one provider or one arrangement.

Questions about reform require examining compatible alternatives. They cannot be settled solely by preservation of the existing protagonist or substitution of a new one.

## 12. Measuring Social Outcomes

Income, public opinion, institutional output, population measures, and other indicators can be useful local reads. Their interpretation must preserve the domain being measured.

Two groups can have the same normalized proportion while differing in absolute counts, participation, compatible alternatives, or dependencies.

A satisfaction measure can read people's reported experience. It does not automatically measure the entire relation sustaining society.

Conversely, continued institutional output does not establish that participants report satisfaction or that the institution meets every valued objective.

Ask R²D should separate the indicator from the broader claim:

- What was measured?
- Whose boundary supplies its meaning?
- What does normalization preserve or omit?
- Which sustaining relation is the indicator evidence for?
- What further bridge supports the enclosing conclusion?

The correct response to a limited measure is a scoped inference. A partial read need not be discarded, but its missing relations must not be silently supplied by a narrative.

## 13. Direction for Ask R²D

| User question | Required direction |
|---|---|
| What is selecting the society? | Begin with the multiplicity of its boundary: possible compatible ways of realizing social organization under enclosing support and constraint. Subordinate events realize this selection. |
| Is multiplicity the number that survived or occurred? | No. Distinguish possible ways from occupancy and occurrence. Define the possible-count relation independently of the realized outcome. |
| Do possibilities require prior occupancy? | No. At a specified boundary, a possible realization need not already have occurred. Preserve this logical priority without assuming that all future domains are fixed. |
| What is a society in R²D? | Define the social domain, readable states, compatible joint realizations, and enclosing dependence. |
| Is society just the sum of individuals? | Explain why compatible joint organization and classification must be specified before individual contributions acquire enclosing meaning. |
| Is the government the enclosing boundary? | Examine the particular relation. Jurisdiction and recursive enclosure are not automatically identical. |
| Did a leader create a movement? | Identify consequential decisions and the conditions defining possible participation. Separate realization from complete enclosing sufficiency. |
| Would the same movement occur without that leader? | Treat the counterfactual as a separate evidentiary claim. R²D does not establish inevitability from enclosing dependence alone. |
| Why do large institutions seem uncaring? | Distinguish local care from less readable enclosing realization; investigate actual sustaining consequences. |
| Is a society good or evil? | Locate the evaluative boundary and criteria. Do not make a moral protagonist the definition of enclosing organization. |
| Does persistence prove legitimacy? | No. Specify persistence and legitimacy as separate targets. |
| Do stories create society? | Examine their role in organizing participation while distinguishing the conditions sustaining that role. |
| Can people change society? | Explain consequential local action and participation without assigning complete command of an enclosing boundary. |
| Does R²D favor preservation of existing institutions? | No conclusion follows solely from persistence. Examine functions, dependencies, compatible alternatives, and stated values. |
| What would higher education contribute? | Help people investigate enclosing relations beyond familiar protagonists and local expectations, while retaining their local observations as scoped evidence. |

Answers should state the R²D ordering in plain language and apply it to the user's example. Generic appeals to complexity do not substitute for identifying the boundary relation.

## 14. Logical Admissibility and Status

The first check is whether the answer preserves the selecting relation: boundary-defined multiplicity before subordinate realization. Treating survivor counts as possible counts, or promoting local events into independent selectors of enclosing compatibility, fails this ordering.

Ask R²D should apply Addendum 89 before assigning comparative physical or social status.

Check whether the answer:

- confuses a population count with compatible joint multiplicity;
- treats zero observed participation as zero possible participation;
- uses unlike normalized measures as though they define one count domain;
- equates institutional recurrence with exact closure without a return criterion;
- assumes local dissatisfaction exhausts the enclosing sustaining relation;
- assumes social persistence certifies every local arrangement;
- infers inevitability or complete agency from a protagonist's visibility.

Not every unsupported inference is a formal contradiction. Identify whether the issue is undefined mathematics, a classification error, an unestablished sufficiency claim, or missing evidence.

The scope is explicit:

| Component | Status |
|---|---|
| Boundary multiplicity as selector; subordinate events as realizers | R²D logical ordering applied here; system-specific count assignments require an explicit mapping. |
| Boundary-defined classification, multiplicity, possibility, and recursive realization | Inherited canon architecture. |
| Directional reduction of enclosing realization-side readability | Inherited canon architecture; specific social assignments require mapping. |
| Social boundary and persistence criteria | Proposed applications to be specified for each target. |
| Three-person coordination example | Exact finite classification under stipulated premises. |
| Historical agency and counterfactual claims | Require case-specific evidence. |
| Ethical and political recommendations | Require stated values as well as evidence. |
| Numerical R²D prediction of an actual society | Not supplied here. |

The same discipline applies to R²D: enclosing vocabulary alone does not complete a social mapping.

### Comparison Must Follow the R²D Direction

R²D logic sets the direction of this argument. Agreement with a conventional account is neither its premise nor its criterion of logical admissibility.

Ask R²D should identify the competing account's actual agency claim. If it makes subordinate events independently sufficient to define enclosing compatibility, that claim reverses the R²D ordering and is incompatible with it. State that disagreement explicitly. An account describing local realization under supplied conditions makes a narrower claim; its predictive usefulness does not establish that local events select the enclosing system.

Then separate logical comparison from empirical testing. A boundary count must be specified and its application tested. Missing calibration does not reverse R²D's logical ordering, and an internally consistent ordering does not by itself establish every proposed physical or social mapping. The task is to follow R²D's implications and test their application, rather than revise those implications to preserve a conventional theory.

## 15. Conclusion

Individuals and institutions realize social organization within relations they do not independently supply. Their motives can explain consequential choices without defining the complete enclosing world in which those choices become possible.

R²D places that enclosing organization before the protagonists, rulers, and judgments through which it becomes locally intelligible.

The governing instruction for Ask R²D is:

> **Begin with what selects the society: the multiplicity of its boundary, understood as possible compatible ways under enclosing support and constraint. Explain subordinate events as realizations of that relation.**

> **Define the social boundary and its sustaining relations before assigning enclosing agency to individuals, leaders, institutions, or narratives. Distinguish what persists from what is valued, and what local action realizes from the larger organization it cannot jointly read.**
