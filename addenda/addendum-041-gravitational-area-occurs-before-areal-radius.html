Addendum 41 — Gravitational Area Occurs Before Areal Radius

Enclosing Area, Inverse-Area Free Fall, and the Projection of Radial Distance

Addendum 41 — Gravitational Area Occurs Before Areal Radius

Abstract

Newtonian gravity is conventionally written in terms of radial distance:

\[g_G=\frac{\mu_G}{r^2},\]

or, after source calibration,

\[g_G=\frac{GM}{r^2}.\]

The appearance of r is so familiar that radial distance is easily treated as the primitive geometric variable from which the enclosing spherical area

\[A=4\pi r^2.\]

is subsequently constructed.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) permits the opposite explanatory order.

Addendum 40 derived the inverse-square relative free-fall relation directly from Keplerian closure:

\[\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}=-\mu_G\frac{\mathbf r}{r^3},\]

with

\[\mu_G=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{T^2}.\]

shared by a family of orbital closures belonging to the same gravitational relation.

That result does not yet establish a primitive spherical radius. It establishes a radial dependence within an empirically readable orbital closure.

The present addendum asks what geometric quantity that radial dependence represents when the enclosing gravitational relation is additionally spherically symmetric.

Let

\[B_G\]

denote the shared gravitational boundary.

The formal R²D boundary is not itself a geometric sphere. When it acquires a spherically symmetric spatial projection, let the corresponding enclosing symmetry surface possess physical area

\[A_G.\]

Only afterward define the associated areal radius

\[r_A\equiv\sqrt{\frac{A_G}{4\pi}}.\]

Thus

\[A_G\longrightarrow r_A.\]

The Keplerian inverse-square law then becomes

\[g_G=\frac{\mu_G}{r_A^2}=\frac{4\pi\mu_G}{A_G}.\]

Therefore,

\[g_GA_G=4\pi\mu_G.\]

The familiar inverse-square radial law is the spherical coordinate representation of a prior inverse-area gravitational relation.

The distinction becomes more important in strong gravity.

In a spherically symmetric spacetime, the areal radius remains defined exactly by

\[A_G=4\pi r_A^2.\]

Curvature does not invalidate that definition.

Instead, curvature changes the relation between areal radius and proper radial distance.

Let

\[\ell_r\]

denote proper radial distance. In general,

\[d\ell_r\ne dr_A.\]

For Schwarzschild geometry,

\[d\ell_r=\frac{dr_A}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r_A}}}.\]

on a Schwarzschild-time spatial slice outside the horizon.

Thus three quantities that coincide approximately in weak-field Euclidean intuition become distinct:

\[r_K\ne r_A\ne\ell_r\]

in their general physical roles.

The Keplerian coordinate rK belongs to orbital peer separation.

The areal radius rA parameterizes enclosing spherical area.

The proper radial distance \(\ell_r\) is reconstructed from the local radial metric.

The complete R²D ordering is therefore

gravitational boundary → enclosing area → areal radius → proper radial ruler.

This provides the geometric coordinate needed for the next step.

Define the area-first compactness

\[\chi_G\equiv\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A}.\]

Since

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{A_G}{4\pi}},\]

we may write

\[\chi_G=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2}\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{A_G}}.\]

The Schwarzschild horizon condition is not imposed here.

The present result is prior:

gravitational area occurs before areal radius.

1. Addendum 40 Leaves a Geometric Question

Addendum 40 reconstructed weak-field gravitational motion without beginning from force.

Kepler I and II imply

\[\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}=-\mu_G\frac{\mathbf r}{r^3}.\]

Kepler III gives

\[\mu_G=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{T^2}.\]

and shows that the same coefficient organizes a family of orbital closures within a shared gravitational relation.

The remaining question is geometric:

What does the r 2 in the inverse-square relation represent?

2. The Keplerian Radius Is a Relative Coordinate

In Addendum 40,

\[\mathbf r=\mathbf x_2-\mathbf x_1\]

is the relative separation of gravitationally related peers.

Its magnitude,

\[r_K=|\mathbf r|,\]

belongs to that peer relation.

Thus

\[r_K\]

should not yet be interpreted as the primitive radial coordinate of an enclosing gravitational geometry.

It is first the radial coordinate of the observable orbital closure.

3. Spherical Enclosure Is an Additional Physical Mapping

Keplerian closure does not by itself prove that the complete gravitational domain is spherically symmetric.

To proceed from orbital radial dependence to enclosing area, introduce the additional physical condition:

spherical symmetry of the enclosing gravitational projection.

Under that condition, the local gravitational relation possesses spherical symmetry surfaces.

Let one such surface have physical area

\[A_G.\]

4. The Formal R²D Boundary Is Not Literally a Sphere

Let

\[B_G\]

denote the gravitational count boundary.

In R²D,

\[B_G\]

is not fundamentally a material membrane, shell, or geometric surface.

Instead,

\[A_G\]

is a physical spatial read assigned to the enclosing gravitational relation under spherical projection.

Therefore,

\[B_G\ne A_G.\]

The relation is

\[B_G\longrightarrow A_G.\]

5. Area Is the Enclosing Geometric Read

The quantity

\[A_G\]

measures the area of the spherical symmetry surface associated with the gravitational enclosure.

This is an enclosing quantity.

It does not require one to first imagine a collection of primitive radial rulers extending outward from a center.

The enclosing measure can be specified directly.

That makes area the natural spherical read of the boundary.

6. Define Areal Radius From Area

Now define

\[r_A\equiv\sqrt{\frac{A_G}{4\pi}}.\]

Then, by definition,

\[A_G=4\pi r_A^2.\]

The explanatory direction is therefore

\[A_G\longrightarrow r_A.\]

The areal radius parameterizes the enclosing area.

It does not primitively generate it.

7. Why “Areal Radius” Matters

The subscript A should remain explicit.

The quantity

\[r_A\]

does not initially mean:

• proper radial distance; • affine distance; • radar distance; • coordinate distance in an arbitrary chart; • or Euclidean ruler length from an origin.

It means exactly:

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{\text{area of the symmetry sphere}}{4\pi}}.\]

That definition remains meaningful even when radial proper distance is strongly curved.

8. Map the Keplerian Radius Onto Areal Radius

In the weak-field spherical regime, the orbital radial coordinate can be identified with the spherical areal coordinate to the relevant approximation:

\[r_K\longrightarrow r_A.\]

Then Addendum 40’s acceleration law becomes

\[g_G=\frac{\mu_G}{r_A^2}.\]

This is now a spherical enclosing-space representation of the Kepler-derived free-fall relation.

9. Rewrite Gravity in Area Form

Since

\[r_A^2=\frac{A_G}{4\pi},\]

we have

\[g_G=\frac{\mu_G}{A_G/(4\pi)}.\]

Therefore,

\[g_G=\frac{4\pi\mu_G}{A_G}.\]

Equivalently,

\[g_GA_G=4\pi\mu_G.\]

This is the area-first gravitational relation.

10. The Inverse-Square Law Is an Inverse-Area Law

The radial form is

\[g_G=\frac{\mu_G}{r_A^2}.\]

The enclosing-area form is

\[g_G=\frac{4\pi\mu_G}{A_G}.\]

They are mathematically equivalent.

But their logical positions differ.

The area form refers directly to the enclosing spherical relation.

The radial form appears only after the enclosing area has been encoded by one scalar parameter.

Thus:

inverse area \(\longrightarrow\) inverse-square areal radius.

11. The Exponent Two Is Geometric

The factor

\[r_A^{-2}\]

arises because spherical area satisfies

\[A_G=4\pi r_A^2.\]

Thus the exponent two is a property of the spherical geometric projection.

It does not by itself identify the underlying physical coupling.

Gravity and other inverse-area phenomena can share this exponent while possessing different local coupling structures.

Therefore:

common \(1/r^2\) \(\not\Rightarrow\) common physical ontology.

12. Kepler Shows That Area Is Not an Arbitrary Rewrite

One could object that replacing

\[r^2\]

with

\[A_G/(4\pi)\]

is merely algebra.

Kepler makes the area interpretation physically motivated.

The orbital closure already supplies a dynamically readable area:

\[\frac{dA_K}{dt}=\text{constant}.\]

Area appears in the empirical gravitational closure before Newtonian force language is introduced.

The enclosing spherical area

\[A_G\]

is not the same area.

But gravity is already organized through an areal coordinate class before the radial force interpretation is imposed.

13. Keep Keplerian Swept Area and Enclosing Area Distinct

The Keplerian swept area is

\[A_K.\]

It is planar and dynamic.

The enclosing gravitational area is

\[A_G.\]

It is the area of a spherical symmetry surface.

Thus:

\[A_K\ne A_G.\]

The legitimate sequence is structural:

areal orbital recurrence \(\longrightarrow 1/r^2\longrightarrow 1/A_G\).

No literal surface transforms into another.

14. The Flux Form Makes the Enclosing Structure Explicit

For a spherically symmetric inward free-fall field,

\[\mathbf g_G=-\frac{\mu_G}{r_A^2}\hat{\mathbf r}.\]

The flux through a symmetry sphere is

\[\Phi_G=\oint\mathbf g_G\cdot d\mathbf A.\]

Because the magnitude is constant over the sphere,

\[\Phi_G=-g_GA_G.\]

Using

\[g_GA_G=4\pi\mu_G,\]

we obtain

\[\Phi_G=-4\pi\mu_G.\]

The orbital calibration supplied by Kepler has become an enclosing flux calibration.

15. The Newtonian Gauss Relation Comes Later

In the dominant-source Newtonian regime,

\[\mu_G=GM.\]

Therefore,

\[\Phi_G=-4\pi GM.\]

This is the familiar Newtonian gravitational Gauss relation.

The logical sequence is

Keplerian closure → μG → enclosing area → gravitational flux → GM calibration.

16. The Area Law Does Not Require a Primitive Source Mass

The area relation

\[g_GA_G=4\pi\mu_G\]

can be written before splitting

\[\mu_G\]

into

\[G\]

and

\[M.\]

This preserves the result of Addendum 40:

\(\mu_G\) is the directly readable shared gravitational calibration.

The decomposition

\[\mu_G=GM\]

comes later.

17. Dimensional Scale Remains a Separate Problem

To assign a numerical area in square meters or a numerical radius in meters requires physical dimensional calibration.

The present addendum does not derive the recursive origin of that calibration.

In particular, it does not require a prior relation of the form

\[\ell_G^{\Phi}=\frac{c}{f_G^{\Phi}}\]

to establish the logical priority of area over areal radius.

Thus the area-first geometry is independent of the unresolved gravitational recurrence-scale program.

18. Area Before Radius Is Therefore Not a Claim About Units

The statement

\(A_G\longrightarrow r_A\)

does not mean that area units are more primitive than length units.

It means that within the spherically symmetric gravitational geometry, the scalar coordinate called areal radius is defined by the enclosing area relation.

The claim concerns geometric ownership and explanatory order.

19. Three Radial Quantities Must Now Be Separated

The word “radius” compresses several distinct physical coordinates.

The new gravitational architecture requires at least three:

Keplerian peer separation

\[r_K.\]

Areal radius

\[r_A.\]

Proper radial distance

\[\ell_r.\]

These quantities coincide only under additional physical conditions.

20. Keplerian Separation Is a Peer Coordinate

The quantity

\[r_K\]

belongs to the relative orbit:

\[r_K=r[B_1,B_2\mid B_G].\]

It describes peer separation in the weak-field closure.

It is not automatically a global coordinate of the enclosing gravitational geometry.

21. Areal Radius Is an Enclosing Coordinate

The areal radius belongs to a symmetry surface:

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{A_G}{4\pi}}.\]

Its meaning is fixed by area.

Thus:

\[r_A=r_A[A_G].\]

It need not equal accumulated proper radial distance.

22. Proper Radial Distance Is a Metric Read

Let

\[\ell_r\]

denote proper spatial distance measured along a specified radial spatial path.

In a metric representation,

\[d\ell_r=\sqrt{g_{r_Ar_A}}\,dr_A.\]

for the relevant radial spatial slice.

Therefore,

\[\ell_r\]

depends on both the areal coordinate and the local radial geometry.

It is farther downstream.

23. The Weak-Field Limit Hides These Distinctions

In weak approximately Euclidean geometry,

\[g_{r_Ar_A}\simeq1.\]

Therefore,

\[d\ell_r\simeq dr_A.\]

Likewise, the Keplerian radial coordinate maps naturally onto the spherical areal coordinate:

\[r_K\simeq r_A.\]

Hence,

\[r_K\simeq r_A\simeq\ell_r\]

in the weak-field regime.

That is why one symbol r works so well in Newtonian mechanics.

24. Strong Gravity Separates the Coordinates

In a strongly curved gravitational geometry,

\[g_{r_Ar_A}\ne1.\]

in general.

Then

\[d\ell_r\ne dr_A.\]

The areal coordinate and the local proper radial ruler are no longer interchangeable.

This is the geometric distinction that the weak-field approximation hides.

25. The Area–Areal-Radius Relation Does Not Fail in Strong Gravity

This point is critical.

For a spherically symmetric geometry,

\[A_G=4\pi r_A^2\]

is the defining relation of areal radius.

Strong gravity does not invalidate it.

Curvature instead changes the relation between

\[r_A\]

and other distance measures.

Thus the correct strong-gravity statement is not

\[A_G\ne4\pi r_A^2.\]

It is

\[r_A\ne\ell_r\]

as physical coordinates in general.

26. Schwarzschild Geometry Makes the Distinction Explicit

In Schwarzschild coordinates, write the exterior line element as

\[ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r_A}\right)c^2dt^2+\left(1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r_A}\right)^{-1}dr_A^2+r_A^2d\Omega^2.\]

The angular sector gives

\[A_G=4\pi r_A^2.\]

Exactly.

The coordinate rA is therefore the Schwarzschild areal radius.

27. Schwarzschild rA Is Not Proper Radial Distance

On a Schwarzschild-time spatial slice in the exterior region,

\[ds^2_{\mathrm{radial}}=\left(1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r_A}\right)^{-1}dr_A^2.\]

Therefore,

\[d\ell_r=\frac{dr_A}{\sqrt{1-\frac{2GM}{c^2r_A}}}.\]

Thus

\[d\ell_r>dr_A\]

outside the horizon for finite positive GM /rA .

The areal radius and proper radial distance are physically distinct.

28. Curvature Does Not Mean the Areal Radius “Bends”

The quantity

\[r_A\]

is a scalar label reconstructed from area.

It does not need to bend as an object.

What changes is the relationship among local rulers, clocks, and neighboring enclosing-area reads.

Thus curved geometry should not be interpreted as a primitive radius physically deforming.

The stronger R²D statement is:

the enclosing area relation remains primary while the local metric determines how radial rulers represent changes i

29. This Is the Gravitational Form of the Coastline Inversion

Differentiate

\[A_G=4\pi r_A^2.\]

Then

\[dA_G=8\pi r_A\,dr_A.\]

Therefore,

\[dr_A=\frac{dA_G}{8\pi r_A}.\]

A fixed areal increment corresponds to different radial increments depending on the enclosing area scale.

Thus radial resolution is reconstructed from the enclosing area relation.

The ruler does not ontologically precede the area it parameterizes.

30. Equal Areal Distinguishability Does Not Mean Equal Radial Distinguishability

Suppose two neighboring enclosing states differ by the same areal interval

\[\delta A_G.\]

Then the corresponding areal-radius interval is

\[\delta r_A\simeq\frac{\delta A_G}{8\pi r_A}.\]

As rA increases,

\[\delta r_A\]

for the same areal distinction becomes smaller.

Therefore:

fixed areal distinction \(\not\Rightarrow\) fixed radial distinction.

This will become important when gravitational multiplicity and horizon-state spacing are considered later.

31. The Primitive Readability Question Should Therefore Be Areal

A later gravitational boundary may appear to possess ever-finer radial intervals.

That alone does not establish loss of physical distinguishability.

The more prior question is:

does the enclosing area distinction remain readable?

Only after the areal distinction has been specified should its radial coordinate interval be calculated.

This corrects the temptation to interpret vanishing radial spacing as a primitive gravitational limit.

32. The Missing Addendum 27 Will Need This Correction

The atmospheric-to-horizon well-depth argument correctly separates total gravitational well depth from elementary distinguishability spacing.

Its later horizon discussion then observes that larger gravitational wells can correspond to finer radial spacing for a fixed logarithmic count increment.

The present result clarifies the logical order.

The primitive gravitational distinction should first be formulated through

\[\delta A_G,\]

with

\[\delta r_A\]

treated as its radial projection.

Thus:

areal distinguishability \(\longrightarrow\) radial distinguishability.

33. Area Also Clarifies the Meaning of Gravitational Flux

The equation

\[g_GA_G=4\pi\mu_G\]

says that under spherical symmetry the same shared gravitational closure coefficient is distributed over an enclosing area.

As the area grows,

\[g_G\]

decreases.

This does not require the source relation itself to weaken.

The same enclosing calibration is represented over a larger spherical area.

34. Gravity and Electrostatics Share Projection Geometry, Not Coupling Ontology

For gravity under spherical symmetry,

\[g_G=\frac{4\pi GM}{A_G}.\]

For electrostatics,

\[E=\frac Q{\epsilon_0A}.\]

for a spherical surface.

The common inverse-area form reflects common spherical projection geometry.

It does not imply that gravitational mass and electric charge are the same physical coupling.

Thus:

common area law \(\ne\) common primitive interaction.

35. Area Is Especially Important in Gravity Because It Survives the Horizon Projection

At weak field, area can remain hidden inside the notation

\[r^2.\]

In Keplerian closure, area becomes dynamically readable through

\[\dot A_K.\]

At a gravitational horizon, spherical area remains a finite physically meaningful geometric coordinate even when exterior clock and radial-ruler relations become strongly distorted.

This gives the progression

orbital areal recurrence \(\longrightarrow\) inverse enclosing area \(\longrightarrow\) horizon area.

36. Introduce Compactness Without Making Radius Primitive

The conventional dimensionless compactness is

\[\chi_G\equiv\frac{2GM}{c^2r_A}.\]

Using

\[\mu_G=GM,\]

write

\[\chi_G=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2r_A}.\]

Since

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{A_G}{4\pi}},\]

we obtain

\[\chi_G=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2}\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{A_G}}.\]

This is the area-first form of compactness.

37. Compactness Compares Gravitational Calibration With Enclosing Area

The quantity

\[\frac{\mu_G}{c^2}\]

has dimensions of length.

But the compactness does not require that length to be interpreted first as a literal material radius.

It compares the gravitational calibration with the areal scale of the enclosing geometry:

\(\chi_G\) = dimensionless mass-area relation.

This is the natural coordinate inherited by the Schwarzschild problem.

38. Do Not Yet Impose the Horizon Condition

Schwarzschild geometry later gives

\[\chi_G=1\]

at the event horizon.

Equivalently,

\[r_H=\frac{2GM}{c^2}.\]

But this is not derived here.

The present addendum establishes only the prior variable:

\[\chi_G=\frac{2\mu_G}{c^2}\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{A_G}}.\]

The condition that makes this coordinate a horizon belongs to Addendum 42.

39. Principle — Gravitational Area Before Areal Radius

For a gravitational boundary BG possessing spherical spatial projection, let

\[A_G\]

denote the enclosing symmetry-surface area.

Define the areal radius only afterward:

\[r_A=\sqrt{\frac{A_G}{4\pi}}.\]

Therefore,

\(B_G\longrightarrow A_G\longrightarrow r_A\).
