# Addendum 129 — Spinorial Sign Reversal as Boundary-Relative Closure

**Finite Compatible Counting, Rotational Parity, and the Mathematical Representation of the 360°/720° Observation**

**Status:** Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026  

## Abstract

A spin-½ system exhibits a characteristic rotational relationship: a 360° rotation changes the sign of its spinorial representation, while a 720° rotation restores that representation. Coherent interferometry makes the relative sign experimentally readable.

Conventionally, this behavior is described using spinors and the double covering of the spatial rotation group $SO(3)$ by $SU(2)$. The mathematical representation is exact. Its success, however, does not establish a persistent microscopic spinning object as the physical cause of the observation.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) begins with the enclosing experimental boundary. That boundary defines compatible realizations, distinguishable states, and the conditions under which rotational and detector relationships become readable. At the quantum readability horizon, the observed recurrence relation can remain readable without preserving a uniquely identifiable subordinate realization history.

Addendum 106 established that normalized symmetric incidence among finite compatible binary assignments generates the angular-momentum algebra. The present addendum extends that result to spinorial rotational parity.

For a finite representation containing $L$ equivalent formal binary distinctions, with centered orientation coordinate $m=k-L/2$, the standard rotationally covariant representation satisfies

$$
\boxed{U(2\pi)=(-1)^L\mathbf1}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{U(4\pi)=\mathbf1.}
$$

The parity factor is independent of the occupied orientation class. It is a property of the complete count-generated representation.

A second construction establishes the equivalent result using a signed complement operation on the finite binary manifold. The construction identifies the mathematical distinction between returning to the original orientation classification and returning to the original phase representative.

The observable sign appears only in a coherent comparison with a reference. An isolated global sign does not constitute a separately readable physical state.

The results reconstruct the rotational mathematics without introducing primitive spin-bearing particles or uniquely identifiable subordinate trajectories. They do not establish that finite cardinality alone uniquely selects a physical spin representation, nor that the quantum horizon permits recovery of the subordinate recurrence structure represented by the spinor.

**The observed rotational relationship is physical. The spinor is its mathematical representation, not its independently established microscopic cause.**

## 1. The observed relationship precedes its mathematical representation

Consider an experimental boundary that defines a preparation, a controlled rotational operation, a coherent reference, and a detector.

A spin-½ rotational measurement exhibits three relevant observations:

1. The ordinary spatial orientation returns after 360°.
2. A coherent comparison distinguishes a sign reversal after that rotation.
3. A further 360° rotation restores the original interference relationship.

These observations are commonly expressed as

$$
|\psi\rangle
\xrightarrow{2\pi}
-|\psi\rangle
\xrightarrow{2\pi}
|\psi\rangle.
$$

The expression is a mathematical description of the measured relationship.

It does not establish that a physically persistent spinor has undergone two complete rotations. Nor does it establish that the experiment follows one continuously identifiable microscopic object.

In R²D, the enclosing measurement defines the observable distinctions before a subordinate representation is assigned.

The correct order is

$$
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{Enclosing experimental relation}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{Admitted compatible realizations}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{Readable rotational and detector relations}\\
\Downarrow\\
\text{Mathematical representation}.
\end{gathered}}
$$

The representation must reproduce the observation. Its predictive success does not invert the causal priority of the enclosing relation.

This is the same distinction developed in Addenda 101, 102, and 105.

## 3. Finite binary compatibility and orientation classes

Following Addendum 106, consider a finite compatible domain of $L$ equivalent formal binary distinctions.

When all assignments are compatible,

$$
\mathfrak M_B=\{0,1\}^{L}.
$$

Let $k$ denote the number of positive assignments.

The enclosing classification contains

$$

$$

with multiplicities

$$
\boxed{W_k=\binom Lk.}
$$

The total multiplicity is

$$
\Theta_B=2^L.
$$

Introduce the centered orientation coordinate

$$
\boxed{m=k-\frac L2.}
$$

The representation contains

$$
m=-\frac L2,-\frac L2+1,\ldots,\frac L2.
$$

It therefore admits $L+1$ centered orientation classes.

For $L=1$,

$$
m=-\frac12,\frac12.
$$

For $L=2$,

$$
m=-1,0,1.
$$

For $L=3$,

$$
m=-\frac32,-\frac12,\frac12,\frac32.
$$

These are formal orientation classes of the finite compatibility construction.

They are not a claim that $L$ microscopic particles or physical switches persist beneath the receiving quantum boundary.

In Addendum 106, $L$ was selected to represent an observed angular manifold. It was not derived independently from the physical composition of the nucleus.

That distinction remains controlling here.

## 4. Symmetric incidence generates the angular-momentum algebra

The binomial multiplicities obey the exact incidence identity

$$
\boxed{W_k(L-k)=W_{k+1}(k+1).}
$$

Define normalized symmetric class vectors

$$
|k\rangle=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{W_k}}
\sum_{|x|=k}|x\rangle.
$$

The vectors $|x\rangle$ are formal coordinates of compatible assignments, not independently occupied physical microstates.

Normalized incidence between adjacent classes gives

$$
J_+|k\rangle
=
\sqrt{(L-k)(k+1)}|k+1\rangle
$$

and

$$
J_-|k\rangle
=
\sqrt{k(L-k+1)}|k-1\rangle.
$$

Define

$$
J_z|k\rangle
=
\left(k-\frac L2\right)|k\rangle.
$$

Then

$$
[J_z,J_\pm]=\pm J_\pm
$$

and

$$
\boxed{[J_+,J_-]=2J_z.}
$$

With

$$
J_x=\frac{J_++J_-}{2},
\qquad
J_y=\frac{J_+-J_-}{2i},
$$

the resulting operators satisfy

$$
[J_i,J_j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}J_k.
$$

The finite count-generated representation therefore possesses the angular-momentum algebra with

$$
\boxed{j=\frac L2.}
$$

This is the exact mathematical result established in Addendum 106.

Normalized linear incidence has now been introduced as a representation of the count relations. Physical spatial rotation has not yet been identified with that representation.

## 5. Theorem 129.1 — Rotational parity is fixed by the count representation

**Theorem.** Let the finite symmetric incidence representation with $j=L/2$ be assigned the standard continuous rotationally covariant representation

$$
U_z(\theta)=e^{-i\theta J_z}.
$$

Then a complete $2\pi$ rotation acts on every centered orientation class with the same factor:

$$
\boxed{U_z(2\pi)=(-1)^L\mathbf1.}
$$

A second complete rotation restores the representation:

$$
\boxed{U_z(4\pi)=\mathbf1.}
$$

### Proof

For orientation class $|k\rangle$,

$$
J_z|k\rangle
=
\left(k-\frac L2\right)|k\rangle.
$$

Therefore,

$$
U_z(2\pi)|k\rangle
=
e^{-i2\pi(k-L/2)}|k\rangle.
$$

Separate the exponent:

$$
e^{-i2\pi(k-L/2)}
=
e^{-i2\pi k}e^{i\pi L}.
$$

Since $k$ and $L$ are integers,

$$
e^{-i2\pi k}=1
$$

and

$$
e^{i\pi L}=(-1)^L.
$$

Consequently,

$$
U_z(2\pi)|k\rangle
=
(-1)^L|k\rangle.
$$

The factor is independent of $k$, so

$$
\boxed{U_z(2\pi)=(-1)^L\mathbf1.}
$$

Applying the transformation twice,

$$
U_z(4\pi)=(-1)^{2L}\mathbf1
=\mathbf1.
$$

Under the full rotationally covariant representation, the result is independent of the selected rotation axis because generators associated with different axes are related by rotation conjugation.

This completes the proof.

### Corollary 129.1a — Even and odd count representations

For even $L$,

$$
U(2\pi)=+\mathbf1.
$$

For odd $L$,

$$
U(2\pi)=-\mathbf1.
$$

Therefore, half-integer and integer angular representations differ by the parity of the finite construction.

### Corollary 129.1b — The sign is independent of occupied orientation

Every $k$ class acquires the same factor.

Thus the complete rotational sign is not selected by the occupancy of one orientation class.

It is fixed by the algebraic structure of the entire representation.

This is the central new mathematical result.

### Corollary 129.1c — The sign does not define a different isolated physical state

For an isolated quantum ray,

$$
[|\psi\rangle]=[-|\psi\rangle].
$$

A global sign change does not create a distinguishable isolated state.

Its significance arises in a coherent comparison where the relative sign changes observable detector relations.

## 6. Theorem 129.2 — The signed complement construction

The same parity factor can be exhibited through a finite complementary operation.

Define the represented half-turn

$$
F_y=e^{-i\pi J_y}.
$$

Its action on the symmetric orientation classes is

$$
\boxed{
F_y|k\rangle=(-1)^{L-k}|L-k\rangle.
}
$$

This identity can be obtained by applying the normalized two-class half-turn operation to each formal binary coordinate and restricting to the symmetric representation.

For $L=1$,

$$
F_y|0\rangle=-|1\rangle,
\qquad
F_y|1\rangle=|0\rangle.
$$

For general $L$, each positive coordinate becomes negative and each negative coordinate becomes positive. The operation complements the binary assignment while contributing one negative factor for every originally negative coordinate.

Since class $k$ contains $L-k$ negative coordinates, the normalized symmetric class acquires the factor $(-1)^{L-k}$.

Apply the transformation again:

$$
F_y^2|k\rangle
=
(-1)^{L-k}(-1)^k|k\rangle.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{F_y^2=(-1)^L\mathbf1.}
$$

For odd $L$, two half-turns restore the orientation classification while changing the sign of its representation. Four half-turns restore the original representative.

For even $L$, two half-turns restore it directly.

### Interpretation

Ordinary binary complementation satisfies

$$
\operatorname{comp}^2=\mathbf1.
$$

The signed complement operation differs because it belongs to the oriented angular representation.

It is important not to reverse the derivation here.

The bare binary complement operation does not independently contain the negative sign. The sign appears in the normalized angular representation and its rotational composition.

The theorem therefore provides a finite algebraic verification of the rotational parity result, not an independent derivation of quantum spin from cardinality alone.

Neither operation must be interpreted as the literal evolution of microscopic binary constituents.

## 7. Rotational closure and phase-representation closure are different

Ordinary spatial orientation obeys

$$
R(2\pi)=R(0).
$$

The final spatial orientation after one complete revolution is identical to the initial orientation.

The half-integer angular representation instead obeys

$$
U(2\pi)=-\mathbf1.
$$

These are not contradictory statements.

The rotation group $SO(3)$ and its double covering group $SU(2)$ encode different mathematical distinctions.

The center of $SU(2)$ contains

$$
\{+\mathbf1,-\mathbf1\}.
$$

Both central elements correspond to the same identity orientation under the covering map to $SO(3)$.

The finite count-generated angular representation assigns the nontrivial central element the action

$$
\boxed{-\mathbf1\mapsto(-1)^L\mathbf1.}
$$

For odd $L$, the representation distinguishes the two central elements.

For even $L$, it does not.

This is the group-theoretic origin of the rotational sign distinction.

Its R²D interpretation must be stated carefully.

The mathematical double covering does not establish two physically occupied subordinate states. Nor does the topology identify a uniquely readable quantum recurrence beneath the horizon.

It establishes that an observed spatial orientation can admit a richer mathematical rotational representation.

Which aspects of that representation are physically readable depend on the enclosing experimental boundary.

## 8. The sign becomes readable through a joint comparison

Consider a coherent interferometric experiment with two alternatives, $a$ and $b$.

The enclosing boundary defines their preparation and recombination conditions before either detector outcome is realized.

One alternative undergoes a controlled rotation represented by $U(\theta)$. The other provides a reference.

Within the conventional coherent quantum projection, the comparison factor is

$$
\Gamma(\theta)
=
\langle\psi|U(\theta)|\psi\rangle.
$$

For a balanced two-output interferometer with calibrated visibility $V$, reference phase $\delta$, and no additional unaccounted relative phase, the detector probabilities are

$$
p_\pm(\theta,\delta)
=
\frac12\left[
1\pm V\operatorname{Re}
\left(e^{i\delta}\Gamma(\theta)\right)
\right].
$$

For one complete rotation,

$$
\Gamma(2\pi)=(-1)^L.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{
p_\pm(2\pi,\delta)
=
\frac12\left[
1\pm(-1)^L V\cos\delta
\right].
}
$$

For odd $L$, the detector preferences reverse relative to the reference measurement.

After two complete rotations,

$$
\Gamma(4\pi)=1,
$$

and the original interference relationship returns.

This reproduces the observed 360°/720° effect.

The experimentally observable quantity is the detector occupancy relation, not the independent existence of a spinorial object.

Let $c_+$ and $c_-$ denote realized detector occurrences under a specified preparation.

Their normalized read is

$$
\widehat p_\pm
=
\frac{c_\pm}{c_++c_-}.
$$

The conventional interference expression provides a calibrated prediction for these observed proportions.

R²D does not identify the mathematical complex amplitude with the underlying compatible multiplicity, nor the predicted probability with a completed detector occurrence.

The detector receives an enclosing physical relation. Its recorded counts realize that relation through the applicable measurement boundary.

The spinor represents the calibrated rotational component of this joint mathematical description.

It does not cause the detector event.

## 9. Experimental correspondence

The characteristic two-cycle interference relationship was tested by Rauch and colleagues in 1975 using neutron interferometry.

A coherent neutron interferometer compared alternatives subjected to different magnetic-field conditions. The resulting interference oscillations were consistent with the predicted $4\pi$ spinor periodicity.

The experiment establishes that a relative rotational phase relationship becomes observable through coherent comparison.

It does not, by itself, establish a unique persistent microscopic trajectory or identify a spinor as a physically existing object.

The relevant experimental boundary includes the preparation, coherent alternatives, magnetic controls, recombination, and detector.

The magnetic control is an experimentally specified relation. Its representation as a spin rotation is part of the successful mathematical projection.

The R²D task is to preserve the distinction between that projection and the causal priority of the enclosing experimental relation.

The present reconstruction reproduces the established rotational prediction under the stated physical mapping. It does not claim a new experimental discovery or a uniquely derived microscopic mechanism.

## 10. The connection to muscle and Fermi–Dirac counting

It establishes that the occupancy mathematics precedes the quantum particle interpretation.

## 12. Why spinorial sign is not yet fermionic exchange

Spinorial sign reversal under a complete spatial rotation is not identical to exchange antisymmetry.

The rotational statement is

$$
U(2\pi)=(-1)^L\mathbf1.
$$

The conventional fermionic exchange statement is

$$
\Psi(\ldots,x_a,\ldots,x_b,\ldots)
=
-\Psi(\ldots,x_b,\ldots,x_a,\ldots).
$$

These statements concern different operations.

The first belongs to a rotational representation.

The second belongs to an exchange representation on a joint state space.

Their relationship in relativistic quantum field theory is governed by additional physical and mathematical assumptions.

Finite binary occupancy alone does not establish that relationship.

A complete R²D reconstruction of the spin-statistics connection would require an independently specified enclosing exchange-compatibility relation and the conditions connecting that relation to physical rotation.

This addendum does not claim such a derivation.

An important distinction remains between representing a quantum relation and identifying its inaccessible subordinate realization.

$$

At the quantum readability horizon, the receiving boundary admits a readable recurrence relation without recovering the subordinate geometric history that realizes it.

Multiple subordinate realization histories may remain compatible with the same enclosing observation.

The observed interference constrains the admissible mathematical representations. It does not uniquely identify their unreadable physical realization.

Consequently, the following inference is invalid:

$$
\text{Observed }4\pi\text{ periodicity}
\Longrightarrow

The valid statement is narrower:

$$
\boxed{

\text{a calibrated spinorial representation of that read}.

$$

The equivalence here concerns the specified mathematical fit to the observed relationship. It is not an equivalence between a representation and a uniquely established physical cause.

This is the observational-inversion distinction of Addendum 105 applied to spin.

## 15. Conclusion — The enclosing representation determines the sign

The 360°/720° rotational relationship admits an exact finite-count mathematical reconstruction.

Starting from compatible binary assignments, normalized symmetric incidence generates the angular-momentum algebra. Under its standard rotationally covariant representation, the sign following one complete rotation is

$$
\boxed{U(2\pi)=(-1)^L\mathbf1.}
$$

The result is independent of the occupied orientation class and depends only on the parity of the representation.

A signed-complement construction reproduces the same algebraic distinction.

The observable physical consequence arises through a coherent joint measurement. The sign is not separately readable as an isolated global phase, but its relative value can determine the interference relation recorded by an enclosing detector.

No primitive microscopic spinor, continuously persistent spinning particle, or uniquely identifiable subordinate trajectory is required to construct this mathematical relationship.

Neither does the reconstruction establish such an object.

The result is consistent with the original R²D distinction between enclosing causal organization and subordinate mathematical realization.

**A spinor does not physically turn negative. A mathematical representation changes sign, and an enclosing measurement can distinguish the resulting relative relationship.**

What is physically observed is the interference read. What is mathematically constructed is the rotational representation. What remains inaccessible is a unique subordinate recurrence history beneath the quantum readability horizon.

The next addendum develops the broader consequence:

A physical system may return to its original local state while an enclosing boundary retains a distinguishable record of the completed occurrence. The cardiac pressure–volume loop and the turbine provide directly readable examples. Quantum rotational interference demonstrates both the power and the limit of that analogy.
