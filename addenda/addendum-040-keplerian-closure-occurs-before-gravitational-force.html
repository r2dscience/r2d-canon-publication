Addendum 40 — Keplerian Closure Occurs Before Gravitational Force

Areal Recurrence, Free-Fall Acceleration, and the Shared Gravitational Boundary

Addendum 40 — Keplerian Closure Occurs Before Gravitational Force

Abstract

Newtonian gravity is conventionally introduced through the force law

\[\mathbf F=-\frac{GMm}{r^3}\mathbf r.\]

The resulting equations of motion then produce the orbital relations discovered empirically by Kepler.

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) permits the opposite explanatory order.

Addenda 35–39 established the relativistic and gravitational reference architecture before returning to the planetary limit.

Addendum 35 showed that motion is defined relative to an enclosing rapidity reference rather than owned intrinsically by the moving boundary.

Addendum 37 showed that free fall is zero differential proper acceleration relative to the immediate gravitational background even while motion relative to a larger reference changes.

Addendum 38 identified differential free-fall motion across neighboring peers as the observable precursor to curvature.

Addendum 39 then obtained the Einstein equation as the minimal covariant compatibility relation within the specified metric projection class.

The planetary problem occupies a simpler readability regime. Orbital geometry, recurrence, and scale are simultaneously observable. Kepler’s laws therefore provide a direct empirical window into the weak-field gravitational closure before force, field, potential, or curved spacetime need be introduced.

For a bound planar orbit, Kepler I supplies the closure geometry

\[r(\theta)=\frac{p}{1+e\cos\theta},\]

while Kepler II supplies constant areal recurrence,

\[\frac{dA_K}{dt}=\text{constant}.\]

Define

\[J_K\equiv r^2\dot\theta=2\frac{dA_K}{dt}.\]

Kepler II gives

\[J_K=\text{constant}.\]

This removes the transverse component of the relative coordinate acceleration.

Writing

\[u(\theta)=\frac1r,\]

the Kepler ellipse satisfies

\[u''+u=\frac1p.\]

Combining this closure geometry with constant areal recurrence gives

\[\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}=-\mu_G\frac{\mathbf r}{r^3},\]

where

\[\mu_G=\frac{J_K^2}{p}.\]

Thus the inverse-square relative acceleration law is already contained in elliptical closure plus areal recurrence.

No gravitational force law has been assumed.

Kepler III then gives

\[\mu_G=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{T^2},\]

showing that μG is shared by a family of orbital closures belonging to the same gravitational relation.

The orbiting body’s mass does not appear.

The gravitational relation therefore organizes the free-fall motion before local mass is used to construct a Newtonian force coordinate.

Within the new R²D architecture, the quantity

\[\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}\]

must be interpreted carefully. It is not the locally measured proper acceleration of a freely falling planet. The planet can satisfy

\[a^{\mathrm{prop}}=0\]

while its relative position changes according to the Keplerian coordinate law above.

The Newtonian force equation is therefore a later mechanical representation of that already-existing shared free-fall relation.

In the dominant-source limit,

\[\mu_G=GM,\]

and the Newtonian test-body force becomes

\[\mathbf F_N=-\frac{GMm}{r^3}\mathbf r.\]

For the exact two-body problem,

\[\mu_G=G(m_1+m_2),\]

which makes the shared-boundary character still clearer.

The central result is therefore:

Keplerian closure occurs before gravitational force.

1. Return From the General Gravitational Projection to the Readable Planetary Regime

Addenda 35–39 moved from reference ownership to Lorentz geometry, free fall, tidal distinguishability, curvature, and finally Einstein compatibility.

Those results establish the general architecture.

The present addendum now asks a simpler empirical question:

What gravitational relation is already readable in a weak-field bound orbit before the full geometric projection is required?

A planetary system provides an unusually transparent regime because three things are simultaneously measurable:

• closure geometry; • recurrence; • scale.

This makes Keplerian motion an ideal R²D readability window.

2. Gravity Is a Shared Relation Among Peers

Gravity differs from a local applied constraint.

An applied constraint can act specifically on one selected boundary.

A gravitationally bound system instead contains multiple peer boundaries participating in one larger relation.

Let

\[B_1,\qquad B_2\]

be two gravitationally related local boundaries.

Let

\[B_G\]

denote their shared gravitational boundary.

Then schematically,

\[B_1,B_2\subset B_G.\]

The gravitational relation belongs first to

\[B_G,\]

not independently to either peer considered in isolation.

3. The Relative Coordinate Belongs to the Peer Relation

Define the relative separation

\[\mathbf r=\mathbf x_2-\mathbf x_1.\]

Its magnitude is

\[r=|\mathbf r|.\]

This coordinate does not belong primitively to either peer.

It belongs to their relation.

Thus:

\[\mathbf r=\mathbf r[B_1,B_2\mid B_G].\]

The gravitational orbit is therefore already a cross-boundary physical read.

4. Orbit Means Closure Before It Means Force

A planetary orbit is conventionally represented as a trajectory

\[\mathbf r(t).\]

That representation is physically successful.

But the prior empirical distinction is simpler:

the relation closes.

The relative configuration returns.

Its geometry recurs.

Its recurrence interval is measurable.

Thus an orbit is first a macroscopically readable gravitational closure.

5. Kepler Supplies Three Independent Empirical Reads

Kepler’s three laws describe three aspects of this closure.

Kepler I — Geometry

The bound relative orbit is elliptical.

Kepler II — Areal recurrence

Equal areas are swept in equal times.

Kepler III — Scale-recurrence relation

For closures governed by the same gravitational relation,

\[T^2\propto a^3.\]

These are empirical physical inputs.

The present addendum asks what follows mathematically from them before gravitational force is introduced.

6. Kepler I Defines the Closure Geometry

Let the semimajor axis be

\[a,\]

the semiminor axis

\[b,\]

and eccentricity

\[e.\]

Define the semilatus rectum

\[p=a(1-e^2).\]

Equivalently,

\[p=\frac{b^2}{a}.\]

Using polar coordinates centered on one focus,

\[r(\theta)=\frac{p}{1+e\cos\theta}.\]

This is a geometric read of bound closure.

It is not yet a force law.

7. Kepler II Defines Areal Recurrence

For angular increment

\[d\theta,\]

the relative radius vector sweeps area

\[dA_K=\frac12r^2\,d\theta.\]

Therefore,

\[\frac{dA_K}{dt}=\frac12r^2\dot\theta.\]

Kepler II requires this quantity to remain constant.

Define

\[J_K\equiv r^2\dot\theta=2\frac{dA_K}{dt}.\]

Then

\[J_K=\text{constant}.\]

8. JK Is Introduced Before Angular Momentum

Conventional mechanics calls

\[r^2\dot\theta\]

specific angular momentum.

That interpretation is valid after the mechanical coordinates have been assigned.

R²D begins earlier.

Here,

\[J_K\]

is first the recurrence-weighted areal coordinate of the gravitational closure.

Only later may a mass calibration produce an angular momentum coordinate.

Thus:

areal recurrence → angular-momentum read.

9. Kepler II Fixes the Direction of Relative Motion Change

In plane polar coordinates,

\[\mathbf a_{\mathrm{rel}}=a_r\hat{\mathbf r}+a_\theta\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}.\]

where

\[a_\theta=r\ddot\theta+2\dot r\dot\theta.\]

Differentiate

\[J_K=r^2\dot\theta.\]

Then

\[\frac{dJ_K}{dt}=2r\dot r\dot\theta+r^2\ddot\theta.\]

Since

\[J_K=\text{constant},\]

we obtain

\[2\dot r\dot\theta+r\ddot\theta=0.\]

Therefore,

\[a_\theta=0.\]

The relative coordinate acceleration is radial.

10. This Result Precedes Torque Language

Within Newtonian mechanics,

\[a_\theta=0\]

is usually expressed as zero torque.

That is a correct later description.

But the empirical ordering is:

\[\dot A_K=\text{constant}\longrightarrow a_\theta=0.\]

Only after force and momentum coordinates are assigned does one write

\[\mathbf r\times\mathbf F=0.\]

Thus the central character of the weak-field gravitational relation is already encoded in orbital closure before gravitational force is invoked.

11. Kepler I Fixes the Radial Dependence

Define

\[u(\theta)=\frac1r.\]

From the elliptical relation,

\[u=\frac{1+e\cos\theta}{p}.\]

Differentiate with respect to θ :

\[u'=-\frac{e\sin\theta}{p}.\]

Differentiate again:

\[u''=-\frac{e\cos\theta}{p}.\]

Therefore,

\[u''+u=\frac1p.\]

This is the differential form of the elliptical closure geometry.

12. Express the Recurrence Through u

Because

\[J_K=r^2\dot\theta\]

and

\[r=\frac1u,\]

we obtain

\[\dot\theta=J_Ku^2.\]

Also,

\[\dot r=\frac{dr}{d\theta}\dot\theta.\]

Since

\[r=\frac1u,\]

\[\frac{dr}{d\theta}=-\frac{u'}{u^2}.\]

Hence,

\[\dot r=-J_Ku'.\]

13. Differentiate the Radial Motion

Since JK is constant,

\[\ddot r=-J_Ku''\dot\theta.\]

Using

\[\dot\theta=J_Ku^2,\]

we obtain

\[\ddot r=-J_K^2u^2u''.\]

The radial coordinate acceleration is

\[a_r=\ddot r-r\dot\theta^2.\]

But

\[r\dot\theta^2=\frac1u(J_K^2u^4)=J_K^2u^3.\]

Thus,

\[a_r=-J_K^2u^2(u''+u).\]

14. Insert the Closure Geometry

Kepler I gave

\[u''+u=\frac1p.\]

Therefore,

\[a_r=-\frac{J_K^2}{p}u^2.\]

Since

\[u=\frac1r,\]

we obtain

\[a_r=-\frac{J_K^2}{p\,r^2}.\]

Define

\[\mu_G\equiv\frac{J_K^2}{p}.\]

Then

\[a_r=-\frac{\mu_G}{r^2}.\]

In vector form,

\[\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}=-\mu_G\frac{\mathbf r}{r^3}.\]

15. The Inverse-Square Relation Has Appeared Before Force

The result follows from only:

  1. elliptical closure;
  2. constant areal recurrence.

Thus:

Kepler I + Kepler II ⇒ inverse-square relative acceleration.

No assumption has been made that

\[F\propto\frac1{r^2}.\]

No gravitational potential has been introduced.

No gravitational field ontology has been assumed.

No curved metric has been required.

16. The Meaning of “Acceleration” Must Now Be Updated

The quantity

\[\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}\]

is a coordinate acceleration of the relative peer relation.

It is not the locally measured proper acceleration of either freely falling body.

Addendum 37 established that a freely falling local boundary can satisfy

\[a^{\mathrm{prop}}=0\]

while its motion relative to a larger enclosing reference changes.

Therefore the Keplerian relation should be read as

free-fall relative coordinate acceleration,

not as evidence for a primitive local force pushing or pulling the orbiting body.

17. Keplerian Motion Is a Common-Mode Gravitational Realization

In the weak-field planetary regime, the freely falling peers participate in the shared gravitational reference relation.

Their local support acceleration vanishes.

Yet their relative separation evolves.

Thus the Keplerian law

\[\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}=-\mu_G\frac{\mathbf r}{r^3}\]

is the weak-field coordinate read of an enclosing gravitational relation of the type developed in Addendum 37.

The law describes how the peer relation evolves.

It does not require local nonzero proper acceleration.

18. The Test-Body Mass Is Absent

The derived relation is

\[\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}=-\mu_G\frac{\mathbf r}{r^3}.\]

No test-body mass appears.

This is not an accidental cancellation after writing a force law.

The mass was never needed to derive the free-fall relation.

Thus:

shared gravitational closure → common free-fall relation

occurs before the local mass coupling is used in a mechanical force coordinate.

19. This Is the Weak-Field Form of Universality of Free Fall

Different sufficiently small test boundaries placed into the same weak gravitational relation satisfy the same coordinate free-fall law.

Their different local masses do not determine

\[\mu_G.\]

Mass enters when one asks for a mechanically calibrated force or momentum read.

The free-fall reference relation itself comes first.

This is precisely the boundary architecture established in Addendum 37.

20. One Orbit Alone Does Not Yet Make μG a Boundary Calibration

From one ellipse,

\[\mu_G=\frac{J_K^2}{p}.\]

This could initially appear to be a parameter specific to that single closure.

Kepler III resolves the ambiguity.

21. Evaluate the Complete Areal Closure

The area of an ellipse is

\[A_{\mathrm{ellipse}}=\pi ab.\]

One complete orbital recurrence sweeps this full area during period T .

Therefore,

\[\frac{dA_K}{dt}=\frac{\pi ab}{T}.\]

Since

\[J_K=2\frac{dA_K}{dt},\]

we obtain

\[J_K=\frac{2\pi ab}{T}.\]

22. Calculate μG

Recall

\[p=\frac{b^2}{a}.\]

Then

\[\mu_G=\frac{J_K^2}{p}.\]

Substitute

\[J_K=\frac{2\pi ab}{T}.\]

Therefore,

\[\mu_G=\frac{4\pi^2a^2b^2}{T^2}\frac a{b^2}.\]

Hence,

\[\mu_G=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{T^2}.\]

23. Kepler III Makes μG a Shared Gravitational Calibration

Kepler III states

\[\frac{T^2}{a^3}=\text{constant}\]

for orbital closures belonging to the same dominant gravitational system.

Therefore,

\[\mu_G=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{T^2}=\text{constant}.\]

across those closures.

The coefficient is not determined by:

• eccentricity; • instantaneous position; • test-body mass.

It characterizes the shared gravitational relation.

24. Kepler’s Three Laws Now Form One Structure

The three laws can therefore be reorganized:

Kepler I

\[u''+u=\frac1p.\]

Closure geometry.

Kepler II

\[J_K=r^2\dot\theta=\text{constant}.\]

Areal recurrence.

Kepler I + II

\[\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}=-\mu_G\frac{\mathbf r}{r^3}.\]

Relative free-fall law.

Kepler III

\[\mu_G=\frac{4\pi^2a^3}{T^2}.\]

Shared gravitational calibration.

Thus the three empirical laws reduce to one coherent gravitational closure relation.

25. Kepler III Identifies the Shared Boundary, Not a Primitive Constant of Nature

The quantity

\[\mu_G\]

has dimensions

\[[\mu_G]=\frac{L^3}{T^2}.\]

Kepler’s observations determine this product-level physical calibration directly.

They do not separately determine:

\[G\]

and

\[M.\]

Therefore R²D should not split

\[\mu_G\]

prematurely.

The directly observed relation is the shared boundary calibration.

26. The Exact Two-Body Problem Strengthens the Boundary Interpretation

In the exact Newtonian two-body problem,

\[\mu_G=G(m_1+m_2).\]

The relative separation satisfies

\[\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}=-G(m_1+m_2)\frac{\mathbf r}{r^3}.\]

Thus the coefficient belongs naturally to the pair relation.

Only in the limit

\[m_2\ll m_1\]

does it reduce to

\[\mu_G\simeq Gm_1.\]

The familiar central-source interpretation is therefore a limiting read of a more symmetric shared-boundary relation.

27. Source Mass and Gravitational Calibration Are Later Decompositions

Once physical source mass is assigned, one may write in the dominant-source regime

\[\mu_G=GM.\]

Here:

\[M\]

is the orientation-even source mass read established by the local boundary architecture, while

\[G\]

is the dimensional gravitational calibration of the mature physical projection.

Keplerian orbital data determine their product.

The decomposition itself requires additional physical information.

28. G Does Not Need to Be Derived Here

The present addendum does not attempt to derive the numerical value or primitive count origin of

\[G.\]

That problem belongs to the later gravitational-calibration program.

The only relation needed here is:

\[\mu_G\overset{\mathrm{calibration}}{=}GM.\]

within the Newtonian dominant-source projection.

This keeps Keplerian closure independent of speculative cross-boundary recurrence scaling.

29. Newtonian Gravitational Force Comes Later

In the test-body Newtonian limit,

\[p=mv,\]

so the mechanical relation

\[F=\frac{dp}{dt}\]

gives

\[\mathbf F_N=m\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}.\]

Substitute the Kepler-derived free-fall relation:

\[\mathbf F_N=-m\mu_G\frac{\mathbf r}{r^3}.\]

After

\[\mu_G=GM,\]

this becomes

\[\mathbf F_N=-\frac{GMm}{r^3}\mathbf r.\]

Newton’s gravitational force law has therefore been recovered after the orbital relation.

30. The Newtonian Force Is a Mechanical Projection, Not Local Proper Acceleration

The equation

\[F_N=ma\]

is valid within the Newtonian coordinate representation.

But it must not be confused with the accelerometer read of Addendum 37.

For gravitational free fall,

\[a^{\mathrm{prop}}=0.\]

The quantity

\[\mathbf a=\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}\]

is the changing coordinate relation among freely falling peers.

Thus:

Newtonian gravitational force = mechanical representation of a shared free-fall relation.

It is not evidence that a freely falling body experiences a local support-like force.

31. The Exact Two-Body Force Uses the Reduced-Mass Projection

For two comparable masses, define the reduced mass

\[m_r=\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}.\]

The relative coordinate then admits the Newtonian mechanical representation

\[\mathbf F_{\mathrm{rel}}=m_r\frac{d^2\mathbf r}{dt^2}.\]

Using

\[\mu_G=G(m_1+m_2),\]

gives

\[\mathbf F_{\mathrm{rel}}=-\frac{Gm_1m_2}{r^3}\mathbf r.\]

This again shows that the force representation is constructed from the already-existing shared relative dynamics.

32. The Dynamic Swept Area Is Not the Enclosing Gravitational Area

Two different areas now appear.

The first is the orbital swept area

\[A_K.\]

Its recurrence satisfies

\[\frac{dA_K}{dt}=\text{constant}.\]

This is a dynamic coordinate of orbital closure.

A different quantity is the enclosing gravitational area

\[\mathcal A_G\]

that will be used to represent an isotropic spherical gravitational boundary.

These must not be identified:

\[A_K\ne\mathcal A_G.\]

33. Kepler Nevertheless Supplies the Bridge to Area Before Radius

The Keplerian relation gives

\[g_G=\frac{\mu_G}{r^2}.\]

If the enclosing gravitational projection is additionally assumed spherical and isotropic, define

\[\mathcal A_G=4\pi r^2.\]

Then

\[g_G=\frac{4\pi\mu_G}{\mathcal A_G}.\]

Equivalently,

\[g_G\mathcal A_G=4\pi\mu_G.\]

Thus the familiar inverse-square radial law can be rewritten as an inverse-area relation.

The full logical significance of this inversion belongs to Addendum 41.

34. Spherical Isotropy Is an Additional Physical Mapping

Kepler I and II imply the radial dependence

\[r^2\]

along the orbital relation.

They do not by themselves prove that the entire gravitational boundary is globally spherical.

Therefore

\[r^2\longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal A_G}{4\pi}\]

requires an additional spherical-isotropy mapping.

The logical status is:

Kepler I + II ⇒ \(1/r^2\).

mathematically,

while

\(1/r^2\) → \(4\pi/\mathcal A_G\).

is the enclosing spherical-area projection.

35. Keplerian Closure Is the Readable Weak-Field Side of the Gravitational Boundary

At planetary scale, both geometry and recurrence remain readable.

We can measure:

\[a,\quad b,\quad e,\quad T,\quad A_K.\]

These jointly determine

\[\mu_G.\]

Thus the weak-field orbit exposes the locally readable side of the gravitational relation directly.

No horizon is required.

No curvature singularity is involved.

No irreversible closure is required.

36. Gravitational Closure Is Not Second-Law Closure

An ideal Keplerian orbit may close reciprocally.
