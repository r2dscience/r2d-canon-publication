Addendum 62 — Boundary Geometry Occurs Before the Friedmann Curvature Term

Spatial-Curvature Class, Inverse-Area Scaling, and the Geometric Read of Cosmological Peer Organization

Addendum 62 — Boundary Geometry Occurs Before the Friedmann Curvature Term

Abstract

Addendum 60 showed that the Friedmann equation partitions one already-defined physical rate magnitude,

\[ H^2\]

into several downstream gravitational coordinates.

Addendum 61 then deprojected the matter-energy contribution,

\[\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon_\zeta\]

into two prior physical reads:

peer-energy read

and

boundary-support read.

The next Friedmann contribution is

\[ F_k=-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}\]

This term has fundamentally different physical ownership.

It is not a source-density term.

For normalized FLRW spatial geometry,

\[ k\in\{-1,0,+1\}.\]

labels the constant-curvature projection class, whereas

\[ K_\zeta\equiv\frac{k}{a^2}\]

is the state-dependent physical sectional curvature of the spatial geometry.

Equivalently, the intrinsic scalar curvature of a constant-cosmological-time spatial slice is

\[{}^{(3)}R_\zeta=\frac{6k}{a^2}=6K_\zeta\]

Thus the Friedmann curvature contribution can be written

\[ F_k=-c^2K_\zeta.\]

or

\[ F_k=-\frac{c^2}{6}\,{}^{(3)}R_\zeta\]

The curvature term is therefore already a physical geometric read.

Addendum 56 established that, for a fixed comoving shell χ,

\[ A_\zeta=4\pi a^2S_k^2(\chi).\]

Solving for the scale factor gives

\[ a^2=\frac{A_\zeta}{4\pi S_k^2(\chi)}\]

Therefore,

\[ K_\zeta=\frac{k}{a^2}=\frac{4\pi k S_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}\]

The spatial scalar curvature is correspondingly

\[{}^{(3)}R_\zeta=\frac{24\pi kS_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}\]

And the Friedmann curvature contribution becomes

\[ F_k=-\frac{4\pi kc^2 S_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}\]

Once the curvature class and comoving comparison domain are fixed, physical spatial curvature therefore scales as the inverse of cosmological boundary area:

\[ K_\zeta\propto A_\zeta^{-1}\]

The geometric ordering is consequently

\[ B_\zeta\to A_\zeta\to K_\zeta\to F_k\]

The normalized label k does not supply physical curvature magnitude by itself.

Instead,

\(k\) = geometric projection class

and

\(A_\zeta\) = state-dependent geometric place value

jointly determine

\(K_\zeta\) = physical spatial curvature

This also explains why cosmological expansion changes the magnitude of curvature without changing the FLRW curvature class.

For fixed nonzero k ,

\[ K_\zeta\propto A_\zeta^{-1}\]

Hence,

\[ d\ln|K_\zeta|=-d\ln A_\zeta\]

Using

\[ H=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\]

we obtain

\[\frac{\dot K_\zeta}{K_\zeta}=-2H\]

and therefore

\[\dot K_\zeta=-2HK_\zeta.\]

Likewise,

\[\dot{{}^{(3)}R}_\zeta=-2H\,{}^{(3)}R_\zeta\]

The curvature class does not evolve:

\[ k=\mathrm{constant}.\]

while its physical magnitude does.

The distinction is crucial for R²D.

The primitive cosmological state is defined through compatible peer-boundary realization before FLRW geometry is assigned.

The curvature class k therefore cannot be identified by definition with R²D constraint, retention, possibility, or asymmetry.

In particular,

\[ k\ne C_\zeta\]

and

\[ K_\zeta\ne R_\zeta\]

Any such relation would require an independent bridge from peer-compatibility structure to the physical curvature class.

The present addendum does not yet derive why a particular R²D cosmological realization projects as

\[ k=+1,\quad k=0,\quad\text{or}\quad k=-1\]

It derives the later relation:

Once an isotropic constant-curvature projection class is selected, the magnitude of its physical curvature is determined by the cosmological boundary-area read.

This separates curvature from matter-energy.

For pressureless conserved source energy, Addendum 61 gave

\[ F_{\mathrm{dust}}\propto A_\zeta^{-3/2}\]

By contrast,

\[ F_k\propto A_\zeta^{-1}\]

The different scalings follow because the terms have different physical ownership.

Matter-energy is a source-energy read divided by a three-dimensional support volume:

\[ V_\zeta\propto A_\zeta^{3/2}\]

Curvature is an inverse squared-length property of the projected boundary geometry:

\[ K_\zeta\propto A_\zeta^{-1}\]

The Friedmann equation can therefore be written, in the pressureless-source limit, almost entirely in area coordinates:

\[ H^2=\frac{8\pi c^2\ell_{E,\zeta}}{3\eta_{k,\chi}A_\zeta^{3/2}}-\frac{4\pi kc^2S_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

The three contributions now reveal three different area-scaling classes:

\[ F_{\mathrm{dust}}\propto A_\zeta^{-3/2}\]

\[ F_k\propto A_\zeta^{-1}\]

and

\[ F_\Lambda\propto A_\zeta^0\]

The conventional scale-factor forms,

\(a^{-3}\), \(a^{-2}\), and \(a^0\), respectively.

are therefore later expressions of different physical boundary reads.

The central statement is:

the Friedmann curvature term is not a primitive negative source contribution. It is the rate-squared

And hence:

Boundary geometry occurs before the Friedmann curvature term.

1. The Curvature Contribution Is Not a Matter Source

The Friedmann curvature contribution is

\[ F_k=-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}\]

It appears additively beside the matter-energy and cosmological-constant terms.

That algebraic placement does not make all three quantities the same type of object.

2. Define the Physical Curvature Coordinate

For normalized FLRW curvature label

\[ k\in\{-1,0,+1\}.\],

define

\[ K_\zeta\equiv\frac{k}{a^2}\]

The quantity Kζ has dimensions of inverse length squared.

3. k and Kζ Must Remain Distinct

The normalized label

\[ k\]

specifies the sign and class of the constant-curvature spatial geometry.

The quantity

\[ K_\zeta\]

specifies its physical curvature magnitude.

Therefore:

\[ k\ne K_\zeta\]

4. The Intrinsic Three-Curvature Is

For an FLRW spatial slice,

\[{}^{(3)}R_\zeta=\frac{6k}{a^2}=6K_\zeta\]

This is an invariant scalar characterization of the intrinsic spatial curvature.

5. Rewrite the Friedmann Curvature Contribution

Since

\[ K_\zeta\equiv\frac{k}{a^2}\]

we have

\[ F_k=-c^2K_\zeta.\] .

Or:

\[ F_k=-\frac{c^2}{6}\,{}^{(3)}R_\zeta\]

6. This Identifies the Physical Ownership of Fk

The curvature term is a geometric quantity converted into rate-squared units.

It is not fundamentally a density.

It does not require matter to be present.

7. Return to the Cosmological Boundary Area

Addendum 56 introduced the area of a fixed comoving shell:

\[ A_\zeta=4\pi a^2S_k^2(\chi).\]

This is the first physical geometric read relevant to the present construction.

8. Solve for the Scale Factor

For fixed χ,

\[ a^2=\frac{A_\zeta}{4\pi S_k^2(\chi)}\]

Therefore the physical curvature can be written directly in boundary-area coordinates.

9. Physical Spatial Curvature Is an Inverse-Area Read

Substituting into

\[ K_\zeta\equiv\frac{k}{a^2}\]

gives

\[ K_\zeta=\frac{k}{a^2}=\frac{4\pi k S_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}\]

10. The Three-Curvature Is Therefore

\[{}^{(3)}R_\zeta=\frac{24\pi kS_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}\]

The intrinsic spatial-curvature magnitude is inversely proportional to the cosmological boundary-area read.

11. The Friedmann Curvature Term Becomes

\[ F_k=-\frac{4\pi kc^2 S_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}\]

This is the area-first form of the Friedmann curvature contribution.

12. Area Occurs Before Curvature Magnitude

The physical ordering is

\[ B_\zeta\to A_\zeta\to K_\zeta\]

Physical curvature magnitude is defined only after the boundary has acquired geometric place value.

13. Curvature Class and Geometric Place Value Perform Different Jobs

The class label is

\[ k\]

The scale of the geometry is supplied by

\[ A_\zeta\]

Their combination produces physical curvature.

14. A Dimensionless Class Does Not Supply Dimensional Magnitude

Knowing only

\[ k=+1\]

does not tell us whether the physical spatial curvature is large or small.

That requires the physical scale

\[ a\]

or equivalently

\[ A_\zeta\]

15. The Curvature Class Is Constant Within One FLRW Projection

For a chosen FLRW spatial class,

\[\dot k=0\]

The cosmological state can change while the projection class remains fixed.

16. Physical Curvature Changes With the State

Because

\[ K_\zeta=k/a^2\]

changing

\(a\) changes while \(K_\zeta\) changes.

even when k remains fixed.

17. In Area Coordinates the Evolution Is Immediate

For nonzero fixed k ,

\[ K_\zeta\propto A_\zeta^{-1}\]

Therefore:

\[ d\ln|K_\zeta|=-d\ln A_\zeta\]

18. Physical Clock Calibration Gives the Curvature Rate

Addendum 56 gave

\[ H=\frac12\frac{d\ln A_\zeta}{dt}\]

Therefore:

\[\frac{\dot K_\zeta}{K_\zeta}=-2H\]

19. Hence

\[\dot K_\zeta=-2HK_\zeta.\]

The physical curvature evolves directly with boundary-area evolution.

20. The Same Is True of the Scalar Three-Curvature

Because

\[{}^{(3)}R_\zeta=6K_\zeta\]

\[\dot{{}^{(3)}R}_\zeta=-2H\,{}^{(3)}R_\zeta\]

21. Positive Curvature Dilutes Toward Zero Under Expansion

For

\[ k=+1\]

\[ K_\zeta>0\]

As

\[ A_\zeta\]

increases,

\[ K_\zeta\to0^+\]

22. Negative Curvature Also Dilutes Toward Zero in Magnitude

For

\[ k=-1\]

\[ K_\zeta<0\]

As area increases,

\[ K_\zeta\to0^-\]

23. Flat Geometry Remains Flat

For

\[ k=0\]

\[ K_\zeta=0\]

for every value of

\[ A_\zeta\]

No area scaling can generate nonzero curvature from the flat FLRW class itself.

24. Principle — Boundary Growth Changes Curvature Magnitude, Not Curvature Class

Thus:

\[\text{state change}\to\text{curvature magnitude change}\]

while

\[\text{projection class}=k\]

remains fixed.

25. The Curvature Class Is Not a Cosmological Macrostate Coordinate in the Same Sense as Area

If successive states

\[ i\to j\]

remain inside one FLRW projection class, then

\[ k_i=k_j\]

Thus k does not distinguish those states.

26. Kζ Does Distinguish Them

Because

\[ A_{\zeta,i}\ne A_{\zeta,j}\]

one generally has

\[ K_{\zeta,i}\ne K_{\zeta,j}\]

for nonzero k .

Thus physical curvature is a state-dependent macrostate read.

27. Curvature Must Pass the Physical-Read Factorization Test

For

\[ K_\zeta\]

to represent cosmological macrostate i, require

\[ K\circ\Pi_G=\bar K_\zeta\circ\pi_\zeta\]

Then all formal microstates classified into one cosmological macrostate share the same physical curvature read.

28. k Has Different Ownership

The curvature class may instead label the geometric projection map itself.

Schematically:

\[\Pi_G^{(k)}:\Omega_\zeta\to\Gamma_G^{(k)}\]

The superscript indicates which constant-curvature family is being used to represent the cosmological state.

29. This Makes k a Projection-Class Coordinate

Within this interpretation,

\(k\) = class of geometric projection

not

\(k\) is not a primitive local count.

30. R²D Constraint Must Remain Distinct

The primitive retention relation is

\[ R=C\lambda\]

The physical curvature class is k .

No identity has been derived between them.

Thus:

\[ k\ne C_\zeta\]

31. Physical Curvature Is Also Not Retention

Likewise:

\[ K_\zeta\ne R_\zeta\]

A sign resemblance or intuitive notion of “confinement” is insufficient to establish a bridge.

32. Nor Is Negative Curvature Primitive Possibility

The class

\[ k=-1\]

should not be interpreted automatically as positive R²D possibility or outward asymmetry.

Its sign belongs to the projected spatial geometry.

33. Curvature Is a Relational Geometric Read of Peer Organization

The cosmological state is defined through compatible joint peer realization.

Therefore any physical spatial curvature associated with that state is naturally a read of the relational organization of the peer set as a whole.

34. No Single Peer Determines k

The curvature class is not naturally a property of one isolated

\[ k\]

It belongs to the enclosing spatial representation of their compatible organization.

35. This Suggests a Global Compatibility Origin

A future derivation may ask whether distinct classes of peer compatibility project as

\[ k=+1,\quad k=0,\quad\text{or}\quad k=-1\]

That possibility is structurally natural.

It is not derived here.

36. The Present Result Begins After Curvature-Class Selection

This addendum assumes an isotropic constant-curvature physical projection class has already been selected.

It then derives the physical magnitude of curvature from boundary area.

37. Matter-Energy and Curvature Have Different Area Scalings

For conserved pressureless source energy, Addendum 61 gave

\[ F_{\mathrm{dust}}\propto A_\zeta^{-3/2}\]

The present addendum gives

\[ K_\zeta\propto A_\zeta^{-1}\]

38. The Different Powers Reflect Different Physical Ownership

Matter-energy is a source read divided by three-dimensional support:

\[\epsilon_{\mathrm{dust}}\sim\frac{E}{V}\]

Since

\[ V\propto A^{3/2}\]

the matter term scales as

\[ A^{-3/2}\]

39. Curvature Is an Inverse Squared-Length Read

Since

\[ A\propto L^2\]

one has

\[ K\propto L^{-2}\propto A^{-1}\]

No source-volume quotient is involved.

40. The Difference Is Therefore Structural, Not Merely Algebraic

The powers

\[-\frac32\]

and

\[-1\]

do not arise because Friedmann happens to contain different exponents.

They arise because the physical reads are different kinds of quantities.

41. Write the First Two Friedmann Terms in Area Form

For a pressureless source domain,

\[ F_\epsilon=\frac{8\pi c^2\ell_{E,\zeta}}{3\eta_{k,\chi}A_\zeta^{3/2}}\]

The curvature term is

\[ F_k=-\frac{4\pi kc^2 S_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}\]

42. The Friedmann Equation Therefore Becomes

\[ H^2=\frac{8\pi c^2\ell_{E,\zeta}}{3\eta_{k,\chi}A_\zeta^{3/2}}-\frac{4\pi kc^2S_k^2(\chi)}{A_\zeta}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\]

This is the area-first form of the pressureless FLRW rate constraint.

The matter term behaves as

\[ A_\zeta^{-3/2}\]

\[ A_\zeta^{-1}\]

\[ A^0\]

46. But the Effective Density Does Not Change Its Geometric Origin

The physical quantity being represented remains

\[ K_\zeta\equiv\frac{k}{a^2}\]

The effective density is only a bookkeeping translation.

\[\epsilon_k\propto a^{-2}\]

If one forces this into the perfect-fluid form

then

\[-3(1+w_k)=-2\]

48. Therefore

This is the effective equation-of-state parameter assigned to curvature when it is written as if it were a fluid.

49. No Curvature Fluid Has Been Discovered

The result

\[ w_k=-\frac13\]

does not imply the existence of a physical substance with that equation of state.

It is the source-density bookkeeping representation of a geometric term.

50. This Reinforces Friedmann Partition Nonuniqueness

A geometric quantity can be algebraically represented as an effective source quantity without changing the physical cosmological rate.

Thus:

\[\text{effective density}\ne\text{primitive source}\]

Within an FLRW projection,

\[ k\]

selects the normalized constant-curvature class.

\[ K_\zeta\equiv\frac{k}{a^2}\]

\[|K_\zeta|\propto A_\zeta^{-1}\]

The curvature contribution is

54. Principle — Curvature-Class Preservation Under Expansion

For a fixed FLRW projection,

\[\dot k=0\]

while

\[\dot K_\zeta=-2HK_\zeta.\]

Expansion changes the physical magnitude of curvature, not its normalized class.

55. Principle — Curvature Is Not a Primitive Source Coordinate

The representation

\[ F_k=\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\epsilon_k\]

is algebraically valid but does not change the geometric ownership of the term.

Thus:

\(\epsilon_k\) = effective source bookkeeping

not a primitive physical source density.
