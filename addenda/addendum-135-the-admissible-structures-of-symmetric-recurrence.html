Enclosing Compatibility, Spatial–Recurrence Incidence, and Possibility Before Physical Propagation

R²D Addendum 135 — The Admissible Structures of Symmetric Recurrence

Enclosing Compatibility, Spatial–Recurrence Incidence, and Possibility Before Physical Propagation

Status: Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026
Canonical basis: R²D Part I (authoritative September 25, 2026 scientific revision); Parts II–IV for downstream context; Addenda 120, 122, and companion Addenda 131–134.
Purpose: Develop the unitless possibility mathematics of symmetric recurrence before assigning thermal, electromagnetic, or gravitational physical representations. No physical propagation or realized occurrence is derived from possible count alone.

Abstract

Part I defines boundary-native multiplicity, enclosing-compatible possibility, realized occurrence, occupancy, and finite recurrence comparison without introducing physical units. Parts II–IV subsequently develop thermodynamic calibration and complementary readability horizons. An intermediate mathematical problem remains: which complete spatial–recurrence response patterns can an enclosing boundary admit, and what can be proved about them before any physical response is realized?

We formulate a finite enclosing-owned domain of complete possible response patterns. Two cyclic response coordinates may be coupled by a relative classification that survives changes of their common presentation reference. Their complete pattern has a count meaning different from its marginal phase or recurrence counts. We define stationary and two oppositely translating incidence classes without a physical time coordinate, a moving constituent, or a wave equation. For a four-address, four-stage, four-response example, each traveling-incidence class contains 256 distinct patterns; their intersection contains 16, and their untagged union contains 496. A second complete pattern with identical spatial and recurrence marginals shows that those marginals do not determine propagation incidence.

The results distinguish what an enclosing owner admits from what nature realizes. Orientation-reversal symmetry pairs permitted positive- and negative-direction patterns but fixes no realized directional populations. Recursive replacement can preserve a readable complete incidence classification while erasing subordinate realization identity; it can also erase that incidence if the read retains only marginal counts. A countermodel proves that the primitive R²D axioms, even supplemented by cyclic responses, reciprocal symmetry, and positive symmetric recurrence, do not uniquely select traveling patterns or exclude stationary ones. The exact missing mathematical input is an independently justified, enclosing-owned spatial–recurrence compatibility restriction. This addendum is a downstream finite development of the Part I counting architecture, not a new primitive axiom or a derivation of light.

135.1. Logical position: between counting and physical calibration

R²D Part I defines the native count domain before the units and ontology of physical thermodynamics. Part II gives compatible count relations thermodynamic representations. Part III studies complementary limits of joint readability; Part IV constructs physical projections from the surviving read classes. These later mappings must not determine the primitive possibilities retroactively.

The present question is more limited and more precise:

Given an enclosing owner and a finite symmetric recurrence comparison, which complete spatial–recurrence response configurations belong to its admitted possibility domain, and which classifications remain meaningful under recursive replacement?

A possible complete record is a candidate joint realization admitted by a specified enclosing compatibility. It is not an assertion that the record has occurred. An admissible recurrence-response class does not supply its physical realization count, its clock calibration, or its physical field type.

The analysis deliberately excludes directional closure-production laws, retaining-work calibration, kinetic transition rates, particle trajectories, physical time, and the numerical constant \(c\). Physical wave propagation is an analogy applied downstream to selected unitless incidence structures.

135.2. Count discipline: possible, admitted, and realized

Fix a receiving boundary \(B\) and an enclosing owner \(J\). At \(B\), a readable class \(k\) has native possible multiplicity

\[ W_{B,k}=|\pi_B^{-1}(k)|. \tag{135.1} \]

The enclosing owner counts compatible extensions \(\Theta_{J\mid B,k}\). The canon’s possibility coordinate is specifically the across-boundary logarithmic ratio

\[ K_{J\mid B,k}=\frac{\Theta_{J\mid B,k}}{W_{B,k}}, \qquad P_{B,k}=\ln K_{J\mid B,k}, \tag{135.2} \]

where the specified counts are positive. Possible pattern counts are not automatically identical to \(P_{B,k}\). They must be assigned to their proper native or enclosing count domain before the ratio (135.2) is formed.

The distinct realized counts \(\nu_{B,k}\) and occupancies \(c_{B,k}\) depend on the actual realization domain. Defining \(\Theta\), \(W\), or \(P\) does not determine either realized count. In particular, a zero admitted possible count excludes a class; a positive admitted possible count does not assert that any member occurs.

Addendum 131’s completed orientation counts \(N_+\) and \(N_-\) may be recorded within candidate complete recurrence histories. A possible history can specify equal forward and reverse complete returns; one must not silently promote those specifications to equal physically realized cycling. This addendum uses symmetric candidate-record support, not a claim of universally realized symmetry.

135.3. A finite spatial–recurrence response domain

Take cyclic address labels \(X=\mathbb Z_L\), cyclic comparison-stage labels \(T=\mathbb Z_L\), and a cyclic response-label set \(Q=\mathbb Z_m\), where \(L,m\ge2\). These sets are dimensionless and need not be physical space, time, or phase.

At each \(s=(j,n)\in X\times T\), suppose the enclosing owner admits two response presentations \(a_s,b_s\in Q\). Define their relative cyclic response

\[ \delta_s=b_s-a_s\pmod m. \tag{135.3} \]

If a common presentation reference \(g_s\in Q\) changes both coordinates by \(a_s\mapsto a_s+g_s\) and \(b_s\mapsto b_s+g_s\), then \(\delta_s\) is unchanged. Accordingly, the presentation-pair quotient by these diagonal reference changes is naturally identified with

\[ \mathcal P=Q^{X\times T}. \tag{135.4} \]

This is a set of candidate complete relative-response patterns, of cardinality

\[ \boxed{|\mathcal P|=m^{L^2}.} \tag{135.5} \]

The cyclic structure and the choice of two coupled response coordinates are additional explicitly declared mathematical structures. Part I allows a boundary to distinguish such relations; it does not require all boundaries to be cyclic or paired in this manner.

The \(g_s\) transformations change the presentation of the same relative classification. Their \(m^{L^2}\) coordinate lifts must not be counted as additional native microstates unless an independently specified boundary makes them physically distinguishable alternatives. Presentation redundancy is not native multiplicity.

135.4. The enclosing owner selects a subset, not a realized history

The enclosing boundary specifies the complete admissible pattern set

\[ \boxed{\mathcal C_J\subseteq\mathcal P.} \tag{135.6} \]

For a pattern property \(\mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal P\), define the number of admitted readable pattern classes

\[ W_J^{\mathrm{pat}}(\mathcal K)=|\mathcal C_J\cap\mathcal K|. \tag{135.7} \]

If a given \(\delta\in\mathcal C_J\) has an enclosingly admitted set \(E_\delta\) of distinct finer possible realizations, the total compatible finer count is instead

\[ W_J^{\mathrm{fine}}(\mathcal K) =\sum_{\delta\in\mathcal C_J\cap\mathcal K}|E_\delta|. \tag{135.8} \]

These counts answer different questions. They coincide only under suitable one-to-one native identification; uniform fibers of size \(r\) yield \(W_J^{\mathrm{fine}}=rW_J^{\mathrm{pat}}\), but uniformity is not assumed. Neither count determines physically realized occurrences. Every use of (135.7) or (135.8) must state the selected count boundary.

We may also attach an independently specified possible complete turbine signature to each compatible pattern, such as \(N_+(h)=N_-(h)=1\) in a twelve-position recurrence comparison. The signature supplies a symmetric candidate recurrence class while leaving \(\mathcal C_J\) free to select stationary or translating response patterns. It introduces no physical rate and no directed closure mechanism.

135.5. Incidence, not a motion law

Define the following subsets of \(\mathcal P\):

\[ \mathcal S=\{\delta:\delta(j,n)=F(j)\text{ for some }F:X\to Q\}, \tag{135.9} \]

\[ \mathcal W_+=\{\delta:\delta(j,n)=F(j-n)\text{ for some }F:X\to Q\}, \tag{135.10} \]

\[ \mathcal W_-=\{\delta:\delta(j,n)=F(j+n)\text{ for some }F:X\to Q\}. \tag{135.11} \]

\(\mathcal S\) is stage-stationary incidence. \(\mathcal W_\pm\) are diagonal-translation incidence classes. These names specify relations among complete patterns only. The labels \(\pm\) denote two mathematically opposed address orientations, not the forward and reverse whole-cycle counts \(N_\pm\) of Addendum 131.

A physical theory might later read a member of \(\mathcal W_\pm\) as a traveling phase pattern. It has not yet been given meters, seconds, or an energy flux. Nor is every cyclic response in \(\mathcal P\) a physical field.

135.6. Theorem 1 — Exact characterization of translating incidence

Theorem 1. A complete pattern \(\delta\in\mathcal P\) belongs to \(\mathcal W_\sigma\), \(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}\), if and only if

\[ \boxed{\delta(j+\sigma,n+1)=\delta(j,n)} \quad\text{for all }j,n\in\mathbb Z_L. \tag{135.12} \]

Proof. If \(\delta(j,n)=F(j-\sigma n)\), substitute \((j+\sigma,n+1)\) to obtain the same argument of \(F\). Conversely, assume (135.12). Repeatedly follow the diagonal comparison \((j,n)\mapsto(j+\sigma,n+1)\) until reaching the reference stage \(0\). Constancy along each diagonal gives \(\delta(j,n)=\delta(j-\sigma n,0)\). Defining \(F(x)=\delta(x,0)\) supplies the required representation. \(\square\)

This is a classification theorem, not an equation of motion. It characterizes an already specified subset of complete possible patterns. It does not choose a particular realization or cause an individual count to propagate.

135.7. Theorem 2 — Exact finite possibility counts

Theorem 2. For the domain (135.4),

\[ |\mathcal S|=|\mathcal W_+|=|\mathcal W_-|=m^L, \tag{135.13} \]

and

\[ \boxed{|\mathcal W_+\cap\mathcal W_-|=m^{\gcd(L,2)}.} \tag{135.14} \]

Consequently,

\[ \boxed{|\mathcal W_+\cup\mathcal W_-| =2m^L-m^{\gcd(L,2)}.} \tag{135.15} \]

Proof. Each class in (135.9)–(135.11) is specified uniquely by one free cyclic profile \(F:X\to Q\), giving \(m^L\) choices. A pattern in both traveling-incidence classes is unchanged under both diagonal shifts. Their composition implies invariance under a two-address shift; hence its profile satisfies \(F(x)=F(x+2)\). The translation \(x\mapsto x+2\) has \(\gcd(L,2)\) orbits on \(\mathbb Z_L\), allowing \(m^{\gcd(L,2)}\) profiles. Inclusion–exclusion yields (135.15). \(\square\)

For \(L=m=4\),

\[ \boxed{|\mathcal P|=4^{16}=4{,}294{,}967{,}296,} \tag{135.16} \]

\[ |\mathcal W_+|=|\mathcal W_-|=256, \qquad |\mathcal W_+\cap\mathcal W_-|=16, \tag{135.17} \]

\[ \boxed{|\mathcal W_+\cup\mathcal W_-|=496.} \tag{135.18} \]

The sixteen overlapping patterns are not sixteen extra patterns to count twice: they possess a two-address repeating profile and satisfy both opposed diagonal incidence conditions. Four of them are fully constant. If an enclosing owner distinguishes orientation as an additional tag, the tagged candidate pairs may be counted separately, but the untagged native pattern classification contains 496, not 512, distinct members.

Stage-stationary patterns number \(|\mathcal S|=256\), and

\[ |\mathcal S\cap(\mathcal W_+\cup\mathcal W_-)|=m=4. \tag{135.19} \]

The class of nonconstant diagonal-translation patterns therefore has 492 members, while 252 nonconstant stationary patterns lie outside it. These are possible pattern counts in the declared pattern domain, not realized frequencies or physical wave populations.

135.8. Theorem 3 — Marginal counts do not determine complete incidence

Define two complete patterns on \(\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_4\):

\[ \delta_T(j,n)=j-n\pmod4, \qquad \delta_X(j,n)=j\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}n, \tag{135.20} \]

where XOR operates on the two-bit labels of \(0,1,2,3\). Their row tables (one row per comparison stage \(n\), one column per address \(j\)) are

\[ \delta_T= \begin{pmatrix} 0&1&2&3\\ 3&0&1&2\\ 2&3&0&1\\ 1&2&3&0 \end{pmatrix},\qquad \delta_X= \begin{pmatrix} 0&1&2&3\\ 1&0&3&2\\ 2&3&0&1\\ 3&2&1&0 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{135.21} \]

Theorem 3. These patterns have identical response-value histograms in every spatial row and in every address column, but \(\delta_T\in\mathcal W_+\) whereas \(\delta_X\notin\mathcal W_+\cup\mathcal W_-\).

Proof. Every row and every column of both matrices contains each label \(0,1,2,3\) exactly once. The first matrix satisfies \(\delta_T(j+1,n+1)=\delta_T(j,n)\). In the second, stage \(n=1\) has row \((1,0,3,2)\), which is not any cyclic shift of row \(n=0\), so it cannot be generated by either fixed one-address diagonal translation. \(\square\)

Thus complete spatial–recurrence incidence is additional count structure beyond the separate marginal multiplicities and recurrence frequencies. In R²D language, one cannot infer the complete enclosing compatibility from independent subordinate count summaries.

135.9. Theorem 4 — Symmetry does not select traveling possibility

Let spatial orientation reversal act on patterns by

\[ (\mathsf R\delta)(j,n)=\delta(-j,n). \tag{135.22} \]

Then \(\mathsf R\) interchanges \(\mathcal W_+\) and \(\mathcal W_-\), while preserving \(\mathcal S\). Stage rotation, cyclic reference offsets, and reversal of cyclic response labels also preserve the respective families.

Theorem 4 (nonselection). The primitive finite boundary/count architecture, positive orientation-balanced candidate turbine recurrence, and the symmetries just named do not uniquely require either (i) \(\mathcal C_J\subseteq\mathcal W_+\cup\mathcal W_-\) or (ii) exclusion of \(\mathcal S\).

Proof by countermodel. On the same \(\mathcal P\), define two possible enclosing owners:

\[ \mathcal C_{J_1}=\mathcal S, \qquad \mathcal C_{J_2}=\mathcal W_+\cup\mathcal W_-. \tag{135.23} \]

Both are nonempty, invariant under spatial orientation reversal and recurrence-stage rotation, and may be equipped with the same symmetric candidate complete-turbine signatures and subordinate possible-realization fibers. \(J_1\) admits nonconstant stationary patterns, and \(J_2\) admits nonconstant translating patterns. Neither choice violates the count definitions or symmetry. Therefore those common premises cannot logically select just one of the two compatibility domains. \(\square\)

The point is not that one owner is physically correct. The theorem establishes the nonuniqueness of admissible compatibility specifications under the stated unitless assumptions. It forbids presenting the exclusion of nonpropagating patterns as already proved by symmetric recurrence alone.

135.10. Theorem 5 — Exact condition for excluding a pattern class

Let \(\mathcal K\subseteq\mathcal P\) be an independently specified receiving-boundary response class, and let \(\mathcal N\subseteq\mathcal P\) denote some specified pattern family excluded by the intended comparison (for instance, the complement of the two diagonal-translation classes).

Theorem 5 (admissibility exclusion). The enclosing owner excludes every complete pattern of class \(\mathcal N\) within \(\mathcal K\) if and only if

\[ \boxed{\mathcal C_J\cap\mathcal K\cap\mathcal N=\varnothing,} \tag{135.24} \]

or equivalently

\[ \boxed{W_J^{\mathrm{pat}}(\mathcal K\cap\mathcal N)=0.} \tag{135.25} \]

Proof. By definition (135.7), the cardinality is zero exactly when its underlying admitted pattern subset is empty. \(\square\)

This theorem is a precise exclusion criterion, not a derivation of the excluded set. Declaring \(\mathcal K\) to mean “propagating” and then obtaining (135.25) merely restates the definition. A substantive R²D theorem must derive (135.25) from independently given enclosing structural requirements that do not encode the desired wave rule in advance.

Even if (135.25) is established, it says nothing about the realized frequency or occupancy of \(\mathcal K\). A distinct realized count \(\nu_J(\mathcal K)\) would require a specified realization domain and an independent realization rule or observation.

135.11. Theorem 6 — What recursive replacement preserves

Let \(\mathcal H_J\) be a set of complete possible enclosing records with a readable response map

\[ q:\mathcal H_J\longrightarrow\mathcal D, \tag{135.26} \]

where \(\mathcal D\) is a receiving-boundary pattern domain. Subordinate beat or presentation details may differ among records in one \(q\)-fiber.

Theorem 6 (fiber-constancy criterion). A property \(I:\mathcal H_J\to\mathcal V\) survives as a well-defined property of the receiving read if and only if it is constant on each fiber of \(q\):

\[ q(h)=q(h')\Longrightarrow I(h)=I(h'). \tag{135.27} \]

Proof. If \(I=\overline I\circ q\), equal reads must have equal values. Conversely, fiber constancy permits \(\overline I(q(h)):=I(h)\) independently of the representative. \(\square\)

This is the general factorization criterion already developed in Addenda 120, 122, and 132, specialized here to possible pattern-incidence classification.

If \(q(\delta,\eta)=\delta\), a property such as \(\delta\in\mathcal W_+\) survives even when all the admitted subordinate labels \(\eta\) become unreadable. However, if \(q\) retains only row and column histograms, the two patterns in Theorem 3 have the same read and disagree about membership in \(\mathcal W_+\). The traveling-incidence property therefore does not survive that poorer read.

Thus loss of subordinate identity and preservation of complete spatial–recurrence incidence are compatible—but one does not automatically guarantee the other. Which property survives depends on the receiving boundary’s native classification.

135.12. What reversal symmetry actually counts

Suppose an enclosing admissible domain \(\mathcal C_J\) is closed under \(\mathsf R\). Then \(\mathsf R\) gives a bijection

\[ \mathcal C_J\cap\mathcal W_+ \longleftrightarrow \mathcal C_J\cap\mathcal W_-. \tag{135.28} \]

Hence

\[ \boxed{|\mathcal C_J\cap\mathcal W_+| =|\mathcal C_J\cap\mathcal W_-|.} \tag{135.29} \]

This is an equality of possible supported pattern counts. It does not imply equal realized propagation orientations. Furthermore, patterns in \(\mathcal W_+\cap\mathcal W_-\) belong to both sets; if the desired classes must be exclusive, their orientation tags or shared-pattern equivalences must be handled explicitly. The actual physical recurrence counts \(N_\pm\) of a realized turbine are separate again.

The enclosing symmetry governs which possibilities occur in matching families. It does not choose a realization or supply a physical rate.

135.13. Native classification versus physical names

The mathematical sets \(\mathcal S\), \(\mathcal W_+\), and \(\mathcal W_-\) are incidence classes of complete possible patterns. Calling \(\mathcal W_\pm\) “propagating” is a convenient interpretation of their diagonal-shift relation, not a claim that a physical disturbance has been observed.

Likewise, a paired cyclic response coordinate is not yet an electromagnetic gauge field; a phase label is not yet a frequency in hertz; an address is not yet a meter; a two-sided recurrence is not yet a photon; and an admitted pattern has no physical propagation speed or energy before a separately justified physical mapping.

The count framework also need not exclude stationary or standing electromagnetic solutions in order to explain radiative propagation. It needs to classify their admissibility correctly. Defining a radiative subclass is legitimate; assuming its exclusivity without a prior enclosing compatibility is not.

There is no reason in Part I to identify the realized background scalar \(b_J\) with \(|\mathcal P|\), \(|\mathcal C_J|\), or a particular admissible recurrence count. Possible multiplicity and realized background remain different count types.

135.14. Relation to the subsequent physical program

Part II. Thermodynamic conjugate coordinates may map some admitted patterns to pressure–density responses or other mechanical reads. These are downstream calibrations of a specified compatible landscape, not native names for \(\mathcal S\) or \(\mathcal W_\sigma\).

Part III. At the proposed quantum horizon, a response-incidence classification may survive despite loss of finer subordinate beat histories. At the gravitational horizon, a distinguishability class may survive while its enclosing recurrence comparison becomes unreadable. Neither horizon automatically selects the admitted subset \(\mathcal C_J\).

Part IV and Addenda 133–134. An electromagnetic physical map could associate suitable admitted patterns with field modes and Doppler reciprocity. A gravitational physical map could associate retained distinctions with mass-bearing energetic reads. This addendum derives neither \(c\) nor \(mc^2\), and does not attempt to explain why they possess different physical forms. It establishes part of the unitless admissibility layer needed before that question is posed.

No primitive assumption that counts themselves move is required. A complete pattern can possess a translating incidence relation without containing the identity of a traveling constituent. What actually propagates, at what speed, and with what measurable energy are independent physical realization and calibration questions.

135.15. Results and remaining derivation

Established for the declared finite mathematical structures:

  1. The coupled cyclic presentation admits an invariant relative-response pattern, without counting reference-representation duplicates as native microstates.
  2. Enclosing possibility is a specified compatible subset of complete patterns; readable pattern count, finer compatible multiplicity, and realized occurrence are distinct.
  3. Diagonal-translation incidence is characterized exactly by a finite shift-invariance relation (Theorem 1).
  4. The traveling-incidence class sizes and overlaps are calculable without physical units (Theorem 2).
  5. Equal spatial and recurrence marginals do not determine complete incidence (Theorem 3).
  6. Symmetric recurrence and reversal-invariant enclosing possibility do not uniquely select propagating alternatives (Theorem 4).
  7. Exact exclusion is equivalent to zero admitted possible count in the selected complete class (Theorem 5).
  8. A complete incidence property survives recursive replacement precisely when it is constant on the receiving read fibers (Theorem 6).
  9. Reversal invariance pairs possible orientations, not necessarily physically realized directional counts.

Not established: That Part I forces cyclic response pairs, a particular number of address directions, a nontrivial gauge or phase group, an exclusive propagating subclass, a unique physical clock, electromagnetic propagation, or any mass-bearing energetic read. No theorem here establishes how many compatible patterns nature realizes.

Next mathematical target. Given a specified unitless enclosing owner, can one derive a nontrivial restriction on its complete spatial–recurrence incidence from independent native-identification and compatibility conditions—without assuming that the admitted response is already a traveling wave? The resulting theorem should return exact admitted sets or zero/nonzero possible counts, not a claimed physical occurrence count.

Conclusion

R²D’s first obligation is to define what is countable and compatible. That includes entire symmetric recurrence-response patterns, not merely separate state multiplicities or rates. Complete spatial–recurrence incidence is a genuine unitless classification: it can distinguish stationary, translating, and other relations even when all their separate marginal recurrence counts agree.

The enclosure can permit reciprocal translating patterns without selecting their realized orientation and can preserve their complete relative phase read without preserving any subordinate beat identity. But the general Part I count axioms do not require those translating patterns to be the only possibilities.

The mathematically correct next step is therefore admissibility before realization:

\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{native distinguishability and reciprocal response classes}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{complete enclosing compatible-pattern domain }\mathcal C_J\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{exact possible counts and admissible incidence invariants}\\ \Downarrow\\ \text{read-factorization under recursive replacement}\\ \Downarrow\quad\text{(independent physical mapping)}\\ \text{thermodynamic, quantum, or gravitational physical reads}. \end{gathered}} \tag{135.30} \]

This addendum supplies an explicit finite beginning to the missing unitless layer between the primitive count architecture and the physical readability horizons. It is an author-approved extension, not a change to Part I’s axioms.
