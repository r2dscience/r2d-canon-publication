# R²D Addendum 132 — The Invariant That Survives Two Horizons

*Common Enclosing Compatibility, Complementary Readability, and the Quantum–Gravitational Calibration Problem*

**Status:** Author-approved for canon publication — November 20, 2026  
**Canonical basis:** R²D Parts I–IV (September 25, 2026); Addenda 69, 105, 112, 120, 122, 124–125, and companion Addendum 131.  
**Scope:** Count-level common invariants of complementary horizon projections; finite compatibility tests; the limits of physical frequency and geometry reads; unresolved origin of a universal $c$.

## Abstract

Addendum 131 distinguished orientation-even recurrence of one turbine from orientation-odd, net directional cycling. A symmetric enclosing compatibility relation can admit completed local returns with no net directional output; an asymmetric relation can bias the same turbine. When a complete enclosing record fixes the number of compatible returns relative to a separately defined background recurrence, it also fixes a dimensionless recurrence ratio. These results do not require primitive molecular trajectories, physical time, or kinetic-rate constants. They do not, however, establish that scalar background count alone uniquely supplies realized recurrence or a physical clock.

R²D Part III identifies complementary horizons of recursive readability. At the quantum horizon, a recurrence-side read may survive while its continuously resolved subordinate distinction is unavailable. At the gravitational horizon, a spatial or geometric distinction may survive while the relevant *enclosing organizing recurrence* is unavailable. Background may remain a scale and frame reference on both sides. These claims invite a sharper question: which invariant of one enclosing recurrence relation can be recovered consistently from both surviving projections?

We formulate this question over a **common enclosing-owned domain of complete comparison records**, without assuming that a persistent subordinate object travels through different native count domains. For a candidate dimensionless invariant $I_J$, we establish the exact necessary and sufficient condition for its factorization through both quantum and gravitational read maps. A bipartite compatibility graph then supplies the maximal common classification: its connected components. Finite examples demonstrate both successful preservation and a precise obstruction when a gravitational read merges records with different recurrence ratios. The common classification is a mathematical result; identifying an actual quantum–gravitational physical comparison satisfying its hypotheses is an additional empirical and theoretical task.

Finally, we prove that preservation of a dimensionless count invariant does not determine a dimensional propagation speed. The identification of electromagnetic $c$ with the calibration appearing in $E_0=mc^2$ requires an independently established joint spatial–recurrence mapping, frame-invariant physical calibration, and verification of the relevant boundary-native observable classes. The shared-background interpretation is a research hypothesis, not a theorem of Part I.

## 132.1. The problem after symmetric recurrence

The motivating causal proposal is one turbine under one enclosing organization. A symmetric enclosing relation supports reciprocal realization; a maintained asymmetric relation can organize net oriented cycling. Temperature is a *physical thermodynamic read* of thermal compatibility, not a primitive molecular energy assigned before the count boundary. Addendum 131 formalized the count distinction as

$$
N_{\mathrm{even}}=N_++N_-,\qquad
N_{\mathrm{odd}}=N_+-N_-.
\tag{132.1}
$$

The first may be positive when the second is zero. For a complete record $h$ with a separately counted positive enclosing background recurrence $M(h)=N_J^\Phi(h)$, define the candidate background-relative recurrence ratio

$$
\boxed{\chi_J(h)=\frac{N_{\mathrm{even}}(h)}{M(h)}.}
\tag{132.2}
$$

This is a ratio of realized *whole-closure counts* to an independently specified background recurrence count. It is not a ratio of one-time state multiplicities, and $M$ is not the scalar realized background count $b_J$ appearing in $\Phi_J=\ln b_J$.

For a given complete enclosing relation, $\chi_J$ is fixed across its admitted realized records precisely when its numerator and denominator have one common ratio. This was the finite specification theorem of Addendum 131. The present addendum asks a different question: **if this count ratio is defined, can two complementary horizon readings retain it without reconstructing the distinctions they respectively lose?**

## 132.2. What the two horizons do and do not read

Part III assigns the quantum and gravitational horizons opposite directions in the recursive hierarchy. The quantum-side limit retains a relevant recurrence read while its associated subordinate distinction is no longer jointly recoverable. The gravitational-side limit retains spatial/geometric distinguishability while its relevant *recursively enclosing directed recurrence* is not jointly accessible.

This does **not** mean that all geometric measurements vanish in quantum experiments. Source and detector positions can still be read at their own enclosing boundaries. Nor does it mean that planetary periods or other local gravitational recurrences cannot be measured. The unreadability statement refers to a **specified native recursive relation**, not to every experimental coordinate called position or time.

Likewise, Part III distinguishes background recurrence from the missing directed enclosing recurrence. Background can remain a scale and frame reference at either horizon. Its physical frequency calibration is not the count $b_J$ itself, and an observed spectral transition frequency is not automatically a rate of completed structural returns.

Thus the proposed joint bridge is not a trajectory connecting a quantum particle to a moving astronomical mass. It is a claim that two boundary-relative physical projections are compatible reads of one enclosing organization.

## 132.3. Common enclosing comparison domain

Let $J$ be a **specified common comparison owner**. Let

$$
\mathcal H_J
$$

be its finite admitted domain of complete comparison records. Each record carries any enclosing context necessary to decide compatibility and the recurrence counts required by the claim. A record may represent a fully specified admissible possible complete realization; an assertion about the observed physical frequency additionally requires an identified realized class. Possible and realized records are not interchangeable.

Define two horizon-reading maps on this common domain:

$$
q_Q:\mathcal H_J\longrightarrow\mathcal Q,
\qquad
q_G:\mathcal H_J\longrightarrow\mathcal G.
\tag{132.3}
$$

$\mathcal Q$ classifies the *surviving quantum-side read*, provisionally a recurrence or spectral-response class. $\mathcal G$ classifies the *surviving gravitational-side read*, provisionally a spatial or geometric distinction class. These labels belong to their respective native observing boundaries; they are not the original $J$-native microstates relabeled and carried intact across scales.

The maps are comparison functions **only after** a common enclosing owner has supplied the complete relation. Where a physical comparison is intrinsically relational or multivalued, one may instead retain a tagged incidence domain of full comparison records and use its two readable projection maps. This adds no persistent cross-boundary object identity.

A claim that independent quantum and gravitational experiments share this owner is **not automatically licensed** by their having similar equations or matching numbers. That claim itself requires an explicit enclosing compatibility relation, consistent native identification, and admissible observed read classes. This preserves Addenda 120 and 122.

## 132.4. Theorem 1 — Necessary and sufficient factorization through both horizon reads

Let $I_J:\mathcal H_J\to\mathcal V$ be any candidate enclosing count invariant, such as $\chi_J$ in (132.2), with the relevant denominators positive. We seek functions

$$
I_Q:q_Q(\mathcal H_J)\to\mathcal V,
\qquad
I_G:q_G(\mathcal H_J)\to\mathcal V
$$

for which

$$
\boxed{I_J=I_Q\circ q_Q=I_G\circ q_G.}
\tag{132.4}
$$

**Theorem 1 (common-read factorization).** Such $I_Q$ and $I_G$ exist if and only if

$$
q_Q(h)=q_Q(h')\ \Longrightarrow\ I_J(h)=I_J(h')
\tag{132.5}
$$

and

$$
q_G(h)=q_G(h')\ \Longrightarrow\ I_J(h)=I_J(h')
\tag{132.6}
$$

for every pair $h,h'\in\mathcal H_J$.

**Proof.** If (132.4) holds, two records with the same $q_Q$ label necessarily have the same $I_Q$ value, and similarly for $q_G$; hence (132.5)–(132.6). Conversely, if (132.5) holds, define $I_Q(q_Q(h))=I_J(h)$; fiber constancy makes this assignment independent of the chosen representative. Apply the same construction using (132.6) to obtain $I_G$. Both identities follow. $\square$

This is the familiar finite-set factorization criterion used in Addenda 120 and 122, applied here to *simultaneous quantum and gravitational readability*. It is not claimed as a newly discovered abstract mathematics theorem.

**Consequence.** A nontrivial value of $I_J$ can survive both horizon reads even though neither horizon reconstructs the entire complete enclosing record. Faithful preservation of the **selected invariant** is weaker than reconstruction of every native count or complete trajectory.

## 132.5. The common compatibility graph

The enclosing owner determines the joint compatibility relation

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma_{QG}
=\{(q_Q(h),q_G(h)):h\in\mathcal H_J\}
\subseteq\mathcal Q\times\mathcal G.
}
\tag{132.7}
$$

Regard $\Gamma_{QG}$ as a bipartite graph. Quantum-readable classes form the left vertices, gravitational-readable classes the right vertices, and each admitted pair supplies an edge. Use only vertices actually reached by an admitted comparison; unreachable labels do not contribute information.

This graph is *not* a new physical coupling postulated between particles and masses. It is a way of auditing an independently specified complete enclosing comparison. If $\mathcal H_J$ contains multiple records with the same pair, preserve their separate records and native weights wherever multiplicity matters; the simple graph only addresses the common **classification** problem.

## 132.6. Theorem 2 — The maximal common horizon classification

Define an equivalence relation on $\mathcal H_J$ generated by two elementary identifications:

$$
h\sim_Q h'\iff q_Q(h)=q_Q(h'),
\qquad
h\sim_G h'\iff q_G(h)=q_G(h').
$$

Let $\approx$ be their smallest joint equivalence closure (generated by finite chains of $\sim_Q$ and $\sim_G$). Two records are $\approx$-equivalent exactly when their read-pairs lie in the same connected component of $\Gamma_{QG}$.

**Theorem 2 (maximal common classification).** The quotient

$$
\boxed{\mathcal I_{QG}=\mathcal H_J/\!\approx}
\tag{132.8}
$$

is the most informative classification that factors through **both** horizon readings. Equivalently, its classes correspond to the connected components of the joint compatibility graph. Every scalar common invariant $I_J$ satisfying Theorem 1 is constant on those components and therefore factors through $\mathcal I_{QG}$.

**Proof.** If an invariant factors through both read maps, its value is constant along every $q_Q$ fiber and every $q_G$ fiber by Theorem 1. It is therefore constant along any chain of such identifications, hence on every $\approx$ class. Conversely, the quotient map $h\mapsto[h]_{\approx}$ is constant on each $q_Q$ fiber and each $q_G$ fiber; by Theorem 1 it factors through both. Any other common invariant is a function of this quotient. $\square$

**Qualification.** “Maximal” means **most informative common quotient**, unique up to relabeling. It does not single out a unique physical scalar or a unique numerical value of $c$. A graph with one connected component admits only a constant scalar common invariant, which has no ability to distinguish different enclosing recurrence classes.

## 132.7. Finite positive example — A nontrivial invariant survives

Take four distinct complete enclosing comparison records $h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4$. Their realized-cycle and background-reference counts are already fixed by their declared complete records. Let $M(h)=12$ for all records, and $N_{\mathrm{even}}(h_1)=N_{\mathrm{even}}(h_2)=4$ and $N_{\mathrm{even}}(h_3)=N_{\mathrm{even}}(h_4)=2$.

Hence

$$
\chi_J(h_1)=\chi_J(h_2)=\frac13,
\qquad
\chi_J(h_3)=\chi_J(h_4)=\frac16.
$$

Specify the independent horizon classifications:

| Complete record | $q_Q$ | $q_G$ | $\chi_J$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $h_1$ | $q_1$ | $g_1$ | $1/3$ |
| $h_2$ | $q_1$ | $g_1$ | $1/3$ |
| $h_3$ | $q_2$ | $g_2$ | $1/6$ |
| $h_4$ | $q_2$ | $g_2$ | $1/6$ |

The compatibility graph has exactly two components:

$$
q_1\text{—}g_1,
\qquad
q_2\text{—}g_2.
$$

The nonconstant invariant $\chi_J$ is constant on every fiber of both projections. Hence

$$
\chi_J=\chi_Q\circ q_Q=\chi_G\circ q_G,
$$

with $\chi_Q(q_1)=\chi_G(g_1)=1/3$ and $\chi_Q(q_2)=\chi_G(g_2)=1/6$.

**Result.** Both complementary projections can retain a shared *dimensionless* recurrence classification even though neither supplies the full enclosing record and neither carries a persistent native microstate identity across boundaries.

The labels $q_1,q_2$ are candidate recurrence-read classes and $g_1,g_2$ candidate geometric-read classes. Their pairing is *specified*, not yet derived from real blackbody, atomic, gravitational, or horizon observations.

## 132.8. Finite obstruction — Geometry merges distinct recurrence classes

Now let three admitted complete records have:

| Record | $q_Q$ | $q_G$ | $\chi_J$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $u_1$ | $q_1$ | $g_1$ | $1/3$ |
| $u_2$ | $q_1$ | $g_2$ | $1/3$ |
| $u_3$ | $q_2$ | $g_2$ | $1/6$ |

The quantum-side reading distinguishes the two recurrence classes, so $\chi_J$ factors through $q_Q$.

But the gravitational-side read maps both $u_2$ and $u_3$ to $g_2$ despite their unequal $\chi_J$. Therefore (132.6) fails:

$$
q_G(u_2)=q_G(u_3),
\qquad
\chi_J(u_2)\ne\chi_J(u_3).
$$

The compatibility graph $q_1\text{—}g_1$, $q_1\text{—}g_2$, $q_2\text{—}g_2$ is connected. The only common scalar classifications are constant.

**Result.** A surviving quantum frequency or recurrence distinction does not automatically have a faithful gravitational geometric image. A single geometric label may be compatible with multiple different enclosing recurrence classes.

This obstruction cannot be repaired by asserting that all measurements are different views of one oscillator. It requires a more discriminating enclosing-compatible geometric read, a restricted physical domain, or a different proposed invariant. These are physical requirements, not mathematical relabeling.

## 132.9. Relation to symmetric bias and the leapfrog

The orientation-even count of Addendum 131 obeys

$$
N_{\mathrm{even}}(\sigma h)=N_{\mathrm{even}}(h)
$$

under orientation reversal $\sigma$, provided reversal exchanges complete-cycle orientations and preserves the background reference count. Thus the ratio $\chi_J$ is itself orientation-reversal invariant:

$$
\boxed{\chi_J(\sigma h)=\chi_J(h).}
\tag{132.9}
$$

That result is distinct from Theorems 1–2. Orientation-even recurrence need not survive **both horizon projections** merely because it survives reversal of the turbine's cycle direction.

The proposed leapfrog is an ordering of explanatory ownership:

$$
\text{enclosing compatibility}
\Longrightarrow\text{compatible turbine realization}
\xrightarrow{\text{recursive read}}\text{new count meaning}.
$$

The first arrow is not a hidden stochastic selector, and the second is not reverse bottom-up causation. Across the horizons, the native count domains differ. The joint owner must classify the compatible readings before either physical representation is compared.

The stronger physical claim—that one symmetric cosmic or thermal background *causes* the observed quantum frequency and gravitational mass dynamics—requires an independently grounded common enclosing relation. The factorization theorems determine what such a claim would have to preserve; they do not establish that the observed systems possess the hypothesized joint owner.

## 132.10. What is actually observed on each side?

**Quantum-side read.** An experiment can report a spectral frequency, correlation, or time-dependent response while the corresponding continuously resolved subordinate motion is unavailable. R²D does not equate an IR energy-gap frequency $\Delta E/h$ with the rate of structural transitions or with a count of complete turbine returns. To claim that a measured $q_Q$ faithfully reads $\chi_J$, one must specify the quantum-side native recurrence class and show that the measured response preserves the claimed classification. Addendum 69 already distinguishes thermal scale, spectral recurrence, and transition rate.

**Gravitational-side read.** One may measure spatial relations, moving masses, or an orbital period at an accessible gravitational boundary. The gravitational horizon does not erase all clocks or all orbital recurrence. It concerns the *selected enclosing organizing recurrence*, which may not be jointly readable with the remaining geometric distinctions. A measured geometric class $g$ therefore need not fix $\chi_J$; the obstruction in Section 132.8 makes that limitation exact.

**Background.** Part III says background can remain a scale and frame reference at either horizon. Its scalar count $b_J$, its recurrence $N_J^\Phi$, and a physical thermal frequency equivalent $k_BT/h$ are distinct. Their identification or calibration must be proved or measured, not inserted into the count theorem.

No uniqueness of the hidden structural history follows from a correct spectral or geometric fit. Conversely, loss of a trajectory read is not evidence that no subordinate physical realization occurs.

## 132.11. The shared calibration problem and a no-go theorem for $c$

Suppose a common dimensionless count invariant survives both horizon projections. Does this establish a universal physical speed?

No. Let an admitted enclosing propagation comparison receive an independently justified physical spatial calibration $\ell_J>0$ per specified spatial distinction and physical clock calibration $\tau_J>0$ per specified recurrence reference. If its dimensionless propagation quotient is $s/m$, its speed read is

$$
v_J=\frac{\ell_J}{\tau_J}\frac{s}{m}.
\tag{132.10}
$$

**Theorem 3 (dimensional calibration nonuniqueness).** Preservation of the complete *dimensionless* compatibility relation, including any common scalar recurrence invariant, does not by itself determine the dimensional value of $v_J$ or a universal finite $c$.

**Proof.** Replace one proposed physical realization by another with $\ell_J'=a\ell_J$ and $\tau_J'=b\tau_J$ for arbitrary $a,b>0$, keeping all dimensionless native counts, full compatibility pairs, horizon labels, and count invariants fixed. Then

$$
v_J'=\frac{a}{b}v_J.
$$

These are different candidate physical calibrations compared in one fixed measurement-unit convention; no primitive dimensionless count relation distinguishes them. Thus the common dimensionless invariant alone does not select a unique dimensional speed. $\square$

The theorem does not imply that measured Maxwellian or relativistic phenomena admit arbitrary $c$. It says **R²D's primitive count specification alone** does not choose their physical conversion scale.

Part IV writes a candidate background propagation calibration

$$
c_\Phi=\ell_\Phi f_\Phi.
$$

To prove that the *same* invariant physical $c$ organizes electromagnetic radiation and the $E_0=mc^2$ mass-energy representation, one must independently establish: (i) a legitimate joint background-owned spatial–recurrence relation; (ii) coherence of its calibrated product across the appropriate boundaries; (iii) invariance under admissible inertial-frame comparisons rather than merely scaling across recursive boundaries; and (iv) correct EM propagation and mass-bearing mechanical identifications.

If any physical map uses $\ell=c/f$ or $E_0=mc^2$ as its *definition* of the projected background, it has imported $c$ and cannot be presented as a derivation of $c$ from the enclosing counts.

The cosmic microwave background can supply an observable cosmological rest frame, but that alone does not make its temperature the source of the local invariant $c$. Whether it is a physical read of a much larger recursively organizing background remains a separate R²D hypothesis.

## 132.12. Exact tests needed for a physical quantum–gravitational bridge

A candidate physical construction must declare, independently of the result it wants:

1. **One common owner:** an enclosing compatibility and native-identification specification relating the two physical read domains, with no assumed persistent subordinate object identity.
2. **One precise symmetric recurrence class:** an enclosingly compatible, completed whole-closure count and an independently identified background recurrence count. Its value may be one count invariant among others.
3. **A faithful quantum read:** a measured recurrence or spectral-response class $q_Q$ justified as preserving the specific count invariant; a spectral energy difference alone does not certify a completed-return count.
4. **A faithful gravitational read:** an independently classified geometric response $q_G$ whose fibers do not merge incompatible invariant values; motion of masses does not automatically expose the relevant enclosing recurrence.
5. **Correct complete pairing:** the observed or derived $\Gamma_{QG}$ must preserve enclosing admissibility; joining partial descriptions must not introduce spurious pairs (Addendum 122).
6. **Independent physical calibration:** spatial and clock standards, and their required invariances, must be established without using the desired $c$ as a premise.

The first computational audit is finite: enumerate $\mathcal H_J$, compute $q_Q$, $q_G$, and candidate $I_J$, form $\Gamma_{QG}$, find connected components, and test constancy of $I_J$ on each component. This is a **mathematical compatibility test**; its physical significance depends on grounding the proposed read maps in independently observed systems.

## 132.13. Results and limits

**Established, conditional on a specified finite enclosing owner:**

- A candidate enclosing count invariant survives a quantum and gravitational projection simultaneously if and only if it is constant on the fibers of both (Theorem 1).
- The maximal common classification is the quotient by the equivalence relation generated by both horizon-read fibers, equivalently the connected-component classification of the joint compatibility graph (Theorem 2).
- Exact finite examples show both nontrivial shared recurrence classification and its obstruction when a surviving geometric read merges distinct recurrence ratios.
- Orientation-even recurrence is preserved under reversal of turbine orientation, but reversal invariance alone is not cross-horizon preservation.
- A shared dimensionless count invariant cannot by itself fix a universal dimensional speed (Theorem 3).

**Existing canonical basis:** Part III proposes opposite limits of joint readability and surviving background scale/frame references. Part IV supplies conditional electromagnetic, relativistic, and gravitational physical calibrations. Addenda 120 and 122 supply the underlying general factorization and enclosing-compatibility tools. The present mathematical theorems specialize those tools to a proposed *common two-horizon invariant*; they are not new primitive axioms.

**Not established:** a unique physical common enclosing owner of quantum EM recurrence and gravitational geometry; $I_J=\chi_J$ for any specific physical pair of horizon observations; equality of spectral frequency and structural closure rate; $f_J^\Phi=k_BT/h$; the reciprocity of physical clock and ruler scales; a universal finite $c$ derived from dimensionless counts; or the identification of CMB temperature with the origin of the invariant $c$.

## Conclusion

Addendum 131 established a finite count language for the orientation-even recurrence of one turbine, separate from its net oriented output. The present addendum asks which part of that enclosing organization can survive when quantum and gravitational observations retain opposite sides of the jointly readable thermal relation.

The exact answer is **the common factor of the two boundary-owned read maps**. It is computed from the enclosing-compatible joint relation, not inferred from a shared microscopic oscillator or a gravitationally moving object. The orientation-even recurrence ratio is one candidate invariant, but it survives both horizon projections only if neither projection merges different values of that ratio.

R²D's proposed common background therefore has a precise mathematical target: construct one *physically grounded* enclosing comparison whose quantum recurrence and gravitational geometric reads possess a nontrivial shared count invariant. The existence of such an invariant would establish count-level coherence across the two horizons. It would **not yet establish** the physical conversion that makes electromagnetic propagation and rest mass share one universal $c$.

**The next open problem is the construction of the smallest physically admissible common quantum–gravitational compatibility owner, followed by a separate proof or empirical calibration of its joint spatial–recurrence invariant.**
