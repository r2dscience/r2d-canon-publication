## Abstract

Recursively Realized Distinguishability (R²D) defines an enclosing boundary, its distinguishable macrostates, and its compatible possible subordinate realizations before any particular realization is occupied. A macrostate transition is therefore not constructed by a collection of independently acting subordinate transitions. The enclosing transition defines the class of subordinate realizations compatible with it.

At the quantum readability horizon, subordinate geometric distinctions are no longer readable as persistent state identities at the enclosing quantum boundary, while recurrence remains readable. Quantum theory consequently cannot be interpreted, within R²D, as a direct observation of the subordinate objects or trajectories presumed to explain the enclosing quantum occurrence. Such objects, when projected beneath the readability horizon, are imagined physical representations rather than independently readable primitive states.

This does not invalidate quantum physical laws within the boundaries where their quantities are defined and experimentally tested. It establishes a limit on what those laws demonstrate about subordinate ontology. R²D cannot reconstruct an unreadable subordinate mechanism from the enclosing observation, nor can it derive the quantitative laws of quantum physics by assigning causal agency to imagined subordinate objects.

This addendum consolidates a four-way consistency result. Interference, measurement, entanglement, and relativistic propagation admit mutually compatible physical representations of the same enclosing-first recursive architecture. Interference is a phase-dependent read of enclosing recurrence. Measurement realizes a new count classification through recursive replacement. Entanglement is the nonseparable representation of a prior joint enclosing relation. Relativistic propagation is a calibrated physical read within a receiving spacetime boundary.

These four readings need not describe one simultaneously occupied count domain. They belong to distinguishable boundaries and physical operations, all governed by the same principle: the enclosing relation defines compatibility before subordinate realization.

The purpose is not to derive quantum or relativistic laws. It is to demonstrate their conditional consistency with R²D's existing count definitions, replacement ontology, and enclosing-first causal ordering, while specifying what remains beyond the information available at the quantum horizon.

## 101.1. The Limit of Quantum Explanation

Quantum physics successfully predicts experimentally readable relations among quantum states, detector outcomes, recurrence frequencies, electromagnetic fields, and other calibrated physical quantities.

These successful physical predictions do not, by themselves, establish the primitive existence of the subordinate objects often imagined to explain them.

R²D begins with a different ontology.

At a selected count boundary $B_n$, distinguishable macrostates are defined by the boundary. Their multiplicities count possible compatible subordinate realizations:

$$
\boxed{
W_{n,i}=|\pi_n^{-1}(i)|.
}
$$

A realized macrostate replaces subordinate state identities as the operative occupied classification.

The subordinate configurations possess microstate meaning relative to the enclosing boundary, but their individual distinctions are not simultaneously readable as independently occupied states at that boundary.

Changing resolution changes the count domain.

The replacement is physical in the R²D ontology, not merely a change in an observer's information.

Consequently, a physical description of a subordinate object cannot simply be projected beneath an observed enclosing quantum state while retaining its identity as an independently occupied primitive constituent.

At the quantum readability horizon,

$$
\boxed{
(\lambda_Q,\nu_Q)=(U,R),
}
$$

where subordinate continuous distinguishability is unreadable and recurrence remains readable.

The unresolved subordinate realizations remain possible. Their specific state identities cannot be reconstructed as persistent objects from the enclosing quantum readout.

R²D therefore distinguishes three things:

1. A mathematically defined class of possible subordinate realizations.
2. A physically realized enclosing quantum state or occurrence.
3. An imagined subordinate object introduced to explain that occurrence.

The first two belong to the R²D count architecture.

The third is an interpretation that exceeds the readable evidence when it assigns persistent primitive identity to the replaced subordinate state.

A physical object may be real and readable at its own measurement boundary. What is not established is its continued identity as an independently occupied object beneath another realized enclosing classification.

The distinction is ontological, not a rejection of experimentally successful mathematics.

**R²D does not derive quantum laws by reconstructing subordinate objects. It asks whether quantum laws are consistent physical representations of enclosing relations whose subordinate realization structure is unreadable.**

## 101.4. Measurement Replaces the Occupied Count Boundary

Interference concerns a quantum relation before previously unresolved alternatives become separately readable in a new measurement classification.

Let $B_Q$ denote a quantum recurrence boundary and $B_D$ a detector boundary.

$$
\boxed{
B_M\Longrightarrow\{B_Q,B_D\}.
}
$$

The detector has its own mathematically defined macrostate landscape before measurement.

This is recursive replacement.

The measurement does not merely disclose which previously hidden subordinate trajectory was always occupied within the unchanged quantum state.

It requires consistency between an established quantum representation and realized R²D detector counts.

A conventional conditional quantum-state update follows the newly readable outcome. It is not a primitive wavefunction collapse that constructs that outcome.

**Measurement is a new boundary-defined realization, not recovery of an unchanged subordinate object.**

## 101.5. Entanglement Is the Read of a Joint Enclosing Boundary

Entanglement presents the same causal-ordering problem at a joint boundary.

Consider two locally distinguishable systems $A$ and $B$.

R²D does not begin with two independently complete primitive objects and subsequently seek a physical mechanism connecting them.

It begins with the enclosing compatibility relation

$$
B_{AB}.
$$

That relation defines the possible joint realization of its peers:

$$
\boxed{
B_{AB}\Longrightarrow\{B_A,B_B\}.
}
$$

The local classifications are boundary-relative reads of the enclosing joint relation.

In the conventional quantum projection, a joint state is represented by

$$
\rho_{AB}.
$$

Entanglement means that this state cannot be represented as a convex mixture of independently factorized local states:

$$
\rho_{AB}
\ne
\sum_k p_k
\rho_A^{(k)}\otimes\rho_B^{(k)}.
$$

The local reduced density operators,

$$
\rho_A=\operatorname{Tr}_B\rho_{AB},
\qquad
\rho_B=\operatorname{Tr}_A\rho_{AB},
$$

are incomplete local representations of the joint relation.

Their mathematical availability does not require independent primitive occupancy of the local state identities alongside the occupied joint classification.

This is the quantum counterpart of recursive replacement.

Joint statehood is defined before independently complete local descriptions.

The same quantum representation can satisfy no-signaling.

For complete local measurement classifications $\{E_i^{(a)}\}$ and $\{F_j^{(b)}\}$,

$$

\rho_{AB}

The local marginal does not depend on Bob's remote setting.

This is a consistency property of the adopted quantum representation.

It does not require a signal to traverse the spatial separation between two detectors, nor does it require independently predetermined subordinate outcomes beneath the joint state.

The enclosing relation owns the compatibility. The local detectors realize their own readable distinctions.

**Entanglement is consistent with R²D because the joint relation is defined before its separately readable local outcomes.**

## 101.8. What R²D Can and Cannot Establish

The logical distinction is essential.

### Established within the R²D count architecture

- State identity is indexed to its defining count boundary.
- An enclosing boundary defines the compatibility and multiplicity of its possible subordinate realizations.
- Mutually exclusive macrostate multiplicities add; compatible joint possibilities compose multiplicatively under the enclosing restriction.
- Recursive replacement changes the operative count identity.
- The enclosing occurrence is causally prior to the compatible subordinate realization class it defines.

### Established as conditional consistency

The existing R²D physical program admits representations compatible with interference, detector realization, quantum entanglement, boundary-preserving unitarity, and relativistic propagation.

### Not established, and not required for the present R²D objective

This addendum does not reconstruct the unreadable subordinate quantum realization structure.

It does not derive arbitrary quantum amplitudes, the Born rule, Hamiltonian dynamics, entanglement statistics, electromagnetic propagation, or relativistic field interactions from primitive counts alone.

It does not demonstrate that the recurrence-weighted R²D law, without additional physical conditions, uniquely enforces the localization of every spacelike-separated quantum measurement operation.

Nor does it claim that the observable success of quantum physics establishes the object ontology often assigned to its mathematical representations.

The inability to recover subordinate objects is not an invitation to invent more detailed hidden mechanisms.

It is the boundary-relative limit that R²D explicitly identifies.

The purpose of the quantum reconstruction program is therefore to establish compatibility between the count architecture and established physical readings, not to derive those readings from presumed subordinate physical objects.
