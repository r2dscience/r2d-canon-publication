## Preface
Part III begins where Part II ends.

Part I developed Recursively Realized Distinguishability before thermodynamics. It defined boundary-relative statehood, multiplicity, possibility, constraint, occurrence, occupancy, recurrence, closure, recursive realization, and background without assuming particles, trajectories, forces, fields, space, or time as primitive objects.

Part II then asked what those primitive count relations look like when they become physically readable through thermodynamics. Its central advantage was **joint readability**.

In an ordinary thermodynamic system, adjacent levels of recursive realization can sometimes be interrogated together. Local closure can be measured. Recurrence can acquire a physical rate. Distinguishability can acquire a mechanical or chemical coordinate. Occupancy, constraint, heat, work, and background can remain simultaneously accessible.

Muscle made this unusually explicit. Molecular-state occupancy can be measured. ATPase reports local recurrence. Force and shortening provide mechanical reads. Heat reports another part of the thermodynamic realization. Muscle therefore allowed Part II to compare adjacent recursive boundaries within one functioning physical system.

Thermodynamics is privileged in R²D for this reason. It is the regime in which recursive realization can be read from both sides at once. Part III begins when that privilege is lost. But before moving toward quantum physics or gravity, two consequences of the primitive R2D architecture must first be recognized. Neither belongs uniquely to either field.

The first is a **recursive clock**.

An enclosing occurrence is realized through multiple subordinate occurrences. Recursive replacement therefore compresses realized recurrence toward larger boundaries. Viewed from the subordinate side, one enclosing occurrence branches into many local realizations. Viewed toward the enclosing side, those many realizations acquire one larger-scale count meaning.

The consequence is structural: recursively enclosing closure recurs more slowly than the subordinate closure through which it is realized. Physical frequency does not create this hierarchy. Frequency is a physical read of a recurrence hierarchy that already exists in the count architecture.

The second consequence concerns **occupancy statistics**.

R²D determines occupancy through relative asymmetry. Once the allowed count domain is specified, familiar statistical forms follow. A binary occupancy domain gives the mathematical form conventionally called Fermi--Dirac statistics. A repeatable occupancy domain gives the Bose--Einstein form. In the sparse limit, both approach the Boltzmann form. These statistics therefore precede particle ontology.

A two-state molecular switch in muscle already possesses the same primitive binary occupancy architecture that later appears in quantum physics as a fermionic occupation law. The primitive distinction is not initially between a fermion and a boson. It is between different admissible ways of counting occupancy.

This changes the logical position of quantum statistics. R²D does not need quantum mechanics to obtain their basic count structure. Quantum physics instead supplies particular physical realizations of count laws that are already present beneath thermodynamics.

These two results organize Part III. **Across boundaries, recursive realization orders recurrence. Within boundaries, admissible count structure orders occupancy.** The physical domains that follow are then not used to invent these principles. They test and reveal them.

The quantum side begins where subordinate recurrence remains readable after its associated distinguishability can no longer be jointly resolved. Blackbody radiation provides the first physical bridge. Planck\'s relation connects a readable physical frequency to a thermal scale. The Bose--Einstein count structure does not originate with the blackbody; it has already followed from repeatable occupancy. The blackbody shows what that count structure looks like when it is read physically at the quantum horizon.

An atom provides the next step. Atomic states persist and spectral differences recur with extraordinary precision, yet no continuously traversed electron trajectory is required as a primitive object. An atomic orbit is therefore treated first as a bound recurrent closure. Its spectral structure provides a recurrence read after continuous subordinate geometry has disappeared from observation.

Measurement completes the quantum-side bridge. A measuring device is itself a thermal turbine. It does not recover the missing microscopic trajectory. It provides a new thermodynamic boundary within which quantum recurrence becomes consequential as a readable detector distinction. A detector can itself be a binary occupancy system. Quantum recurrence changes detector asymmetry; detector occupancy changes; a macroscopic distinction appears.

Measurement therefore returns one-sided quantum recurrence to thermodynamic distinguishability without requiring a special classical ontology or a primitive act of wavefunction collapse.

The gravitational development proceeds in the opposite recursive direction. Here distinguishability remains increasingly prominent while the relevant enclosing recurrence becomes progressively less accessible. The atmosphere provides the first bridge because the thermal reference is still visible. Temperature, occupancy, and mechanically organized spatial distinction can be read together. The atmosphere therefore remains close to the jointly readable thermodynamic regime.

Planetary closure moves farther from it. A planet returns. Its orbit closes. Its geometry is readable and its period remains measurable, but the larger thermodynamic recurrence relation organizing that closure is no longer part of the ordinary description.

For this reason, Part III begins gravity with Kepler rather than Newton. Kepler described the surviving read before gravitational mechanics supplied a projected cause. His first law gives the geometry of bound closure. His second law joins area to recurrence. His third law shows quantitatively that larger orbital closure recurs more slowly. R²D does not need Kepler\'s third law to invent the recursive clock. Recursive realization has already established the direction of recurrence compression. Kepler supplies the gravitational scaling law.

Moving still farther toward the enclosing side brings the black-hole event horizon. There, thermodynamics again appears before gravitational projection. Horizon entropy is read through area. Distinguishability remains available while the relevant enclosing directed recurrence is no longer accessible to the exterior observer.

The blackbody and black hole therefore occupy opposite limiting positions. At the blackbody horizon, recurrence remains readable while subordinate distinguishability becomes unavailable. At the black-hole horizon, distinguishability remains readable while enclosing recurrence becomes unavailable. Thermodynamics lies between them because both can be read together.

Their shared word *black* is historically accidental, but it is unexpectedly appropriate in R²D. In both cases, blackness marks absence of a direct read rather than absence of physical realization. The blackbody is a readable body whose subordinate side has gone dark. The black hole is a readable boundary around an enclosing relation that has gone dark. A black hole need not be a hole in reality. It is a hole in recursive readability.

Background is different from either horizon coordinate. Background is realized count after the relevant subordinate directional classification is no longer readable at the boundary where it is background. It is neither possibility nor lost information, and it is not a third directed horizon coordinate. Its nondirectionality is not an absence of physical role: it supplies the boundary-relative reference against which directional realization can be read.

Because background participates in the same recursive hierarchy, its recurrence also inherits recursive compression. Small-scale background recurrence is fast. Large-scale background recurrence is slow. Recurrence therefore gives background a scale reference. Its nondirectionality provides the complementary frame reference: where the physical background is isotropic with respect to a directional coordinate, motion is read relative to the frame in which the background has no net directional bias. Temperature does not itself define that frame; in a thermal system it calibrates the spread of subordinate realization about it. Background therefore supplies both scale and frame while remaining distinct from the directed turbine coordinates.

This becomes particularly important at cosmological scale. The cosmic radiation background is ordinarily interpreted as a remnant of an earlier hot universe. R²D begins from a more primitive observation: a pervasive thermal background is readable now.

Because background is a primitive R2D relation rather than a historical residue, the cosmic background admits another interpretation. It may be the physical read of a very large recursively realized background scale and frame. If that identification is correct, its low characteristic recurrence is not an additional assumption. Low recurrence at large recursive scale is already required by recurrence compression, while its large-scale nondirectionality supplies a physical frame reference. What remains to be established empirically is whether the observed cosmic background is in fact the physical realization of that large-scale R2D background and whether the theory can reproduce its detailed observations.

This also changes the problem of origin. R²D does not begin by asking what happened at the first moment of time, because physical time is itself a recurrence read. A derived clock coordinate cannot simply be assumed as the primitive container in which the hierarchy producing recurrence first came into existence. The R²D origin question is therefore structural rather than initially temporal: **Does recursive realization terminate?**

There may be a smallest subordinate boundary. There may be a largest enclosing boundary. There may be neither. The largest presently readable boundary need not be the largest boundary that exists. If a largest enclosing boundary exists but has not completed closure, an observer within it cannot directly count one full recurrence of that boundary. There is therefore no internally completed master clock from which a primitive age of the entire recursive hierarchy can be read.

A cosmological age may still be constructed from observations. A history may still be inferred. But those are projected physical coordinates, not primitive starting assumptions.

This is where Part III ends and Part IV begins. The intellectual map of the transition is:

Part III asks what the R²D primitives require and how those primitive relations become physically readable. Part IV asks how are the surviving one-sided reads reconstructed as primitive physical objects.

Thus, Part III is not an alternative quantum mechanics, an alternative theory of gravity, or an alternative cosmology. It comes before all three. ## Chapter 1 — The Two Sides of a Thermal Turbine
### 1.1 Why Begin With a Turbine?
Part II established the physical regime in which R²D is most directly testable.

There, recursive count relations acquire thermodynamic reads. Distinguishability can appear as displacement or another physical coordinate. Recurrence can be read as physical rate. Constraint can be manipulated experimentally. Occupancy can be measured. Heat and work can be separated. Background can be observed through its thermodynamic consequences.

The central object connecting these relations was the thermal turbine. The word *turbine* should not be taken too literally. An R²D thermal turbine need not contain a rotor. It need not generate macroscopic mechanical work. It need not even be irreversible. A thermal turbine is a recursively realized relation between adjacent count boundaries. At one boundary, a local closure is realized. At an enclosing boundary, that closure has a different count meaning as part of an enclosing occurrence.

The same architecture can appear in a molecular switch, a chemical reaction, a muscle, an atmosphere, an atom, or a planetary system if the appropriate recursive relation is physically realized. Thus, the turbine is not an object. It is a relation:

$$\text{local~closure} \leftrightarrow \text{enclosing~occurrence}$$

Part III follows this relation away from the regime in which both sides can be read together. Before doing so, the meaning of the two sides must be made precise.

### 1.2 Adjacent Boundaries Define Different Count Domains
Let

$$B_{n}$$

be a local boundary. Within that boundary, a completed closure is denoted

$$\mathcal{C}_{n,a},$$

where $a$ labels the particular closure class when more than one is available. Now consider the next enclosing boundary,

$$B_{n + 1}.$$

At $B_{n + 1}$, the completed $B_{n}$ closure does not remain the same state with the same identity. The count domain has changed. The local closure participates in an enclosing occurrence,

$$\chi_{n + 1}.$$

Thus,

$$\mathcal{C}_{n,a} \neq \chi_{n + 1}$$

They are not one event viewed at two resolutions. They are two boundary-relative classifications of one recursively organized realization. This distinction is fundamental.

If the local event simply retained its identity at the enclosing boundary, recursive replacement would reduce to coarse graining. R²D instead requires a change in count domain. The enclosing boundary defines a new state architecture.

### 1.3 Causality and Readout Point in Different Logical Directions
The two-sided language can easily create a causal mistake. It can sound as though local closures occur first, accumulate upward, and thereby create the enclosing event. That is not the R²D causal architecture.

The enclosing occurrence defines which subordinate realizations are compatible with it. Causally,

$$\chi_{n + 1} \Rightarrow \mathcal{C}_{n,a}$$

The larger relation constrains the local realization. The subordinate closure is necessary for the enclosing occurrence to be locally realized, but it does not independently originate the direction of the enclosing event. After local completion, however, the closure can be counted from the enclosing boundary. Schematically,

$$\mathcal{C}_{n,a} \mapsto \chi_{n + 1}$$

This second arrow is not reverse causation. It is recursive reclassification. The distinction can therefore be summarized: enclosing organization acts downward while completed realization is counted upward. The thermal turbine connects these two boundary reads without reversing their causal order.

### 1.4 A Turbine Relates Closure to Recurrence
Let

$$N_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}\left( \mathcal{C}_{n,a} \right)$$

denote the number of completed local closures counted within some common recurrence-comparison domain

$$\mathcal{D}_{R}.$$

Within that same comparison domain, let

$$N_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}\left( \chi_{n + 1} \right)$$

denote the number of corresponding enclosing occurrences. Their adjacent-boundary recurrence ratio is

$$\rho_{n + 1 \mid n,a} = \frac{N_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}\left( \chi_{n + 1} \right)}{N_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}\left( \mathcal{C}_{n,a} \right)}$$

This is a count ratio. It is defined before physical time. If a common physical observation interval is subsequently supplied,

$$r_{n} = \frac{N_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}\left( \mathcal{C}_{n,a} \right)}{\Delta t}$$and

$$r_{n + 1} = \frac{N_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}\left( \chi_{n + 1} \right)}{\Delta t},$$so that

$$\rho_{n + 1 \mid n,a} = \frac{r_{n + 1}}{r_{n}}$$

Physical rate therefore reads recurrence. It does not define it.

This distinction will become critical in Chapter 2, where recursive replacement will be shown to impose an ordering on these recurrence counts before any physical clock is introduced.

### 1.5 The Two Sides Are Not Two Objects
The word *side* should also be interpreted carefully. A turbine does not contain a local object on one side and an enclosing object on the other. It contains two count relations. At $B_{n}$, the relevant quantities include local distinguishability, recurrence, multiplicity, possibility, retention, and asymmetry. At $B_{n + 1}$, the same recursively organized realization is represented through a different boundary-defined distinction and a different recurrence. The R²D bridge relates these quantities.

In count-weighted form, the adjacent-boundary relation can be written schematically as

$$\Delta A_{n,a\mathcal{,C}}N_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}\left( \mathcal{C}_{n,a} \right) = C_{n + 1}\lambda_{n + 1}N_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}\left( \chi_{n + 1} \right)$$Equivalently,

$$\Delta A_{n,a\mathcal{,C}} = C_{n + 1}\lambda_{n + 1}\rho_{n + 1 \mid n,a}$$

The left side reads the local closure. The right side reads the enclosing occurrence. The equation does not state that one side contains a substance transferred to the other. It states that one recursively realized event possesses two boundary-relative count descriptions.

### 1.6 Local Retention and Enclosing Background Must Remain Distinct
Part II also established an important refinement of the enclosing constraint. Write

$$C_{n + 1} = C_{n + 1}^{out} + C_{n + 1}^{\Phi}.$$

The productive component,

$$C_{n + 1}^{out},$$

acts in the local retention relation:

$$\boxed{R_{n} = C_{n + 1}^{out}\lambda_{n}.}$$Thus, the local asymmetry is

$$\boxed{A_{n} = P_{n} - C_{n + 1}^{out}\lambda_{n}.}$$The background-associated component,

$$C_{n + 1}^{\Phi},$$

does not supply an additional local retention term. It appears on the enclosing realization side. This distinction is important for Part III because background will remain readable even when one directed side of the turbine does not.

Background is not therefore the missing side of the turbine. Nor is it the local constraint that determines retention. It is a distinct realized relation.

### 1.7 What Joint Readability Actually Means
Part II relied heavily on the idea of joint readability. That phrase must not be allowed to contradict boundary-relative statehood. An enclosing boundary does not suddenly become capable of directly resolving the internal state identities of a subordinate boundary.

By construction, subordinate event identity is replaced when the count boundary changes. Joint readability means something more precise. A common experimental or physical comparison domain can separately access physical reads belonging to adjacent recursive boundaries. Thus, an observer may count

$$N_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}\left( \mathcal{C}_{n} \right)$$and

$$N_{\mathcal{D}_{R}}\left( \chi_{n + 1} \right)$$

without claiming that $B_{n + 1}$ directly sees the internal identity of $B_{n}$.

Likewise, an experiment may measure a local molecular recurrence and an enclosing mechanical output. The two quantities remain boundary specific. They simply become physically comparable. Therefore,

$$\text{joint~readability} \neq \text{collapse~of~adjacent~boundaries~into~one~count~domain}$$

It means that the physical reads belonging to both boundaries remain accessible within one comparison structure.

### 1.8 Thermodynamics Is the Jointly Readable Regime
This is what makes thermodynamics unusually important in R²D. Consider muscle. At a local molecular boundary, closure recurrence can be read through ATP turnover. A local distinguishability can acquire a mechanical read through molecular displacement. At the enclosing muscle boundary, force and shortening velocity can be measured. Heat production can be measured. Occupancy can be measured. Constraint can be altered experimentally. The corresponding physical quantities can therefore be compared within the same functioning system.

This does not make muscle the primitive basis of the theory. Part I deliberately removed muscle-specific vocabulary from the primitives. Muscle is important because it exposes the recursive architecture unusually well. Thermodynamics more generally occupies the same privileged position:

$$\text{local~closure~and~enclosing~realization~can~remain~jointly~readable}$$

Part III asks what happens as that condition fails.

### 1.9 Readability Is Boundary Relative
A readability horizon cannot be defined without specifying from where the read is attempted. To make this explicit, let

$$\mathcal{R}_{B_{O}}(X)$$

denote schematically whether quantity $X$ is readable from observing boundary $B_{O}$:

$$\mathcal{R}_{B_{O}}(X) = 1$$

when the relevant distinction is physically resolvable from that observing boundary, and

$$\mathcal{R}_{B_{O}}(X) = 0$$

when it is not.

This is only shorthand. It is not a new primitive R²D quantity. Its purpose is to prevent an important mistake. A quantity can be perfectly readable at its own boundary while being unreadable from another. Thus,

$$\mathcal{R}_{B_{n}}\left( \lambda_{n} \right) = 1$$

does not imply

$$\mathcal{R}_{B_{n + 1}}\left( \lambda_{n} \right) = 1.$$

This is exactly what boundary-relative statehood requires. The horizons of Part III are therefore not places where a physical quantity ceases to exist. They are boundaries beyond which a particular recursive relation is no longer jointly readable from the observing domain.

### 1.10 One-Sided Readability
Suppose the turbine remains recursively realized. Its local closure still occurs. Its enclosing relation still exists. But the observer can no longer access both sides in one physical comparison domain.

The turbine has become **one-sidedly readable**. This is the central physical condition of Part III. There are two logically opposite ways for it to happen. Toward subordinate closure, recurrence can remain readable after the distinguishability associated with that closure is no longer resolvable. Toward enclosing closure, distinguishability can remain readable after the corresponding enclosing recurrence is no longer resolvable.

These are the two readability horizons.

### 1.11 The Quantum-Side Horizon
Toward the subordinate side of an observing boundary, recursive realization eventually reaches a regime in which the local geometry of closure can no longer be read in the same manner as its recurrence. The observer may still read a spectral frequency. The observer may still count a discrete occurrence. The observer may still measure a reproducible transition. But the continuously resolved subordinate distinction that would provide a classical trajectory is unavailable. Schematically,

$$\mathcal{R}_{B_{O}}(\rho) = 1, \mathcal{R}_{B_{O}}\left( \lambda_{sub} \right) = 0.$$

This is the quantum readability horizon. The relation does not say that subordinate distinguishability has been destroyed. It says that the observing boundary retains recurrence while losing direct access to the associated subordinate geometry. Blackbody radiation and atomic closure will provide the principal physical tests of this claim.

### 1.12 The Gravitational-Side Horizon
The opposite condition occurs toward enclosing closure. A persistent distinction may remain directly readable. A spatial or areal relation may remain observable. A local closure may remain apparent. Yet the larger recurrence within which that local closure participates can become inaccessible. Schematically,

$$\mathcal{R}_{B_{O}}(\lambda) = 1, \mathcal{R}_{B_{O}}\left( \rho_{enc} \right) = 0$$

This is the gravitational readability horizon. Again, unreadability does not imply disappearance. The enclosing recurrence need not have stopped. It has ceased to remain jointly resolvable from the observing boundary. Planetary closure will show the approach toward this condition. The black-hole horizon will provide its limiting physical expression.

### 1.13 The Two Horizons Point in Opposite Recursive Directions
The symmetry can therefore be stated without invoking quantum or gravitational equations. Toward smaller subordinate closure recurrence survives the read; subordinate distinguishability does not. Toward larger enclosing closure distinguishability survives the read; enclosing recurrence does not. The observer lies between these two directions:

$$\text{unreadable~subordinate~closure} \leftarrow B_{O} \rightarrow \text{unreadable~enclosing~closure}$$

Thermodynamics occupies the interval in which unusually much of both directions remains accessible. This is the basic geometry of Part III.

### 1.14 Background Is Not a Third Horizon Coordinate
Background must now be placed within this architecture. At $B_{n}$,

$$b_{n}$$

denotes realized background count, with logarithmic coordinate

$$\Phi_{n} = \ln b_{n}.$$

Background is nondirectional relative to the landscape in which it is background. Thus,

$$\Delta\Phi_{n} = 0$$

across the local directional alternatives being compared. This means background is fundamentally different from the two directed turbine reads. It is not another version of distinguishability. Its nondirectionality nevertheless has a positive physical role: it supplies the local reference against which a directional distinction can be assigned.

It is not another version of recurrence. It is not possibility. And it does not define a third horizon. Instead, background is realized count whose relevant subordinate directional classification is no longer readable at that boundary. Where the physical realization of that background is isotropic with respect to a directional coordinate, the background defines the boundary-relative zero-direction frame for that coordinate.

This makes background unusually capable of persisting across changing readability regimes. Its recurrence can supply a scale reference, while its nondirectionality supplies a frame reference. The quantum horizon can remove a subordinate directed distinction from the read while background remains. The gravitational horizon can remove an enclosing directed recurrence from the read while background remains. The physical recurrence and frame-reference roles of that background hierarchy will be developed in Chapter 2.

### 1.15 A Readability Horizon Is Not Landscape Collapse
Part II introduced a different phenomenon. A mathematically definable enclosing landscape need not remain recursively realized. If the subordinate distinctions and recurrences required to sustain an enclosing count domain are no longer sufficient, the enclosing landscape itself can cease to be realized. That is landscape collapse.

A readability horizon is different. At a horizon, the recursive relation remains physically consequential. What changes is whether an observing boundary can read both sides. Thus,

$$\begin{aligned}
\text{landscape~collapse} & = \text{loss~of~the~realized~enclosing~count~domain}, \\
\text{readability~horizon} & = \text{loss~of~one~read~of~a~still-realized~recursive~relation}.
\end{aligned}$$

A quantum horizon is not automatically the destruction of an enclosing landscape. A black-hole horizon is not automatically collapse of the recursive hierarchy. Unreadability and nonrealization are different conditions.

### 1.16 A Readability Horizon Is Not Irreversibility
The horizons must also remain distinct from the Second Law. Part I defines closure nonreciprocity through

$$L_{\tau} = - \ln\eta \geq 0$$

and relates nonreciprocity to background production through

$$\delta_{\mathcal{C}}\Phi = L_{\tau}.$$Neither horizon requires

$$L_{\tau} > 0.$$

A perfectly reciprocal idealized closure may still be unreadable from another boundary. Conversely, an ordinary thermodynamic system may be irreversible while both relevant sides remain readable.

Therefore, readability is not reciprocity. Part III concerns the first. The Second Law concerns the second. Real physical systems may involve both, but they should not be identified.

### 1.17 A Readability Horizon Is Not Projection
A third distinction defines the boundary between Parts III and IV. A horizon concerns what remains readable. Projection concerns what an observer constructs from that surviving read. At the quantum horizon, recurrence remains. A later theory may represent that recurrence through particles, wavefunctions, operators, amplitudes, and phase.

At the gravitational horizon, distinguishability remains. A later theory may represent that surviving distinction through mass, force, potential, field, metric, and curvature. Thus, horizon is the loss of joint readability whereas projection is the reconstruction of the missing relation from what remains.

Part III stops before assigning primitive ontology to that reconstruction. Part IV begins there.

### 1.18 A Measuring Device Does Not Escape the Architecture
This distinction also prevents the observer from being placed outside the theory. A measuring device has its own boundary. It has its own multiplicity. It has occupancy, recurrence, constraint, closure, and background. It is another turbine.

Thus, a measuring apparatus cannot provide a view from nowhere. It can only create a new enclosing boundary within which a previously one-sided relation acquires a new readable distinction.

This will become central on the quantum side. Measurement does not remove boundary relativity. It recursively realizes another boundary.

### 1.19 The Observer Never Reads the Whole Hierarchy
The same limitation applies generally. No finite observing boundary has direct access to every subordinate and enclosing count domain simultaneously. This is not an accidental technological limitation. It follows from recursive replacement itself. At every change of boundary, the state classification changes. Therefore, complete readability of the entire hierarchy is not the primitive condition from which quantum and gravitational physics deviate.

The opposite is closer to the R²D architecture. Limited readability is primitive. Joint readability is exceptional. Thermodynamics is important precisely because it provides a physical window through which unusually many adjacent recursive relations can be compared.

### 1.20 Two Count Consequences Will Organize Part III
Once the two-sided turbine has been defined, the rest of Part III follows from two consequences of the primitive theory.

The first acts **across boundaries**.

Recursive replacement is many-to-one. Multiple subordinate closure occurrences acquire one enclosing occurrence meaning. This produces recurrence compression. The result is the recursive clock. Chapter 2 will show that larger recursively enclosing closure necessarily possesses lower recursively mapped recurrence within a common comparison domain. Physical frequency will then appear as a read of that already ordered count hierarchy.

The second consequence acts primarily **within a boundary**. R²D asymmetry determines relative occupancy.

Once the admissible occupancy domain is specified, normalization produces familiar statistical forms. Binary occupancy gives the Fermi--Dirac form. Repeatable occupancy gives the Bose--Einstein form. The sparse limit gives the Boltzmann form. Chapter 3 will derive these relations before particles, spin, exchange symmetry, or wavefunctions are introduced.

Thus, the remainder of Part III will be built from two primitive structures:

$$\text{across~boundaries:~recurrence~ordering}$$and

$$\text{within~boundaries:~occupancy~ordering}$$

Quantum physics and gravity will then be approached as physical readability regimes of those relations.

### 1.21 The Physical Program
The empirical sequence of Part III can now be stated. Blackbody radiation will test repeatable occupancy at the high-frequency quantum horizon. Atomic closure will test persistent recurrence after continuous subordinate geometry has disappeared from the read. Measuring devices will show how quantum recurrence becomes a new thermodynamic distinction. The atmosphere will provide a gravitational system in which thermal background, occupancy, and mechanical distinction remain jointly readable. Planetary closure will expose bound geometry and recurrence after the enclosing thermal reference has become hidden. The black-hole horizon will test the limiting gravitational condition in which distinguishability remains while directed enclosing recurrence is unavailable. The cosmic background will then ask what remains readable of the enclosing background hierarchy itself.

These systems do not define the R²D principles. They test their physical realizations.

### 1.22 Principle — Two-Sided Thermal-Turbine Readability
**Principle -- Two-Sided Thermal-Turbine Readability**

A thermal turbine is a recursively realized relation between a local closure and an enclosing occurrence defined at adjacent count boundaries. The enclosing occurrence determines the compatible local realizations through which it is physically realized. Completed local closure is subsequently reclassified within the enclosing count domain. These are distinct causal and counting directions.

Thermodynamic joint readability occurs when physical reads belonging to both boundaries can be compared within a common observation domain without collapsing their boundary-relative identities. A quantum readability horizon occurs when recurrence remains readable while the associated subordinate distinguishability no longer remains jointly accessible. A gravitational readability horizon occurs when distinguishability remains readable while the relevant enclosing recurrence no longer remains jointly accessible. Background is a distinct nondirectional realized count relation and does not define a third horizon.

A readability horizon therefore marks neither landscape collapse nor irreversibility nor projection. It marks the loss of one physical read of a still-realized recursive relation.

### 1.23 What Has Been Established
The first chapter establishes the object that Part III will follow. A thermal turbine is not a mechanism added to R²D. It is the physical realization of the adjacent-boundary relation already defined by recursive counting. Its two sides are not two independent causes. They are two boundary-relative reads of one recursively organized realization.

The thermodynamic regime is unusual because those reads can sometimes remain accessible within a common physical comparison domain. The two horizons arise when this joint readability fails in opposite recursive directions. Toward subordinate closure, recurrence survives while distinguishability disappears from the read. Toward enclosing closure, distinguishability survives while recurrence disappears from the read. Background remains distinct from both.

Nothing in this chapter yet requires quantum mechanics or gravitational mechanics. Before those physical regimes are examined, one structural consequence of recursive realization must now be developed.

## Chapter 2 — The Recursive Clock: Recurrence Compression Across Boundaries
### 2.1 The Clock Is Already in Recursive Realization
Chapter 1 defined a thermal turbine as an adjacent-boundary relation between a local closure and an enclosing occurrence. At boundary $B_{n}$, a closure is realized:

$$\mathcal{C}_{n}.$$

At the enclosing boundary $B_{n + 1}$, that completed closure acquires a different count meaning within

$$\chi_{n + 1}.$$

The causal direction is

$$\chi_{n + 1} \Rightarrow \mathcal{C}_{n}.$$

The enclosing occurrence defines which subordinate realizations are compatible with it. But the counting relation has another property. One enclosing occurrence is realized through **many occurrences below it**. Viewed from $B_{n + 1}$ toward $B_{n}$, the enclosing occurrence branches. Viewed from $B_{n}$ toward $B_{n + 1}$, multiple completed subordinate occurrences are recursively replaced by fewer enclosing occurrences. That many-to-one count structure already contains an ordering of recurrence.

The recursive clock does not have to be added to R²D. It follows from recursive realization itself.

### 2.2 One Enclosing Occurrence Branches Into Many Subordinate Realizations
Consider one occurrence at $B_{n + 1}$,

$$\chi_{n + 1}.$$

Its local realization may require closure across multiple subordinate boundaries,

$$\mathcal{C}_{n,1},\mathcal{C}_{n,2},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{n,d}.$$

Schematically,

$$\chi_{n + 1} \Rightarrow \left\{ \mathcal{C}_{n,1},\mathcal{C}_{n,2},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{n,d} \right\}$$

The notation does not imply that all subordinate closures are identical or occur in a simple temporal sequence. Some may occur repeatedly. Some may belong to different peer boundaries. Some may be realized in parallel. The essential property is only that the enclosing occurrence has a **degenerate subordinate realization**.

More than one completed subordinate occurrence belongs to the realization of one enclosing occurrence. This is recursive branching. It is also recursive replacement. At $B_{n + 1}$, the individual subordinate closure identities no longer remain primitive state identities. Their realized consequence belongs to the enclosing occurrence count. Thus:

$$\text{one~occurrence~above} \longleftrightarrow \text{many~realized~occurrences~below}$$

The left-to-right relation expresses realization. The right-to-left relation expresses count replacement. Neither reverses the causal architecture established in Chapter 1.

### 2.3 Realized Closure Degeneracy
For the present derivation, let

$$d_{n + 1 \mid n}$$

denote the **realized closure degeneracy** between adjacent boundaries: the number of completed $B_{n}$ closure occurrences counted within the recursive realization of one $B_{n + 1}$ occurrence in the relevant comparison domain.

This is not a new primitive R²D quantity. It is shorthand for the many-to-one recurrence relation. For a nontrivial recursive replacement,

$$d_{n + 1 \mid n} > 1.$$If

$$N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}$$

subordinate closure occurrences are realized while

$$N_{\mathcal{C}_{n + 1}}$$

enclosing closures are counted over the same recursive comparison domain, then

$$N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}} = d_{n + 1 \mid n}N_{\mathcal{C}_{n + 1}}.$$Therefore,

$$\frac{N_{\mathcal{C}_{n + 1}}}{N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}} = \frac{1}{d_{n + 1 \mid n}}.$$

The ratio on the left is already the adjacent-boundary recurrence ratio introduced in Chapter 1.

Thus,

$$\rho_{n + 1 \mid n} = \frac{1}{d_{n + 1 \mid n}}$$

For nontrivial recursive replacement,

$$0 < \rho_{n + 1 \mid n} < 1.$$This is recurrence compression.

### 2.4 Theorem — Recursive Recurrence Compression
**Theorem -- Recursive Recurrence Compression**

If one completed enclosing occurrence at $B_{n + 1}$ is recursively realized through more than one completed subordinate closure occurrence at $B_{n}$, then recursively mapped closure recurrence is compressed toward the enclosing boundary. That is,

$$N_{\mathcal{C}_{n + 1}} < N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}$$

within a common recursive comparison domain. Equivalently,

$$0 < \rho_{n + 1 \mid n} < 1.$$

The theorem requires only the many-to-one structure of recursive realization. No physical clock is required. No energy is required. No particle, trajectory, force, or field is required. Recurrence is ordered before it is assigned a physical rate.

### 2.5 Recurrence Compression Is the Counting Complement of Branching
The theorem can be understood from either recursive direction. Viewed downward,

$$\text{one~enclosing~occurrence} \rightarrow \text{many~subordinate~realizations}$$

This is branching. Viewed upward,

$$\text{many~subordinate~closures} \rightarrow \text{fewer~enclosing~counts}$$

This is compression.

They are the same recursive architecture viewed from opposite count boundaries. The word *compression* should not imply that subordinate events are physically squeezed together or destroyed. Their identity is replaced because the count domain changes. Recurrence compression occurs when many realized subordinate closures acquiring fewer enclosing occurrence identities.

This is a count relation, not a mechanism of energy transfer.

### 2.6 Recurrence Compression Accumulates Across Scale
Now consider a hierarchy

$$B_{n} \rightarrow B_{n + 1} \rightarrow B_{n + 2} \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow B_{N}.$$

At each nontrivial bridge,

$$0 < \rho_{j + 1 \mid j} < 1.$$Then

$${N_{\mathcal{C}_{n + 1}} = \rho_{n + 1 \mid n}N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}},
}{N_{\mathcal{C}_{n + 2}} = \rho_{n + 2 \mid n + 1}N_{\mathcal{C}_{n + 1}},
}$$and so on. Therefore,

$$N_{\mathcal{C}_{N}} = N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}\prod_{j = n}^{N - 1}\rho_{j + 1 \mid j}$$Equivalently,

$$\frac{N_{\mathcal{C}_{N}}}{N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}} = \prod_{j = n}^{N - 1}\rho_{j + 1 \mid j}$$Because each factor is less than one,

$$N_{\mathcal{C}_{N}} < N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}$$

Recursive recurrence therefore forms an ordered hierarchy. At increasingly enclosing boundaries, completed closure becomes progressively sparse relative to the subordinate recurrence through which it is realized.

### 2.7 The Logarithmic Recurrence Ladder
Because R²D is naturally logarithmic, the same relation can be expressed additively. Taking logarithms,

$$\ln\frac{N_{\mathcal{C}_{N}}}{N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}} = {\sum_{j = n}^{N - 1}\ln}\rho_{j + 1 \mid j}.$$Since

$$0 < \rho_{j + 1 \mid j} < 1,$$

each term satisfies

$$\ln\rho_{j + 1 \mid j} < 0.$$Thus, recurrence compression accumulates monotonically across nontrivial recursive replacement. The multiplicative hierarchy

$$\prod_{}\rho$$

therefore becomes an additive logarithmic ladder

$${\sum_{}\ln}\rho.$$

This is important for the horizon architecture developed later. A small change at each adjacent boundary can accumulate into an enormous separation of recurrence across many boundaries. No single bridge must contain an extreme scale difference. The hierarchy creates it recursively.

### 2.8 Physical Time Reads the Count Hierarchy
So far, no physical time has been introduced. Now compare the closure counts over one common physical observation interval,

$$\Delta t.$$Define

$$r_{n} = \frac{N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}}{\Delta t}$$and

$$r_{n + 1} = \frac{N_{\mathcal{C}_{n + 1}}}{\Delta t}.$$Then

$$\frac{r_{n + 1}}{r_{n}} = \frac{N_{\mathcal{C}_{n + 1}}}{N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}}.$$Therefore,

$$\frac{r_{n + 1}}{r_{n}} = \rho_{n + 1 \mid n}$$For nontrivial recursive replacement,

$$r_{n + 1} < r_{n}.$$

Physical rate has not produced this ordering. The ordering existed in the recurrence counts. Physical time merely makes it readable as a rate. Thus:

$$\text{recursive~count~ordering} \rightarrow \text{physical~rate~orderin}\text{g.}$$

### 2.9 Frequency Is a Physical Read of Recurrence
When the recurrent physical realization is naturally represented as a frequency, write

$$f_{n}.$$

Then, under a common physical clock calibration,

$$\frac{f_{n + 1}}{f_{n}} = \rho_{n + 1 \mid n}.$$Therefore,

$$f_{n + 1} = \rho_{n + 1 \mid n}f_{n}.$$For a nontrivial recursive bridge,

$$f_{n + 1} < f_{n}.$$Across the hierarchy,

$$f_{N} = f_{n}\prod_{j = n}^{N - 1}\rho_{j + 1 \mid j}.$$

Thus, physical frequency can expose the recursive recurrence ladder directly. This is the **physical recursive clock**.

### 2.10 A Necessary Qualification: Aggregate Recurrence and Individual Oscillators
The recurrence-compression theorem should not be overstated. Suppose one enclosing occurrence is realized through ten subordinate peer boundaries, each completing one closure in parallel. Then there are ten subordinate closure occurrences for one enclosing occurrence:

$$N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}} = 10N_{\mathcal{C}_{n + 1}}.$$

But this alone does not prove that every one of those ten subordinate systems possesses a temporal period one tenth that of the enclosing system.

Parallel branching increases the aggregate subordinate recurrence count without necessarily increasing the temporal cycling frequency of every subordinate constituent. Therefore the theorem establishes directly that aggregate recursively mapped closure recurrence is compressed upward.

A characteristic physical frequency follows when the counted recurrence defines a coherent boundary-level closure class whose count per physical interval is the relevant physical frequency read. Thus, R²D does **not** claim that every microscopic process is faster than every macroscopic process. It claims that recursively mapped boundary-level closure recurrence is compressed toward enclosing boundaries. That is the relation required by the recursive clock.

### 2.11 Recurrence Is Not Multiplicity
The recursive clock must also remain separate from native multiplicity. At state $i$,

$$W_{n,i}$$

counts the compatible realizations belonging to that state. It describes possible structure. By contrast,

$$N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}$$

counts completed realized closures. Therefore,

$$W_{n} \neq N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}$$

A state architecture can remain unchanged while recurrence changes dramatically. Thus,

$$\Delta W_{n} = 0 \Rightarrow \Delta N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}} = 0$$

This distinction is essential because the recursive clock does not follow merely from having more possible states below than above. It follows from the **realized many-to-one recurrence relation**. Native multiplicity structures what can occur. Recursive recurrence counts how often closure is realized.

### 2.12 Enclosing Organization Can Change Recurrence Without Changing Multiplicity
This separation also explains why recurrence rates can change under altered conditions while the state architecture remains fixed. A molecular switch may have the same two states before and after a change in load. An atom may possess the same allowed state structure under conditions in which transition recurence changes. A thermal system may preserve its native multiplicity landscape while its recurrence frequency changes with temperature. Thus:

$$\text{fixed~state~architecture} + \text{changed~enclosing~relation} \rightarrow \text{changed~recurrence}$$

The recursive clock is therefore not a rigid table assigning one universal frequency to every boundary. It is an ordering relation produced by recursive realization. Its actual physical frequency depends on the realization geometry, compatibility, constraint, and comparison domain.

What recursion fixes is the direction of the many-to-one recurrence relation.

### 2.13 Background Participates in the Same Recursive Architecture
Part I defines background independently of recurrence frequency. At boundary $B_{n}$,

$$b_{n}$$

is realized background count, with

$$\Phi_{n} = \ln b_{n}.$$

Background is nondirectional relative to the local alternatives under consideration. Its relevant subordinate directional classification has already been lost at the boundary where it is background. But background has not thereby ceased to be realized. It can recur. Let

$$N_{n}^{\Phi}$$

denote a count of recurrent background realization within an appropriate comparison domain. Recursive replacement applies to those realized occurrences just as it does to other boundary-level recurrence. Therefore, where background at $B_{n + 1}$ is recursively realized through multiple background-associated occurrences at $B_{n}$,

$$N_{n + 1}^{\Phi} = \rho_{n + 1 \mid n}^{\Phi}N_{n}^{\Phi}$$with

$$0 < \rho_{n + 1 \mid n}^{\Phi} < 1$$

Background therefore inherits recurrence compression.

### 2.14 Background Count Is Not Background Frequency
The distinction between

$$\Phi_{n}$$

and its physical recurrence read must remain explicit. Background is defined as

$$\Phi_{n} = \ln b_{n}.$$

That equation contains no physical frequency. If recurrent background realization is counted over physical time,

$$r_{n}^{\Phi} = \frac{N_{n}^{\Phi}}{\Delta t}.$$

Where that recurrence is naturally expressed as a frequency, denote the physical read by

$$f_{n}^{\Phi}.$$

Thus:

$$\Phi_{n} = \text{logarithmic~background~count}\text{,}$$whereas

$$f_{n}^{\Phi} = \text{physical~frequency~read~of~recurrent~background~realization}$$

These are not synonymous. But the physical frequency inherits the count ordering:

$$f_{n + 1}^{\Phi} = \rho_{n + 1 \mid n}^{\Phi}f_{n}^{\Phi}.$$Therefore,

$$f_{n + 1}^{\Phi} < f_{n}^{\Phi}$$

for a nontrivial recursive background bridge.

### 2.15 Background Defines Scale and Frame
This clarifies the status of the background hierarchy. Part III does not independently postulate that small-scale background is fast and large-scale background is slow. Recursive replacement already orders background recurrence. That ordering gives one part of background\'s reference function: recurrence supplies a scale against which faster and slower directed realization can be read. Formally,

$$0 < \rho_{n + 1 \mid n}^{\Phi} < 1.$$

Physical frequency then reads that recurrence compression as

Thus:

$$\text{recursive~branching} \rightarrow \text{recurrence~compression} \rightarrow \text{physical~background-frequency~hierarchy}$$

Only the final calibration to a particular physical quantity remains system dependent. For a thermal boundary, later chapters will ask whether a frequency scale such as

Background has a second reference function that does not follow from recurrence magnitude but from nondirectionality. Where the physical background is isotropic in a velocity coordinate, let the physical velocity of the background frame be denoted by u with boundary index n and background superscript Φ.

$$v_{\text{rel}} = v - u_{\text{n}}^{\Phi}$$

The directional velocity read is therefore relative to the background. In the frame defined by that background, the mean directional read vanishes:

Temperature does not define this frame. In a thermal gas, temperature calibrates the width of the molecular velocity distribution about the background-defined zero. The thermal background defines the frame; temperature calibrates the spread of subordinate molecular velocities within it.

The two roles are therefore complementary. Background recurrence supplies a scale reference. Background nondirectionality supplies a frame reference. Neither requires absolute space or a universal external clock; both are boundary relative.

### 2.16 Scale Is First a Recursive Relation, Not a Length
This result also clarifies what *scale* means in R²D. The index

The phrase *recursive clock* might also suggest a single master oscillator driving all subordinate systems. That is not the R²D meaning. Each boundary has its own recurrence structure. The hierarchy is relational:

$$\rho_{n + 1 \mid n} = \frac{N_{\mathcal{C}_{n + 1}}}{N_{\mathcal{C}_{n}}}$$

No external universal clock is needed to define this ratio. Physical clocks are introduced only when the recurrence hierarchy is mapped onto measured rates. Thus the recursive clock is not one clock outside the hierarchy. It is the ordered recurrence relation of the hierarchy itself.

This distinction will become especially important at cosmological scale, where an external clock for the entire hierarchy cannot simply be assumed.

Repeated recursive compression has an immediate consequence for an observer. Toward successively subordinate closure, the recurrence count increases relative to the observer\'s boundary. Schematically,

Gravity therefore lies toward the slow side of the recursive clock.

### 2.20 The Two Horizons Follow the Same Clock in Opposite Directions
The recursive clock now supplies a common scale architecture for Part III. Toward the subordinate side recurrence increases. Toward the enclosing side recurrence decreases. ## Chapter 3 — Occupancy Statistics Before Quantum Mechanics
### 3.1 The Statistics Are Already in the Count Architecture
Chapter 2 established the first general consequence of recursive realization. Across boundaries, many-to-one recursive replacement compresses recurrence. Physical frequency subsequently reads that count ordering as the recursive clock. A second general consequence appears within a boundary.

R²D does not begin with probability. It begins with realized occupancy. For a state $i$,

$$c_{i}$$

is the realized occupancy count, and

$$G_{i} = \ln c_{i}$$

is its logarithmic occupancy coordinate. The primitive question is therefore not: what is the probability that a particle occupies this state? It is: how does recursive asymmetry bias realized occupancy among the distinctions defined by this boundary? Once those relative occupancies are known, normalization can be applied afterward.

The familiar Fermi--Dirac, Bose--Einstein, and Boltzmann forms then emerge from two ingredients:

$$R^{2}\text{D~occupancy~asymmetry} + \text{admissible~count~domain}$$

No particle ontology is required to obtain the count laws themselves.

### 3.2 Asymmetry Determines Occupancy Odds
Consider two distinguishable states,

$$a \text{and} b.$$

Their realized occupancies are

$$c_{a} \text{and} c_{b}.$$

The logarithmic occupancy difference is

$$G_{a} - G_{b} = \ln\frac{c_{a}}{c_{b}}.$$

Under the proportional-occurrence relation developed in Parts I and II,

$$\Delta G \approx \Delta S + \Delta P - \Delta R.$$

If the two alternatives have equal native multiplicity,

$$\Delta S = 0,$$

then their occupancy odds are determined entirely by possibility and retention:

$$\ln\frac{c_{a}}{c_{b}} = P - R.$$

Define the local asymmetry

$$A = P - R.$$Then

$$\frac{c_{a}}{c_{b}} = e^{A}$$

This is the primitive occupancy relation. Probability has not yet appeared.

### 3.3 Native Multiplicity Can Be Retained When Required
The equal-multiplicity case is especially useful because it exposes the occupancy statistics cleanly. But the more general relation should remain visible. If the two states have different native multiplicities,

$$W_{a} \neq W_{b},$$

then

$$\Delta S = \ln\frac{W_{a}}{W_{b}}.$$

The occupancy odds become

$$\frac{c_{a}}{c_{b}} \approx \frac{W_{a}}{W_{b}}e^{P - R}$$

Thus, R²D separates two effects. Native multiplicity structure the alternative while asymmetry biases their realized occupancy.

The familiar statistical distributions arise most transparently when one isolates a single occupancy class and asks what values of occupancy that class is permitted to take.

### 3.4 Binary Occupancy
Consider one boundary-defined occupancy class that can be either

$$m = 0$$or

$$m = 1.$$

These are the only admissible occupancies:

$$m \in \left\{ 0,1 \right\}$$

Let the occupancy asymmetry for adding one realized count be

$$A.$$

Then the relative count weights are

$$c_{0} \propto 1$$

and

$$c_{1} \propto e^{A}.$$Define

$$q = e^{A}.$$Then

$$c_{0}:c_{1} = 1:q.$$

The normalization count is

$$Z_{F} = 1 + q.$$

The normalized occupancy of the occupied state is therefore

$${\overline{m}}_{F} = \frac{q}{1 + q}.$$Substituting

$$q = e^{A}$$gives

$${\overline{m}}_{F} = \frac{1}{e^{- A} + 1}.$$The $+ 1$ has a very simple origin. There are exactly two admissible occupancy states: $0 \text{and} 1.$

### 3.5 The Fermi—Dirac Form
Under the conventional thermal calibration,

$$A = - \frac{\epsilon - \mu}{k_{B}T},$$

the binary occupancy law becomes

$${\overline{m}}_{F} = \frac{1}{e^{\frac{(\epsilon - \mu)}{\left( k_{B}T \right)}} + 1}$$

This is the Fermi--Dirac occupancy form. R²D reaches it without beginning with

-   fermions,

-   half-integer spin,

-   antisymmetric wavefunctions,

-   exchange operators,

-   or the Pauli principle as an independent statistical postulate.

The count derivation requires only an R²D asymmetry $A$ and an admissible binary occupancy domain. Thus, at the primitive level,

$$\text{Fermi–Dirac~form} = \text{normalized~binary~occupancy}$$

This statement concerns the occupancy mathematics. The additional conventional claims connecting that mathematics to spin, field operators, antisymmetric many-particle states, and relativistic spin-statistics belong to a later representational layer.

### 3.6 Muscle Already Contains the Binary Law
The significance of this result becomes clear outside quantum physics. Consider a molecular switch in muscle with mutually exclusive states

$$a \rightleftharpoons b.$$

Let their occupancies be

$$c_{a} \text{and} c_{b}.$$

For equal native multiplicity,

$$\frac{c_{a}}{c_{b}} = e^{A}.$$

Normalize over the two states:

$$p_{a} = \frac{c_{a}}{c_{a} + c_{b}}.$$

Then

$$p_{a} = \frac{1}{e^{- A} + 1}.$$

The same binary occupancy mathematics appears. No electron spin has been introduced. No quantum exchange argument is needed. The switch is simply a two-state realized count domain.

Thus, the mathematical form conventionally encountered as Fermi--Dirac occupancy is not uniquely quantum. It is the generic normalized law of a binary R²D occupancy relation.

### 3.7 Binary Statistics Precede Spin
This changes the logical status of spin statistics. At the primitive R²D level, there is nothing specifically quantum about a state that admits only two mutually exclusive realizations. The binary architecture is already

$$0 \text{or} 1.$$

That count structure generates the corresponding occupancy law. Quantum theory later represents particular binary state architectures through concepts such as spin and fermionic exclusion.

R²D therefore reverses the conventional explanatory order. It does not begin with:

$$\text{half-integer~spin} \rightarrow \text{Fermi~statistics}.$$

It begins with:

$$\text{binary~distinguishability} \rightarrow \text{binary~occupancy~statistics}\text{.}$$

Part IV can then ask why quantum projection represents particular instances of that count structure as spin-$\frac{1}{2}$, antisymmetric exchange, and fermionic identity. The primitive count law itself requires none of them.

### 3.8 Repeatable Occupancy
Now consider a different admissible count domain. Suppose the same boundary-defined occupancy class can be realized repeatedly:

$$m = 0,1,2,3,\ldots$$

with no finite upper occupancy imposed by that state definition. Thus,

$$m \in \left\{ 0,1,2,\ldots \right\}.$$

Suppose each additional occupancy contributes the same logarithmic asymmetry $A$. Then

$$\frac{c_{m + 1}}{c_{m}} = e^{A}.$$Again define

$$q = e^{A}.$$Then

$$c_{m} \propto q^{m}.$$For normalization,

$$q < 1,$$or equivalently,

$$A < 0.$$

The count normalization is the geometric series

$$Z_{B} = \sum_{m = 0}^{\infty}q^{m}.$$

Therefore,

$$Z_{B} = \frac{1}{1 - q}.$$

### 3.9 Mean Repeatable Occupancy
The mean occupancy is

$${\overline{m}}_{B} = \frac{\sum_{m = 0}^{\infty}mq^{m}}{\sum_{m = 0}^{\infty}q^{m}}.$$

Using

$$\sum_{m = 0}^{\infty}mq^{m} = \frac{q}{(1 - q)^{2}},$$gives

$${\overline{m}}_{B} = \frac{q}{1 - q}.$$Since

$$q = e^{A},$$we obtain

$${\overline{m}}_{B} = \frac{1}{e^{- A} - 1}.$$

The $- 1$ has an equally simple count origin. The occupancy series does not terminate at one. It continues:

$$0,1,2,3,\ldots$$

The denominator records the difference between a binary count domain and an indefinitely repeatable one.
